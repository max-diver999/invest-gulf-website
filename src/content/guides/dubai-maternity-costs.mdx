Expat maternity in Dubai runs through licensed private hospitals or DHA public facilities, with DHA-regulated insurance on most resident plans. Budget across pre-natal visits, scans, delivery, hospital stay, and newborn admin, not only the delivery package price.

Insider tip: Buy or upgrade maternity cover at least 12 months before you try to conceive. Once pregnancy is confirmed, insurers treat maternity as a pre-existing condition and will not add a rider in our research files.

## How does maternity care work in Dubai?

Maternity care typically runs from an OB-GYN confirmation visit through monthly antenatal checks, hospital delivery, and a 6-week post-natal review. DHA plans often cap maternity at AED 7,000 to 50,000 with 10 to 12 month waits. Our underwriting treats the insurance certificate as a clinical checklist.

Trimester 1 (weeks 1 to 12):
- Initial OB-GYN consultation and dating scan
- Blood work and baseline health screening
- Folic acid and early medication review with your doctor

Trimester 2 (weeks 13 to 28):
- Monthly OB-GYN consultations
- Anomaly scan (20-week morphology scan)
- Gestational diabetes screening (24 to 28 weeks)
- Optional additional genetic screening

Trimester 3 (weeks 29 to 40):
- Bi-weekly and then weekly OB-GYN consultations
- Group B Streptococcus test
- Birth plan discussion
- Pre-admission to chosen delivery hospital

Delivery:
- Hospital admission, delivery team, anaesthesia (if epidural/C-section), paediatric team for newborn assessment

Post-natal:
- 1 to 3 nights hospital stay (vaginal delivery), 3 to 5 nights (C-section)
- Post-natal check for mother (6-week review)
- Newborn paediatric follow-ups

Understanding this timeline helps budget accurately, costs accumulate across all phases, not just the delivery day.

## What do Dubai hospitals charge for maternity?

Dubai hospital maternity charges typically run AED 15,000 to 55,000 all-in at private facilities, or roughly AED 8,000 to 18,000 at DHA public hospitals for qualifying residents. Room category and C-section versus vaginal delivery move totals more than brand name. Invest Gulf starts with the package.

Dubai private hospital maternity packages typically run AED 15,000 to 55,000 all-in for a standard journey covering antenatal visits, delivery, and a short post-natal stay, while DHA public facilities such as Latifa Hospital often land near AED 8,000 to 18,000 for resident patients on a vaginal delivery pathway. Mid-range private vaginal delivery in a standard room commonly costs AED 12,000 to 20,000; C-section in the same tier runs AED 18,000 to 30,000. Premium private rooms push vaginal packages toward AED 20,000 to 35,000 and C-section toward AED 30,000 to 55,000. Employer maternity caps often sit between AED 7,000 and AED 50,000 by plan tier, with waiting periods of 10 to 12 months before cover activates. Budget the gap between hospital package and insurance limit in cash before trimester three.

## How much does insurance cover for maternity in Dubai?

Maternity insurance typically caps at AED 7,000 to 50,000 on employer plans, with many imposing a 10 to 12 month waiting period before payout. Read the certificate for network hospitals and elective C-section rules. Invest Gulf flags a job switch inside that window as a cash red flag.

### Annual Benefit Limits by Plan Tier

| Plan Tier | Typical Maternity Limit | Coverage Adequacy |
|---|---|---|
| EBP / Basic | Not covered or AED 5,000 to 7,000 | Barely covers pre-natal only |
| Standard employer plan | AED 7,000 to 12,000 | Covers basic delivery at public hospital or contributes to mid-range |
| Enhanced employer plan | AED 12,000 to 20,000 | Covers mid-range vaginal delivery |
| Premium employer plan | AED 25,000 to 50,000 | Covers most deliveries including C-section |
| Executive / International plan | AED 50,000 to unlimited | Full coverage including premium hospital choices |

### What Insurance Typically Covers

- Pre-natal consultations (number of covered visits varies, typically 8 to 12)
- Anomaly scan and first trimester screening
- Delivery (vaginal or C-section, some plans require pre-authorisation for elective C-section)
- Hospital stay for mother (typically 1 to 3 nights vaginal, 3 to 5 nights C-section)
- Newborn care in hospital (typically first 30 days covered under mother's plan)
- Post-natal check at 6 weeks

### What Insurance Often Excludes

- Private room upgrades above standard category
- Choice of obstetrician above the plan tier (some plans restrict to "assigned" OB-GYN)
- Epidural anaesthesia listed as "elective" in some basic plans
- Additional newborn screening tests beyond standard protocol
- Private lactation consultant sessions
- Elective procedures performed at delivery (cord blood banking, etc.)

### Top-Up Maternity Insurance

If your employer plan excludes or inadequately covers maternity, top-up maternity riders are available from most major insurers. Annual premiums for maternity riders run AED 1,500 to 4,000/year for AED 20,000 to 40,000 of additional coverage. Always purchase before conception, insurers will not add maternity cover once pregnancy is confirmed (this is considered a pre-existing condition for insurance purposes).

## How do vaginal and C-section costs compare?

A C-section at a private hospital typically costs AED 10,000 to 25,000 more than a vaginal delivery in the same facility, plus a longer 3 to 5 night stay versus 1 to 3 nights. Insurance often covers both when medically indicated. Our research keeps the clinical call with the OB-GYN.

## How do you choose an obstetrician in Dubai?

Choosing an obstetrician requires a DHA-licensed doctor with admitting rights at your delivery hospital, plus credentials such as MRCOG, ACOG or FRCSC where relevant. Meet-and-decide consults typically cost AED 400 to 800. Our underwriting treats hospital affiliation mismatch as a process red flag.

Selection checklist:

- **DHA licence:** Verify at dha.gov.ae for current specialty registration
- Hospital affiliation: Admitting privileges at your chosen delivery hospital
- **Qualification:** MRCOG (UK), ACOG (US), FRCSC (Canada), or equivalent European board
- Communication: Same-day callback habits matter across a 40-week pregnancy
- Case fit: High-risk, advanced maternal age, VBAC, or twins experience when relevant

## How do you register a newborn in Dubai?

Newborn registration must start within 30 days of birth at the emirate civil authority, then move through birth certificate, passport, UAE residency and insurance. Civil fees are often only AED 50 to 200. Our research keeps attested marriage documents ready before the due date.

Step 1, Birth Registration (within 30 days of birth):
Visit the relevant emirate's civil registration office (in Dubai: Vital Records & Statistics at Dubai Health Authority or UAE Ministry of Interior online portal). You will need: both parents' passports, marriage certificate (attested), hospital birth report.

Step 2, Birth Certificate:
Dubai birth certificate issued in Arabic. Have it legally translated for international use.

Step 3, Home Country Passport:
Apply for newborn's passport at your country's consulate in Dubai. Processing times vary: British (2 to 4 weeks), Indian (3 to 6 weeks), US (4 to 8 weeks), European (2 to 6 weeks).

Step 4, Emirates ID and Residency Visa:
Apply for the newborn's UAE residency visa, sponsored by a parent. For families already on UAE residency, this is a dependant visa application. Processing typically takes 2 to 4 weeks. For details on family sponsorship, see the [UAE family visa sponsorship guide](/guides/uae-family-visa-sponsorship/).

Step 5, Health Insurance for Newborn:
The newborn must be added to a health insurance policy within 30 days of birth. Most insurance plans allow free addition of a newborn in the first 30 days of life. After 30 days, underwriting applies.
## What should expat parents plan before delivery?

Expat parents should book hospital and obstetrician in trimester two, confirm insurer network hospitals, and keep attested marriage documents ready for birth registration within 30 days. Late-pregnancy travel also requires a fit-to-fly letter. We treat missing attestation as the admin red flag.

## What admin costs sit outside the hospital bill?

Admin costs outside the hospital bill typically include government fees of AED 500 to 5,000, attestation of AED 200 to 800 per document, typing-centre charges of AED 100 to 350, and PRO handling of AED 1,500 to 5,000. We budget these lines separately for foreign buyers before trimester three.

Newborn administration in Dubai sits outside the hospital invoice and still needs a cash buffer. Birth registration must complete within 30 days at the emirate civil authority, with civil fees often only AED 50 to 200, while attestation and typing-centre work commonly add AED 200 to 800 per document and AED 100 to 350 per application. End-to-end PRO handling can run AED 1,500 to 5,000. Passport timelines vary by consulate: British applications often take 2 to 4 weeks, Indian 3 to 6 weeks, and US 4 to 8 weeks, then UAE residency for the baby typically needs another 2 to 4 weeks. Health insurance must add the newborn within 30 days of birth or underwriting applies. UAE Federal Labour Law provides 60 days paid maternity leave for female employees, first 45 days at full salary and 15 days at half.

## What extra maternity questions do expats ask?

Expat maternity FAQs usually cover doula access, NICU daily rates of AED 2,000 to 10,000, civil registration fees of AED 50 to 200, and 60 days of statutory paid maternity leave. Hospital policy on birth companions varies. Our analysis keeps NICU limits on the insurance checklist.

Is a doula allowed in the delivery room in Dubai?
Some hospitals allow doulas (birth companions) with advance booking; others restrict the delivery room to the birthing partner and medical staff. Confirm the policy with your chosen delivery hospital. Private suites generally have more flexibility.

What happens if my baby needs NICU care?
Most insurance plans cover NICU care for newborns as part of the delivery episode or under the baby's own first-30-days coverage. NICU costs are significant, AED 2,000 to 10,000/day at private hospitals, so confirm your coverage limit before discharge. Latifa Hospital's NICU is publicly funded and available at lower cost for families who need extended care.

Do I need to pay for the birth registration in Dubai?
Civil registration fees in Dubai are minimal (AED 50 to 200). The main cost is document attestation (home-country documents typically need apostille or notarisation), which varies by country. Expedited services are available at additional cost.

Can I get paid maternity leave as an expat in Dubai?
UAE Federal Labour Law provides 60 days paid maternity leave for female employees (first 45 days at full salary, remaining 15 days at half salary). Some employers offer enhanced maternity packages. Check your employment contract for specifics.
