# Dubai Special Needs Schools: SEN Provision, KHDA Ratings & Fees 2026

TL;DR: Dubai's private schools cannot legally refuse students with special educational needs. All 209 KHDA-inspected schools are required to provide inclusive education, 27 achieved Outstanding for inclusion in 2023/24. There are no standalone SEN schools for expats, but around a dozen schools have specialist units for higher-level needs. SEN support adds AED 5,000–50,000 to base fees depending on level. Start with the KHDA inspection report for each school you are shortlisting: the inclusion section is the most direct signal of real provision quality.

## What "Students of Determination" means in Dubai

Dubai uses the term Students of Determination for all children and young people with a special educational need or disability. This covers a wide spectrum:

- Specific learning differences: dyslexia, dyscalculia, dyspraxia
- Attention and executive function: ADHD, attention and executive function difficulties
- Autism Spectrum Condition (ASC/ASD): at all levels of the spectrum
- Speech, language and communication needs (SLCN)
- Social, emotional and mental health (SEMH) needs
- Physical and sensory disabilities: mobility, visual, or hearing impairment
- Intellectual or developmental disabilities
- Multiple and complex needs

The term is consistent across KHDA inspection reports, school policies, and UAE federal legislation. Schools that use different terminology in their materials are generally referring to the same cohort.

## Dubai's legal framework: what schools must provide

Every private school in Dubai is legally bound by Executive Council Resolution No. 2 of 2017, which governs all private schools including those in free zones. Key obligations for SEN families:

- Schools cannot refuse a child's enrolment based solely on a diagnosis or SEN status. Refusal without KHDA-acceptable reason is a regulatory offence with financial penalties.
- Schools must maintain a special needs-friendly environment and provide academic programmes appropriate for Students of Determination.
- Schools must supply the devices, equipment, and resources KHDA deems necessary for SEN students.
- All support must be delivered without discrimination on grounds of special educational needs or disability.

The Dubai Inclusive Education Policy Framework 2017 translates these legal requirements into operational school standards. This includes having a named Inclusion Coordinator, an IEP process, anti-discriminatory admissions procedures, and a system of graduated school-based support before any external referral.

KHDA's 2019 handbook *Realising the Rights of Students of Determination* spells out specific school-level requirements that DSIB inspectors check during every inspection cycle.

## How KHDA inspects and rates SEN provision

KHDA's Dubai Schools Inspection Bureau (DSIB) grades inclusion as a separate domain within the UAE School Inspection Framework. It is not bundled into overall quality, a school can be Very Good overall and only Good for inclusion, or vice versa.

The 2023/24 inspection round assessed 209 private schools:

| Inclusion rating | Number of schools | % of total |
|---|---|---|
| Outstanding | 27 | 13% |
| Very Good | 57 | 27% |
| Good | 75 | 36% |
| Acceptable | 35 | 17% |
| Weak/Very Weak | 15 | 7% |

76% of Dubai private schools were rated Good or better for inclusion, a significant improvement from earlier cycles. Each school's full inspection report, including the inclusion section with narrative commentary, is free to download at [khda.gov.ae](https://www.khda.gov.ae).

The inclusion domain covers: how effectively staff identify and assess SEN; quality of IEPs and learning plans; training and skills of the SEN/inclusion team; how well students of determination progress compared to their peers; physical accessibility; and parent communication.

For SEN families, the inclusion section of the KHDA inspection report is more useful than the headline overall rating. A school rated Good overall with Outstanding for inclusion may serve your child better than an Outstanding overall school with a weaker inclusion grade. Read the [full guide to KHDA ratings](/guides/khda-school-ratings-explained/) to understand how to interpret each section.

## Schools with specialist SEN units

Most mainstream schools manage SEN through differentiated teaching and IEPs within ordinary classrooms. A smaller number have dedicated specialist units or provisions for students with higher-level needs who may benefit from more intensive support before integration into mainstream classes:

- GEMS Metropole School (Motor City): has a specialist provision unit for students with more complex learning profiles, alongside mainstream classes.
- Safa Community School (Al Quoz): known for structured specialist support; regularly cited by families with ASD and ADHD children.
- Horizon English School (Umm Suqeim): Strong inclusion team with specialist staff and a specific programme for SLCN and ASD.
- GEMS Wellington Academy Al Khail: launched a specialist educational pathway for Students of Determination aged 14–18, linking academic support with career and independence guidance.
- Cambridge International School: Rated Good overall and Very Good for inclusion, considered by families seeking a school with a particularly supportive ethos that does not carry the highest fees.

These provisions do not make a school a "special school" in the UK sense, they are mainstream schools with enhanced capability. Places in specialist units are limited and typically require an additional assessment meeting before admission.

## SEN support centres in Dubai (DHA-governed)

For children with complex or intensive therapeutic needs, Dubai has several specialist centres that operate **outside the KHDA school framework**. These are registered with the Dubai Health Authority (DHA) or Community Development Authority (CDA) and focus on therapy, rehabilitation, and life-skills education:

**Al Noor Training Centre** (Deira, Al Quoz, Sharjah branches): Accepts students aged 4–35 with intellectual and developmental disabilities including ASD, physical disabilities, and multiple complex needs. Provides classroom education, occupational therapy, speech therapy, and a work placement programme for adults. Ages 4–17 in classroom settings; adults 18–35 in skills training.

**Dubai Centre for Special Needs (DCSN)**: Community-based organisation providing academic and therapeutic programmes tailored to individual profiles. DCSN reports students from multiple nationalities and covers an extensive range of diagnoses.

**Rashid Centre for People of Determination**: Government facility providing rehabilitation, therapy, and vocational training for Emirati nationals and long-term residents with physical and developmental disabilities.

**The Developing Child Centre** and **Jad's Inclusion**: Private therapy centres offering ABA, speech and language therapy, occupational therapy, and educational support for children with ASD and developmental delays. These work alongside (not instead of) mainstream school placements.

Families of children with **moderate to severe ASD, intellectual disability, or complex physical needs** typically combine a DHA centre placement with part-time inclusion in a mainstream KHDA school, or transition between settings as the child develops.

## SEN fees: what to expect beyond base tuition

Mainstream school fees in Dubai already stretch from **AED 40,000 to over AED 100,000** per year. SEN provision adds further cost depending on the level of support required:

| Support type | Approximate additional annual cost |
|---|---|
| Standard IEP + in-class differentiation | Included in base fees at most schools |
| Learning support withdrawal sessions (small group) | AED 5,000–15,000 |
| Part-time in-class learning assistant | AED 15,000–30,000 |
| Full-time dedicated shadow teacher | AED 30,000–50,000 |
| External therapy (speech, OT, ABA) at school | AED 15,000–45,000 depending on hours |
| External assessments (DHA/CDA licensed professional) | AED 2,500–6,000 per assessment |

These are indicative ranges, individual school pricing varies significantly. Some schools include a base level of SEN support in their tuition model; others itemise every additional service. **Always request the full written SEN fee schedule** before submitting an application.

## The IEP and support application process

Schools in Dubai follow a graduated approach to SEN identification and support, aligned with KHDA policy:

**Step 1, In-school observation and support.** Teachers monitor, adjust teaching strategies, and document outcomes. No external assessment is triggered at this stage.

**Step 2, Referral to the Inclusion Coordinator.** If in-class adjustments are insufficient, the class teacher refers to the school's Inclusion Coordinator (equivalent to a SENCO). Parents are involved from this point.

**Step 3, IEP development.** A meeting is held with parents, the Inclusion Coordinator, and relevant staff. An Individualized Education Program is drafted with specific targets, accommodations, and support services. Written parental consent is required before any plan is put in place.

**Step 4, Specialist referral or external assessment.** If progress stalls or a diagnosis is needed to inform provision, the school may recommend an external assessment by a DHA/CDA-licensed professional (psychologist, speech therapist, or paediatrician). This requires explicit written parental consent, schools cannot proceed without it. KHDA's 2025 updated guidance emphasises that external assessments occur only after documented in-school support, not as a first response.

**Step 5, Review.** IEPs are reviewed regularly, typically termly. Progress is measured against agreed targets.

For families arriving with an existing EHCP, IEP, or 504 plan from another country, most schools will use this as the starting point rather than beginning the process from scratch. Bring full copies of all documentation, translated into English if needed.

## What to ask schools before applying

A school visit tells you whether inclusion is a real institutional priority or a policy document on a shelf. Questions that reveal actual provision quality:

**About the team:**
- How many Inclusion Coordinators or learning support staff does the school employ?
- What specialist qualifications do they hold (e.g. ASD, SLCN, specific learning differences)?
- Is there an in-house educational psychologist or contracted access to one?

**About the process:**
- What is the process for developing and reviewing IEPs?
- How often do learning support teachers co-teach in mainstream classrooms versus pull-out?
- How are parents kept informed of progress and IEP adjustments?

**About provision for your child's specific need:**
- How many current students have a similar profile to my child?
- What communication/behaviour systems does the school use for ASD students?
- Is there a specialist reading programme for students with dyslexia?

**About fees and transparency:**
- What SEN support is included in the base fee? What is charged additionally?
- How is shadow teacher provision arranged and costed?
- What is the school's policy if our child's needs change significantly?

Download the school's most recent KHDA inspection report before your visit and read the inclusion narrative section, it will tell you where DSIB found strength and where inspectors flagged areas for development. This gives you precise questions to put to the head of inclusion during your meeting.

## Choosing an area with SEN schools in mind

The schools with Outstanding or Very Good inclusion ratings are spread across Dubai, from Jumeirah and Al Barsha in the west to Mirdif, DSO, and Deira in the east. Families with SEN children tend to have one additional constraint that other families don't: **the school-to-therapy route**. If your child attends twice-weekly occupational therapy at a centre in Umm Suqeim, a school 40 minutes away in the opposite direction becomes impractical.

Map the triangle: school, home, and any therapy or medical centre your child attends regularly. In Dubai traffic, the difference between 15 minutes and 40 minutes at 8:00 AM is not marginal, it is the difference between a sustainable routine and a daily crisis.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I access the inclusion section of a KHDA inspection report?**
Go to khda.gov.ae, click Schools, search by school name or area, open the school profile, and download the most recent inspection report PDF. The inclusion domain is usually in the second half of the report. Look for the overall grade and then the detailed narrative, the commentary is more useful than the grade alone.

**Can a Dubai school reduce or withdraw SEN support without telling me?**
No. Any change to your child's IEP or support provision must involve you as a parent. Schools must communicate proposed changes and obtain consent. If support is reduced without explanation, you can raise a formal complaint with the school's leadership and, if unresolved, escalate to KHDA's Inclusive Education team.

**Do international SEN assessments carry weight in Dubai?**
Yes, though the school will want reports translated into English and may ask for an updated assessment by a DHA/CDA-licensed professional if your previous report is more than two years old or uses terminology that does not map to the UAE classification system. Bring the originals alongside translated versions.

**What happens if my child needs more support than the school can provide?**
Schools are required to refer to KHDA if they genuinely cannot meet a student's needs. KHDA will assess the situation and may assist in identifying an appropriate placement. In practice, families with children who have very complex needs often supplement mainstream school with a DHA centre placement or private therapy.

**Is the GEMS Wellington Academy Al Khail pathway for older students of determination free?**
It is part of the school's fee structure, parents pay standard school fees. The specialist pathway for students aged 14–18 is a curriculum enhancement within the school, not a separately chargeable programme. Confirm current details directly with the school's admissions and inclusion teams.

**How does the KHDA inclusion rating affect a school's fee increase allowance?**
KHDA links annual fee increase caps to overall school ratings, not specifically to the inclusion grade. However, schools with Outstanding overall ratings, which often correlates with strong inclusion, can increase fees by a higher percentage each year. The 2025/26 cap for Outstanding schools was 5.13%. See [KHDA school ratings explained](/guides/khda-school-ratings-explained/) for the full fee-increase framework.

## Family schooling scenarios

**Scenario A — FS1 entry:** Apply 6–12 months ahead for KHDA/ADEK Outstanding schools. Registration fees are non-refundable once offered a place.

**Scenario B — mid-secondary transfer:** Curriculum continuity (IGCSE vs IB MYP) matters more than saving 10% on fees. Switching exam boards at Year 10 is costly.

**Scenario C — fee-sensitive family:** CBSE and mid-tier British schools reduce cost but may limit university destination flexibility. Model hidden costs: bus, uniforms, exams.

## School selection risks

- Waitlist offers expire quickly. Confirm seat in writing before terminating your current school's place.
- Fee schedules in marketing PDFs may exclude registration, bus, uniform, and exam fees.
- KHDA/ADEK ratings can change year to year. Check latest inspection report, not blog summaries.
- Curriculum switches mid-stream (British to IB) can delay university applications by 12 months.

## Practical next steps for SEN families relocating to Dubai

1. **Download KHDA inspection reports** for your shortlisted schools and read the inclusion section before any visit.
2. **Request a pre-admissions SEN meeting** rather than a standard open day: ask to meet the Inclusion Coordinator specifically.
3. **Bring all existing documentation:** diagnosis reports, IEPs, EHCPs, therapy records. English copies where possible.
4. **Ask for the full SEN fee schedule in writing** before accepting a place.
5. **Identify nearby DHA-registered therapy centres** if your child already receives or is likely to need OT, SALT, or ABA support outside school.
6. **Map your three-point route:** school, home, therapy centre: before signing a lease or completing a property purchase.
7. **Register interest early.** Outstanding-rated schools with strong inclusion track records attract SEN families specifically, waiting lists at these schools can be 6–12 months for mid-year entry.
