## What "Students of Determination" means in Dubai

Student of Determination is the official UAE term for learners with special educational needs or disabilities, used consistently across KHDA reports and visa paperwork for more than 8 years under the inclusion framework. Invest Gulf maps the same phrase for foreign buyers so admissions and IEP plans stay consistent across Dubai's private school market where 76% of inspected campuses were Good or better for inclusion.

- Specific learning differences: dyslexia, dyscalculia, dyspraxia
- Attention and executive function: ADHD, attention and executive function difficulties
- Autism Spectrum Condition (ASC/ASD): at all levels of the spectrum
- Speech, language and communication needs (SLCN)
- Social, emotional and mental health (SEMH) needs
- Physical and sensory disabilities: mobility, visual, or hearing impairment
- Intellectual or developmental disabilities
- Multiple and complex needs

The term is consistent across KHDA inspection reports, school policies, and UAE federal legislation. Schools that use different terminology in their materials are generally referring to the same cohort.

## What legal inclusion rules apply to Dubai private schools?

Dubai private schools typically cannot refuse enrolment solely on SEN status under Executive Council Resolution No. 2 of 2017, and refusal without a KHDA-acceptable reason is a regulatory offence with financial penalties that can stall a 12-month admissions plan. We treat inclusive admissions as a hard checklist for foreign buyers shortlisting British or IB campuses where SEN add-ons may still reach AED 20,000 to 50,000.

- Schools must maintain a special needs-friendly environment and provide academic programmes appropriate for Students of Determination.
- Schools must supply the devices, equipment, and resources KHDA deems necessary for SEN students.
- All support must be delivered without discrimination on grounds of special educational needs or disability.

The Dubai Inclusive Education Policy Framework 2017 translates these legal requirements into operational school standards. This includes having a named Inclusion Coordinator, an IEP process, anti-discriminatory admissions procedures, and a system of graduated school-based support before any external referral.

KHDA's 2019 handbook *Realising the Rights of Students of Determination* spells out specific school-level requirements that DSIB inspectors check during every inspection cycle.

## How KHDA inspects and rates SEN provision

KHDA inclusion ratings typically separate from the overall school grade, and 76% of Dubai private schools were Good or better for inclusion in the 2023/24 cycle. We prioritise the inclusion narrative for foreign buyers because a Good-overall school with Outstanding inclusion can outperform an Outstanding-overall school with a weaker SEN section.

The inclusion domain covers: how effectively staff identify and assess SEN; quality of IEPs and learning plans; training and skills of the SEN/inclusion team; how well students of determination progress compared to their peers; physical accessibility; and parent communication.

For SEN families, the inclusion section of the KHDA inspection report is more useful than the headline overall rating. Read the [full guide to KHDA ratings](/guides/khda-school-ratings-explained/) to understand how to interpret each section.

## What do specialist SEN units cost inside mainstream schools?

Specialist SEN units inside mainstream Dubai schools typically sit at GEMS Metropole, Safa Community School, and Horizon English School rather than on separate campuses. We ask foreign buyers whether placement fees stack on tuition and whether shadow teacher hours bill hourly or as term packages of AED 20,000 to 50,000.

Ask whether the unit charges a placement fee on top of standard tuition, whether therapy is on-site or outsourced, and how shadow teacher hours are billed (hourly vs term packages). Units may cap intake by diagnosis, so apply early and share prior reports.

## Which DHA-governed SEN support centres operate in Dubai?

DHA-governed SEN centres typically include Al Noor Training Centre, Dubai Centre for Special Needs, and Rashid Centre programmes for ages 4 to 35 depending on pathway and diagnosis. Invest Gulf pairs these centres with mainstream KHDA placements for foreign buyers whose children need ABA, SLT, or OT at AED 300 to 800 per session outside school hours.

DHA centre planning checklist:

- Confirm age band (often 4 to 17 classroom; 18 to 35 skills tracks)
- Budget AED 300 to 800 per therapy session outside school
- Ask whether the centre coordinates IEP targets with the KHDA school
- Keep Emirates ID and medical reports ready for intake

Al Noor Training Centre (Deira, Al Quoz, Sharjah branches): Accepts students aged 4 to 35 with intellectual and developmental disabilities including ASD, physical disabilities, and multiple complex needs. Provides classroom education, occupational therapy, speech therapy, and a work placement programme for adults. Ages 4 to 17 in classroom settings; adults 18 to 35 in skills training.

Dubai Centre for Special Needs (DCSN): Community-based organisation providing academic and therapeutic programmes tailored to individual profiles. DCSN reports students from multiple nationalities and covers an extensive range of diagnoses.

Rashid Centre for People of Determination: Government facility providing rehabilitation, therapy, and vocational training for Emirati nationals and long-term residents with physical and developmental disabilities.

The Developing Child Centre and Jad's Inclusion: Private therapy centres offering ABA, speech and language therapy, occupational therapy, and educational support for children with ASD and developmental delays. These work alongside (not instead of) mainstream school placements.

Families of children with **moderate to severe ASD, intellectual disability, or complex physical needs** typically combine a DHA centre placement with part-time inclusion in a mainstream KHDA school, or transition between settings as the child develops.

SEN fee add-ons in Dubai typically range from AED 0 for in-class differentiation to AED 5,000-15,000 for pull-out support and AED 20,000-50,000 for shadow teachers. We consistently model external therapy at AED 300-800 per session for foreign buyers before they sign a tenancy near one school only.

| In-class differentiation | 0 (within tuition) | IEP targets, seating plans, minor accommodations |

## How does the IEP and support application process work?

IEP processes in Dubai typically start with in-school observation for one term before any external DHA or CDA assessment, and written parental consent is mandatory under KHDA 2025 guidance. We tell foreign buyers with an existing EHCP or 504 plan to bring full English translations so schools skip a cold start lasting 4 to 8 weeks.

IEP timeline checklist:

- Document in-class trials before external assessment
- Sign written consent before DHA/CDA evaluation
- Review IEP targets termly, not annually only
- Bring prior EHCP/IEP/504 copies translated into English

Step 1, In-school observation and support. Teachers monitor, adjust teaching strategies, and document outcomes. No external assessment is triggered at this stage.

**Step 2, Referral to the Inclusion Coordinator.** If in-class adjustments are insufficient, the class teacher refers to the school's Inclusion Coordinator (equivalent to a SENCO). Parents are involved from this point.

**Step 3, IEP development.** A meeting is held with parents, the Inclusion Coordinator, and relevant staff. An Individualized Education Program is drafted with specific targets, accommodations, and support services. Written parental consent is required before any plan is put in place.

**Step 4, Specialist referral or external assessment.** If progress stalls or a diagnosis is needed to inform provision, the school may recommend an external assessment by a DHA/CDA-licensed professional (psychologist, speech therapist, or paediatrician). This requires explicit written parental consent, schools cannot proceed without it. KHDA's 2025 updated guidance emphasises that external assessments occur only after documented in-school support, not as a first response.

**Step 5, Review.** IEPs are reviewed regularly, typically termly. Progress is measured against agreed targets.

## What to ask schools before applying

School visit checklists typically cover cohort size for similar profiles, dyslexia programmes, shadow-teacher costing, and what sits inside base fees versus AED 5,000 to 50,000 add-ons each year. Invest Gulf downloads the latest KHDA inclusion narrative first so foreign buyers ask precise questions rather than generic 20-minute tour prompts.

**About provision for your child's specific need:**
- How many current students have a similar profile to my child?
- What communication/behaviour systems does the school use for ASD students?
- Is there a specialist reading programme for students with dyslexia?

**About fees and transparency:**
- What SEN support is included in the base fee? What is charged additionally?
- How is shadow teacher provision arranged and costed?
- What is the school's policy if our child's needs change significantly?
