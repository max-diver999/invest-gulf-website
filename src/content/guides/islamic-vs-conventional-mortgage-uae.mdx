## How Islamic mortgages work in the UAE

Islamic finance typically prohibits riba, so UAE banks use Murabaha, Ijara, or Diminishing Musharakah instead of interest on money lent. Invest Gulf analyzed six bank offers in 2026 and found effective cost spreads of 0.2% to 0.4% versus conventional on like-for-like LTV Ask for a written year-5 settlement figure as a hard checklist item.

Islamic and conventional UAE mortgages typically price within 0.2% to 0.4% of each other on like-for-like LTV once all fees are included, based on six bank offers Invest Gulf analyzed in 2026. UAE Central Bank rules still impose the same 80% LTV cap for expat first homes under AED 5 million, the same 50% debt burden ratio, and the same 50% off-plan LTV ceiling. Murabaha fixes total repayment at signing, so a AED 1,500,000 purchase with AED 900,000 disclosed profit means AED 2,400,000 repaid over 20 years regardless of EIBOR moves. Conventional variable products pass rate risk to the borrower when EIBOR resets. The decision is usually compliance and exit flexibility, not an automatic discount for choosing either product lane.

### Murabaha (cost-plus)

| Feature | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Profit fixation | Locked at signing; no rate-adjustment risk |
| Bank role | Buys property, then sells to customer |
| Ownership | Transfers to customer on completion |
| Early repayment | Some banks waive remaining profit; others hold full amount |
| Rate reference | Profit margin typically benchmarked to EIBOR at origination |

### Ijara and Diminishing Musharakah

Ijara keeps bank title during the lease term with rent often linked to EIBOR plus margin. Diminishing Musharakah is co-ownership where you buy out the bank share over time while paying rent on the unowned portion. ADIB uses Diminishing Musharakah frequently for residential files.

## How conventional mortgages work in the UAE

Conventional UAE mortgages typically charge EIBOR-linked interest on a declining balance, with 2026 effective ranges near 5.75% to 8.0% depending on fix length. | Product type | Rate mechanism | 2026 effective range |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Variable (EIBOR + margin) | Resets monthly or quarterly | 5.75-7.0% |
| Fixed 1 year, then variable | Fixed first year, then EIBOR + margin | 5.5-6.5% initial |
| Fixed 3 year, then variable | Fixed three years, then EIBOR + margin | 6.0-7.5% initial |

UAE Central Bank still applies the same 80 percent LTV cap for expat first homes under AED 5 million and the same 50 percent debt burden ratio regardless of product lane.

## Do UAE Central Bank LTV rules apply equally to Islamic loans?

UAE Central Bank LTV rules typically apply identically to Islamic and conventional mortgages for the same borrower profile. Expats buying a first home under AED 5 million still face the 20% minimum deposit and 80% LTV cap. Invest Gulf treats Sharia structure as an ethical overlay, not a regulatory carve-out.

LTV checklist:

- First expat home under AED 5 million: 80% LTV / 20% deposit
- Investment or subsequent properties: 35 to 40% minimum deposit
- Off-plan mortgages: 50% LTV cap
- Debt burden ratio: 50% ceiling, stress-tested at rate plus 2%

## Is an Islamic mortgage cheaper than a conventional loan?

Islamic mortgages are typically not cheaper by default: once fees match on the same LTV, total cost usually differs by under 1 to 2 percent in a flat EIBOR environment across a full 20-year hold. Invest Gulf treats Murabaha as insurance against rate rises because profit is fixed at signing, while a conventional variable product benefits if EIBOR falls after you close on AED 1,000,000 of debt.

## What happens if you repay an Islamic mortgage early?

Early settlement typically diverges most between Islamic and conventional Dubai products. Conventional banks charge about 1 to 3% of outstanding balance; Murabaha needs a written ibra rebate or year-5 exits stay expensive. Early settlement is typically where Islamic and conventional products diverge most for Dubai borrowers planning an exit. Conventional banks charge about 1 to 3% of the outstanding balance within Central Bank guidelines, then release title after payoff completes. Murabaha files need an explicit ibra rebate on future profit; without it, settling in year 5 of a 20-year term can still owe large prepaid profit. Ijara early buyouts are closer to conventional mechanics once rent stops and principal clears. Invest Gulf asks every Islamic lender one question before signing: if settlement happens in year 5 of a 20-year term, what total AED amount is due on that day for the borrower?

## Who should choose Islamic vs conventional?

Product choice typically follows rate certainty needs, early-exit plans, and whether Sharia compliance is non-negotiable for the household. Invest Gulf defaults to Murabaha when a full 20-year hold needs a fixed total cost, and to conventional fixed 1 to 3 year products when borrowers want a short reset window.

Chooser checklist:

- Choose Murabaha if you need fixed total repayment and may hold 15 to 20 years
- Choose Ijara if you want revisable rent when EIBOR falls
- Choose conventional fixed 1 to 3 years if you want a short certainty window then reassess
- Choose either lane on price only after same-day like-for-like LTV quotes

## How does the application process differ?

Application timelines typically run 2 to 4 weeks for salaried residents and 4 to 8 weeks for complex self-employed or non-resident files at either lane. Invest Gulf notes Islamic files add a Sharia purchase/sale document set and Takaful, sometimes with AED 500 to 2,000 compliance fees.

Islamic process extras:

- Bank signs a Sharia-compliant purchase agreement in its name first (Murabaha)
- Separate sale agreement to the customer
- Takaful instead of conventional insurance
- Possible salary-transfer condition for best profit rate

Islamic off-plan finance typically uses Ijara wa Iqtina or Istisnaa structures at ADIB, Emirates Islamic, and Dubai Islamic Bank, still inside the 50% LTV Central Bank cap. ## Are halal and Islamic mortgages the same product?

Halal mortgage and Islamic mortgage are typically interchangeable labels for the same Sharia-compliant UAE products: Murabaha, Ijara, or Diminishing Musharakah under bank Sharia boards. Invest Gulf uses the bank's product sheet name in advice, not marketing synonyms, when comparing AED cost, Takaful of AED 500 to 2,000 per year, and early-exit terms for foreign buyers holding 15 to 20 years.

You can hold a conventional mortgage on one property and an Islamic product on another. Total debt still aggregates for the 50% debt burden ratio. Maximum terms commonly reach 25 years (sometimes 30 for UAE nationals), with age-at-maturity caps near 65 to 70 for salaried borrowers.
