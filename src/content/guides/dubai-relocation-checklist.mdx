## What should you complete 8 to 12 weeks before arrival?

Eight to twelve weeks before arrival typically means passport attestation, school waitlist emails, housing shortlists without signing a lease, and a cash buffer of AED 15,000 to AED 25,000 for singles or AED 40,000 to AED 70,000 for families. Employer PRO timelines should be written in days, not "soon."

Dubai relocation checklists for foreign buyers and employed expats split into pre-arrival attestation, arrival immigration, then Ejari before DEWA. Passport validity of six or more months, attested degrees for skilled visas, and attested marriage or birth certificates for dependants sit on the critical path eight to twelve weeks out. Families should email at least three schools for year-group seats, and school deposits should precede a safe twelve-month villa lease. Cash buffers of AED 15,000 to AED 25,000 for singles and AED 40,000 to AED 70,000 for families cover security cheques, temporary housing at AED 300 to AED 800 per night for two to four weeks, and first admin fees before salary lands. Ejari registration costs roughly AED 220 plus typing fees and remains mandatory before DEWA, most bank accounts, and many school enrolments. Invest Gulf sequencing keeps property SPA and Golden Visa tracks parallel until DLD registration completes.

Insider tip: Put the landlord Ejari appointment, PRO medical slot, and school deposit deadline on one shared calendar; the move fails when those three dates drift apart.

Weeks one to four after landing typically cover entry on the correct visa, medical fitness testing, Emirates ID biometrics, SIM setup, and short-stay housing at AED 300 to AED 800 per night if the twelve-month lease is not ready. Employment routes also sign the labour contract through the PRO in the same window.

## When do Ejari, DEWA, and visa stamping happen?

Ejari, DEWA, and visa stamping typically happen in weeks two to three after a tenancy is signed, and the order is fixed: register Ejari first (about AED 220 plus typing), then DEWA, then passport stamping and Emirates ID collection. Skipping Ejari blocks utilities and bank openings on the our checklist.

## What are common failure points?

Common failure points include DEWA before Ejari, bank appointments without Ejari or Emirates ID, losing school seats after an early twelve-month lease, and undercounting rent cheques when landlords want four post-dated cheques. Temporary housing at AED 300 to AED 800 per night for two to four weeks is a frequent Invest Gulf miss.

| Failure | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| DEWA before Ejari | Register Ejari first |
| Bank without Ejari or EID | Wait for tenancy + ID |
| School seat lost | Apply before 12-month lease |
| Cheque count surprise | Negotiate 1 vs 4 cheques upfront |
| Driving on tourist licence too long | Check RTA nationality rules |

**Rent cheques:** 1 cheque often discounts rent; 4 cheques eases cash flow. **Temporary housing:** budget AED 300 to 800/night for 2 to 4 weeks. **Shipping:** send container 6 to 8 weeks before needed.

## What banking steps follow visa stamping?

Banking after visa stamping typically requires a booked appointment with stamped passport, Emirates ID, Ejari or title deed, and salary or investor documents, with account activation often taking one to two weeks after stamping. Most retail banks want Ejari plus Emirates ID before rent cheques on the checklist.

| Task | Done |
| --- | --- |
| Book bank appointment (salary certificate or investor docs) | ☐ |
| Bring stamped passport, EID, Ejari, salary slip or title deed | ☐ |
| Order cheque book if paying rent by cheque | ☐ |
| Set up mobile banking and beneficiary limits | ☐ |
| Link multi-currency account if receiving overseas income | ☐ |

Most banks want **Ejari plus Emirates ID**; property owners may use title deed instead of lease. Allow **1 to 2 weeks** after stamping for account activation.

Banking checklist:
- Bring stamped passport, EID, and Ejari or title deed
- Budget 1 to 2 weeks for activation before rent cheques
- Keep AED cash buffer until the cheque book arrives

## What digital apps should you install week one?

Week-one apps typically include UAE PASS for government services, Dubai REST for property and Ejari lookup, RTA Dubai for Salik and licences, DEWA for bills, KHDA for school ratings, and DUBAI NOW for DHA services. Install them before biometrics so OTP linking does not delay the AED 220 Ejari step in weeks two to three.

| App | Use |
| --- | --- |
| UAE PASS | Government services |
| Dubai REST | Property, Ejari lookup |
| RTA Dubai | Salik, fines, licence |
| DEWA | Bills |
| KHDA | School ratings |
| DUBAI NOW | DHA services |

App checklist for Invest Gulf arrivals: UAE PASS first, then Dubai REST before the landlord Ejari appointment and the AED 220 registration fee.

## What should pet owners add to the checklist?

Pet owners should add import permits, vaccination records, a landlord pet clause in the tenancy, municipality registration if required, and a nearby vet clinic, because pet import lead times can exceed 30 days and must start before household goods ship. Missing the pet clause is a lease red flag after arrival for Invest Gulf movers.

| Task | Done |
| --- | --- |
| Import permit and vaccination record | ☐ |
| Landlord pet clause in tenancy | ☐ |
| Register with municipality if required | ☐ |
| Vet clinic selected near home | ☐ |

Pet import lead times can exceed **30 days**; start before shipping household goods.

## How do remote workers and freelancers differ?

Remote workers and freelancers typically skip employer PRO blocks and need freelance or green visa documents, self-sponsored insurance, and Ejari in their own name before banking. Cash buffers toward AED 25,000 or more for singles matter because no first salary date anchors cash flow on the our checklist.

Freelance movers should add green visa documentation, self-sponsored insurance, and Ejari in their own name before banking. Cash buffer toward the upper end (AED 25,000+ singles) because no first salary date anchors cash flow.

Freelance checklist:
- Self-sponsored health insurance active
- Residential Ejari (not co-working) before banking
- Cash buffer at AED 25,000+ until first invoice clears

## What should you pack vs buy locally?

Packing versus buying locally typically means bringing prescription medications with a doctor letter, laptops, attested copies, and small sentimental items, while furniture and appliances are cheaper to buy in Dubai. Avoid shipping large furniture unless value is high on a six to eight week container timeline for Invest Gulf movers.

| Bring | Buy in Dubai | Timing note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prescription meds + doctor letter | Furniture, appliances | Meds from day one |
| Laptops, phones, adapters | Bulky kitchen gear | Buy after Ejari |
| Key documents (attested copies) | Car after licence sorted | Keep attested copies |
| Small sentimental items | Most clothing (malls active) | Skip 6 to 8 week furniture containers |

Avoid shipping **large furniture** unless sentimental; IKEA, Home Centre, and second-hand marketplaces are mature. Container lead times of 6 to 8 weeks still apply when you do ship.

## Who owns each task: PRO vs you?

PRO teams typically own entry permits, visa stamping, and labour contract filing, while you personally own school tours, community viewings, lease negotiation, Ejari confirmation with the landlord, and the bank interview. Write PRO scope into email so week-two DEWA is not blocked by a missing AED 220 Ejari appointment on the checklist.

| PRO typically handles | You personally handle |
| --- | --- |
| Entry permit, visa stamping | School tours and deposits |
| Medical appointment booking (some employers) | Community viewings and lease negotiation |
| Labour contract (employment) | Ejari confirmation with landlord |
| Dependent visa submission (sometimes) | Bank interview and cheque book |

Write PRO scope into email so gaps do not appear in week two when DEWA is blocked for missing Ejari. A missing Ejari appointment can waste 2 to 5 days of utility and bank setup.

## What should you settle in months 3 to 6?

Months three to six typically settle banking reviews, insurance optimisation, home-country tax reporting where applicable, and a housing upgrade if the commute failed. Investor visa holders also track ICP renewal and property value for ten-year Golden Visa continuity after AED 2 million property routes on Invest Gulf plans.

| Task | Done |
| --- | --- |
| Review banking and credit products | ☐ |
| Optimise insurance (health, home, car) | ☐ |
| Plan home-country tax reporting if applicable | ☐ |
| Evaluate housing upgrade if commute failed | ☐ |

Free zone vs mainland employment affects spouse work permissions; investor visa holders focus on **ICP renewal** and **property value maintenance** for Golden Visa.

## How should Invest Gulf sequence relocation for Gulf buyers?

Our relocation sequencing typically fails when the Ejari date slips past a school registration deadline or a DEWA application, so build one Gantt with PRO dates, landlord Ejari, and school deposits on a single page. Property buyers should run visa and purchase tracks separately until DLD registration completes, then merge for Emirates ID and banking.

Sequencing checklist:
- One calendar for PRO, Ejari, and school deposit dates
- Separate purchase track until DLD registration
- Merge tracks for Emirates ID and banking afterward

Arrival-to-settlement sequencing for Dubai typically keeps medical and Emirates ID biometrics in week one, Ejari and DEWA in weeks two to three, and banking plus family visas across weeks four to twelve. Singles should hold AED 15,000 to AED 25,000 and families AED 40,000 to AED 70,000 before first salary, including temporary housing at AED 300 to AED 800 per night when the long lease is not ready. Ejari at roughly AED 220 plus typing remains the hard gate before utilities and most retail bank accounts. Shipping containers need a six to eight week lead, and RTA licence conversion should be planned inside six months of residency depending on nationality rules. Our checklists treat school seat confirmation as a precondition to a twelve-month family lease, not an afterthought once furniture arrives.
