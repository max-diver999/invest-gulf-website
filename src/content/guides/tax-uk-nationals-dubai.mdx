# UK Nationals in Dubai: Tax, Residency and HMRC Rules 2026

British buyers often assume that zero UAE income tax ends their UK obligations. It does not. HMRC still cares about **where you are tax resident**, **what you earn from Dubai rentals**, and **whether your estate is UK-domiciled**. This guide maps the main UK rules for Dubai property and UAE residency in plain language. It is general information only, not personal tax advice.

## Why does UK tax still apply when you live in Dubai?

The UAE does not charge personal income tax on most individual rental income under current practice. That only removes **UAE** tax on the same pound or dirham. If HMRC treats you as **UK tax resident** for the year (6 April to 5 April), you remain taxable on **worldwide income**, including Dubai rent, unless a specific exemption applies. Moving to Dubai without clearing the Statutory Residence Test (SRT) leaves you exposed.

Owning Dubai property, holding a Golden Visa, or opening a UAE bank account does **not** by itself make you non-UK resident. The SRT looks at **days in the UK** and **ties** (family, accommodation, work, and related tests). Property investors who keep a UK home available for personal use often retain a strong UK tie even while living abroad.

## How does the UK Statutory Residence Test work in brief?

You are **automatically UK resident** if any of these apply in the tax year:

- You spend **183 or more days** in the UK.
- Your **only home** is in the UK and it is available for at least **91 days**, and you spend at least **30 days** there.
- You work **full-time in the UK** under the statutory definition.

You can be **automatically non-resident** if you meet an overseas test, for example fewer than **16 UK days** when you were UK resident in any of the prior three tax years. Between those extremes, HMRC applies **sufficient ties** tests: the more UK ties you keep, the fewer days you can spend in the UK without becoming resident.

| UK days in tax year (indicative band) | Typical outcome if you left the UK recently |
| --- | --- |
| 0-15 | Often non-resident if overseas tests met |
| 16-45 | Depends on number of UK ties |
| 46-90 | Usually resident unless very few ties |
| 91-120 | Usually resident with most tie profiles |
| 183+ | Automatically UK resident |

Count **midnight to midnight** days carefully. Flights that touch UK airspace without landing usually do not count, but partial days can matter in edge cases. Keep a simple travel log and share it with your adviser before year-end.

For how the UAE side defines tax residency (183-day and economic substance tests), see [UAE tax residency and property](/guides/uae-tax-residency-property/) and [DMCC company setup](/guides/dmcc-company-setup/) if you use a corporate structure.

## Do you pay UK tax on Dubai rental income?

**UK tax residents:** Declare net rental profit on self-assessment (SA105 overseas property pages). Allowable costs generally mirror UK lettings: agent fees, insurance, repairs (not improvements), and accountancy. Mortgage interest on residential property is restricted to a **20% tax credit** under current UK rules, not a full deduction.

**Non-UK residents:** Overseas rental from Dubai is usually **outside** UK income tax if you are genuinely non-resident and the income is not UK-source. UK-source items (UK pension, UK rental, UK dividends) can still be taxable in the UK even when you live in Dubai.

| Income type | UK tax if you are UK resident | UK tax if non-resident |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dubai apartment rent | Taxable worldwide | Generally not taxable |
| UAE employment salary | Taxable worldwide | Generally not taxable |
| UK buy-to-let rent | Taxable | Often taxable (UK source) |
| UK pension drawdown | Taxable | Often taxable |

The UAE not taxing the rent does **not** reduce UK tax. There is usually **no foreign tax credit** for UAE income tax that was never paid.

## Is capital gains tax due when you sell Dubai property?

**UK residents:** Gains on Dubai property can fall within UK Capital Gains Tax (CGT). Compute proceeds minus acquisition cost, plus qualifying improvement spend and disposal costs. Residential property CGT rates for higher-rate taxpayers have moved with recent Budgets; confirm the rate that applies in your disposal year with HMRC guidance or a qualified adviser.

**Non-UK residents at disposal:** Gains on **overseas** property are generally **outside** UK CGT for individuals who are non-resident under the SRT in the year of sale. Rules for **UK** land and property held by non-residents are stricter and separate. Split-year treatment can apply if you arrive or leave mid-year.

| Seller status (year of sale) | UK CGT on Dubai gain (typical) | UAE tax on gain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| UK tax resident | Often chargeable | None at individual level |
| UK non-resident | Generally not on overseas asset | None at individual level |

Keep **purchase contracts, payment proofs, and improvement invoices** from day one. CGT disputes usually fail on missing cost basis, not on headline tax law.

## Does UK inheritance tax cover Dubai property?

UK Inheritance Tax (IHT) follows **domicile**, not where you happen to live this year. If you remain **UK-domiciled**, your worldwide estate, including Dubai property, can be within IHT even after years in the UAE. Changing domicile of origin is difficult: it requires a genuine, long-term intention to remain outside the UK permanently, supported by evidence (ties cut, wills, lifestyle).

Non-domiciled individuals are generally taxed on **UK-situs** assets only, but complex rules apply to deemed domicile after long UK residence. Dubai **succession law** and UK **IHT** can both affect the same asset. Registering a UAE will for non-Muslims and taking UK estate planning advice is standard for British owners of UAE property.

## How does CRS reporting from UAE banks affect HMRC?

Under the Common Reporting Standard, UAE financial institutions collect your **declared tax residency** and report account details to the UAE authorities, which exchange data with partner countries. If you tell your bank you are **UK tax resident**, account balances and certain income can reach HMRC even when you file from Dubai.

That makes **consistent self-certification** critical. Declare UAE residency only when you genuinely meet UAE tax residency tests and have updated your UK position. Full detail: [CRS and FATCA for UAE bank accounts](/guides/uae-crs-fatca-banking/).

| Document | What to align |
| --- | --- |
| UAE bank CRS form | Actual tax residency you claim |
| UK self-assessment | Same worldwide income CRS may show |
| UAE Tax Residency Certificate | Only if you meet MOF presence tests |

## What does the UK-UAE double tax treaty change for individuals?

The UK and UAE have a treaty, but the UAE does not levy broad personal income tax or individual capital gains tax in the way the UK does. For most **individual property investors**, the treaty mainly **allocates taxing rights** rather than wiping out a large UAE tax bill you never paid. It is more useful for **companies**, employment with cross-border elements, and specialist structures.

Do not assume the treaty exempts Dubai rent from UK tax while you remain UK resident. Treat treaty articles as something your adviser maps to **your** income types.

## How do UK purchase taxes compare with buying in Dubai?

If you **keep** a UK home while buying in Dubai, Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT) on the UK purchase is unchanged by your Dubai move. Dubai acquisition costs are different: mainly the **4% DLD-style transfer fee**, trustee fees, and agent commission (see [Dubai property taxes explained](/guides/dubai-property-taxes-explained/)). SDLT and DLD are separate regimes; planning a UK exit does not remove SDLT on a concurrent UK purchase.

## What practical steps should British Dubai buyers take?

- Run the **SRT** with day counts and ties **before** 5 April each year.
- File **self-assessment** on time if UK resident; report **worldwide** rent and gains.
- Match **CRS self-certification** at UAE banks to your actual residency story.
- Separate **UK domicile / IHT** planning from **UAE visa** paperwork.
- Use a UK adviser who handles **overseas property** and a UAE adviser for **local registration**, not one generalist on both.

## What mistakes do UK nationals make most often?

**Assuming Dubai residency visa equals UK non-residence.** Visa status in the UAE does not override the SRT.

**Retaining a UK property available for personal use.** That accommodation tie shrinks the number of safe UK visit days.

**Not declaring Dubai rent because the UAE did not tax it.** HMRC taxes **net rental profit**, not whether the UAE charged tax.

**Selling Dubai property in a year you are still UK resident** without modelling CGT first.

**Ignoring CRS** after telling a UAE bank you remain UK tax resident.
