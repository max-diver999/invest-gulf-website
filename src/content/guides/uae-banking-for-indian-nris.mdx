## Who qualifies as an NRI in the UAE?

An Indian citizen with a UAE residence visa typically qualifies as an NRI under FEMA and can open NRE, NRO, and FCNR accounts within the first 30 to 60 days, and we treat paper visas without genuine UAE presence as a DTAA red flag.

This distinction matters because:

- NRIs access exclusive Indian bank account types (NRE, NRO, FCNR) not available to resident Indians
- Tax treatment of Indian-sourced income changes once FEMA NRI status applies
- Repatriation rules are governed by FEMA, not resident capital controls
- India-UAE DTAA benefits require genuine UAE tax residency and physical presence

With an estimated 3.4 million Indian nationals in the UAE, Dubai and Abu Dhabi have more India-facing banking infrastructure than almost anywhere outside India.

**Insider tip:** Open the NRE account in the same week as your UAE salary account, because funding Dubai property from NRO later hits the USD 1 million yearly outward cap and complicates sale repatriation.

## How do NRE, NRO, and FCNR(B) accounts compare?

UAE-based NRIs typically keep salary flowing into NRE deposits earning about 6.5 to 7.5% p.a. tax-free for 1 to 3 year tenors while NRO receives Indian rent, and Invest Gulf pairs both accounts before any Dubai purchase for foreign buyers across a full relocation year in Dubai.

Practical pairing checklist:

- NRE for UAE salary and Dubai purchase funds
- NRO for Indian rent and local INR expenses
- FCNR(B) when you want to stay in USD or AED

Indian NRIs in the UAE typically remit AED salary into NRE savings or fixed deposits earning about 6.5 to 7.5% p.a. tax-free for 1 to 3 year tenors at major banks, while NRO accounts collect Indian rental income with 30% TDS on interest. FCNR(B) deposits hold USD, GBP, EUR, or AED inside Indian banks without forcing an INR conversion at all. Dubai freehold purchases funded from NRE or UAE salary usually repatriate cleanly after sale, but NRO funding can hit the USD 1 million yearly outward remittance cap. Transfers under AED 3,500 often favour Wise or exchange houses settling in 24 to 48 hours, while amounts over AED 50,000 usually need bank SWIFT desks. Keep FIRCs and SPA copies for every property-linked remittance on file.

### FCNR(B) Accounts

FCNR(B) accounts typically let NRIs hold USD, GBP, EUR, AED and other currencies inside Indian banks with tax-free interest, and Invest Gulf uses them when clients want to park AED or USD earnings for 1 to 5 years without forcing INR conversion for foreign buyers.

## What does the India-UAE DTAA cover for NRIs?

The India-UAE DTAA typically keeps genuine UAE employment income outside Indian tax and confirm UAE property gains are taxed only where the asset sits, and we still require real physical presence after the 2020 MLI principal purpose test for foreign buyers who keep real UAE economic ties.

### Key DTAA Provisions for UAE-Based NRIs

- Salary: not taxed in India for genuine UAE tax residents with visa, presence, and economic ties
- NRE interest: exempt under Section 10(4); DTAA adds treaty certainty
- NRO interest: usually 30% TDS; treaty rarely cuts that rate
- UAE property capital gains: taxed only in UAE (no personal CGT)
- Indian property gains: taxed in India; long-term rates near 12.5% without indexation or 20% with indexation (verify 2026 Finance Act)
- Dividends from Indian companies: often 20% TDS for NRIs
- UAE corporate tax: 9% from June 2023 above AED 375,000 taxable turnover for relevant persons

> **MLI impact:** Arrangements built only for treaty shopping without substantive UAE residency fail the Principal Purpose Test from 2020.

NRIs can typically buy Dubai freehold property with no RBI bar in designated zones, and Invest Gulf funds purchases from UAE salary or NRE rather than NRO because the USD 1 million yearly outward cap is a repatriation red flag for foreign buyers planning a clean sale exit later.

Payment sources that keep repatriation clean:

- AED salary credited to a UAE bank account
- Funds transferred from an NRE account in India
- FCNR(B) liquidation proceeds

- Eligible income: UAE salary, documented overseas salary, rent, business profit

4. No Indian tax on UAE capital gain under the immovable-property article

## Which FEMA rules apply when NRIs buy property in India?

FEMA typically allows NRIs to buy residential or commercial property in India without RBI approval except agricultural land, and we require NRE, NRO, or FIRC-backed remittances rather than cash for foreign buyers who still keep Indian real estate in this market in this market.

### Payment Rules

Repatriation from NRO is typically capped near USD 1 million per financial year. All payments for Indian property must come from:

- NRE or NRO account in India
- Inward remittances from UAE with FIRC
- FCNR(B) liquidation

### Tax on Indian Property Income

**Rental income:** Must be credited to NRO. After TDS, report in Indian ITR with standard 30% maintenance-style deductions where applicable.

Capital gains on sale:

- Short-term under 24 months: slab rates up to about 30% plus surcharge
- Long-term over 24 months: 12.5% without indexation (post-July 2024 Finance Act, verify)

Buyer TDS often sits at 12.5% for LTCG property and 30% for STCG. Repatriation from NRO is capped near USD 1 million per financial year unless the NRE trail is clean.

## What common NRI banking mistakes should you avoid?

Common NRI banking mistakes typically include funding Dubai property from NRO, skipping Schedule FA overseas-asset disclosure, and using unlicensed exchanges, and our research treats each as a FEMA or tax red flag after the first 12 months for foreign buyers during the first UAE assignment year in this market.

1. Using NRO funds for UAE property and hitting the USD 1 million yearly outward cap later
2. Missing Schedule FA overseas asset disclosure (penalties can reach INR 10 lakh per violation)
3. Ignoring UAE corporate tax at 9% above AED 375,000 taxable turnover for business activity
4. Using unlicensed exchange houses or hawala channels
