## What bank transfer method is actually required?

UAE law mandates that all property transactions settle through licensed banking channels. The Dubai Land Department (DLD) and its approved trustee offices will not process a title deed transfer unless every payment is traceable, authenticated, and cleared through the UAE banking system.

In practice, this means three payment instruments cover virtually all UAE property closings:

| Payment instrument | Typical use | Who can issue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Manager's cheque | Purchase price, DLD fee, trustee fee | UAE bank account holder |
| Local bank transfer (UAEFTS) | Mortgage disbursement, developer invoices | UAE bank account holder |
| International SWIFT wire | Incoming funds from overseas → UAE account | Any bank worldwide |

Cash payments, cryptocurrency, and foreign-issued bank drafts are not accepted. The UAE's Financial Intelligence Unit and Real Estate Regulatory Agency (RERA) require a full audit trail from source to settlement.

For buyers wiring funds from outside the UAE, the typical flow is: **international wire → UAE bank account → manager's cheque at branch → trustee appointment**. Each step has lead times, and skipping the middle step creates problems at closing.

## Why manager's cheques: not wire transfers, dominate closings

Most buyers assume they can wire funds directly to the seller. In Dubai's DLD trustee system, this is not how transactions close.

A manager's cheque (also called a banker's draft) is a guaranteed instrument: the bank debits your account immediately and issues a cheque that cannot bounce. Trustees require guaranteed instruments because they need to verify cleared funds before handing over the title deed, a personal or corporate cheque creates settlement risk.

**Standard cheques required at trustee appointment for a ready property:**

| Payment | Payee | Typical amount (AED 2M example) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purchase price | DLD-approved trustee | AED 2,000,000 |
| DLD registration fee (4%) | Dubai Land Department | AED 80,000 |
| Trustee service fee | Trustee office | AED 4,200 + VAT (approx.) |
| Agency commission (2%) | Real estate agency | AED 40,000 |

On a standard AED 2M purchase, you walk into the trustee office with 4 manager's cheques totalling approximately AED 2,127,000+. Some trustees accept the agency commission directly from the seller's proceeds, confirm arrangements with your broker before issuing cheques.

Manager's cheques are available at any UAE bank branch where you hold an account with sufficient cleared funds. Fee: typically AED 10–30 per cheque. Processing: same-day if branch is not busy. You cannot obtain manager's cheques without a UAE bank account, which is why [opening an account as a non-resident](/guides/gulf-banking-comparison-expats/) is a prerequisite, not an afterthought.

## Off-plan purchases: RERA escrow accounts

Off-plan properties sold before construction completion are governed by RERA Law No. 8 of 2007, which mandates a separate escrow account for each development project. Every dirham a buyer pays for an off-plan unit must flow into the project's dedicated escrow, not the developer's operating account.

How escrow works:

1. Developer registers the project with RERA and opens a project escrow account at a UAE Central Bank-licensed trustee bank.
2. Buyer makes each payment per the payment plan schedule: via bank transfer or manager's cheque payable to the escrow account.
3. An independent escrow agent audits the account and approves withdrawals only when DLD-verified construction milestones are hit.
4. Minimum 20% of sales value must be in escrow before the developer can break ground; 50% before they can draw more than infrastructure costs.
5. At handover, remaining buyer payments flow from escrow to the developer.

This structure protects buyers from developer insolvency, funds cannot be redirected to other projects. However, it also means payment timelines are strict. If your bank transfer arrives late, the developer's milestone schedule and your contractual payment date may conflict, triggering a 1% penalty per month on late installments under standard off-plan contracts.

What you need for each off-plan payment:

- Manager's cheque or UAEFTS transfer payable to the specific escrow account number provided in the SPA
- Payment reference including unit number, development name, and installment number
- Retain all receipts, they form your proof of contribution in case of developer default

For the full picture on escrow accounts and developer risk, see the [step-by-step buying process guide](/guides/how-to-buy-property-dubai-step-by-step/).

## International bank transfers: how to get funds into UAE on time

Non-resident buyers transferring from overseas have one compulsory step before anything else: funds must clear in a UAE bank account before manager's cheques can be issued.

SWIFT international transfers to UAE typically take 1–5 business days from initiation to credited balance, but the real-world range is wider. Delays come from:

- Correspondent bank hops, a wire from a smaller regional bank may route through 2–3 intermediary banks, each deducting USD 10–25 and adding 24–48 hours
- AML holds, large first-time inbound transfers above AED 500,000 often trigger a manual review by the UAE receiving bank, adding 1–2 days
- Currency conversion queues; if your wire arrives in USD or EUR and the account is AED, the bank's treasury converts same-day only during business hours
- Documentation requests, the UAE bank may ask for source of funds documentation mid-transfer, freezing funds until you respond

**Rule: initiate the international wire at least 7 business days before your trustee appointment.** For your first transfer above AED 1M to a new account, plan 10 business days.

Always send a small test amount, AED 1,000, 2 weeks before the main transfer if you are using a new route. Confirm it arrives and your bank can see the incoming SWIFT MT103. This eliminates technical routing errors before they block your transaction.

## What Source of Funds Documentation Is Required at Each Transfer Stage?

UAE banks and DLD trustees apply anti-money laundering checks at multiple points. Preparing documentation in advance prevents delays.

At the UAE bank when receiving an international wire:

| Bank check | Document required |
| --- | --- |
| Source of funds declaration | Signed SOF form, provided by the bank |
| Evidence of purchase intent | Signed SPA or MOU with unit reference |
| Fund origin documentation | 6-month bank statements showing accumulation |
| Employment or business income | Pay slips, tax returns, company financials |

At the trustee office on transaction day:

| Trustee check | Document required |
| --- | --- |
| Passport / ID | Original + copy |
| Manager's cheques | Drawn on UAE account only |
| NOC from developer | For off-plan or mortgaged properties |
| Original SPA or MOU | Signed by both parties |

High-value transactions, generally above AED 5M, trigger enhanced due diligence at both the bank and trustee level. Prepare source-of-wealth documentation (investment statements, business ownership records, inheritance probate) in advance. See the full requirements breakdown in the [proof of funds guide](/guides/proof-of-funds-property-uae/).

## Payment stages timeline: ready property

Below is the typical bank transfer and payment sequence for a ready secondary-market property in Dubai.

| Stage | Action | Bank transfer needed | Typical timeline |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reservation / MOU | 10% security deposit | Manager's cheque to seller / agent escrow | Day 0–3 |
| **Mortgage application** | Bank valuation, approval | No transfer, documentation stage | Day 0–21 |
| **NOC from developer** | Release existing mortgage | Transfer if seller has mortgage | Day 14–30 |
| **International wire** | Funds from overseas to UAE account | SWIFT wire | Day -10 to -3 before trustee |
| **Collect manager's cheques** | At UAE bank branch | Account debit | Day -2 to -1 before trustee |
| **Trustee appointment** | Title deed transfer, all payments | Manager's cheques presented | Trustee date |
| **Mortgage bank disbursement** | Bank pays seller directly (mortgage buyers) | UAE bank wire to seller | Same day as trustee |
| **Title deed issued** | DLD registers in buyer's name | No payment, registration confirmation | 1–5 business days post-trustee |

The MOU deposit (10%) is typically held by the real estate agency as an escrow or paid directly to the seller. This initial payment is usually a manager's cheque or wire, and it becomes non-refundable if the buyer withdraws without cause, confirm escrow terms before issuing this cheque.

## Payment stages timeline: off-plan payment plan

Off-plan payment structures are different, payments spread across construction milestones rather than a single closing day.

| Plan type | Typical structure | Bank transfer frequency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Standard milestone plan** | 10% booking + 40% during construction + 50% on handover | Every 2–6 months |
| **Post-handover plan** | 20% during construction + 80% over 2–5 years after handover | Monthly or quarterly post-handover |
| **Bulk payment (discount plan)** | 50–80% upfront for 3–7% price discount | 1–2 transfers |

Each installment is a separate bank transfer or manager's cheque, not a single wire at the start. Many off-plan buyers set up UAE bank standing orders (UAEFTS) to automate installments once funds arrive in-country. This is particularly useful for buyers based outside the UAE who want to avoid logging in to transfer each installment manually.

## Mortgage buyers: how bank disbursement works

If you are financing the purchase, the bank, not you, makes the final purchase payment to the seller. Your role is to get cleared funds to the bank for your equity contribution (down payment) and then authorize the mortgage disbursement.

**UAE mortgage LTV limits as of 2026:**

| Buyer type | First property | Second property |
| --- | --- | --- |
| UAE resident expat | 80% LTV (under AED 5M) / 70% (over AED 5M) | 65% LTV |
| Non-resident | 50% LTV | 40% LTV |
| UAE national | 85% LTV (under AED 5M) | 75% LTV |

Your down payment (equity portion) must be in your UAE bank account before the mortgage bank will schedule the disbursement. The mortgage bank issues a **liability letter** confirming the loan amount, this is presented at the trustee along with your equity manager's cheque. The bank then wires its portion directly to the seller in UAE dirhams.

For the highest LTV as a non-resident (50%), on a **AED 3M** property you need **AED 1.5M** cleared in UAE before trustee day, plus approximately AED 210,000 in fees. Total UAE bank balance required: around **AED 1.71M** minimum.

## Choosing the right UAE bank for property transfers

Not all UAE banks handle non-resident property buyer accounts with equal efficiency. Key differences that affect your transfer experience:

| Factor | Matters because |
| --- | --- |
| **Inbound SWIFT speed** | Faster processing = less buffer time needed |
| **AML pre-clearance process** | Some banks allow SOF review before wire arrives |
| **Manager's cheque availability** | Branch network, same-day vs next-day |
| **AED 1M+ transfer support** | Relationship manager vs call centre handling |
| **Account opening non-resident timeline** | 2–6 weeks varies significantly by bank |

Emirates NBD, ADCB, ENBD, Mashreq, and First Abu Dhabi Bank all have dedicated real estate banking desks that can pre-clear large inbound transfers if you submit documentation in advance. International private banks (HSBC Premier, Standard Chartered Priority) can use their correspondence relationships for faster SWIFT routing. ## What Are the Most Common Mistakes That Delay UAE Property Transfers?

**1. Ordering manager's cheques with wrong payee name**
The payee on the manager's cheque must match the trustee office's registered legal name exactly. "DLD Trustee" vs the specific company name (e.g. "Bins Property Services LLC") will cause rejection. Get the exact payee names from your broker before visiting the bank.

**2. Transferring funds the day before the trustee appointment**
UAEFTS domestic transfers clear same-day, but if your receiving bank applies a hold or a manual AML review triggers on the same day, funds will not be available for manager's cheques. Build in at least 2 business days domestic buffer, 7 days international.

**3. Using a foreign-issued banker's draft**
A bank draft from Barclays, HDFC, or Sberbank is not accepted by UAE trustees. Funds must be in a UAE-licensed bank account with a UAE-issued instrument.

**4. Forgetting VAT on trustee fee**
The trustee service fee is subject to 5% UAE VAT. Budget approximately **AED 4,200–6,300 + VAT** (5%) depending on property value bracket. An under-prepared cheque cannot simply be topped up in the room.

**5. Paying without an NOC**
For properties with an existing mortgage or developer services charge arrears, the seller must obtain a No Objection Certificate (NOC) before the trustee can transfer title. Without the NOC, the appointment cancels. Confirm NOC status 2 weeks before trustee date.

## Transfer scenarios: how buyers typically fund UAE property

**Scenario A — first-time buyer wiring from Europe or UK:** Open the UAE account 3–4 weeks before MOU signing. Initiate SWIFT 7–10 business days before trustee. Keep 5% buffer above SPA total for FX drift and VAT on trustee fees.

**Scenario B — Russian or CIS buyer using AED corridor:** Confirm your UAE bank's current source-of-funds checklist before you transfer. Split large amounts across documented tranches if your bank requests trade invoices or sale contracts. Manager's cheques still must come from a UAE-licensed bank.

**Scenario C — off-plan milestone payment:** Developer escrow accounts have different payee names than DLD trustees. Never reuse a manager's cheque template from a resale deal. Request the developer's registered escrow account details in writing for each milestone.

**Red flags:** trustee appointment booked before wire clears; payee name mismatch on cheques; seller NOC not issued; funds sitting in exchange house instead of UAE bank account.

## Summary: pre-transfer checklist for non-resident buyers

Before your trustee appointment, confirm every item:

- UAE bank account open, active, and able to receive international wires ✓
- International wire initiated at least 7 business days before trustee ✓
- Cleared AED balance covers: purchase price + 4% DLD + trustee fee + VAT + agency commission ✓
- Source of funds documentation submitted to UAE bank ✓
- Proof of funds letter obtained for trustee ✓
- Manager's cheques ordered 1–2 days before appointment with correct payee names ✓
- Seller's NOC from developer confirmed ✓
- SPA and MOU copies available on appointment day ✓

The bank transfer process for UAE property is systematic rather than complex, the key is sequencing: account open first, funds wired with buffer, manager's cheques collected last. Missing any step delays closing and can expose you to MOU penalty clauses.
