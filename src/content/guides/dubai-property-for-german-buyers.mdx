German nationals buy Dubai freehold in designated zones under the same DLD process used by other foreign buyers, with no separate nationality approval layer beyond passport KYC and zone verification. Market share for German buyers is roughly 2 to 3% of foreign transactions, yet average ticket sizes of AED 2.8 to 3.0 million place the segment in villa and premium apartment stock rather than entry-level studios. UAE rental income remains taxed at 0% locally, while German tax residents must still declare Welteinkommen to the Finanzamt, including Dubai rent banked offshore. Closing costs typically add 6 to 9% above purchase price through the 4% DLD transfer fee, trustee charges near AED 4,000, secondary-market broker commission around 2%, and legal review of AED 5,000 to 15,000. A registered purchase at AED 2 million or above also opens a 10-year Golden Visa application path, but the visa stamp does not cancel German reporting duties for residents.

German tax planning for Dubai property starts with residency status, not the marketing claim of a zero-tax city. A German tax resident collecting AED 80,000 annual rent (about EUR 20,000) from a Dubai Hills apartment reports that income as Vermietung und Verpachtung even when UAE tax is AED 0 and no foreign tax credit offsets Einkommensteuer. Private capital gains on a sale can become tax-free in Germany after a 10-year holding period under Section 23 EStG when the asset is held privately, which is why many German buyer scenarios favour long holds over flip strategies. Wegzugsteuer can still matter if a resident relocates to the UAE while holding large unrealised gains on German shares or business interests above EUR 500,000, so Steuerberater modelling should precede both relocation and purchase. Foreign bank and asset reporting via Anlage AO remains mandatory for residents with UAE accounts.

## How does German tax residency treat Dubai income?

German tax residency determines how Dubai rental income and sale gains are taxed because Germany requires residents to report worldwide income to the Finanzamt on this market. A Dubai Hills flat generating AED 80,000 rent (about EUR 20,000) still needs Einkommensteuer filing for foreign buyers even when UAE tax is 0%, so run a tax checklist first.

| Income type | German tax treatment |
|---|---|
| Dubai rental income | Einkommensteuer at progressive rates (14-45%) |
| Dubai property sale gain | Private capital gain, tax-free after 10-year holding period for private sales; otherwise taxable |
| Foreign tax paid | Anrechnung ausländischer Steuer per DBA, limited when UAE tax is 0% |
| Werbungskosten | Deductible expenses (management, maintenance, travel) against rental income |

Practical buyer scenario: a German tax resident owns a Dubai Hills apartment generating AED 80,000 annual rent (approximately EUR 20,000). The rent stays in a UAE bank account.

- UAE tax: AED 0
- German tax: EUR 20,000 reportable as Einkünfte aus Vermietung und Verpachtung
- German tax due: At progressive Einkommensteuer rate (14-45% depending on total income)
- DBA relief: Germany-UAE treaty exists, but 0% UAE tax means no foreign credit

### The 10-year capital gains exemption

Germany exempts private capital gains on real property if held for more than 10 years (Section 23 EStG). This is a significant advantage for German buy-and-hold investors:

| Holding period | German CGT on Dubai sale |
|---|---|
| Under 10 years | Taxable at individual rate |
| 10 years or more | Tax-free (private disposal) |
| Business property | Different rules, always taxable |

German buyers planning long-term Dubai holds benefit from this exemption, provided the property is held privately, not through a business structure.

### Wegzugsteuer (exit tax)

When a German tax resident relocates abroad, Wegzugsteuer may apply:

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Trigger | Relocation with unrealised gains on certain assets exceeding EUR 500,000 |
| Covered assets | Shares, business interests, certain other assets |
| Real property abroad | Generally not subject to Wegzugsteuer at departure |
| Real property in Germany | Subject to extended taxation (extended unlimited tax liability) |
| Deferral | Tax deferral available if moving to EU/EEA country |
| UAE relocation | Deferral may not apply, UAE is not EU/EEA |

Key point for Dubai buyers: Purchasing Dubai property after relocating to the UAE is clean. Holding German property while buying Dubai property requires separate planning. Germans with large unrealised gains in German assets who plan UAE relocation should consult a Steuerberater before moving.

### Anmeldung ausländischer Konten und Vermögen

German tax residents must report foreign bank accounts and assets to the Finanzamt:

- Anlage AO, foreign bank and financial accounts
- Ausländische Betriebsstätten; if property held through structure
- Dubai property itself declared in the regular Einkommensteuererklärung

Non-reporting carries penalties under German tax law and the Schwarzgeldgesetz. Treat missing Anlage AO as a red flag before you expand the portfolio.

## What purchase steps should German buyers complete?

German buyers should verify tax position and freehold status before completing a purchase, because the DLD process still requires Form F or SPA, a typical 10% deposit, and a 4% transfer fee on this market. Foreign buyers purchasing remotely usually need a notarised Power of Attorney for trustee day completion after the document checklist.

Purchase checklist:

1. Confirm tax position with Steuerberater: especially if relocating
2. Select property in a designated freehold zone
3. Verify via Dubai REST app (Unit Profile, ownership, encumbrances)
4. Agree terms: MOU (Form F) on secondary; SPA on off-plan
5. Pay deposit: typically 10% on secondary market
6. Transfer funds: SWIFT from German bank to UAE escrow or trustee
7. Mortgage (if applicable): 3-6 weeks processing
8. NOC from developer (secondary market)
9. DLD registration: 4% transfer fee at trustee centre
10. Title deed or Oqood certificate issued
11. Golden Visa application (if AED 2M+)
12. Ejari registration (if renting out)

German buyers purchasing remotely use notarised Power of Attorney, typically at the UAE Embassy in Berlin or through a German Notar with UAE attestation.

## What mistakes should German buyers avoid?

German buyers should avoid tax-reporting and transaction mistakes that treat UAE 0% income tax as Finanzamt silence on this market. Welteinkommen reporting still applies to residents, the 10-year German CGT clock starts at purchase, and missing Ejari or Anlage AO creates avoidable red flag exposure for foreign buyers.

Common red flag checklist:

1. **Assuming UAE 0% tax means Finanzamt 0% tax.** Welteinkommen applies to German residents.
2. **Ignoring Wegzugsteuer on relocation.** Plan asset position before moving to UAE.
3. **Selling before 10 years.** German CGT exemption requires decade-long hold.
4. **Buying on gross yield.** Model net yield after service charges, management, and German tax.
5. **No Ejari registration.** Without Ejari, you cannot enforce tenancy terms at RDC.
6. **Skipping independent legal review** on German-translated SPA: translation errors happen.
7. **Not declaring foreign accounts.** Anlage AO is mandatory.
8. **Using unlicensed agents.** Verify RERA BRN at rera.gov.ae.

Golden Visa eligibility at AED 2 million registered value does not change German tax obligations for residents, and the visa typically allows longer UAE presence without rewriting Finanzamt rules on this market. German buyers with average purchases of AED 2.8 to 3.0 million often qualify on first deal as foreign buyers.

Visa checklist after the title deed:

- Threshold: AED 2M registered property value
- Typical German ticket: AED 2.8-3.0M
- Tax effect: none on Welteinkommen duties
- Process: separate GDRFA or ICP filing after DLD registration
