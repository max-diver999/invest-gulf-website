Nationality-specific buying guides are almost all built the same way: a document checklist, a note about bank transfers, a paragraph telling you to get tax advice, and steps that are identical for everybody.

The German position does not fit that shape, because the thing that most affects the outcome is not a document assembled before completion. It is a period that starts at completion and runs for a decade.

## Why is a clock a different kind of constraint?

Because it cannot be dealt with and closed. A document pack is assembled, submitted and finished; a holding period starts at a moment you can no longer change and stays relevant for years.

That difference decides where the planning effort should go. For most nationalities the home-country work clusters around the transaction: prove the source of funds, satisfy a bank, file something afterwards. For a German buyer the transaction itself is comparatively ordinary and the consequential decision is about time. Once the acquisition date exists, every subsequent question about when to sell is answered partly by a calendar set on that day, and no amount of later diligence moves it. The general question of holding for income against holding for a gain is on the [appreciation versus yield page](/guides/dubai-capital-appreciation-vs-yield/).

## What is the claim, precisely, and how sure is it?

That German law contains a ten-year speculation period for privately held real estate, under Section 23 of the Income Tax Act, after which a private disposal can fall outside the taxable category.

This site holds that claim in its external-claims register, and it is recorded there as not independently verified. That status is deliberate and it is the honest thing to publish rather than an admission of weakness. It means the claim is well enough established to shape the question you ask, and not established enough to act on without confirmation. The right use of it is to walk into a conversation with a German adviser already knowing what to ask about, which is a far better position than arriving with a general question about foreign property, and a far worse one than believing you already have the answer.

## What does a clock change about the purchase?

It moves the hold period from an afterthought into an input. Two buyers of the same apartment with different intended holds are in different positions from the day they sign.

| Decision | If the hold is short | If the hold is long |
|---|---|---|
| What the acquisition date means | A date that will still matter at sale | A date that will have passed |
| How to weigh income against gain | Income carries more of the case | The gain is part of the plan |
| Off-plan versus completed | The start of the clock is a live question | Less sensitive |
| What to confirm before buying | The whole timing position | The whole timing position |
| Where the advice belongs | At the start | At the start |

The bottom two rows are identical on purpose. Whichever hold you intend, the confirmation belongs at the beginning, because that is when the answer can still influence what you do.

## Why does off-plan complicate it?

Because there are several candidate dates and only one of them is the right one. A completed purchase has a single obvious moment; an off-plan purchase has a contract date, a sequence of instalments, a completion and a registration.

Which of those starts a holding period is a question of law rather than of common sense, and it is precisely the kind of question that produces a confident wrong answer from someone who has not looked it up. A buyer intending a long hold may not care much. A buyer whose plan sits anywhere near the boundary should establish the answer before choosing between off-plan and completed stock, because it can genuinely change which one is the better purchase. The trade-off between the two, on all the other grounds, is on the [off-plan versus ready page](/guides/off-plan-vs-ready-property-dubai/).

## What runs alongside the clock?

A recurring obligation that never finishes. German tax residents have reporting duties relating to foreign holdings and foreign income, and those repeat annually for as long as the property is owned.

Keeping these two things separate in your head is worth the effort, because they are both loosely called tax and they behave nothing alike. One is an annual administrative task that has to be done every year and produces no strategic decisions. The other is a single date whose consequences arrive once, years later, and which is entirely determined at the start. The practical result is that a German owner needs both a routine and a plan, and most people set up neither because the two get merged into a vague intention to sort out the tax at some point.

## Where should the research effort actually go?

Onto the German side, which is the reverse of what buyers do. The Dubai half of this transaction is well documented, standardised and identical for every nationality.

Registration, the trustee office, agency, the developer's process and the property itself are the same whoever is buying, and they are covered exhaustively across this site and everywhere else. The German half is specific, consequential and covered almost nowhere, because it belongs to a different profession in a different country. Buyers nonetheless spend their preparation on the visible half, because it is the half with photographs. An hour with a German adviser before choosing a property is worth more than a week of reading about Dubai conveyancing, and it is the hour almost nobody books. The market-side reading is on the [Dubai investment guide](/guides/dubai-property-investment-guide/).

## Does holding the property in a company change it?

It changes the category, which is why people suggest it and why it is rarely the simple answer it sounds like. A holding-period rule that applies to privately held property is a rule about private ownership.

Move the asset into a structure and you are no longer in that category, which removes the clock and introduces a different set of rules covering the structure itself, its own obligations, its costs and what happens when you eventually unwind it. That trade can make sense for a portfolio and rarely makes sense for one apartment, because the running cost and administrative weight of a structure are the same whether it holds one property or ten. The instruction is the same as everywhere else on this page: it is a question for a German adviser, asked before the purchase, when the answer can still change what you do. Asking it after the property is registered in your own name means asking about a transfer rather than about a choice, and transfers have consequences of their own.

## What should a German buyer have settled before signing?

Four things, and all of them belong to advisers rather than to agents. The holding-period position for the specific purchase, including which date starts it. The annual reporting routine and who will run it. The currency approach across the payment schedule. And whether the property is held personally or otherwise.

None of those is a Dubai question and none of them can be answered by the people selling you the apartment, which is exactly why they get deferred. A buyer who has all four settled walks into the transaction with the consequential decisions already made and can then concentrate on the property itself, which is what the visible half of the process is actually for.

## Is there a currency question too?

Yes, and it is a smaller version of the British one rather than something new. The euro floats against the dollar and the dirham is pegged to the dollar, so a euro buyer on a payment plan converts repeatedly at rates that do not exist yet.

The exposure is real and the handling is the same: convert early, convert as each instalment falls, or fix the rate ahead for some or all of the schedule. It is a smaller problem than the holding-period question for most German buyers, and it has the same property of being decided at signing whether or not anybody decides it. Since both conversations belong before the purchase rather than after it, they are worth having together. The full treatment of the mechanics is on the [British buyers page](/guides/dubai-property-for-british-buyers/), which faces the same structure through a different currency, and the eventual unwinding of a UAE position is on the [exit checklist](/guides/leaving-dubai-exit-checklist/).
