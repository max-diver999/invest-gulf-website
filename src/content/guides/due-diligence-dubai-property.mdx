Dubai's property market has one of the most transparent title registration systems in the Gulf. The Dubai Land Department's digital infrastructure, from the Dubai REST app to the Oqood off-plan registry, gives buyers tools that simply did not exist a decade ago. That transparency is an advantage. It does not, however, mean the process is risk-free.

The cases where buyers get hurt in Dubai are almost always traceable to skipped due diligence: the SPA clause that wasn't read, the escrow account that wasn't verified, the service charge that was taken at developer estimate. This guide gives you the full checklist, what to verify, how to verify it, and what each check actually protects you against.

## How is Dubai property due diligence structured?

Dubai property due diligence typically runs a 23-point checklist, yet three items catch about 80% of problems Invest Gulf sees: escrow history, seller NOC status, and service charge arrears. Pull the Owners Association statement before any SPA is signed on resale units.

Dubai property due diligence typically fails in the same three places Invest Gulf sees across resale files: missing escrow history on off-plan conversions, seller NOC status with the developer, and service charge arrears that transfer because the SPA stayed silent. A DLD Unit Profile costs AED 100 to 200, while independent SPA review runs AED 5,000 to 15,000 on purchases above AED 1 million. Acquisition costs of 6 to 9% sit on top of the headline price, and Mollak service charges of AED 13 to 25 per sqft for mid-rise towers often exceed brochure estimates by 30 to 50%. Foreign buyers who skip the Owners Association statement before signing inherit debt that was avoidable with a one-day document pull. Sequence title, escrow, SPA, and Mollak before any transfer appointment is booked.

Due diligence in Dubai differs by transaction type:

- Secondary market: title already registered; risks are encumbrances, condition, and service charge arrears
- Off-plan: Oqood instead of title deed; risks are escrow, delivery, and SPA remedies
- Shared checks: developer standing, cost stack of 6 to 9%, and independent legal review

## What DLD title checks matter on ready resale?

DLD title checks on ready resale typically start with a Unit Profile request costing AED 100 to 200 and taking minutes online. We treat a skipped title pull as a deal blocker for foreign buyers. Off-plan files need the Oqood certificate matched to the DLD portal before SPA work continues.

Title checklist:

- Freehold vs leasehold stated on the Unit Profile
- Mortgage or other encumbrances listed for discharge at transfer
- Seller identity matches the registered owner
- For off-plan: Oqood number verified on the DLD portal

If the seller has a mortgage, buyer funds held by the Registration Trustee typically repay the bank before transfer. Do not allow transfer until DLD clearance is issued.

## How do you verify off-plan escrow and developer registration?

Off-plan escrow verification typically means confirming a RERA-registered project account under UAE Law No. 8 of 2007 before any buyer funds move. Invest Gulf walks away when a developer cannot show an active escrow number in the DLD Dubai REST app or RERA portal within the first review days.

Off-plan developer screening typically starts with RERA Trakheesi registration and an escrow account in the project name, then moves to delivery track records. Our research treats Emaar near 95% on-time and Aldar or Nakheel near 90 to 93% as lighter diligence, while Azizi or Binghatti bands of 78 to 82% need enhanced project-level evidence. New developers with no completions require maximum scrutiny and independent counsel before deposits leave the buyer account. Law No. 8 of 2007 escrow rules exist to stop funds sitting in a developer's general account, so a missing or suspended RERA listing is a walk-away signal within the first week of review. Ask for the last five delivered projects with SPA completion dates versus actual handover dates before trusting any developer marketing deck.

| Developer tier | Estimated on-time delivery | Diligence intensity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Emaar | ~95% | Light |
| Aldar, Nakheel, Sobha | 90 to 93% | Standard |
| DAMAC, Omniyat, Meraas | 87 to 93% | Standard |
| Azizi, Binghatti | 78 to 82% | Enhanced |
| Samana, Danube, Imtiaz | 65 to 80% | Enhanced |
| New developers, no completions | Unknown | Maximum |

Insider tip: Demand the escrow bank name and account number in writing before the reservation fee leaves your account; verbal "RERA compliant" claims without a portal match are the most common off-plan failure we see.

## Which SPA clauses need independent legal review?

SPA legal review typically costs AED 5,000 to 15,000 and is the highest-ROI check on a Dubai purchase above AED 1 million. We flag penalty cliffs of 0.5 to 1% per month, force majeure delay language, and short snagging windows before any buyer signs the SPA.

Critical SPA checklist:

1. Late-payment penalties: often 0.5 to 1% per month, sometimes escalating toward 20 to 30% before termination
2. Force majeure and delay: Law No. 14 of 2008 buyer rights after 6 months can be waived in weak SPAs
3. Handover acceptance: reject 48-hour defect windows without a written snagging period
4. Service charge language: estimates must not be framed as fixed caps against the OA
5. Spec changes: materials and layouts should sit in a schedule, not open "development rights"
6. Pre-handover resale: some SPAs require 30 to 40% paid before NOC

On a AED 1 million+ purchase, AED 5,000 to 15,000 of legal fees is 0.5 to 1.5% of deal value for protection against clauses that can cost multiples of that.

## How do you validate service charges and rental assumptions?

Service charge validation typically means pulling Mollak filings rather than brochure estimates, which understate real costs by 30 to 50% on many new towers. Our team underwrites mid-rise bands of AED 13 to 25 per sqft and premium bands of AED 25 to 50+ before any yield model runs.

Yield checklist:

- Mollak filed rate for the building, not developer estimate
- Ejari transaction rents, remembering listings run 5 to 10% above achieved
- Vacancy at 7 to 8% citywide baseline unless building data is tighter
- Management fees of 5 to 8% of rent
- Acquisition cost stack of 6 to 9% before net yield

## When do physical inspections and snagging matter?

Physical inspections typically matter most on ready resale and at off-plan handover, when defects still create negotiating room for the buyer. Invest Gulf books independent snagging before keys change hands, because UAE DLP cover is 1 year on finishes and 10 years on structure when notified in writing promptly.

Snagging checklist:

- Tiling, grouting, and finishing quality versus SPA schedule
- Window sealing in desert climate exposure
- Plumbing pressure and drainage
- Electrical fittings completeness
- Written defect list inside the SPA notification window

Do not accept keys until the snagging report is filed. Defects found pre-purchase create leverage; defects found later become your problem.

## Which Due diligence for Dubai property checks matter most before you sign?
A pre-sign checklist typically covers title or Oqood, escrow, SPA legal review, Mollak service charges, and a full 6 to 9% acquisition cost stack. We will not treat a deal as ready until each item has a dated document rather than a broker verbal assurance for foreign buyers.

**Off-plan additional:**

- Oqood registration verified
- RERA escrow confirmed in DLD portal
- Developer delivery track record reviewed
- RERA project registration active

**SPA review:**

- Independent solicitor retained (not developer-referred)
- Penalty clauses modelled against cash flow
- Force majeure and delay provisions reviewed
- Handover defect window confirmed
- Service charge estimate labelled as estimate with Mollak cross-check
- Unit specifications confirmed in SPA

**Financial and physical:**

- Full cost model with 6 to 9% acquisition costs
- Rental income from Ejari, not listings
- Independent inspection or handover snagging booked

## How does due diligence differ by deal type?

Due diligence intensity typically changes by deal type: off-plan needs escrow first, ready resale needs NOC and cost stacking, and buy-to-let needs Ejari rents not listings. We model 12 months of handover delay buffer on recent launches before any cash-flow claim is accepted by clients.

| Deal type | First check | Extra focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Off-plan | RERA escrow | Delivery history, SPA delay rights, 50% LTV cap if mortgaged |
| Ready resale | DLD Unit Profile (AED 100 to 200) | Mortgage discharge, NOC, snagging |
| Buy-to-let | Mollak + Ejari | Net yield after 6 to 9% acquisition costs |
