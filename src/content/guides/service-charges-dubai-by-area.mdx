  - question: "Can service charges increase in Dubai?"

In practice, service charges in Dubai have increased at roughly 5-10% annually in many buildings over the past five years, driven by utility cost inflation, maintenance cycles on aging buildings, and facilities upgrades. Service charges are the silent eroder of Dubai property returns. Every agent's pitch leads with gross yield. Almost none of them open with service charges. Yet in a building where service charges run AED 20/sqft and your unit is 1,000 sqft, you are paying AED 20,000 per year, before management fees, before vacancy, before a single day of your mortgage. On a property earning AED 90,000 in rent, that is more than 22% of your gross income gone before you receive it.

Understanding service charges, where to find the real figures, and how to model them accurately separates investors who know their actual return from those who discover it post-purchase.

## What do Dubai service charges pay for?

Dubai service charges are annual OA fees that cover common areas, lifts, security, insurance, shared utilities, and a RERA-mandated reserve equal to 10% of collections. Invest Gulf separates those line items from DEWA and district cooling before underwriting net yield. Foreign buyers should treat service charges as mandatory owner costs.

Reserve fund: RERA mandates that 10% of annual service charge collections go into a reserve fund for major capital expenditure. Older buildings with depleted reserves may levy special assessments. Ask whether any outstanding special assessments exist before buying.

District cooling: In parts of Downtown, Business Bay, JBR, and EIFC, chilled water is sometimes included in service charges and sometimes billed separately. Clarify which applies to your unit.

## What are typical service charges by Dubai area?

Typical service charges by Dubai area ranges from about AED 12 to AED 20 per sqft in JVC and Sports City, and typically reach AED 30 to AED 50 or more on Palm stock. Invest Gulf verifies each building on RERA Mollak rather than brochure bands. Foreign buyers should model rent share, not only per-sqft stickers.

### Mid-Market Residential (Highest Yield Communities)

| Community | Typical service charge AED/sqft/year | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Jumeirah Village Circle (JVC) | 13-20 | Wide variance building to building; check Mollak per building |
| Dubai Sports City | 12-18 | Generally well-managed; lower vacancy in established towers |
| Discovery Gardens | 11-16 | Older buildings; lower charges but older infrastructure |
| International City | 8-13 | Low charges; limited facilities; lower tenant quality ceiling |
| IMPZ / Production City | 14-18 | Mid-range; growing facilities |
| Dubai South | 13-17 | Newer buildings; charges still settling |
| Town Square | 12-15 | Good community management; Nshama operator |
| Dubailand | 10-15 | Varies by sub-community; less oversight history |

JVC practical example: A 650 sqft studio in a mid-range JVC tower at AED 16/sqft service charge = AED 10,400/year. If that studio earns AED 65,000 rent, service charges represent 16% of gross income. Subtract management (6%) and vacancy (7%) and your net yield drops from 10% gross to approximately 6.8% net.

A 650 sqft JVC studio at AED 16 per sqft pays about AED 10,400 annually, or roughly 16% of AED 65,000 rent before management and vacancy drag net yield from about 10% gross toward 6.8% net. ## Why do developer service charge estimates mislead buyers?

Developer service charge estimates mislead buyers because brochures often understate stabilized charges by 30% to 50% on new buildings. Invest Gulf baselines Mollak filed rates from a comparable completed tower and typically adds a 10% to 15% buffer. Foreign buyers who model the brochure number systematically overstate net yield.

The corrected approach checklist:

- Find the nearest comparable completed building of similar age, spec, and facilities in Mollak
- Use that charge rate as baseline
- Add 10% to 15% for settlement risk on new stock
- Re-run net yield before you commit

## How much do service charges cut net rental yield?

Service charges cut net rental yield by about 1.5 to 2.5 percentage points when high per-sqft buildings are compared with low-charge stock at similar gross rent. We see Downtown charges near twice JVC while rents scale only about 2 times. Foreign buyers chasing 8% gross can land near 5% net.

The pattern is structural: rents do not scale linearly with service quality the way service charges do. Model service charge as a share of rent, not only as AED per sqft.

## How do you verify service charges before signing in Dubai?

Verifying service charges before signing requires pulling the Mollak rate and confirming whether district cooling is included for the unit this year. We also request reserve fund statements plus special assessment history in writing within 7 days. Foreign buyers should treat schedule refusal as a walk-away signal.

Questions checklist:

- Is central district cooling included in service charges or billed separately?
- What is the reserve fund status for existing buildings?
- Is there any outstanding special assessment or capital works levy?
- Which company manages the Owners Association, and what are their standards?

## How do service charges rise as buildings age?

Service charges typically rise as buildings age because new towers start soft, then face lift and facade upgrades between years 6 and 15. We track JVC near 7.2% five-year CAGR and Palm near 11.2% over 2020 to 2025. Foreign buyers should budget 5% to 15% annual increase bands by age cohort.

| Building age | Typical service charge pattern | Investor implications |
|---|---|---|
| 0-2 years (new) | Below stabilized rate, developer subsidy common | Expect 15-25% increases as subsidy ends |
| 3-5 years | Settling to market rate | True operational costs emerging |
| 6-10 years | Market rate with moderate increases | Predictable 5-8% annual increases |
| 11-15 years | Above-market increases likely | Budget 10-15% annual increases |
| 15+ years | Significant renovation cycles | Special assessments common |

| Community | 5-year service charge CAGR | Primary cost drivers |
|---|---|---|
| JVC | 7.2% | Utility inflation, facility upgrades |
| Sports City | 5.8% | Stable management |
| Business Bay | 8.1% | Premium location maintenance |
| Dubai Marina | 6.9% | Marine environment premium |
| Downtown Dubai | 9.3% | Iconic infrastructure costs |
| Palm Jumeirah | 11.2% | Marine infrastructure, luxury standards |

Reserve fund health: buildings with reserves below 1.5 times annual service charges may face special assessments. Buildings over 12 years old should often hold reserves equal to 2 to 3 times annual service charges.

## How can investors optimize service charge exposure?

Investors optimize service charge exposure by preferring smaller units where charges take a lower share of rent, such as a JVC studio near 13.8% of gross. We typically model three JVC units near AED 14 to AED 16 per sqft against Marina at AED 26 to AED 28. Foreign buyers maximising net yield usually cap charges near AED 15.

| Investment thesis | Optimal community selection | Service charge ceiling | Expected net yield |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maximum net yield | JVC, Sports City, Discovery Gardens | AED 15/sqft maximum | 6.0-6.8% net |
| Yield + quality balance | Business Bay (selective), JLT, Al Barsha | AED 20/sqft maximum | 4.5-5.5% net |
| Golden Visa + moderate yield | Marina (selective), Business Bay premium | AED 25/sqft acceptable | 4.0-5.0% net |
| Capital appreciation focus | Downtown (selective), DIFC | Service charges secondary to location | 3.0-4.5% net |

Property managers change service charge levels because tier-one firms such as JLL often price 15% to 30% above averages while preserving occupancy. We typically verify who runs the OA before pricing yield. Management transition years can add volatility on budgets of 5% to 10% annually.

| Asteco | +10-20% vs community average | Mixed established areas |
| Better Homes FM | +5-15% vs community average | Marina, JLT, mid-premium |

Service charges affect Golden Visa economics because a AED 2 million Downtown unit at AED 30 per sqft on 1,000 sqft costs about AED 30,000 per year. We compare that with Business Bay near AED 22,000 yearly on a similar ticket over 10 years. Foreign buyers chasing visa qualification still need carry-cost discipline.

**Citable factual block.** Golden Visa motivated purchases still carry service charges as hard cash: an AED 2 million Downtown unit charged at AED 30 per sqft on a 1,000 sqft floorplate pays about AED 30,000 per year, which compounds to roughly AED 300,000 across a 10 year hold before management, vacancy, or mortgage interest. Redeploying the same AED 2 million into a Business Bay building near AED 20 per sqft on 1,100 sqft cuts annual service charges to about AED 22,000 and decade carry to about AED 220,000, a AED 80,000 difference that can sit beside better net yield. We underwrite visa eligibility and service charge drag in the same spreadsheet so location prestige does not hide occupancy cost. Foreign buyers should request Mollak rates and reserve fund statements before they treat any AED 2 million ticket as a pure immigration hedge.

## When can special assessments hit Dubai owners?

Special assessments typically hit Dubai owners when major repairs sit outside the annual budget and need OA approval, with Marina stock seeing events every 2 to 4 years. We budget 0.5% to 1.0% of property value annually as risk on buildings older than 8 years. Foreign buyers should request 5 years of history.

| Community | Special assessment frequency | Typical assessment size |
|---|---|---|
| JVC | Moderate (every 3-5 years) | AED 3,000-8,000 per unit |
| Sports City | Low (every 5-8 years) | AED 2,000-5,000 per unit |
| Business Bay | Moderate (every 4-6 years) | AED 4,000-10,000 per unit |
| Dubai Marina | High (every 2-4 years) | AED 5,000-15,000 per unit |
| Downtown Dubai | Low (every 6-10 years) | AED 8,000-20,000 per unit |

Owners billed above the approved rate have a documented route to challenge it, covered in [how to challenge a Dubai service charge](/guides/dubai-service-charge-dispute-escalation/).

Mitigation checklist: verify 3-year reserve statements, avoid thin reserves near major cycles, prefer professional management that plans works ahead, and confirm installment options on assessments over AED 10,000.

## Should you model district cooling separately from service charges?

District cooling should be modelled separately because in Marina and Downtown chilled water can rival service charges inside total occupancy cost for a full year. We typically rank communities only after DEWA plus cooling plus SC sit on one combined AED cost line. Foreign buyers in split-AC buildings trade higher SC for predictable DEWA bills.

| Check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| DEWA + district cooling combined | Can exceed all-in SC in Marina/Downtown towers |
| Chiller capacity vs unit size | Studios sometimes over-billed on shared plant |
| Cooling deposit on transfer | Passed to buyer at resale in some buildings |
| Split AC buildings | Higher SC but predictable DEWA only |

## What service charge checklist should run before you sign?

Service charge checklist before you sign requires confirming Mollak rate, cooling inclusion, reserve fund health, assessment history, and management tier in writing. Invest Gulf walks away when brokers cannot produce schedules on yield-focused deals. Foreign buyers should re-verify annually because 5% to 10% OA increases are common.
