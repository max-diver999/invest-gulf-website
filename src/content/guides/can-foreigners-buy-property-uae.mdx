Non-UAE nationals buy thousands of properties in Dubai and Abu Dhabi each year. The rules are ** clearer than many emerging markets**, but "freehold for foreigners" only applies in ** designated zones** — and off-plan purchases require escrow discipline.

## Freehold vs leasehold

**Freehold** grants full ownership of the unit (and defined land share in common areas) in approved zones. **Leasehold** grants long-term use rights — less common for international investors targeting Dubai apartments.

Always confirm on the **official title** or Oqood registration, not the sales gallery map.

## Step-by-step purchase flow (ready unit)

1. **Budget** — price + ~4% DLD + broker + moving/fit-out
2. **Mortgage pre-approval** (optional) — non-resident LTV caps apply
3. **Offer & Form F** — signed via licensed broker
4. **NOC from developer** (if applicable on resale)
5. **Transfer at DLD** — buyer/seller (or POA) present or via trustee
6. **Title deed issued** — register DEWA and property management

Off-plan: SPA with escrow-linked payment plan → Oqood → handover → title deed.

## Documents to review before paying

- Title deed or Oqood certificate (seller name matches)
- Service charge statement and sinking fund status
- Tenancy contract if tenanted (Ejari)
- Developer NOC requirements
- SPA schedules: penalties, variation clauses, handover date force majeure

## Abu Dhabi and other emirates

Abu Dhabi allows foreign ownership in designated investment zones (e.g. parts of Saadiyat, Yas, Al Reem). Ras Al Khaimah has expanded freehold offerings with different price points and buyer mix. Rules are **emirate-specific** — do not assume Dubai process copies 1:1.

## Common mistakes

- Paying off-plan deposits outside escrow
- Skipping independent snagging at handover
- Ignoring service charge arrears on resale
- Confusing **Golden Visa marketing** with guaranteed approval
