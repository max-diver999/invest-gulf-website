Saudi Arabia opened foreign property ownership on 22 January 2026. Royal Decree Law M/14 allows non-Saudi nationals to buy in designated zones under REGA supervision. This is not a mature market: implementing regulations, zone lists, and fee schedules are still being published in phases.

What Saudi offers is exposure to Vision 2030 capital deployment in NEOM, Red Sea Project, Qiddiya, Riyadh residential districts, and Jeddah's coast. What it does not yet offer is Dubai-scale liquidity, established rental indices, or a long foreign-buyer transaction history. Treat every brochure as a starting point and REGA portal confirmation as the gate before any deposit.

## What changed for foreign property buyers in Saudi Arabia in 2026?

Royal Decree Law M/14 took effect on 22 January 2026. It creates a legal basis for non-Saudi ownership inside REGA-approved designated zones. General residential stock in Riyadh, Jeddah, or Dammam outside those zones remains restricted for foreigners.

| Element | Status (2026) |
| --- | --- |
| Law M/14 | In force since 22 January 2026 |
| REGA implementing regulations | Being finalised (confirm current official rules) |
| Designated zone list | Published in phases (confirm current official rules) |
| Property types allowed | Residential and commercial per zone rules (confirm current official rules) |
| Ownership form | Freehold in designated zones (confirm current official rules) |
| Premium Residency | Separate programme, SAR 4M investment track commonly cited (confirm current official rules) |

Financing friction is the surprise most Gulf buyers report: Saudi mortgage rules for non-Saudis differ from UAE bank workflows. Budget 8 to 12 weeks for bank KYC and legal entity checks even on cash deals that need escrow registration.

## Which zones can foreigners buy property in under Law M/14?

Foreign ownership is limited to REGA-designated zones. You cannot buy an arbitrary apartment in central Riyadh as a non-Saudi; you buy inside a REGA-approved project within a published zone.

| Zone / project | Location | Profile | Status (confirm with REGA) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| NEOM | Tabuk Province | Giga-project | Designated |
| Red Sea Project | Red Sea coast | Tourism and residential | Designated |
| Qiddiya | Riyadh | Entertainment and residential | Designated |
| Riyadh select districts | Central Riyadh | Urban residential | Phased designation |
| Jeddah waterfront | Red Sea coast | Coastal residential | Phased designation |
| ROSHN communities | Multiple cities | PIF-backed residential | Select projects |
| Diriyah Gate | Riyadh | Heritage and luxury | Designated |

Request the zone certificate and developer REGA registration number in writing before a reservation fee. ## Does buying Saudi property automatically grant Premium Residency?

No. A SAR 3M apartment in a designated zone is property ownership; Premium Residency is a separate government programme commonly cited at SAR 4 million on an investment track (confirm current official rules). You may qualify for one, both, or neither depending on category rules at application time.

| Pathway | Typical capital anchor | Residency outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Law M/14 zone purchase | Project-specific (confirm REGA) | Property title in designated zone |
| Premium Residency investment track | ~SAR 4M cited publicly (confirm) | Long-stay residency if approved |
| Employer iqama | Salary and sponsorship | Work-linked residency |

Pair every property enquiry with a Premium Residency Centre enquiry if residency is part of your thesis. Parallel tracks have different timelines and document packs.

## What yields should foreigners underwrite in Saudi designated zones?

Saudi property is primarily a growth and presence play, not a yield optimisation market like mid-tier Dubai. Gross yields on Riyadh and Jeddah designated-zone apartments are commonly estimated at 4% to 6% (confirm current official rules). NEOM and other giga-projects often model at zero yield until handover and tenant demand exists.

## What are the purchase costs for foreign buyers in Saudi Arabia?

Budget Saudi-side fees only. Total acquisition costs commonly run 7% to 10% on residential purchases (confirm current official rules with REGA and your lawyer).

| Cost line | Planning range | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Transfer tax (residential) | ~5% commonly cited (confirm) | Verify rate at signing |
| Broker commission | 2% to 2.5% | Negotiate before viewing |
| REGA registration | Project-specific (confirm) | Part of closing pack |
| Legal review | SAR 10,000+ | Independent counsel |
| Developer off-plan charges | SPA schedule | Milestone-linked |

On a SAR 2,000,000 ready unit, all-in cash need often lands near SAR 2,140,000 to SAR 2,200,000 before furnishing or Premium Residency programme fees.

## How do foreigners complete a Saudi property purchase step by step?

Typical sequence: zone verification, reservation, SPA review, escrow or developer account wiring per lawyer instructions, REGA registration, and title handover. Remote purchase may use power of attorney (confirm current official rules). Independent Saudi legal review is non-negotiable in early 2026.

1. Confirm REGA designated zone status for the exact unit, not just the masterplan marketing map.
2. Verify developer registration and escrow account details on official channels.
3. Review SPA with Saudi property counsel; developer templates favour sellers.
4. Wire only to named escrow accounts listed in the SPA after legal sign-off.
5. Complete REGA registration and retain dated screenshots of portal confirmations.

Timeline often stretches 8 to 12 weeks when financing or cross-border compliance is involved.

## Should foreigners buy off-plan or ready stock in Saudi zones?

Off-plan in giga-projects is a multi-year commitment with construction and timeline risk. ROSHN and other PIF-backed communities may offer nearer-term delivery, but each project needs its own REGA check. Ready units suit buyers who want to test rental demand before scaling capital.

| Format | Best for | Main risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Off-plan giga-project | Long Vision 2030 thesis | Delivery delay |
| ROSHN ready/near-ready | First Saudi purchase | Zone list changes |
| Secondary resale | Rare early 2026 | Thin comps |

## How does Saudi property compare to UAE for investors?

UAE offers mature freehold depth, Golden Visa at AED 2M, and documented resale liquidity. Saudi offers earlier-stage designated zones and Vision 2030 optionality at higher regulatory uncertainty. Most portfolio buyers treat them as complementary sleeves, not substitutes.

| Factor | Saudi designated zones | UAE (Dubai focus) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regulatory clarity | Evolving weekly in 2026 | Mature DLD/RERA stack |
| Residency link | Premium Residency separate track | Golden Visa property routes |
| Gross yield planning | 4% to 6% cited | 7% to 9% mid-market often cited |
| Secondary liquidity | Limited | High transaction volume |

## What red flags should pause a Saudi property purchase?

Walk away when zone status stays verbal, escrow details differ from the SPA, or yield slides use developer marketing without three live rentals in the same project.

1. Broker cannot produce REGA zone confirmation for the exact unit.
2. Reservation fee requested before independent legal review.
3. Yield deck with no rental comps in the same building or community.
4. Premium Residency promised in the same email as a zone off-plan deposit.
5. Implementing regulation updates ignored since Law M/14 signing week.

Law M/14 zone lists and fee schedules will evolve through 2026 and 2027. Subscribe to REGA portal updates and keep a Saudi property lawyer on retainer through your first closing.

Practical entry strategy for 2026: start with a REGA-verified ROSHN or Diriyah Gate unit where handover dates are published, complete one cash closing, then decide whether giga-project off-plan fits your hold period. Pair every property enquiry with a Premium Residency Centre enquiry when residency is part of your thesis, because capital thresholds and processing timelines differ.
