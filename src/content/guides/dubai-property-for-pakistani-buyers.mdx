Every other buyer's guide starts with the property. Which area, what budget, off-plan or ready, what the yield looks like. For a Pakistani family that sequence begins one step too late.

The first question is not what to buy. It is who is able to buy it, because the answer determines whose name reaches the deed, and the name on the deed is what ownership actually means.

## Why does the buyer's identity come first?

Because it is a constraint rather than a preference, and constraints have to be resolved before anything downstream can be planned. Pakistan's rules on residents moving capital abroad shape who is practically able to fund an overseas purchase.

The consequence, in a very large number of real cases, is that the family member who transacts is not the family member whose money it is. A son working abroad, a brother resident in the Gulf, a daughter with a foreign passport: somebody with a non-resident position becomes the buyer, and the purchase goes ahead in their name. That is an entirely ordinary arrangement and it is where the complications begin, because the register records one name and the family understands a different reality.

## What actually follows the name on the deed?

More than most families expect, and all of it. This is the part worth reading slowly, because each item is a genuine legal consequence rather than an administrative label.

| What attaches to the registered owner | What the funder holds without documentation |
|---|---|
| Legal title and the right to sell | An understanding |
| The right to mortgage or encumber | An understanding |
| Eligibility for a property-based residence | Nothing |
| Entitlement to the rental income | An understanding |
| The asset's position in an estate | Whatever that estate's law provides |
| Standing in any dispute about it | Very little |

The right-hand column is not an exaggeration and it is not a comment on any family's honesty. It is a description of what a register and a court can see. A person who funded a purchase and holds no document is relying on the continued agreement of the person who did not fund it, and on nothing else.

## When does an informal arrangement fail?

At exactly the moments families are least able to deal with it. It works while everybody agrees, everybody is alive, and nobody's circumstances have changed, which is the condition under which no arrangement is ever tested.

The events that test it are ordinary: a death, a divorce, a business failure that brings creditors, a serious illness, a change in someone's residence or tax position, or a straightforward disagreement between people who had always agreed before. Each of those introduces a third party, and third parties read the register. An heir, a spouse's lawyer, a creditor or a court sees one registered owner and no evidence of anybody else's interest. The family's account of what was intended is not evidence of anything unless somebody wrote it down at the time.

## What should be written down, and by whom?

The relationship between the two people, prepared by someone competent in both jurisdictions, before the purchase completes. It is a short document and it is the entire remedy.

It should record what each party contributed and when, what each is entitled to, who has the power to decide on a sale and on what terms, how rental income is to be treated and paid, and what happens on the death or incapacity of either person. None of that is complicated to draft and all of it is difficult to reconstruct later. The reason it goes undone is social rather than legal: proposing a document feels like an accusation inside a family. The reframing that helps is that the document protects the registered owner as much as the funder, since it is the registered owner who otherwise inherits an unfunded obligation to somebody's heirs. Where the estate side sits, and how a common-law will interacts with UAE property, is on the [DIFC and ADGM wills page](/guides/uae-will-difc-adgm/).

## Does a will cover it?

Partly, and only along one axis. A will governs what happens when somebody dies, which is one of the several ways this arrangement gets tested.

It says nothing about a divorce, a creditor's claim, a dispute during everybody's lifetime, or the question of who is entitled to this year's rent. So a will is a necessary component and an incomplete answer, and families who have made one often believe the matter is closed. The complete position needs the will for succession and the underlying agreement for everything else, and the two should be prepared together so they say the same thing. A will that contradicts an informal understanding is worse than either alone, because it creates a written record pointing one way and a family expectation pointing the other.

## What happens to the residence route?

It follows the name, which surprises families who funded a purchase partly for that reason. A property-based residence application rests on registered ownership.

That means funding a qualifying purchase in a relative's name has bought eligibility for the relative. If the residence was part of the motivation, and for many families it is the main one, this needs establishing before the purchase rather than at the application. It may change who should be the buyer, whether the arrangement works at all, or whether a different route makes more sense. Discovering it afterwards means either accepting the outcome or unwinding a completed registration, which is expensive and slow. The programme's requirements are on the [Golden Visa property page](/guides/uae-golden-visa-property/).

## Does a mortgage change who has to be involved?

It narrows the field further, because a lender assesses the borrower rather than the family. The registered owner is the applicant, and the application stands or falls on their income, their residence status and their credit position.

That has two practical effects worth knowing before a property is chosen. The relative in whose name the purchase sits has to be someone a bank will actually lend to, which is a different test from being someone who can lawfully transact, and the two are frequently confused. And the borrowing creates an obligation in that person's name that appears in their own financial life, affecting what else they can borrow and appearing in any assessment of their position. A family choosing a nominal buyer on the basis of convenience can hand that person a liability they did not fully weigh. Non-resident lending criteria are set out on the [non-resident mortgage page](/guides/non-resident-mortgage-dubai/).

## What does a well-structured version look like?

Four documents and one conversation, all completed before an offer is made. The Pakistani position confirmed by a Pakistani adviser. The agreement between funder and registered owner, drafted for both jurisdictions. A will covering the property. And clarity about whose residence eligibility, if any, the purchase creates.

None of that is expensive relative to the transaction and all of it is difficult to add afterwards. A family that does it holds an ordinary investment with an ordinary set of risks. A family that skips it holds an asset whose ownership is a matter of interpretation, and interpretation is decided by whoever is left when the people who understood the arrangement are no longer able to explain it.

## What has to be confirmed rather than read here?

The current Pakistani position, for your own circumstances, from a Pakistani adviser. This page explains why that question determines the shape of the purchase and it cannot tell you what today's answer is.

Rules on outward capital movement are policy instruments, they change, and the consequences of getting them wrong land at home rather than in Dubai. Anyone in this position needs somebody looking at the current position against their own residence status, source of funds and family circumstances. What this page can do, and what almost nothing else written for this audience does, is establish that the ownership question is the first question rather than a detail to sort out at the trustee office. A family that starts there makes a series of ordinary decisions. A family that starts with the property makes one irreversible decision and then discovers what it meant. The neighbouring case, where the constraint is a calendar rather than an identity, is on the [Indian buyers page](/guides/dubai-property-for-indian-buyers/), and the market itself on the [Dubai investment guide](/guides/dubai-property-investment-guide/).
