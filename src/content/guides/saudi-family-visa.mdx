Almost every account of Saudi family sponsorship leads with a salary figure, which sets up the wrong expectation. Income is checked, and for a professional whose employer is doing the sponsoring it is established before the question is asked. Files that fail rarely fail there.

They fail on two documentary chains that nobody warns about because neither is dramatic: the lease that proves where the family will live, and the certificate legalisation that proves who they are. Both are slow, both run through offices you do not control, and both are usually started too late.

## Why does the salary threshold get all the attention?

Because it is the only number in the process, and numbers are what get written down. The rest of the requirement is a set of documents that agree with each other, which does not summarise into a headline.

The practical result is a family that arrives at the application with the income question answered and the document question untouched. They have an offer letter, a contract and a payslip, and none of those is what the reviewer is looking for. This is a reporting artefact rather than a policy: the salary figure is checkable in advance and quotable, so it dominates coverage, while the housing and legalisation requirements vary by case and by issuing country and get compressed into a line about "supporting documents". The sponsor's own residency, which precedes all of this, is covered on the [iqama process page](/guides/saudi-iqama-process/).

## What does the housing document have to establish?

That a specific, identifiable residential property is leased by the sponsor and is adequate for the declared family. Three things have to line up.

| Element | What it must show | How it fails |
|---|---|---|
| Registered lease | Sponsor named as tenant, in the Ejar system | Unregistered, or in the employer's name |
| Employer housing letter | Tenant details matching the registered contract | Letter and contract naming different parties |
| The property itself | Rooms consistent with declared dependants | A studio filed for a family of five |
| The iqama | Sponsor identity matching both documents | A name or number transcribed differently |

The failure that recurs is not fraud or carelessness in any ordinary sense. It is an employer holding the lease as a matter of course, because that is how corporate housing works, and the sponsor not realising that the arrangement which makes their own life simple makes their dependants' file harder. Fixing it after the fact means either re-registering the contract or obtaining a letter that matches it precisely, and both take longer than they would have taken in advance.

## How does the legalisation chain actually run?

Through the country that issued each document, in a fixed order, at a speed set by offices with no interest in your travel date. Attestation happens where the certificate was issued; legalisation for Saudi use follows it.

The compounding factor is that the chain is per document and per person rather than per family. A couple with three children is running a marriage certificate and three birth certificates, and if those were issued in different countries, which is common in an expatriate household, the chains are separate and parallel and progress at different speeds. Eight to twelve weeks is the working figure, and it assumes nothing needs re-issuing. Where an original is lost, or a name appears differently on two documents, the clock restarts on that item while everything else waits.

Start the chain when the sponsor's employment is confirmed rather than when the visa application opens. Nothing in the process penalises early attestation, and everything about it penalises a late start.

## Why does the sponsor's own iqama come first?

Because sponsorship is a capacity that residency confers, and it does not exist before the iqama does. A sponsor with a signed contract and an entry visa cannot sponsor anyone.

That ordering sets the real family timeline and is the single most useful thing to explain to a spouse before a move. The sequence is: sponsor travels, sponsor completes medical and biometrics, iqama issues, housing is secured and registered, dependant applications are filed, dependants travel. Each of those steps has a duration, and the sum is why families are apart for a period at the start of a Saudi posting. A household that has planned around that separation manages it. One that expected to travel together and discovers the ordering on arrival spends the same weeks apart with none of the preparation. The wider first-90-days sequence sits on the [relocation guide](/guides/saudi-arabia-relocation-guide/).

## What does the school year do to the timing?

It removes the flexibility the rest of the process appears to offer. A file that completes in March and a file that completes in September produce very different years for a child, and only one of those is a normal entry point.

Saudi schools admit on a calendar, and international schools in Riyadh and Jeddah hold places against a deposit rather than indefinitely. A family whose legalisation chain runs three weeks longer than planned does not simply arrive three weeks later; they can arrive after an intake has closed, with a deposit paid and a place gone. That converts a documentary delay into a year of a child's schooling, which is the point at which an eight-week chain that could have started earlier becomes expensive in a way no fee schedule captures.

The planning move is to work backwards from the intake date rather than forwards from the job offer. Fix the term you need the children in place for, subtract the dependant application, subtract the housing registration, subtract the sponsor's own iqama, and the date the certificates should have entered attestation is usually already behind you.

## What sits outside the routine dependant category?

Adult sons, parents, and any arrangement that is not a spouse with minor children. These are handled case by case rather than by the standard path, and treating them as an extension of the main file is where planning goes wrong.

The practical rule is to establish the position for each non-standard dependant before making commitments in the home country: before a property is sold, before a school place is given up, before an elderly parent's own housing is unwound. A refusal that arrives after those decisions is a different kind of problem from one that arrives before. This is the part of a Saudi move where the cost of asking early is a few emails and the cost of assuming is measured in somebody else's living arrangements. Healthcare cover for dependants once they arrive, which is a separate requirement again, is covered on the [healthcare page](/guides/saudi-healthcare-expats/).

## What does a prepared file look like?

Assembled in a specific order, because the slow items have to start before the fast ones are possible.

1. Certificates into the attestation and legalisation chain, per person, as soon as employment is confirmed.
2. Sponsor's iqama completed.
3. Lease registered in the sponsor's name, or the employer housing letter obtained and checked against the registered contract line by line.
4. Property confirmed as consistent with the declared family size.
5. Dependant applications filed with all three document sets agreeing on names, numbers and addresses.

The check worth running before filing is mechanical and takes twenty minutes: put the iqama, the lease and the certificates side by side and confirm that every name is spelled identically and every number matches. Transliteration differences between an Arabic contract and a European birth certificate are the quietest cause of a returned file, and they are entirely fixable at a desk before submission. A surname rendered one way on a passport and another on a legalised certificate is not a discrepancy anyone is trying to catch you on; it is simply two correct documents that a reviewer cannot reconcile, and reconciling them after submission means re-entering a queue you have already waited in once. Housing cost context for the lease itself sits on the [Riyadh cost of living page](/guides/riyadh-cost-of-living/), and the ownership alternative on the [foreigner living guide](/guides/saudi-property-foreigner-living/).
