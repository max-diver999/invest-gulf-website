## What is a Dubai Police Clearance Certificate?

It is the official record, formally called the Good Conduct Certificate, confirming whether you have criminal cases registered against you in Dubai. It is issued digitally by Dubai Police, costs AED 200, and is the document employers, immigration authorities and professional regulators ask for when they want proof of character rather than proof of identity.

Two limits define what it is not. It does not report traffic fines, which sit with the RTA and the Traffic Police as a separate matter. And it covers Dubai emirate only, so a record from another emirate needs that emirate's own certificate. Current residents, former residents and applicants abroad can all apply, which is why this document turns up so often in relocation files years after someone has left the UAE.

## When do you actually need one?

Most requests fall into four buckets, and knowing which one you are in tells you whether you also need attestation.

Residency and visa transitions come first. Certain UAE Golden Visa categories, sponsor-to-self-sponsor residency changes, and professional licensing applications all ask for a good conduct certificate. If your residency route runs through property rather than employment, the [Golden Visa via property guide](/guides/golden-visa-mortgage-property-uae/) sets out where this document sits in the wider file, and the [Gulf residency by investment guide](/guides/gulf-residency-by-investment-guide/) compares the equivalent requirements across the region.

Employment background checks are the second bucket. Multinational employers and regulated industries ask routinely: financial services under DFSA or SCA supervision, healthcare professionals under DHA and HAAD licensing, legal professionals including DIFC Courts admissions, the education sector, and government or semi-government employment.

Third, property and licensing. Some property management companies, RERA-registered brokerages and business licence applications include character verification in their onboarding. Fourth, and often the most time-critical, overseas immigration: another country's visa, residency or citizenship process asking for police records from every country you have lived in for six months or more.

## Who can apply, and from where?

Current Dubai residents with an Emirates ID have the simplest path, applying through UAE PASS with their existing digital identity. Former residents who have left the UAE apply remotely using passport details and their former residency information, which is the scenario the online portal is genuinely built to handle rather than an exception it tolerates.

The category that needs extra thought is the applicant with multiple or interrupted residency periods, which is common among Gulf expats who left and returned. Both periods are typically captured in a single certificate, provided you declare all of them on the application. Leaving out a period because it was short or a long time ago is the fastest way to receive a certificate the requesting authority then rejects as incomplete.

| Applicant type | Route that usually works | Practical note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Current Dubai resident | Online via UAE PASS | Fastest path, 3 to 5 business days |
| Former resident inside the UAE | Online, walk-in or typing centre | Bring former residency details |
| Former resident abroad | Online portal, or a UAE PRO service | Processing may take longer while records are searched |
| Applicant with name variations | Walk-in or PRO, with a covering letter | Clarify before applying, not after |
| Lived in Abu Dhabi or Sharjah too | Separate certificate per emirate | Dubai Police covers Dubai only |

## How do you apply online, step by step?

The online route is the default and works well. Six steps, in order:

1. Go to dubaipolice.gov.ae or open the Dubai Police app, then find Services, Individuals, Good Conduct Certificate.
2. Log in. Current residents use a UAE PASS account. Former residents can use passport details, and the portal guides the verification.
3. Complete the form: full name exactly as on the passport, passport number, Emirates ID number for current residents, date of birth, nationality, UAE residency period, reason for the application, and the country the certificate is for.
4. Upload documents. A digital passport-size photograph on a white background meeting the Dubai Police specification, typically JPG and under 2MB, plus the passport bio data page and either your Emirates ID or your previous residency details.
5. Pay AED 200 by card or UAE digital payment. Fees are non-refundable regardless of the outcome.
6. Receive the certificate as a PDF in your Dubai Police account, usually within 3 to 5 business days. For official use you may need the printed official version or a physical copy.

| Document | Current resident | Former resident abroad |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Passport bio data page | Required | Required |
| Emirates ID copy | Required | Provide former Emirates ID number if available |
| Digital photograph | Required, white background, under 2MB | Required, same specification |
| Residency dates | Prefilled from records in most cases | Provide approximate dates yourself |
| Reason and destination country | Required | Required, and drives the attestation question |

## What are the walk-in and PRO alternatives?

Walk-in exists for urgency and for complicated files. Dubai Police General HQ in Al Twar, Deira, handles applications Sunday to Thursday, 07:30 to 14:00, and processes in 1 to 3 business days, occasionally same day where the urgency is justified. Bring the original passport, Emirates ID, photographs and payment. Dubai Police also runs Customer Happiness Centres across the emirate, and their locations change periodically, so check the official site before travelling.

Authorised typing centres, Tasheel and Amer, will complete and submit the form for you for a typing fee of roughly AED 30 to 60 on top of the AED 200 government fee. That is the sensible option if you are unfamiliar with the portal or need help with translation.

A PRO service is the right answer for the harder cases: applying from outside the UAE, an Arabic name with multiple transliterations, an expired Emirates ID, or a file where the certificate needs attestation immediately after issue. Expect AED 400 to 800 all inclusive, with a 5 to 10 business day turnaround. It costs more than doing it yourself, and for a relocation file with a hard deadline it usually buys back more time than it costs.

## What does the full process cost and how long does it take?

The certificate itself is a fixed and modest cost. Attestation is what turns a two-day errand into a two-week project, and it is the part applicants most often forget to plan for.

| Stage | Indicative cost | Indicative time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dubai Police certificate, online | AED 200 | 3 to 5 business days |
| Walk-in urgent processing | AED 200 | 1 to 3 business days |
| Typing centre assistance | AED 30 to 60 plus the AED 200 fee | Same as the chosen channel |
| PRO service, all inclusive | AED 400 to 800 | 5 to 10 business days |
| MOFA attestation | AED 150 to 200 per document | 1 to 5 business days |
| Apostille after MOFA | AED 150 to 300 per document | 3 to 7 business days |
| Embassy legalisation, non-Hague | Varies by embassy | 5 to 20 business days |

Read the table as a sequence rather than a menu. A straightforward certificate for use inside the UAE is done in under a week. A certificate that needs an apostille for an overseas immigration file realistically takes around 10 to 15 business days end to end, and embassy legalisation for a non-Hague destination can double that. Apply well before the deadline, because none of these steps can be compressed by paying more.

## When do you need MOFA attestation or an apostille?

Only when the certificate is going overseas. A certificate used inside the UAE, for an employer or a residency step, is normally accepted as issued. The moment a foreign authority is the reader, one of three levels applies.

Level one is UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs attestation. This is required by countries that accept MOFA attestation, including most Arab states, India and Pakistan. Submit the original certificate at a MOFA attestation desk in Dubai or Abu Dhabi, or use Tasheel and Amer centres. Cost is roughly AED 150 to 200 per document and processing runs 1 to 5 business days. MOFA also offers online attestation for eligible documents via mofa.gov.ae.

Level two is apostille. The UAE joined the Hague Apostille Convention in 2021, so for the UK, EU members, Australia, Canada, the USA and the other Hague states, an apostille replaces the full embassy legalisation chain. The sequence is fixed: obtain the certificate, complete MOFA attestation, then apply for the apostille through MOFA. Budget AED 150 to 300 and 3 to 7 business days. Once apostilled, the document is accepted across all Hague Convention member countries without further embassy verification, and there are now more than 120 of them.

Level three is embassy legalisation, which applies to countries outside the Convention. MOFA attestation comes first, then the destination country's embassy in Abu Dhabi or Dubai attests the document, and some countries require a further step at home. Fees and timelines vary widely by embassy, and some accept postal submission while others insist on attendance. The general attestation logic, including which documents need which chain, is covered in the [UAE attestation guide](/guides/uae-attestation-documents/).

## How long is the certificate valid?

Most authorities accept a Dubai Police Clearance Certificate issued within the previous six months. Some accept certificates up to twelve months old, and some insist on three months, so the validity window is set by the recipient rather than by Dubai Police. Confirm the requirement before you apply, because a certificate that expires mid-application means paying the AED 200 again and losing another week.

The practical scheduling rule for immigration files: order the certificate after your other documents are substantially ready, not first. It is one of the fastest items in a relocation pack and one of the shortest-lived, so front-loading it wastes the validity window.

## What goes wrong, and how do you fix it?

Four problems account for most delayed applications.

Name mismatches come first. Your name on the certificate must match your passport exactly. Arabic transliteration variants and dropped middle names are the usual culprits, and the fix is to clarify with Dubai Police before applying, bringing all relevant documents plus a short covering letter explaining the discrepancy.

A previous UAE record is the second. If you have a UAE criminal record, including older bounced cheque matters under previous UAE law or a labour dispute that escalated to criminal court, the certificate will reflect it. Speak to a UAE-licensed lawyer before applying, because you need to know whether the record is still active and whether clearance is achievable before you generate a document that goes into someone else's file.

Third, a lost or expired Emirates ID. Former residents who cannot supply their old Emirates ID number can apply on passport details alone, giving full name, date of birth, nationality and approximate residency dates. Processing takes longer because records are searched manually, so add a week to your plan.

Fourth, incomplete residency history. Declare every period you lived in the UAE, including short ones. Records get matched against what you declare, and an omission looks worse than the gap it was meant to hide.

## How does Dubai's certificate differ from other emirates?

Each emirate police force issues its own. Dubai Police covers Dubai. If you also lived in Abu Dhabi, apply through adpolice.gov.ae, where the process mirrors Dubai's with an online application, an AED 200 fee and 3 to 5 business day processing, and Abu Dhabi documents also fall under the Hague apostille framework. Sharjah certificates come from sharjahpolice.gov.ae.

Which certificates you actually need is decided by the requesting authority, not by you. Immigration advisers and destination-country authorities will specify the emirates they want covered based on your residence history, so ask that question first and order once. ## Pre-application checklist

Run this before you pay, because the AED 200 is non-refundable:

- Confirm which emirates the requesting authority needs certificates from.
- Confirm the validity window the recipient applies: three, six or twelve months.
- Confirm whether the destination is a Hague Convention country, which decides apostille versus embassy legalisation.
- Check your name spelling matches across passport, Emirates ID and any prior UAE documents.
- List every UAE residency period, including short and interrupted ones.
- Prepare the photograph to specification, white background and the correct file size.
- If attestation is needed, plan for the printed original rather than the PDF alone.
- Add the timeline honestly: roughly a week for the certificate, roughly two weeks in total if an apostille is required.

The Dubai Police Clearance Certificate is one of the better-run pieces of UAE government admin, with a genuinely functional online system and a predictable fee. The failure mode is almost never the certificate itself. It is starting the attestation chain a week too late, or ordering certificates from the wrong emirates.
