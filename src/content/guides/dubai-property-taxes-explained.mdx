Dubai property taxes for individuals are typically 0% on rental income, 0% on capital gains, and there is no annual property tax bill comparable to council tax or taxe foncière. Foreign buyers still face mandatory fees that change underwriting: the 4% DLD transfer fee at purchase, trustee charges near AED 4,000, and annual service charges commonly between AED 12 and 40 per sq ft depending on the area and building grade. VAT at 5% applies to many property services such as agent commission and legal fees, while residential property resales themselves remain VAT-exempt. On an AED 1.5 million ready apartment, the DLD line alone is AED 60,000 before broker, trustee, and first-year service charges. Home-country tax residency can still tax Dubai rent or gains even when UAE tax is AED 0, so the local zero-tax claim is never the full after-tax story for foreign buyers.

A practical first-year cost stack on an AED 1.5 million Dubai apartment typically includes AED 60,000 DLD transfer fee, about AED 4,200 trustee fee with VAT, roughly AED 31,500 agent fee with VAT at 2% plus 5%, and service charges near AED 14,400 on an 800 sq ft unit at AED 18 per sq ft. Ejari and minor admin often add AED 500 to 1,000, while municipality fee mechanics on long-term lets and Tourism Dirham rules on holiday homes sit outside classic income tax. Corporate tax at 9% above AED 375,000 profits matters mainly when property sits in a UAE company, not when a natural person holds a personal freehold title. Foreign buyers should keep title deeds, DLD receipts, Ejari contracts, Mollak invoices, and commission invoices as one evidence pack for home-country advisers and later resale. Model the fee stack before you celebrate the zero income-tax headline.

## Does Dubai charge income tax, capital gains tax, or annual property tax?

Dubai typically charges individuals 0% income tax on salaries and rental income, 0% capital gains tax on property sales, and no annual property tax bill on this market. Foreign buyers still need a fee checklist for DLD, service charges, and home-country reporting even when UAE tax lines stay at AED 0.

Tax checklist for personal ownership:

- Personal income tax: 0%
- Capital gains tax: 0%
- Annual property tax: none
- Wealth tax and inheritance tax: none at UAE level

Personal income tax: 0%. The UAE imposes no income tax on individuals. Salaries, rental income, dividends, and business profits received by natural persons are not taxed by the UAE as of 2026.

Capital gains tax: 0%. Buy a unit at AED 800,000 and sell it at AED 1.2M, and the AED 400,000 gain is not taxed by the UAE.

Annual property tax: none. There is no council-tax style ownership levy. Service charges are OA costs, not a government property tax.

## What one-time and recurring fees do Dubai buyers actually pay?

Mandatory costs typically sit at purchase through the 4% DLD transfer fee, trustee fee, and agent VAT, then every year through service charges, Ejari, and DEWA-linked municipality mechanics. Foreign buyers modelling an AED 1 million deal should budget AED 40,000 for DLD alone before the annual service-charge checklist on this market.

### 1. DLD transfer fee (4% of price)

| Purchase price | DLD transfer fee (4%) |
|---|---|
| AED 600,000 | AED 24,000 |
| AED 1,000,000 | AED 40,000 |
| AED 2,000,000 | AED 80,000 |
| AED 5,000,000 | AED 200,000 |

Who pays: Market convention is that the buyer pays the full 4%, though formal allocation is 2% each side. When paid: at the trustee centre on transfer day, or as Oqood registration on off-plan SPA signing.

### 2. Trustee Registration Fee: Acquisition

| Property value | Trustee fee |
|---|---|
| AED 500,000 and above | AED 4,000 + VAT |
| Below AED 500,000 | AED 2,000 + VAT |

### 3. Service Charges (Annual): Ownership

| Community type | Annual service charge range |
|---|---|
| Discovery Gardens, Dubai Sports City | AED 11 to 16 per sq ft |
| JVC (established towers) | AED 14 to 18 per sq ft |
| Business Bay | AED 18 to 24 per sq ft |
| Dubai Marina | AED 20 to 28 per sq ft |
| Downtown Dubai | AED 22 to 32 per sq ft |
| Palm Jumeirah (apartments) | AED 25 to 40 per sq ft |

Worked buyer scenario: an 800 sq ft Business Bay apartment at AED 20/sq ft equals AED 16,000/year in service charges.

### 4. VAT (5%): On Services, Not Property

- Agent commissions (2% + 5% VAT = 2.1% effective)
- Solicitor and professional fees
- Property management fees
- DEWA bills
- Commercial property sale and lease VAT treatment

Residential property sales are exempt from VAT. The 4% DLD fee is separate.

### 5. Municipality Fee: If You Rent Out the Property

Long-term landlords see a municipality fee of 5% of annual rent collected via DEWA on the tenant side.

### 6. Short-Term Rental Levies: If You Use Holiday Home

- Tourism Dirham: about AED 15 per room per night
- Municipality fee: 7% of accommodation value per booking
- Holiday Home Permit: AED 1,520 per year for apartments and studios, AED 3,570 for villas and townhouses

## Will your home country tax Dubai rental income or sale profits?

Home-country tax residency typically requires reporting of Dubai rental income or sale profits even when UAE tax is 0%, so foreign buyers should plan filings before the deposit wire. UK residents after the 2025 non-dom changes, US citizens on worldwide income, and many EU residents still need a cross-border tax checklist on this market.

Home-country checklist:

- UK tax residents: declare worldwide income including UAE rent
- US citizens: report rent and gains to the IRS; FBAR/FATCA may apply
- EU residents: treaty treatment varies by state
- UAE tax residency certificate: usually needs about 183 days or primary economic centre evidence; Golden Visa alone is not enough

## Does UAE corporate tax apply to property in your personal name?

UAE corporate tax typically does not apply to property held in a personal name because the 9% rate targets business profits above AED 375,000 in qualifying entities. Foreign buyers using UAE companies for larger portfolios should run a structure checklist before assuming the personal 0% result still holds on this market.

| Investment Structure | Corporate Tax Applies? | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Individual ownership | No | Personal property holding is not business income |
| UAE Free Zone company | No (if qualifying) | 0% rate for Free Zone qualifying income |
| UAE mainland company | Yes | 9% on profits above AED 375,000 |
| Foreign company structure | Depends | May create UAE taxable presence |

Structure checklist: personal title for simple rentals; corporate advice for AED 10M+ portfolios, commercial assets, or multi-jurisdiction planning.

## Do UAE double tax treaties help property investors?

UAE double tax treaties can typically reduce withholding on dividends and certain cross-border income, but they do not replace home-country reporting of rental profits for foreign buyers who remain tax resident abroad. Treaty shopping without substance is a red flag; about 183 days of UAE presence or primary economic-centre evidence still sits on the tax-residency checklist for this market.

Treaty checklist:

- Confirm whether your home country has a UAE treaty
- Separate Golden Visa status from tax residency
- Keep Ejari, bank, and 4% DLD transfer evidence for advisers
- Do not treat a Dubai flat alone as automatic treaty relief after AED 0 UAE tax

## Which Dubai ownership costs rise faster than investors expect?

Service charges and major works in aging towers typically rise faster than investors expect, often 5 to 15% a year in some buildings, while the 4% DLD fee is a one-time line. Foreign buyers should treat Mollak history as part of the acquisition checklist on this market rather than relying on launch estimates.

Escalation checklist:

- Current Mollak charge per sq ft
- Reserve-fund and major-works notices
- OA mismanagement or arrears history
- STR levies if holiday-home use is planned

## Is Dubai's zero income tax policy likely to change soon?

There is no announced personal income tax on property rentals, and corporate tax plus VAT already cover business and service lines. Foreign buyers should still model today's 4% DLD fee and service charges rather than speculative federal taxes, because property investment already drives a large share of land-related government revenue on this market.

Policy checklist:

- Personal rental income tax: not announced
- Corporate tax and VAT: already in force for relevant cases
- Underwriting basis: current fees, not rumour
- Home-country rules: often the larger variable

## How does Dubai compare with taxed markets for property investors?

Zero UAE personal tax on rent and gains typically keeps more cash in pocket than UK or US landlord stacks, but acquisition fees still run about 4 to 7% upfront for foreign buyers. Compare markets with a fee-and-tax checklist, not a slogan, because service charges replace annual property tax on this market.

| Market | Annual property tax on mid flat | CGT on resale | Income tax on rent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dubai | None (service charges instead) | 0% | 0% UAE |
| UK (illustrative) | Council tax band | Up to 24% on gains for higher-rate landlords | Up to 45% |
| US citizen worldwide | Varies by state | Federal + state | Federal + state |

Planning checklist: model DLD and Mollak first, then home-country reporting. See the [UAE tax guide for expats](/guides/uae-tax-guide-expats/).

## What records should Dubai property investors keep for tax and resale?

Dubai property investors should typically keep every document that proves cost basis, the 4% DLD fee paid, and rental history for the full hold period. Foreign buyers who skip the record checklist create avoidable friction for home-country returns, refinancing, and resale on this market after year one.

Record checklist:

- Title deed and DLD transfer receipt showing 4% fee paid
- Oqood or SPA for off-plan purchases
- Ejari contracts and rent receipts for each tenancy
- Service charge invoices and Mollak statements
- Agent commission invoices with VAT shown separately
- Mortgage statements if financed

## When should you hire a UAE lawyer or cross-border tax adviser?

Foreign buyers should typically hire a UAE property lawyer on every purchase, and add a home-country tax adviser when they remain tax resident abroad while collecting Dubai rent. Fee ranges of AED 5,000 to 15,000 for UAE counsel and AED 3,000 to 10,000 for annual home-country advice are common planning inputs on this market.

| Professional Service | When Required | Typical Cost Range |
|---|---|---|
| UAE property lawyer | All transactions | AED 5,000-15,000 |
| Home country tax advisor | Annually | AED 3,000-10,000 |
| International tax planning | High net worth | AED 15,000-50,000 |
| Corporate structure advice | Portfolio investors | AED 10,000-25,000 |
| UAE tax residency planning | Residency seekers | AED 5,000-15,000 |

Adviser checklist: SPA review is not optional; cross-border advice is optional only if you already know your residency and filing duties.

Foreign buyers should treat the table below as a buyer scenario checklist for ready resale stock on this market.

| Cost line | Typical amount (AED) |

| DLD transfer fee (4%) | 60,000 |
| Trustee fee + VAT | ~4,200 |
| Agent fee 2% + VAT | ~31,500 |
| Service charges (800 sq ft @ AED 18/sq ft) | 14,400 |
| Ejari + minor admin | 500 to 1,000 |

Figures are illustrative for ready resale purchases in 2026. Off-plan may defer DLD until transfer but still carries Oqood registration.
