faq:
  - question: "Can I open a bank account in Qatar on a visit visa?"
    answer: "Generally no for full salary products. Most retail banks require a valid residence Qatar ID (QID) before opening salary accounts. You'll need to complete the work visa process and receive your residence QID first."
  - question: "Which bank should I choose for my salary account in Qatar?"
    answer: "Your employer will typically mandate or strongly prefer a specific bank for WPS (Wage Protection System) salary transfer. HR will tell you which bank they use - it's usually QNB, CBQ, QIB, or Dukhan Bank. Start with your employer's preferred bank first."
  - question: "What documents do I need to open a bank account in Qatar?"
    answer: "You'll need a valid passport, active Qatar ID (QID), salary certificate from your employer, proof of address, Qatar mobile number for OTP, and initial deposit of QAR 0-3,000 depending on the product."
  - question: "How much do remittances cost from Qatar?"
    answer: "Transfer fees are typically QAR 15-25, but the real cost is in the FX spread. Total monthly cost ranges from QAR 25-75 to India, QAR 30-90 to Philippines, and QAR 40-120 to UK, depending on the bank and amount sent."
  - question: "What's the difference between Islamic and conventional banking in Qatar?"
    answer: "Islamic banks use profit-sharing structures without interest (murabaha, ijara, wakala), while conventional banks use interest-based products. Both are QCB-regulated. Compare total costs, not just branding - Islamic auto finance uses instalment sale structures vs conventional interest APR."
  - question: "How long does it take to get a credit card in Qatar?"
    answer: "You typically need 3-6 months of salary history and a minimum salary of QAR 10,000-15,000. Initial credit limits are usually 1-3 times monthly salary, with caps of QAR 5,000-25,000 for new residents until you build 6-12 months of history."

## What changes between Islamic and conventional banking?

Islamic banking typically means murabaha, ijara and wakala contracts instead of interest-line loans, while conventional banks quote APR-style interest, and Invest Gulf converts Islamic profit-rate quotes into total cash paid over 3 to 5 years before foreign buyers choose auto finance. | Topic | Islamic | Conventional |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Auto finance | Instalment sale / ijara | Interest APR |
| Credit cards | Repay full balance to avoid profit charges | Interest on revolving balance |
| Regulation | QCB supervised | QCB supervised |
| Choice | Optional for all nationalities | Optional for all nationalities |

- Autopay full card balance monthly where the product charges profit on revolving amounts
- Read the schedule of charges even when marketing says 0% interest
