## What net yield should you model on Palm Jumeirah stock?

Net yield on Palm Jumeirah stock typically lands near 3% to 4% after service charges, management, and vacancy in the area, even when gross sits at 4.5% to 6.5%. A worked AED 2.4M trunk one-bed with AED 130,000 rent shows about 3.37% net versus higher JVC percentage returns.

Invest Gulf yield models always subtract Mollak, 6% management, and a 7% vacancy line.

A worked Palm Jumeirah trunk one-bedroom at AED 2.4 million with AED 130,000 gross rent shows how Nakheel economics diverge from inland yield plays. Gross yield lands near 5.42%, yet service charges at AED 32 per sq ft on 900 sq ft equal AED 28,800, property management at 6% takes AED 7,800, and a 7% vacancy allowance removes about AED 9,100, leaving roughly AED 80,900 net income or 3.37% net yield. A JVC one-bedroom at AED 650,000 earning AED 55,000 gross can clear about 5.58% net with far less capital. Palm Jebel Ali remains a 7 to 10 year appreciation bet with handover phases extending toward 2028, thin early assignment liquidity, and limited Ejari history until communities stabilise. Model Mollak-filed charges on comparable handed-over island buildings and add contingency, because SPA estimates on new Nakheel phases often understate operating costs by 20% to 30%.

## Which Nakheel documents should you see first?
A Nakheel due diligence checklist typically covers Trakheesi registration, escrow on Dubai REST, SPA delay clauses, Mollak comps, and Ejari rents for the developer phase you are buying. Government backing reduces default risk but not project-specific delay risk. Ready Palm stock often needs 60 to 120 days to sell when priced correctly.

**Insider tip:** Model Palm Jebel Ali and early Dubai Islands with a 15% service-charge contingency because no Mollak history exists until the building has operated for 12 months.

Invest Gulf files also snag at handover even on Tier 1 names.

Diligence checklist:
1. Trakheesi project registration
2. Escrow account on Dubai REST
3. SPA delay penalties and handover conditions
4. Mollak service-charge comps
5. Ejari transacted rents (not listings)
6. Independent snagging

| Risk | Control |
|---|---|
| Delay | SPA penalties + escrow |
| Fee surprise | Mollak comps + 15% buffer |
| Thin assignment | Confirm NOC policy early |

## Who should buy from Nakheel in 2026?

Nakheel buyers typically want waterfront scarcity and 7 to 10 year capital preservation in the area rather than maximum rental yield. Skip Nakheel if you need 7%+ net, flexible post-handover plans, or fast off-plan flips. Palm absolute dirhams can beat JVC cash, but return on capital is lower.

Invest Gulf suitability screens start with hold horizon, not island photography.

Buyer checklist:
- Fit: trophy address, long hold, Dubai Holding comfort
- Avoid: yield-first, short flip, lowest service charges
- Alternatives: Emaar for delivery, DAMAC/Danube for payment flexibility, Sobha for build quality focus

| Short-hold flipper | Weak |
