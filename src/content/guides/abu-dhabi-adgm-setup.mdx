## What is ADGM and who actually uses it?

ADGM, Abu Dhabi Global Market, is Abu Dhabi's financial free zone on Al Maryah Island, operating under English common law with its own courts, company law and financial regulator, the Financial Services Regulatory Authority. It is the Abu Dhabi counterpart to Dubai's DIFC rather than a general trading free zone, and that shapes who benefits from it.

The recurring profiles in ADGM are institutional or wealth-driven rather than retail:

- Fund managers and private equity firms targeting Abu Dhabi sovereign wealth capital
- Family offices managing multigenerational wealth for GCC ultra-high-net-worth families
- International banks establishing an Abu Dhabi presence, including HSBC, Standard Chartered and JP Morgan, which hold ADGM-licensed entities
- Abu Dhabi government-linked enterprises and their counterparties
- Law firms and professional services firms serving Abu Dhabi institutional clients
- Fintech and blockchain businesses seeking FSRA-supervised sandboxes

If your business is straightforward trading or services with no Abu Dhabi institutional angle, a conventional free zone is likely cheaper and simpler. Compare [DMCC company setup](/guides/dmcc-company-setup/) and the wider [Dubai business setup guide](/guides/dubai-business-setup-guide/) before assuming a financial centre is the right container.

## Which ADGM entity type should you register?

ADGM offers five main vehicles, and the choice usually comes down to whether you are holding assets, operating a business or running a fund. A Special Purpose Company is the cheapest entry point because it does not require a physical ADGM office; an operating company does.

| Entity type | Typical use | Office requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Special Purpose Company (SPC) | Holding assets, SPVs, family office vehicles | Registered office agent only |
| Private Company Ltd by Shares | Operating business, consulting, technology | Registered office in ADGM |
| Public Company Ltd by Shares | Listed or widely held structures | Registered office in ADGM |
| Private Company Ltd by Guarantee | Non-profit and membership structures | Registered office in ADGM |
| Incorporated Limited Partnership (ILP) | Fund structures with GP and LPs | Registered office in ADGM |

The Special Purpose Company is the most cost-effective ADGM entry for private wealth structures. It has no minimum share capital and can be maintained through an ADGM-registered registered office agent, which means no physical office lease. SPCs are used to hold UAE and international real estate, estate and succession planning structures, securities and financial assets, intellectual property, and family office master holding vehicles.

The Private Company Limited by Shares is the standard operating structure. It needs a registered office in ADGM, whether physical or through an agent, and at least one director and one shareholder. It suits professional services, consulting, technology and other non-regulated activities in Abu Dhabi.

The Incorporated Limited Partnership is the fund vehicle. The general partner manages the fund and the limited partners are investors, with LP liability limited to committed capital. Note that the ILP itself is not a licence to manage money: FSRA-regulated fund management requires a separate fund manager entity holding a Category 3C permission or equivalent.

## How much does ADGM company setup cost in 2026?

A lean non-regulated ADGM entity runs roughly USD 5,000 to 10,000 per year all-in, and up to USD 6,000 to 12,000 in year one once registration and premises are counted. ADGM publishes its registry fees in US dollars while office and visa costs are AED-denominated, so any budget mixes the two currencies.

| Cost line | Indicative amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Registration fee, ADGM Company (Ltd) | USD 2,000 to 4,000 | One-time at incorporation |
| Annual licence, non-regulated | USD 2,500 to 5,000 or more | Recurring |
| Registered office agent, SPC route | AED 8,000 to 15,000 per year | Replaces a physical office |
| Co-working, Al Maryah Island | AED 20,000 to 35,000 per year | Operating companies |
| Dedicated office, Al Maryah Island | From AED 80,000 per year | Scales with area |
| FSRA application, regulated entity | USD 10,000 to 100,000 or more | By licence category |

The gap between a holding structure and a regulated operating business is the thing to internalise. An SPC maintained through an office agent is a five-figure-dirham annual commitment. An FSRA-regulated firm is a six-figure exercise once legal, compliance and capital requirements are added, and the application fee is often the smallest line in that budget.

## How does ADGM compare with DIFC?

Both are English common law jurisdictions with their own courts and financial regulators, so the decision is commercial rather than legal. ADGM sits on Al Maryah Island under FSRA supervision; DIFC sits in Dubai under the DFSA. Costs for non-regulated entities are broadly comparable.

| Factor | ADGM | DIFC |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Location | Al Maryah Island, Abu Dhabi | DIFC, Dubai |
| Regulator | FSRA | DFSA |
| Legal basis | English common law, own courts | English common law, own courts |
| Strongest fit | Abu Dhabi sovereign wealth, family offices, funds | Regional banking, funds, Dubai-centric business |
| Service provider ecosystem | Growing, more concentrated | Larger and longer established |

Choose ADGM when your clients, capital or counterparties are in Abu Dhabi. Proximity to ADIA and Mubadala, and to Abu Dhabi government-linked enterprises, is a practical advantage that does not transfer.

Choose DIFC when your primary market is Dubai-centric, when you need the larger existing ecosystem of service providers and institutional co-tenants, when your investors are more familiar with DFSA than FSRA regulation, or when a more competitive provider market matters to your budget. The two centres have mutual recognition of judgments, with ADGM and DIFC Courts having signed recognition protocols, and firms regulated in one can establish cross-emirate arrangements with the other.

For the full structure comparison across mainland and the major free zones, see the [UAE free zone vs mainland guide](/guides/uae-free-zone-vs-mainland/).

## Step by step: how to register a company in ADGM

### Step 1: Confirm whether your activity is regulated

This determines the entire route. Non-regulated activities include holding companies, consulting, technology and professional services that do not constitute regulated financial services. SPCs are always non-regulated. Anything involving managing, advising on or arranging investments, taking deposits or operating a fund needs FSRA authorisation on top of incorporation, which changes the timeline from weeks to months.

### Step 2: Prepare documentation

Standard KYC for all shareholders and directors: a certified passport copy, proof of address in the form of a utility bill or bank statement under three months old, a CV or professional profile, a source of funds declaration, and a business plan for operating companies.

Corporate shareholders additionally need a certificate of incorporation, certificate of good standing, constitutional documents, a board resolution and UBO declarations. Map the ownership chain to natural persons before filing, because complex UBO chains are the most common cause of extended review.

### Step 3: Reserve the company name

ADGM's online portal handles name reservation. Names must comply with ADGM naming guidelines, which exclude names implying government affiliation, names implying regulated status without authorisation, and names that are offensive or misleading under ADGM Rules. Processing is typically 1 to 2 business days.

### Step 4: Submit the incorporation application

File through the ADGM Registration Authority portal. The ADGM team reviews KYC, UBO declarations and proposed activities. A standard non-regulated entity review takes 5 to 10 business days. Complex applications, particularly those with corporate shareholders sitting behind multiple holding layers, take longer.

### Step 5: Arrange the registered office

Every ADGM entity needs a registered address inside ADGM. For SPCs and holding structures, use an ADGM Registered Office Agent, typically a law firm or licensed corporate service provider on Al Maryah Island, at annual fees of roughly AED 8,000 to 15,000.

Operating companies structured as a Private Limited need physical space or ADGM-approved co-working on Al Maryah Island. The ADGM Hub co-working facility, Gate Tower and Al Maryah Island commercial buildings all offer options. Co-working packages start from approximately AED 20,000 to 35,000 per year, with dedicated offices from AED 80,000 per year.

### Step 6: FSRA application, regulated entities only

FSRA applications are filed separately through the FSRA portal after ADGM Registry incorporation. The categories in outline:

| FSRA category | Scope | Practical note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Category 1 | Deposit-taking, full bank licence | Highest capital, most complex application |
| Category 2 | Dealing and asset management | Significant capital and governance requirements |
| Category 3A, 3B, 3C | Advisory, arranging, non-discretionary | Common entry point for boutique firms |
| Category 4 | Specific non-discretionary services | Lightest category, limited scope |

FSRA application timelines run 3 to 12 months depending on category complexity, and legal and compliance advisory costs are substantial. Confirm current capital, governance and reporting requirements directly with FSRA, since these are reviewed periodically.

### Step 7: Receive the certificate of incorporation and begin operations

ADGM Registry issues the certificate of incorporation and the ADGM commercial licence. From there you proceed to bank account opening and visa applications.

## Which banks work with ADGM companies?

Abu Dhabi's own banks are the natural first stop, and account opening typically takes 2 to 6 weeks for non-regulated entities with complete documentation. FSRA-regulated firms often find the process smoother once regulatory approval is in hand, because the bank can rely on the regulator's own due diligence.

- First Abu Dhabi Bank (FAB): the UAE's largest bank, headquartered in Abu Dhabi, and a natural fit for ADGM entities with Abu Dhabi government or sovereign wealth relationships
- ADCB (Abu Dhabi Commercial Bank): strong ADGM corporate banking capability
- Abu Dhabi Islamic Bank (ADIB): Sharia-compliant banking for ADGM entities
- HSBC, Abu Dhabi branch: international bank with ADGM presence
- Standard Chartered: active in ADGM for institutional and professional clients

Start the account application the week your licence is issued rather than treating it as a later administrative step. ## Why family offices use ADGM for private wealth

ADGM's appeal to family offices rests on three things: an English-law holding vehicle, succession planning tools that UAE civil law does not readily provide, and proximity to Abu Dhabi institutional capital.

Tax-efficient consolidated holding. The SPC can hold assets globally, UAE real estate, international securities, operating companies and intellectual property, inside a single English-law vehicle. For families with assets spread across jurisdictions, that consolidation delivers legal certainty and governance clarity that a patchwork of local companies does not.

Succession planning. ADGM common law principles allow trust-like structures and tailored succession arrangements that are difficult to achieve under UAE civil law. Combined with an ADGM Will, families can plan cross-generational asset transfer in English-law terms.

ADGM Wills. Non-Muslim residents with UAE assets can register an ADGM Will, and a DIFC Will is equally valid in Abu Dhabi, overriding Sharia succession principles for those assets. This is a material advantage for international families holding UAE property or business interests. The [UAE will DIFC ADGM guide](/guides/uae-will-difc-adgm/) sets out the registration mechanics for both.

Access to Abu Dhabi institutional capital. For family offices seeking co-investment opportunities or mandates from ADIA or Mubadala, an ADGM presence is frequently a practical prerequisite. Both sovereign wealth funds have ADGM-registered subsidiaries and prefer counterparties inside the jurisdiction.

Where an ADGM structure is intended to hold UAE property, the registration side needs its own advice. Property held through an ADGM SPC carries specific DLD or DMT registration implications, covered in the [guide to buying property through a UAE company](/guides/buy-property-through-uae-company/). Families who are still deciding which asset to hold often start on the acquisition side instead, and [Abu Dhabi off-plan launches](/invest-abu-dhabi-off-plan/) with escrow-backed payment plans are a common first placement.

## Do ADGM entities pay UAE corporate tax?

Yes, in principle. Since June 2023, UAE corporate tax at 9% on profits above AED 375,000 applies to all UAE entities, including ADGM companies. The free zone address is not an exemption.

Qualifying Free Zone Person treatment. ADGM entities may qualify for 0% tax on Qualifying Income, broadly income from transactions with other free zone entities or from overseas. Non-qualifying income, including mainland UAE-sourced income and certain passive income, is taxed at 9%. Qualification is conditional and must be maintained rather than claimed once.

SPC income specifically. Where an SPC generates passive holding income such as dividends from subsidiaries or property rental income, the treatment depends on the nature of that income and on QFZP qualification. Pure holding income from overseas subsidiaries may qualify for 0%; UAE-sourced rental income may be taxable. Take asset-class-specific advice rather than applying one answer across the portfolio.

Economic substance. Entities claiming QFZP treatment must demonstrate genuine economic substance in ADGM, not merely a registered address. Employees, decision-making and core income-generating activities should be demonstrably present in ADGM for the relevant category of qualifying activities. For a family office run remotely, this is the condition most likely to fail on review. The [UAE corporate tax guide for expats](/guides/uae-corporate-tax-expats/) covers the broader framework.

## Is ADGM the right choice for your business?

ADGM is the right jurisdiction when your clients and capital are in Abu Dhabi, when you need an English-law holding or fund vehicle, or when your business requires FSRA authorisation and Abu Dhabi is your regulatory home. It is the wrong jurisdiction when your activity is ordinary trading or services with no Abu Dhabi institutional dimension, in which case a conventional free zone costs less and asks less of you.

Practical decision points worth testing before you commit:

- Where do your first ten clients sit? If the honest answer is Dubai, ADGM's core advantage does not apply to you.
- Do you actually need a regulated permission, or only a corporate vehicle? The cost difference is an order of magnitude.
- Can you meet economic substance for QFZP treatment, or is the structure genuinely run from elsewhere?
- Is the driver the operating business, or family wealth and succession? Those point to different entity types.

For the residency dimension, including the thresholds that let founders and investors sponsor themselves and their families, see the [Gulf residency by investment guide](/guides/gulf-residency-by-investment-guide/), and if your route involves financed property, [golden visa with a mortgaged property](/guides/golden-visa-mortgage-property-uae/) covers the conditions.

## What Invest Gulf sees on ADGM enquiries

Working with families and founders who set up in Abu Dhabi, the most frequent misdiagnosis in our files is scale. People arrive asking about ADGM because they have read that it is Abu Dhabi's financial centre, when what they actually need is either a plain company for a consulting business or, at the other end, a regulated fund manager licence they have badly under-budgeted. The middle case, the family that genuinely needs an English-law holding vehicle plus an ADGM Will, is where the jurisdiction earns its cost.

The second pattern is sequencing. The two-stage structure of ADGM, incorporation with the Registry and then authorisation with FSRA, catches people who assumed one application. Non-regulated incorporation in 5 to 10 business days sits next to an FSRA process measured in months, and running them as if they were parallel leads to signed office leases sitting idle. Budget the lease from the date you expect authorisation, not the date you incorporate.

The third pattern is property arriving after the structure, not before. Families set up the SPC, settle the Will, and only then decide which Abu Dhabi asset it should hold. That order is usually correct, but it means the property decision gets made under time pressure once the vehicle is already paying fees.
