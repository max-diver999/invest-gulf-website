### What the MOJ Search Reveals

| Information | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Registered owner name and nationality | Confirms seller has legal title |
| Title deed number and date | Confirms registration is current |
| Zone classification (freehold/leasehold/usufruct) | Determines foreign resale eligibility |
| Registered encumbrances | Mortgages, liens, court orders |
| Property dimensions and unit number | Confirms you are buying the correct unit |
| Any registered disputes | Prior buyer claims or inheritance issues |

A clean MOJ search shows: owner name matches seller identity document, zone is designated foreign freehold, no encumbrances, dimensions match SPA. Any discrepancy is a deal condition - not a minor issue to move past.

### Freehold Zone Verification: Critical for Exit

Qatar's freehold zones for foreign buyers are defined by Cabinet Resolution. The designated zones as of 2026:

| Zone | Status | Foreign ownership type |
|---|---|---|
| The Pearl-Qatar | Established | 100% freehold |
| Lusail City | Designated (verify per parcel) | 100% freehold in qualifying plots |
| West Bay Lagoon | Partial | Selected freehold towers |
| Al Daayen | Partial | Designated compound areas |
| Gewan Island | New (UDC development) | 100% freehold |
| Mushayrib (Heart of Doha) | Designated | 100% freehold in approved buildings |
| Al Khor and Jabal Tharwan | Leasehold/usufruct only | 99-year usufruct - not freehold |

Lusail is the highest-risk area for zone confusion. Lusail City has multiple parcels with different ownership structures - some foreign-freehold, some Qatari-only leasehold, some government land with no private ownership. The fact that a building is in Lusail City does not guarantee foreign freehold eligibility. Verify the specific parcel number against MOJ zone classification, not just the developer's marketing materials.

### Municipality Fees and Utilities

Qatar municipality fees (kahramaa registration, utility connection) are primarily a tenant's obligation during tenancy, but can leave outstanding balances if previous tenant departed owing fees.

Verify at purchase:
- Kahramaa (electricity and water) balance is zero - request utility settlement statement.
- No outstanding municipality tax (5% of annual rent) balance from previous tenancy.
- Parking allocation (if applicable) is included in the title or management agreement.

For any Qatar property transaction, you need a lawyer from a Qatar-licensed firm. Requirements:

- Firm must be licensed by the Qatar Ministry of Justice.
- Partner must hold a Qatar Lawyers licence (Qatari national or a foreigner with valid legal licence).
- The firm can be an international firm with Qatar office (many international firms operate in Qatar Financial Centre, but QFC firms may not handle onshore MOJ registrations - verify).

Typical lawyer scope for a Qatar purchase:
- MOJ title search
- SPA review and negotiation
- Arabic translation verification
- Power of attorney drafting (for remote buyers)
- Attendance at MOJ transfer (or as attorney)

Cost: QAR 5,000–20,000 for standard residential purchase.

### Power of Attorney for Non-Resident Buyers

Non-resident foreign buyers can complete a Qatar property purchase using a Power of Attorney (POA) appointing a local representative. Steps:

1. Draft POA in English with Arabic translation.
2. Notarise POA in your home country.
3. Apostille or legalise at Qatar Embassy (if your country requires).
4. Submit to Qatar Ministry of Foreign Affairs for authentication.
5. Attorney appears at MOJ on your behalf.

Processing time: 3–6 weeks from signing to Qatar authentication. Build this into your deal timeline. Sellers may pressure for a faster timeline - resist if your POA is not yet authenticated.

Trusting the listing agent for title confirmation. In Qatar, the listing agent is paid by the seller and has no obligation to verify freehold zone eligibility independently. The agent quoting "freehold" for a Lusail property does not substitute for an MOJ title search by your lawyer. Freehold zone boundaries are complex and parcel-level - not all Lusail postcodes carry the same eligibility.

Accepting verbal SC quotes. The most common post-purchase surprise is the actual service charge being 20–40% higher than the figure quoted during negotiation. Verbal quotes from agents and even sellers are often based on outdated information. Require a current SC invoice - the actual most recent quarterly or annual bill - as a deal condition. See [Qatar service charges guide](/guides/qatar-service-charges-guide/) for area benchmarks.

Skipping the municipality fee clearance. Qatar municipality charges (5% of annual rent) are typically the tenant's obligation during tenancy, but a tenant who departed without paying can leave an outstanding balance registered against the address. Clear this before transfer - it can create complications with new tenancy registration and Kahramaa.

No escrow verification for off-plan. Paying a reservation fee or first stage payment before seeing a written confirmation from the escrow bank is the single highest-risk action for off-plan buyers in Qatar. The escrow law protects buyers but requires active verification - it does not self-enforce. See [Qatar off-plan vs ready property](/guides/qatar-off-plan-vs-ready-property/) for the full off-plan checklist.

Power of attorney gaps. Buying from abroad with a POA that does not explicitly cover lease registration, SC clearance, and Kahramaa transfer means your lawyer or manager cannot act on these items without additional authority. Draft the POA broadly enough to cover all property-related administrative acts.

### Transaction cost snapshot

Purchasing property in Qatar incurs transfer fees of 2–4 % of the sale price, registration charges of 500–5,000 AED, agency commission of 1–2 % (buyer side, where applicable), and conveyancing/NOC fees of 500–5,000 AED. Mortgage arrangement fees add 0.25–1 % of the loan amount. Total acquisition costs typically sit at 7–9 % above the headline price when financing is involved, or 4–6 % for cash purchases.
