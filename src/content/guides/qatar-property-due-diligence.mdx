Qatar's property due diligence process is simpler than Dubai's in some ways (no RERA system, no DLD self-service portal) and more opaque in others (no central online register, SC records held by developers). The investors who get caught out in Qatar are those who apply Dubai shortcuts - trusting listing agent data, skipping title search, not getting SC clearance in writing. This guide gives you the complete checklist.

## The Qatar Property DD Framework

### Four Non-Negotiable Checks

Before any Qatar property purchase, these four checks must be completed - in writing, not verbal confirmation:

| Check | What you verify | Who provides it | Consequence if skipped |
|---|---|---|---|
| MOJ title search | Owner identity, zone type, encumbrances | Qatar-licensed lawyer | You may buy a leasehold or disputed title |
| Freehold zone confirmation | Foreign buyer eligibility | MOJ register / lawyer | You cannot resell to another foreign buyer |
| SC clearance certificate | Zero SC arrears | Management company/developer | You inherit seller's debt, SC block on future NOC |
| Municipality tax clearance | No outstanding municipality charges | Municipality of Qatar | Title transfer may be blocked |

For off-plan properties, add a fifth check:
| Escrow account confirmation | Payments in project-specific escrow | Developer's escrow bank | Funds at risk if developer distress |

## Title Verification: Qatar MOJ Register

### How Qatar Title Works

Qatar real estate is registered in the Ministry of Justice (MOJ) Real Estate Registry. Unlike Dubai's REST app or UAE's eServices portals, Qatar's register is not publicly accessible online. All title searches require:

- A Qatar-licensed lawyer (Qatar Law Firm or Qatari-national-owned firm) to submit an official search request.
- The buyer's authorisation (written authority or power of attorney if buyer is abroad).
- Turnaround typically 3–7 business days.
- Fee: QAR 500–2,000 depending on firm.

### What the MOJ Search Reveals

| Information | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Registered owner name and nationality | Confirms seller has legal title |
| Title deed number and date | Confirms registration is current |
| Zone classification (freehold/leasehold/usufruct) | Determines foreign resale eligibility |
| Registered encumbrances | Mortgages, liens, court orders |
| Property dimensions and unit number | Confirms you are buying the correct unit |
| Any registered disputes | Prior buyer claims or inheritance issues |

A clean MOJ search shows: owner name matches seller identity document, zone is designated foreign freehold, no encumbrances, dimensions match SPA. Any discrepancy is a deal condition - not a minor issue to move past.

### Freehold Zone Verification: Critical for Exit

Qatar's freehold zones for foreign buyers are defined by Cabinet Resolution. The designated zones as of 2026:

| Zone | Status | Foreign ownership type |
|---|---|---|
| The Pearl-Qatar | Established | 100% freehold |
| Lusail City | Designated (verify per parcel) | 100% freehold in qualifying plots |
| West Bay Lagoon | Partial | Selected freehold towers |
| Al Daayen | Partial | Designated compound areas |
| Gewan Island | New (UDC development) | 100% freehold |
| Mushayrib (Heart of Doha) | Designated | 100% freehold in approved buildings |
| Al Khor and Jabal Tharwan | Leasehold/usufruct only | 99-year usufruct - not freehold |

**Lusail is the highest-risk area for zone confusion.** Lusail City has multiple parcels with different ownership structures - some foreign-freehold, some Qatari-only leasehold, some government land with no private ownership. The fact that a building is in Lusail City does not guarantee foreign freehold eligibility. Verify the specific parcel number against MOJ zone classification, not just the developer's marketing materials.

## Service Charge and Municipal Clearance

### Qatar SC Clearance Process

Qatar has no centralised SC registration system (unlike Dubai's Mollak). Service charges are held by:
- The developer (for buildings still under developer management)
- A contracted property management company
- An owners' association (formed in mature communities like The Pearl)

**Required documents:**

1. SC clearance certificate: confirms QAR 0 outstanding on the specific unit, signed by the management company.
2. Current SC rate confirmation: the monthly/annual SC amount you will pay as new owner.
3. Management company identification: name, registration number, contact for post-purchase SC queries.

**Warning:** Some sellers provide a "balance statement" instead of a clearance certificate. A balance statement shows what is owed - it is not a clearance. Require a zero-balance clearance letter dated within 30 days of planned transfer.

For full SC rate data by area: [Qatar service charges guide](/guides/qatar-service-charges-guide/).

### Municipality Fees and Utilities

Qatar municipality fees (kahramaa registration, utility connection) are primarily a tenant's obligation during tenancy, but can leave outstanding balances if previous tenant departed owing fees.

Verify at purchase:
- Kahramaa (electricity and water) balance is zero - request utility settlement statement.
- No outstanding municipality tax (5% of annual rent) balance from previous tenancy.
- Parking allocation (if applicable) is included in the title or management agreement.

## Lawyer Selection and Power of Attorney

### Qatar-Licensed Legal Representation

For any Qatar property transaction, you need a lawyer from a Qatar-licensed firm. Requirements:

- Firm must be licensed by the Qatar Ministry of Justice.
- Partner must hold a Qatar Lawyers licence (Qatari national or a foreigner with valid legal licence).
- The firm can be an international firm with Qatar office (many international firms operate in Qatar Financial Centre, but QFC firms may not handle onshore MOJ registrations - verify).

Typical lawyer scope for a Qatar purchase:
- MOJ title search
- SPA review and negotiation
- Arabic translation verification
- Power of attorney drafting (for remote buyers)
- Attendance at MOJ transfer (or as attorney)

Cost: QAR 5,000–20,000 for standard residential purchase.

### Power of Attorney for Non-Resident Buyers

Non-resident foreign buyers can complete a Qatar property purchase using a Power of Attorney (POA) appointing a local representative. Steps:

1. Draft POA in English with Arabic translation.
2. Notarise POA in your home country.
3. Apostille or legalise at Qatar Embassy (if your country requires).
4. Submit to Qatar Ministry of Foreign Affairs for authentication.
5. Attorney appears at MOJ on your behalf.

Processing time: 3–6 weeks from signing to Qatar authentication. Build this into your deal timeline. Sellers may pressure for a faster timeline - resist if your POA is not yet authenticated.

## Off-Plan Due Diligence: Additional Steps

If purchasing off-plan, the standard DD checklist expands:

| Additional check | How to verify | Red flag |
|---|---|---|
| Developer registration | MOJ developer licence number | No licence or expired |
| Project MOJ registration | Off-plan project registered under escrow law | Not registered = no buyer protection |
| Escrow bank confirmation | Letter from escrow bank confirming project account | Verbal only, no letter |
| Prior project delivery | Certificate of handover from previous project | No prior delivery |
| Completion % and timeline | Construction inspection, not developer claim | Less than 20% complete with 50% sold |
| QCCI clearance (large projects) | Qatar Chamber of Commerce and Industry | Missing for large-scale development |

## Transaction Process: Step by Step

### For Ready Resale Property

| Step | Action | Timeline |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Instruct Qatar-licensed lawyer | Day 1 |
| 2 | MOJ title search and review | Days 2–8 |
| 3 | Negotiate and sign SPA | Days 8–15 |
| 4 | SC clearance certificate obtained | Days 10–20 |
| 5 | Municipality fee clearance | Days 10–20 |
| 6 | Pay 0.25% transfer tax (Ministry of Finance) | Day of transfer |
| 7 | MOJ transfer registration - both parties or POA | Day of transfer |
| 8 | Receive title deed in buyer name | Day of transfer or +2 days |
| 9 | Apply for Kahramaa connection | Post-transfer |
| 10 | Register with property management for SC | Post-transfer |

**Total timeline:** 3–6 weeks for uncomplicated cash purchase. Allow 8–12 weeks if POA is required.

### For Off-Plan Purchase

| Step | Action | Timeline |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Verify escrow and project registration | Before any payment |
| 2 | Review and sign SPA (Arabic and English) | Before stage 1 payment |
| 3 | Pay stage 1 into escrow account | Per SPA milestone |
| 4 | Oqood registration at MOJ | Within 30 days of signing |
| 5 | Construction stage payments per milestones | Per SPA schedule |
| 6 | Pre-handover inspection | 30–60 days before handover |
| 7 | Snagging list and rectification | 30–90 days |
| 8 | Final payment on handover | Per SPA |
| 9 | Title deed issued in buyer name | At handover |
| 10 | Residency application (if using for permit) | After title deed |

## Qatar vs UAE Due Diligence: Key Differences

| Factor | Qatar | UAE (Dubai) |
|---|---|---|
| Title search | MOJ - requires lawyer | DLD - partially self-service via REST |
| SC clearance | No central registry - verify with management co | Mollak government registry |
| Transfer tax | 0.25% | Dubai: 4% DLD + fees |
| Agency fee regulation | Less regulated | RERA-registered agents, 2% standard |
| Escrow (off-plan) | 2014 law - functional but less transparent | RERA escrow, project-specific tracking |
| Mortgage market (foreigners) | Limited (50–60% LTV, residents primarily) | More developed (50–75% LTV, non-residents possible) |
| Dispute resolution | Qatari courts or arbitration | RERA, Dubai courts, or arbitration |
| Foreign zone complexity | Per-parcel verification required (Lusail) | Zone list well-published (freehold/leasehold clear) |

The most important difference for first-time Qatar buyers: no online portal equivalent to Dubai's REST or Abu Dhabi's DARI. Every verification requires a human intermediary. Budget 3–4 weeks longer than a comparable UAE purchase.

## Common Qatar Due Diligence Mistakes by Foreign Investors

Understanding the typical failure points helps you structure your process correctly from day one.

**Trusting the listing agent for title confirmation.** In Qatar, the listing agent is paid by the seller and has no obligation to verify freehold zone eligibility independently. The agent quoting "freehold" for a Lusail property does not substitute for an MOJ title search by your lawyer. Freehold zone boundaries are complex and parcel-level - not all Lusail postcodes carry the same eligibility.

**Accepting verbal SC quotes.** The most common post-purchase surprise is the actual service charge being 20–40% higher than the figure quoted during negotiation. Verbal quotes from agents and even sellers are often based on outdated information. Require a current SC invoice - the actual most recent quarterly or annual bill - as a deal condition. See [Qatar service charges guide](/guides/qatar-service-charges-guide/) for area benchmarks.

**Skipping the municipality fee clearance.** Qatar municipality charges (5% of annual rent) are typically the tenant's obligation during tenancy, but a tenant who departed without paying can leave an outstanding balance registered against the address. Clear this before transfer - it can create complications with new tenancy registration and Kahramaa.

**No escrow verification for off-plan.** Paying a reservation fee or first stage payment before seeing a written confirmation from the escrow bank is the single highest-risk action for off-plan buyers in Qatar. The escrow law protects buyers but requires active verification - it does not self-enforce. See [Qatar off-plan vs ready property](/guides/qatar-off-plan-vs-ready-property/) for the full off-plan checklist.

**Power of attorney gaps.** Buying from abroad with a POA that does not explicitly cover lease registration, SC clearance, and Kahramaa transfer means your lawyer or manager cannot act on these items without additional authority. Draft the POA broadly enough to cover all property-related administrative acts.

## Post-Purchase Checklist

After title deed is received:

- Title deed copy stored securely (digital + physical)
- SC payment method set up with management company
- Kahramaa connected in landlord name
- Qatar bank account opened for rent receipt (if desired)
- Property management agreement signed
- Landlord insurance in place
- Lease agreement template reviewed for Qatar law compliance
- Residency application filed (if using property for permit) - see [Qatar residency by property](/guides/qatar-residency-by-property/)
