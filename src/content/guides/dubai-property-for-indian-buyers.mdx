For a resident Indian buyer, the binding constraint on a Dubai purchase is not the price and not the mortgage. It is an annual ceiling on how much money may leave the country per person, resetting every 1 April.

That single fact converts an off-plan purchase from a financing decision into a scheduling one. Two buyers with identical savings and identical target properties can find one plan comfortable and the other impossible, purely because of where the instalment dates fall relative to the end of March.

So an off-plan payment plan has to be mapped onto those windows before the sale agreement is signed. Two instalments either side of 31 March draw on two allowances; two inside one year draw on one. TCS applies above a threshold and is collected at transfer, so budget the gross figure on the day even though it is creditable later. ## Why is this a calendar problem rather than a money problem?

Because the constraint is not how much you have but how much may move in a given twelve-month window. A buyer with the full price in hand still cannot send it in one year if it exceeds the allowance.

This is unlike almost every other buyer cohort in the Dubai market, and it is why generic off-plan advice fits Indian buyers badly. The standard guidance is about affordability: can you carry the instalments. The question here is different: can each instalment be remitted in the window it falls in. A plan that is affordable on income can still be impossible on capacity, and the failure shows up not at the point of purchase but eighteen months later when a milestone payment falls due in a year whose allowance is already committed.

## How do the windows actually bite?

Through the position of each instalment relative to 31 March. Two payment schedules with the same total and the same number of instalments can have completely different capacity profiles.

| Schedule shape | Instalments | Allowance years used | Practical effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feb and May, same calendar year | 2 | 2 | Comfortable, nine months of slack |
| May and Feb, following year | 2 | 2 | Comfortable, same structure reversed |
| June and December, one year | 2 | 1 | Both draw on a single allowance |
| Quarterly across 18 months | 6 | 2 | Concentration depends on the start month |
| Handover payment on completion | 1, large | 1 | The single riskiest date in the plan |

The last row is where plans break. Handover payments are the largest single instalment in most off-plan schedules and their date is set by construction rather than by agreement, which means the one payment least able to move is the one most likely to land inconveniently. A buyer whose plan already commits most of an allowance year before handover has no headroom for a completion date that arrives early. Payment plan structures and how developers set them are covered on the [off-plan payment plans page](/guides/off-plan-payment-plans-dubai/).

## What does the per-person structure allow?

More aggregate capacity than one buyer has alone, provided the arrangement is designed before the paperwork rather than after it. The allowance attaches to each resident individual, so a household holds several.

The design question is not whether family members can remit but whether the resulting structure makes sense on the title. The person sending money and the person named as owner have to be reconcilable, and a plan that has a spouse or parent remitting for a property held solely in one name is a structure somebody will eventually have to explain, at a bank, at a registration, or on a future sale. Families who decide this at the outset produce clean documents. Families who decide it when an instalment approaches produce arrangements that work commercially and read awkwardly, which is a problem deferred rather than solved. Where a mortgage forms part of the structure, the sequencing gets tighter again, because a lender is assessing the same people.

## Where does TCS sit in the plan?

On the transfer date, as a cash item, at the point the money leaves. It applies to outward remittances above a threshold and is collected then rather than billed later.

The important distinction is between cost and timing. TCS is creditable against your Indian tax position, so it is recoverable rather than a fee on the purchase; what it is not is available on the day. A buyer who budgets the net instalment and finds the gross amount leaving the account has a shortfall at the worst moment, and the recovery arrives on a tax calendar that has no relationship to the developer's schedule. Budget gross at every transfer and treat the credit as a later refund rather than as part of the purchase arithmetic. The mechanics of moving the money itself, including corridors and costs, are on the [currency transfer page](/guides/currency-transfer-buy-property-uae/).

## Who is this page not for?

Non-resident Indians, and the distinction matters more than any other on this page. The remittance scheme described here is a facility for Indian residents. An NRI operates under a different framework, with different account types and different rules on moving proceeds back.

Getting this wrong in either direction is expensive. A resident who reads NRI guidance concludes they have flexibility they do not have. An NRI who reads resident guidance builds a payment plan around a ceiling that does not apply to them and buys a smaller property than they could have. Establish the category first, because everything downstream depends on it: the account you remit from, the ceiling that applies, and the route by which sale proceeds eventually come back. The NRI position is set out on the [NRI property tax page](/guides/tax-indian-nri-dubai-property/) and the account structures on the [NRI banking page](/guides/uae-banking-for-indian-nris/).

## What should you negotiate before signing?

The instalment dates, and it is a cheaper ask than a discount. Payment schedules are a commercial term, and developers who will not move on price will often move on timing because timing costs them less.

The request is specific rather than general. Rather than asking for a longer plan, ask for the two or three largest instalments to fall in separate allowance years, and name the months. A developer's scheduling team can usually accommodate a shift of a few weeks at the point of sale, and the same request after the sale agreement is signed is a contract variation with a different answer. Buyers who arrive with the mapping already done, showing which payments sit where, get a better hearing than buyers asking for flexibility in the abstract.

## What does a mapped plan look like on paper?

A single sheet with three columns, produced before the sale agreement rather than after it. Instalment date, allowance year, running total against the ceiling for each person contributing.

The value of writing it down is that it makes the failure visible while it is still cheap. A schedule that looks manageable in conversation resolves, on paper, into two payments 40 days apart that both fall in the same allowance year, or a handover instalment sitting in a year already 80% committed. Neither of those is obvious from a developer's schedule, which is presented as a percentage breakdown against construction milestones and says nothing about March.

Two refinements make the sheet more useful. Add a column for the gross amount including TCS, so the figure in the row is the money actually leaving rather than the money reaching Dubai. And add a row for the closing costs, which fall due at registration alongside the final payment and are frequently left out of a plan that was mapped on the purchase price alone.

## What if the plan still does not fit?

Then the property is too large for the structure, and the honest responses are all available before signing rather than after.

Three of them work. A smaller unit, which fits the same schedule inside the same capacity. A longer plan, which spreads the same total across more allowance years. Or additional family members formally brought into the ownership and the remittance structure at the outset, with the title reflecting it. What does not work is signing on the assumption that capacity will be found later, because the instalment date is contractual and the allowance is not negotiable. A missed developer payment is governed by the sale agreement, and the remedies there are the developer's rather than yours. Total closing costs, which sit on top of every scenario above, are itemised on the [cost of buying page](/guides/cost-of-buying-property-dubai/).
