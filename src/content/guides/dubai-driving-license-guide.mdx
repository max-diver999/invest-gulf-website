# Dubai Driving Licence 2026: RTA Conversion, Salik & Parking

**Quick answer:** Most Western and GCC licence holders convert directly via **RTA** in 1–5 days for AED 220–550 plus eye test. All others complete a driving school course (AED 3,500–8,000, 6–14 weeks). Dubai has **Salik tolls** (AED 4 per gate) and a **metro network** — making the mobility calculus different from Abu Dhabi.

**TL;DR:** Dubai licensing runs through **RTA** (Roads and Transport Authority) — not Muroor as in Abu Dhabi. Salik tolls add AED 100–400/month for regular drivers. Metro makes car-free life possible in central areas. Golden-country holders convert in days; Indian, Pakistani, Philippine, Egyptian nationals complete full driving school. ## Dubai vs Abu Dhabi licensing — key differences

If you have already read the Abu Dhabi driving licence guide, here is what changes in Dubai:

| Factor | Dubai (RTA) | Abu Dhabi (Muroor) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Licensing authority | RTA (Roads and Transport Authority) | Muroor (Abu Dhabi Police Traffic) |
| Daily tolls | Salik — AED 4 per gate | No Salik within Abu Dhabi emirate |
| Public transport | Metro + tram + bus network | Bus only — car essential |
| Conversion lists | RTA approved countries | Muroor approved countries — partially overlap |
| Parking apps | RTA Dubai Parking app | Mawaqif app |
| Fine payment | RTA / Dubai Police apps | Abu Dhabi Police app |
| Car necessity | Optional in central areas | Near-mandatory everywhere |

The core licence document is the same UAE federal plastic card. The **process** and the **daily cost structure** differ significantly.

## RTA golden countries — direct conversion eligibility

RTA maintains an approved list of countries whose licence holders can convert without a practical test. This list changes periodically — always verify at rta.ae at the time of booking, not based on community forum posts.

| Region | Typical treatment |
| --- | --- |
| GCC states (Saudi, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman, Qatar) | Direct conversion — common |
| UK and Ireland | Direct conversion — confirm current status |
| EU member states | Most eligible — per-country basis |
| USA and Canada | Eligible — some states and provinces vary |
| Australia and New Zealand | Eligible |
| Japan, Singapore, South Korea | Eligible |
| India, Pakistan, Philippines, Egypt | Full driving school required |
| Other nationalities | Check RTA official portal |

**Key document requirements for conversion:**

- Valid original foreign licence (not expired, not on a ban)
- Emirates ID showing valid UAE residency
- Eye test certificate from RTA-approved optical centre
- Passport (original plus copy)
- Passport photos (white background, specific size)
- Payment for RTA fees

## Full driving school route — costs and timeline

If your country is not on the conversion list, you will enrol in an RTA-approved driving school. This is a structured process with a fixed sequence.

| Stage | Typical AED | Typical Duration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| File opening + registration | 1,200–2,000 | Day 1 |
| Theory classes and hazard perception | 600–1,200 | Week 1–2 |
| Practical lessons (minimum 20–40 hours) | 2,500–5,500 | Week 2–8 |
| RTA yard and road tests | 600–1,200 | Week 8–12 |
| Licence issuance | 200–350 | Same week as pass |
| **Total all-in** | **3,500–8,000** | **6–14 weeks** |

**Automatic vs manual transmission:** Most RTA-approved schools offer both. Taking the automatic-only test gives an automatic restriction stamp — fine for UAE roads, restricts manual hire cars on overseas trips. Budget accordingly.

**Test failure:** Each retest has a fee of AED 200–500 and a minimum 7-day wait. Students who rush lessons typically fail twice; budgeting AED 1,000 extra for retests is prudent.

## Step-by-step RTA conversion workflow

1. **Obtain Emirates ID** — licencing system requires active UAE residency.
2. **Eye test** — visit an RTA-approved optical centre. Cost AED 100–200. Certificate valid for 30 days.
3. **Book RTA appointment** — via RTA Smart app or rta.ae. Choose the nearest Customer Happiness Centre.
4. **Submit documents** — foreign licence, Emirates ID, eye test certificate, photos, passport copy.
5. **Pay fees** — AED 220–550 depending on licence category and validity period you choose.
6. **Collect UAE licence** — some centres print same-day; others require a return visit in 1–3 days.
7. **Activate Salik** — register plate at salik.ae before using toll roads.
8. **Get car insurance** — comprehensive policy required before vehicle registration.

**Pro tip:** Use the RTA app to track queue times at Customer Happiness Centres. Mirdif and Jumeirah centres often have shorter waits than the Al Barsha hub.

## Salik tolls — how they work in Dubai

Salik is Dubai's electronic toll system, operated by RTA. Tolls do not exist in Abu Dhabi emirate — if you are coming from there, this is a new monthly expense.

| Salik gate | Key roads affected |
| --- | --- |
| Sheikh Zayed Road gates | Main artery; multiple gates |
| Al Maktoum Bridge | Deira to Bur Dubai crossing |
| Al Garhoud Bridge | Airport-area crossing |
| Al Safa South | Interchange via SZR |
| Al Barsha | Opposite direction SZR |
| Jebel Ali | Southern entry |

**How charges work:**

- AED 4 per gate crossing
- Tag reads automatically via windshield tag (account pre-funded)
- Minimum balance top-up AED 100; low-balance SMS alerts
- Monthly typical cost: AED 80–400 depending on commute pattern

**Registering Salik:**

- Visit salik.ae or RTA app
- Register vehicle plate number and link payment card
- Tag ships by post or can be collected at RTA centres
- Account links to vehicle registration — keep positive balance at all times

**Negative balance:** If balance goes negative, a fine applies. Check balance monthly, especially when using infrequently.

## Metro and public transport — the Dubai advantage

Unlike Abu Dhabi which relies almost entirely on cars, Dubai has a functional metro that allows central-area residents to manage without a vehicle — at least during the week.

| Route | Metro accessibility |
| --- | --- |
| Downtown Dubai / Business Bay | Red Line — direct |
| DIFC / World Trade Centre | Red Line |
| Marina / JBR | Red Line (DMCC station) |
| Mall of the Emirates | Red Line |
| Dubai Airport T1 and T3 | Red Line (airport stops) |
| Deira / Gold Souk | Green Line |
| Silicon Oasis | No direct metro |
| Jumeirah Beach Road | Tram (Marina area) |

**Daily Nol card cost:** AED 4–8 per trip in zones; monthly unlimited passes available. Taxis top up AED 20–50 per trip.

**Family reality check:** Schools in Arabian Ranches, Mirdif, Jumeirah Village Circle, or Damac Hills are not metro-accessible. Families with school-age children almost always need at least one car. See [Dubai transport costs](/guides/dubai-transport-costs-metro-taxi/) for the full breakdown.

## Parking in Dubai — zones, apps and costs

Dubai parking is managed primarily via the RTA Dubai Parking app, with a tiered zone system.

| Zone colour | Typical charge | Areas |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Silver | AED 2/hour | Deira, Bur Dubai, older districts |
| Gold | AED 3/hour | Business Bay, DIFC approaches |
| Purple | AED 5–8/hour | Downtown, SZR prime |
| No charge | Free | Residential compounds with allocated bays |
| Valet | AED 20–60 | Premium venues |

**Residential parking:**

Most apartment towers include one or two allocated bays. Visitors pay at ground-floor meters or use app payment. Event days at Dubai Mall, Expo City, and Global Village cause spillover that affects nearby residential blocks.

**Free parking exceptions:**

- Fridays from midnight to noon in most paid zones
- Public holidays — check RTA announcements
- After 22:00 in many zones until 08:00

## RTA fines and traffic violations

Dubai fines are issued by Dubai Police (not RTA). Black points accumulate on your federal traffic file.

| Violation | Fine (AED) | Black points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Speeding 20–30 km/h over | 600 | 6 |
| Speeding 30–40 km/h over | 700 | 6 |
| Speeding 40–60 km/h over | 1,000 | 12 |
| Speeding over 60 km/h over | 2,000+ | 24 |
| Running red light | 1,000 | 12 |
| Using phone while driving | 800 | 4 |
| Not wearing seatbelt | 400 | 4 |
| Dangerous driving | 2,000 | 23 |

**Paying fines:** Dubai Police app, Dubai Police website, or RTA Customer Happiness Centres. Pay within 60 days; overdue fines attract additional charges.

**Black point suspension thresholds:** 24 points in 12 months triggers a 3-month suspension review. Accumulating 12+ points in a short window may trigger a mandatory awareness course.

**Vehicle impound:** Certain offences trigger immediate impoundment. Retrieval costs AED 500–1,500 plus daily storage fees.

## Vehicle registration in Dubai

After licence issuance, if you are buying or leasing a vehicle:

| Step | Authority |
| --- | --- |
| Insurance policy (comprehensive or third-party) | Insurer — must be active before registration |
| Vehicle inspection (used cars and annual renewal) | RTA-approved testing centres |
| Registration at RTA | Al Barsha, Umm Ramool, or online |
| Salik tag registration | salik.ae |
| Plates issued | On registration day or shortly after |

**Annual renewal:** Registration expires every 12 months. Online renewal possible if vehicle passes technical inspection and no outstanding fines.

**Car insurance indicative costs (2026):**

| Profile | Comprehensive AED/year |
| --- | --- |
| Sedan under 3 years old, clean record | 2,500–4,200 |
| SUV, clean record | 3,500–6,000 |
| New driver, no UAE history | 5,000–9,500 |
| Sports or luxury vehicle | 6,000–18,000+ |

Third-party insurance is cheaper but most lenders and lease companies require full comprehensive cover.

## Family mobility — two licences, two cars

Suburbs popular with families (Arabian Ranches, Mirdif, Springs, Jumeirah Park) require two-car households in practice. School runs often go in opposite directions to work commutes.

| Driver | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Primary earner | Convert or enrol week one of Emirates ID issuance |
| Trailing spouse | Parallel booking — do not wait for primary to finish |
| Au pair or live-in nanny | Cannot drive family car on visitor visa — own UAE licence required if driving children |

**Child car seats:** Dubai Police enforcement has increased. Ensure seat fits vehicle and matches child age/weight — rear-facing for under 13 kg, forward-facing up to 36 kg, booster for older children. Fine up to AED 400 plus black points.

## FAQ

**How do I get a driving licence in Dubai as an expat?**
Convert your foreign licence at an RTA Customer Happiness Centre if your country is on the approved list (AED 220–550 plus eye test). Otherwise enrol at an RTA-approved driving school for AED 3,500–8,000.

**Is the UK driving licence valid in Dubai?**
Yes — UK licence holders can directly convert at RTA without a practical test, subject to current list status. Always verify on rta.ae before booking.

**How much is car insurance in Dubai?**
Comprehensive insurance for a standard sedan with clean history runs AED 2,500–4,200 per year. New arrivals with no UAE driving history pay more. Compare at least three insurers.

**Can I drive in Abu Dhabi with a Dubai licence?**
Yes — UAE licences are federal documents valid across all emirates. Update your traffic file address when you change emirate of residence.

**What is the Salik fine for negative balance?**
AED 50 administrative charge per incident of driving through a gate with insufficient balance, plus the AED 4 toll. Monitor balance via the Salik app.

**How do I pay Dubai traffic fines?**
Dubai Police app, police.gov.ae, or RTA Customer Happiness Centres. Early payment discounts sometimes apply. Never ignore a fine — it blocks vehicle registration renewal.

**Do I need a car in Dubai?**
Depends on location. Central areas (Downtown, Marina, JLT, DIFC) are metro-accessible. Suburban family communities require a car — often two per household.

**How long is a Dubai driving licence valid?**
Linked to your UAE residency visa validity. Upon visa renewal, renew the licence through RTA.

**Can a woman drive in Dubai?**
Yes — no restrictions. Same licence process and rules apply regardless of gender.

**What is the eye test cost in Dubai?**
AED 100–200 at RTA-approved optical centres. Certificate valid for 30 days from issue date.
