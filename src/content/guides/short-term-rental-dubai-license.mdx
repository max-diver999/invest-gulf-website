Dubai's short-term rental market runs on a clear legal framework. The Department of Economy and Tourism (DET), formerly DTCM, regulates all holiday home operations under a permit system that has been in place since 2015 and has become progressively more enforced since 2022. As of 2025, approximately 91% of active Airbnb and Booking.com listings in Dubai carry a valid DET permit number.

## Who regulates Dubai short-term rentals?

DET (Department of Economy and Tourism) licenses all Dubai stays under 12 months as holiday homes, and there is no informal Airbnb grey zone in the market. Permits, Civil Defence checks, and guest registration are mandatory. About 91% of active listings already show permit numbers. Invest Gulf treats unlicensed operation as a fine risk of AED 5,000 to AED 200,000, not a soft compliance gap.

Insider tip: Request the OA community rules in writing before you reserve a unit for Airbnb underwriting, because a DET permit never overrides a building ban.

| Regulator / actor | Role |
| --- | --- |
| DET | Permit, Tourism Dirham, guest data |
| Civil Defence | Fire safety certificate |
| Building OA | May ban or restrict STR |
| Platforms | Require permit number on listing |

## How do you get a Dubai Holiday Home Permit?

A Dubai Holiday Home Permit typically costs AED 1,520 per year for apartments and studios or AED 3,570 for villas and townhouses, with late renewal adding AED 500. Applications via the DET portal usually take 3 to 7 working days when title, ID, floor plan, Civil Defence, and any OA letter are complete. Invest Gulf models unit-level fire kit spend at AED 200 to 500 for the area.

| Requirement | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| Title deed / Oqood | Owner proof |
| Emirates ID / passport | Owner or authorised rep |
| Floor plan | Unit-specific |
| Civil Defence certificate | Smoke, extinguisher, notice |
| OA NOC if required | Building-specific |

## What is the Tourism Dirham?

The Tourism Dirham is a per-night guest levy of about AED 15 per bedroom for classified holiday homes, plus a Municipality Fee of 7% of rental revenue that hosts must collect and remit on a monthly cycle. Airbnb remits for many qualifying properties; direct bookings usually mean owner remittance. Our analysis treats missed remittance as permit-suspension risk in the market, often AED thousands in arrears before suspension.

| Collection method | Who remits to DET |
| --- | --- |
| Airbnb (qualifying) | Platform |
| Booking.com | Confirm per listing |
| Direct bookings | Owner / manager |

## How does guest registration work?

Guest registration requires name, nationality, and ID details in the DET system within hours of check-in, and failure is a compliance violation that can stack with unlicensed fines from AED 5,000 upward during audits. Unregistered guests create liability for the permit holder. Invest Gulf treats same-day registration as a non-negotiable SOP for managers in the area.

| Step | Deadline | Owner risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capture guest ID | At check-in | Incomplete file |
| Submit to DET | Within 3 hours | Fine on audit |
| Keep logs | Ongoing | Permit review |

## Can your building ban short-term rentals?

Owners Associations can restrict or ban short-term rentals even when you hold a valid DET permit, and roughly 20% to 30% of buildings in major investment communities already do. Violating OA rules can mean building fines and prohibition notices. Our analysis puts OA rule collection before SPA if the purchase thesis is Airbnb in the area.

| Check before buy | Pass signal |
| --- | --- |
| OA rules obtained | Written STR allowed |
| Seller cannot produce rules | Contact management |
| STR prohibited | Walk or reprice to long-let |

Permit numbers must appear on Airbnb and Booking.com listings because platforms de-list units without them, and about 91% of active Dubai inventory already complies. Operating without a visible permit is a choice to sit outside the formal market. Invest Gulf treats missing permit fields as an immediate underwriting fail for the area.

## When is STR economically worth it in Dubai?

Dubai Marina, JBR, and Downtown STR with professional management typically needs 75% to 85% occupancy and rates that clear management fees of 15% to 20% of revenue after DET, Tourism Dirham, cleaning, and wear. Gross STR can run 30% to 50% above long-term rent, yet net often loses below 60% occupancy. Invest Gulf models location tier before furniture spend in the market.

| Premium (Marina, Downtown, Palm) | AED 450-850 | ~60% break-even |
| Mid-market (Business Bay, JLT) | AED 280-450 | ~70% |
| Secondary | AED 200-350 | ~80%+ |

STR beats long-term rent when occupancy stays above about 70% to 75% in tourist-strong pins and nightly rates clear 15% to 20% management plus AED 1,520 permit and cleaning stacks. Below 60% occupancy, net STR often underperforms a clean 12-month lease. Our analysis of Marina 1BR scenarios shows operational costs can consume the entire gross premium in the area.

## What penalties apply for unlicensed STR?

Unlicensed short-term rental in Dubai risks fines from AED 5,000 on a first offence up to AED 100,000 to 200,000 for repeat violations, and DET has tightened audits since 2022 in high-density zones. Permit cost is trivial beside that fine stack. Invest Gulf treats non-compliance as economically irrational for the market once enforcement is priced in.

## What is the step-by-step licensing path?

The licensing path typically starts with OA confirmation, then Civil Defence readiness, DET portal filing, and permit issuance in about 3 to 7 working days before listings show the permit number. Annual renewal at AED 1,520 or AED 3,570 keeps the unit legal. Our analysis sequences OA first so buyers do not spend AED 20,000-plus furnishing a banned building in the area.

1. Obtain OA rules and NOC if required
2. Fit smoke detectors, extinguisher, evacuation notice (AED 200-500 typical)
3. File DET application with title and ID
4. Display permit on every listing
5. Register guests within 3 hours; remit Tourism Dirham if platform does not

## What ongoing compliance is required?

Ongoing compliance means annual DET renewal, monthly Tourism Dirham remittance when platforms do not collect, guest registration within 3 hours, and insurance that covers short-term commercial use at roughly AED 1,500 to 3,000 extra per year. Corporate Tax may apply if UAE revenue exceeds AED 375,000 across activities. Invest Gulf budgets 15% to 25% of revenue for accelerated wear on STR units in the market.
