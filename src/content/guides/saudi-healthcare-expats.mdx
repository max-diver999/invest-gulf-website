## What is CCHI insurance for Saudi expats?

CCHI insurance is the Council of Health Insurance framework that requires private-sector employers to cover workers and sponsored dependants at regulated class minimums. Class C is the typical baseline, while Class A packages reduce co-pays and widen hospital networks for senior hires. Maternity waits often run 10 to 12 months.

| Class (label varies) | Inpatient | Outpatient | Maternity | Dental | Network breadth |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Class C (minimum) | Covered with limits | GP; caps | Often excluded/waiting | Excluded | Basic |
| Class B | Improved room | Specialist co-pay | Partial after wait | Optional | Wider |
| Class A / VIP | Private room | Low co-pay | Covered after wait | Partial | Premium hospitals |

- Confirm spouse and children sit on the same class as the employee
- Keep Arabic and English benefit schedules in one folder for the full 12 months
- Diary renewal dates against iqama renewals within 30 days of expiry
- Run a benefits checklist before you sign the offer

## How do employer insurance tiers differ in practice?

Employer insurance tiers typically differ by room class, specialist co-pay, maternity sub-limits, and whether dental or psychiatric outpatient care is included. A Class C plan can still force SAR 200 to 500 specialist friction when the network is thin. Upgrade decisions should follow the Schedule of Benefits checklist, not the HR welcome slide.

| Upgrade lever | Why it matters | Planning check |
|---|---|---|
| Network list | Direct billing vs 50-100% self-pay | Hospitals near home and school |
| Maternity wait | 10-12 months common | Join before pregnancy |
| NICU sub-limit | Days exhaust basic policies fast | Ask for written days and SAR caps |
| Dental / optical | Often excluded on Class C | Budget SAR 1,500-4,000/year |

Our clients compare three written schedules before accepting a 5 to 10 percent “cheaper” package that removes maternity.

## When should maternity waiting periods be confirmed?

Maternity planning for expat spouses typically requires written confirmation of the 10 to 12 month waiting period, neonatal ICU day caps, and in-network delivery hospitals before pregnancy. Enhanced Class A or Class B tiers often cover delivery after the wait, while Class C may exclude it. Keep NICU SAR sub-limits on your checklist.

- Ask for NICU sub-limit in SAR and covered days
- Confirm paediatrician network for the first 12 months
- Keep ultrasound pre-authorisation contacts for the full pregnancy term of about 9 months

Pre-existing waiting periods of 6 to 12 months remain common on basic classes. Bring limited supply only where rules allow, then transfer care locally for the next 12 months.

## What does private mental healthcare look like for expats?

Private mental healthcare for expats in Saudi Arabia is available mainly through insurer networks and cash-pay clinics, and policies typically cap psychiatric outpatient sessions each year. Availability has grown in Riyadh and Jeddah private sectors over the last 5 years, yet Class C plans may exclude cover. Ask for session caps and SAR co-pays in the first 30 days.

- Confirm annual session caps and SAR co-pays in writing
- Prefer clinics with English-speaking therapists if needed
- Keep employer EAP numbers separate from clinical cover for 12 months
- Add mental-health lines to the same benefits checklist as maternity

## How do dental and optical benefits usually work?

Dental and optical benefits on Saudi employer plans typically start excluded or capped on Class C, with partial cover appearing on Class B and Class A upgrades. Families often budget SAR 1,500 to 4,000 per year for routine dental if the plan is thin. LASIK remains self-pay on most tiers even after 12 months of employment.

| Benefit | Class C pattern | Enhanced pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Routine dental | Often excluded | Partial annual SAR cap |
| Optical | Often excluded | Frames/lenses sub-limit |
| Orthodontics | Self-pay | Rare partial after 6-12 months |

Use a simple checklist: quote, network dentist, annual cap, and kids’ orthodontic exclusion before school term.

## What emergency numbers and ER steps should you save?

Emergency care for expats in Saudi cities typically starts with 997 for ambulance support, while 911 operates as a unified number in many regions. Carry iqama and insurance card copies because ER registration still needs identity on arrival. Map the nearest in-network ER during the first 7 days and refresh every 12 months.

1. Call 997 (or 911 where unified) and state location
2. Show insurance card at triage within the first hour
3. Notify employer PRO or HR if admission lasts more than 24 hours
4. File pre-authorisation paperwork inside insurer SLAs, often 24 to 72 hours for follow-on care

Keep SAR 500 to 2,000 cash buffer for deposits some private ERs still request before insurer confirmation in the first 1 to 2 days.

## What red flags should pause a Saudi medical package?

Red flags on Saudi expat medical plans typically include networks that exclude the hospital next to your compound, maternity waits of 10 to 12 months that clash with arrival, and formularies that omit long-term drugs. Sales claims that property purchase creates automatic premium medical perks should be separated from CCHI employment cover. Keep policy PDFs for 12 months.

- Brochure network without your preferred hospital (50-100% elective risk)
- Maternity wait longer than your pregnancy timeline of about 9 months
- No written pre-authorisation SLA for planned surgery inside 14 days
- Cash requests for “faster CCHI registration” outside official fees

Our analysis treats missing Arabic schedules as a hard stop on the offer checklist.

## What first-30-days medical checklist should you run?

The first-30-days medical checklist for Saudi arrivals typically covers policy activation, dependant enrolment, nearest direct-billing GP, and chronic medication transfer inside the network. Book overdue dental or specialist reviews before work travel in week 3 or 4. Ask HR which temporary cover letter works for the first 7 to 14 days.

| Check | Detail to confirm | Stat to capture |
|---|---|---|

Saudi versus UAE healthcare for expats typically comes down to insurance class and network depth, not country branding alone.
