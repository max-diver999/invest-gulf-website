# US Citizens in Dubai: FATCA, FBAR and Tax Guide 2026

The United States taxes **citizens and green card holders on worldwide income**, no matter where they live. Dubai's zero personal income tax removes **UAE** tax on the same dirham, but it does **not** remove US filing, FBAR, or FATCA duties. This guide summarises the main US rules for expats who live in Dubai and may own UAE property or bank accounts. It is general information, not US tax advice.

## Why must US citizens still file from Dubai?

Citizenship-based taxation means your **US passport or green card** keeps you in the US tax net. A UAE residency visa, Emirates ID, or Dubai property deed does not replace Form 1040. The UAE not taxing your salary simply means you usually **cannot claim a Foreign Tax Credit** for UAE income tax you never paid.

| Filing (typical) | Due date | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Form 1040 | 15 April | Automatic extension to 15 June for overseas filers |
| Further extension | 15 October | File Form 4868 |
| FBAR (FinCEN 114) | 15 April | Auto extension to 15 October |
| Form 8938 | With 1040 | Asset thresholds apply |

**State taxes:** Some states (notably California and Virginia) may still treat you as resident until you formally break ties. Plan state exit alongside federal expat planning.

## How does the Foreign Earned Income Exclusion work in Dubai?

The FEIE (IRC Section 911) lets qualifying taxpayers exclude a **indexed amount** of foreign **earned** income. You must pass either the **Bona Fide Residence Test** (genuine foreign resident for a full tax year) or the **Physical Presence Test** (330 days outside the US in a 12-month period).

**Critical limit:** FEIE does **not** cover Dubai **rental income**, dividends, or capital gains. A Dubai salary can be largely excluded; a Dubai buy-to-let portfolio cannot.

| Income type | FEIE applies? | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dubai employment salary | Yes, if tests met | Up to annual exclusion cap |
| UAE freelance fees | Often yes | Watch self-employment tax |
| Dubai rental income | No | Taxable in full on US return |
| US dividends | No | US-source, fully taxable |
| Gain on selling Dubai property | No | US CGT applies |
| US Social Security | No | Partially taxable under US rules |

## Do you need FBAR for UAE bank accounts?

Yes, if you are a US person and the **aggregate maximum value** of all foreign financial accounts exceeded **USD 10,000** at any time in the calendar year. UAE current accounts, savings, and brokerage accounts count. So can accounts where you have **signature authority** (for example a UAE company account you control).

File electronically at **bsaefiling.fincen.treas.gov**, separate from the IRS. List each account and its **peak balance** during the year. Non-wilful penalties are material; wilful failures are far worse. If you have years of non-filing, speak to a US tax attorney before submitting late forms.

CRS reporting at UAE banks is a **separate** regime from FBAR. See [CRS and FATCA for UAE bank accounts](/guides/uae-crs-fatca-banking/).

## How is Form 8938 different from FBAR?

Form 8938 attaches to your **1040** and reports **specified foreign financial assets** above higher thresholds (for example USD 200,000 year-end / USD 300,000 any time for single filers abroad; joint filers have higher limits).

| Requirement | FBAR | Form 8938 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Agency | FinCEN | IRS |
| Threshold | USD 10,000 aggregate | Higher asset tests |
| Typical UAE account | Often yes | Sometimes yes |
| UAE company interests | If account-related | Often yes |

You may owe **both** for the same account. A USD 150,000 UAE balance usually triggers FBAR; Form 8938 depends on your total foreign asset picture.

## How is Dubai property taxed on your US return?

Report rental income on Schedule E (or appropriate forms). Convert AED to USD consistently (annual average or transaction spot rates). Claim depreciation on the building component where allowed; keep schedules from year one.

### Capital gains when you sell

Gain equals net proceeds minus **adjusted basis** (purchase price plus improvements minus depreciation taken). Long-term rates (0%, 15%, or 20%) apply if held over one year, plus **3.8% NIIT** when income exceeds thresholds. The UAE does not tax the gain, so there is typically **no foreign tax credit**.

| Holding period | US treatment (indicative) |
| --- | --- |
| Under 1 year | Ordinary income rates |
| Over 1 year | Long-term capital gains rates |
| Depreciation claimed | Possible recapture at higher rates |

## What are PFIC rules for UAE funds?

Many UAE mutual funds and non-US pooled investments are **Passive Foreign Investment Companies (PFICs)** under US rules. PFIC taxation is punitive unless you make timely elections. US citizens buying local funds or certain insurance wrappers should get PFIC analysis **before** investing, not at sale.

## How should US citizens open UAE bank accounts?

Banks will ask for **US person** status (Form W-9 or W-8). Expect FATCA reporting to the IRS if you are a US citizen. Choose banks used to US clients where possible. Process walkthrough: [opening a bank account in Dubai](/guides/open-bank-account-dubai/).

## Is there a US-UAE tax treaty?

There is **no comprehensive income tax treaty** between the US and UAE as of publication. You cannot rely on treaty relief to exempt Dubai wages or rent. Use FEIE, FTC (where foreign tax was actually paid elsewhere), and compliant reporting instead.

## What extra forms apply if you use a UAE company?

- **Form 5471** if you own 10%+ of a foreign corporation (including many UAE LLCs).
- **Form 8832** if you made a check-the-box election for entity classification.
- **FBAR** on company accounts you control or sign for.

Property via a UAE company: [buy property through a UAE company](/guides/buy-property-through-uae-company/). A [Golden Visa via AED 2M property](/guides/golden-visa-mortgage-property-uae/) helps UAE immigration only; it does not change US tax.

## What planning steps reduce surprises?

- Calendar **1040, FBAR, and 8938** deadlines every January.
- Track **330-day** or bona fide residence tests if you rely on FEIE.
- Keep **USD basis records** for Dubai property from purchase.
- Align UAE bank **CRS/FATCA forms** with what you file in the US.
- Use a CPA with **expat and property** experience, not only domestic US practice.

## What if you have never filed FBAR while in Dubai?

Many US expats discover FBAR only after several UAE years. Do not ignore prior years. A CPA or tax attorney can recommend **streamlined** or other compliance paths based on your facts. Filing current-year returns without addressing past FBAR gaps leaves penalty exposure open.
