## Fujairah yield market structure

Fujairah does not share the west coast Dubai employment gravity that drives Ajman and Sharjah commuter demand. Rental demand is anchored in port operations, bunkering, logistics yards, free-zone trading firms, municipal employment, and a growing but still modest tourism base. That local anchor cuts churn from cross-emirate job moves but caps absolute tenant depth.

| Segment | Gross yield | Entry (AED, 1-bed) | Risk profile |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sakamkam (inland) | 7.5-10% | 350-550K | Older stock, port-linked tenants |
| Merashid corridor | 7-9% | 320-520K | Mixed age, logistics staff |
| Town centre / older towers | 7-8.5% | 300-480K | Building quality varies |
| Dibba border (Fujairah side) | 7-9% | 280-450K | Industrial adjacency, inspect noise |
| Beach / newer waterfront | 6-7.5% | 450-750K | Lifestyle premium, lower gross |
| Villas (select freehold) | 6-8% | 800K-2M | Lower turnover, thinner resale |

Insight: Fujairah often matches or beats Dubai gross percentages because prices lag rents, not because absolute rents exceed Dubai. A one-bed that rents for AED 32K-42K/year against a AED 400K-520K ticket produces the 7-10% band investors quote in broker decks.

## Area-by-area yield analysis

### Sakamkam

Sakamkam is the default yield hunt zone for foreign buyers: established apartment stock, proximity to town services, and tenant pools linked to port shifts, logistics offices, and government departments. Gross bands run highest on studios and compact one-beds where AED per sqft is lowest.

| Unit | Rent (AED/yr) | Price (AED) | Gross |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Studio | 22-30K | 250-380K | 7.5-10% |
| 1-bed | 30-42K | 350-520K | 7-9.5% |
| 2-bed | 38-52K | 500-720K | 6.5-8.5% |

Net example: AED 480,000 purchase, AED 38,000 rent, AED 9,200 costs (service charge, management, one month void) yields AED 28,800 net, about 6.0% net. Push void to two months in a poorly managed tower and net can fall toward 5.2%.

Risks: Older towers without documented maintenance reserves, parking shortages, and illegal partitioning that triggers municipality action and forced vacancy.

### Merashid corridor

Merashid offers similar yield math with slightly more industrial adjacency. Logistics firms, quarry contractors, and free-zone back-office staff rent here for affordability versus beach premiums. Yields of 7-9% gross are common on 2010-era mid-rises when fit-out is functional rather than premium.

| Factor | Sakamkam | Merashid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gross yield | 7.5-10% | 7-9% |
| Tenant mix | Port, gov, retail | Logistics, industrial |
| Building age | Mixed | Often 2005-2015 |
| Resale depth | Slightly better | Similar thin |
| Noise / truck routes | Lower | Verify location |

Choose Merashid when price per sqft undercuts Sakamkam by 8% or more on comparable size; choose Sakamkam when resale to end-users matters because name recognition is stronger among local agents.

### Dibba border districts

The Fujairah side of the Dibba corridor attracts tenants tied to cross-border trade, fishing industry support services, and industrial suppliers serving Oman-facing routes. Gross yields can touch 7.5-9% on small units priced AED 280K-450K. Liquidity is thinner than Sakamkam and tenant credit checks matter more: verify employer stability before accepting a single corporate lease as 100% of income.

Underwrite conservatively: model 5-6 weeks void, inspect truck traffic and dust exposure, and confirm freehold status project by project. Border micro-locations are not interchangeable with town-centre Sakamkam on exit timelines.

### Beach and waterfront stock

East coast beach access supports weekend demand from UAE residents, but gross yields typically compress to 6-7.5% because entry prices carry a lifestyle premium. See [Fujairah beach property investment](/guides/fujairah-beach-property-investment/) for STR and holiday-home angles. Pure yield investors often prefer inland Sakamkam unless they buy at a discount to portal asking prices.

## Port and logistics tenant profile

Port of Fujairah is among the world's largest bunkering hubs. That creates shift-worker housing demand, contractor rotations, and mid-level staff who prefer apartments over company camps. Free-zone trading and warehousing firms add white-collar and supervisory tenants who sign 12-month leases with quarterly cheques.

| Tenant type | Typical lease | Yield impact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Port operations staff | 12 months | Stable, price-sensitive |
| Logistics / warehouse supervisors | 12 months | Moderate turnover |
| Government / municipality | 12 months | Lower void |
| Industrial contractors | 6-12 months | Higher void risk |
| UAE weekend residents (beach) | Seasonal / 12 mo | Variable by tower |

Lease norms: predominantly 12 months, cheques 1-4 per year, one-month deposit common. Unlike Dubai, Ejari depth is shallow. Pull rent evidence from closed broker deals in the same building, not portal asking prices alone.

Citability block (June 2026): Fujairah buy-to-let underwriting should assume a locally employed tenant, not a Dubai commuter saving on rent. Median one-bedroom rent in Sakamkam runs roughly AED 30,000-42,000 per year against purchase prices of AED 350,000-520,000, producing 7-9% gross before fees. Port-linked employers include bunkering firms, stevedoring contractors, and free-zone logistics operators within a 20-minute drive. Vacancy of four to six weeks per year is a prudent base case; two months is appropriate for border industrial stock or towers with deferred maintenance. Net yield after 5-8% management, AED 5-12/sqft service charges, and void typically lands at 5.5-7.5% on mid-market apartments.

Rule: Fujairah gross above 8% often nets 6-7% when charges stay moderate. Gross above 10% without inspection usually signals deferred capex, illegal splits, or inflated rent comps.

## Freehold verification (mandatory)

Foreign ownership exists only in designated freehold projects, not emirate-wide. Sakamkam and Merashid include eligible towers, but title must be verified per unit. Read the [Fujairah property investment guide](/guides/fujairah-property-investment-guide/) freehold section before offer.

| Check | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| SPA freehold clause | Avoid leasehold mis-sell |
| Municipality registration | Legal lease enforcement |
| Seller NOC timing | Exit discount if delayed |
| Building compliance | Partition fines hit yield |

## Liquidity and exit planning

Fujairah's secondary market is the primary risk for yield investors who need capital back within 24 months. Annual transaction volumes are a fraction of Ajman, let alone Dubai. Discounts of 5-10% to achieve faster sale are common. Price the yield premium only if your hold horizon is five years or longer.

| Hold horizon | Realistic exit |
| --- | --- |
| under 24 months | High discount risk |
| 3-5 years | Moderate discount |
| 5-7 years | Yield carried return |
| 7+ years | Appreciation optional |

Compare northern alternatives: [RAK property investment guide](/guides/ras-al-khaimah-property-investment-guide/) offers stronger tourism branding and deeper resale than Fujairah with somewhat higher entry tickets.

## Risks and common mistakes

1. Buying beach stock for peak yield: gross often below 7%.
2. Ignoring port/industrial noise in Dibba border units: tenant churn erodes net.
3. Assuming Dubai commute demand: it does not exist at scale in Fujairah.
4. Gross-only underwriting: net disappointment when void runs two months.
5. Skipping freehold verification: title errors freeze exit.
6. Single-tenant dependency: one logistics firm equals 100% income risk.
7. Comparing Fujairah to Dubai Marina liquidity: invalid benchmark.

## Decision framework

Choose Fujairah rental yield if:

- You want UAE east coast exposure with 7%+ gross on sub-AED 550K tickets
- Hold horizon is 5+ years
- You accept 90-180 day resale marketing
- Tenant underwriting focuses on local employers, not Dubai jobs

Choose Ajman instead if:

- Dubai commuter tenant depth is your occupancy backstop
- You want slightly faster Northern Emirates resale
- You prefer maximum gross bands on border stock

Choose Dubai instead if:

- Resale within 12 months is possible
- You need Ejari-backed rent comps
- Golden Visa qualifying stock matters

## Service charges and building age

| Building era | Gross | Capex risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1990s-2000s low-rise | 8-10% | High |
| 2005-2015 mid-rise | 7-9% | Medium |
| 2018+ branded | 6-8% | Lower |

Buildings older than 10 years may see maintenance levies rise 10-15% per renewal cycle. Request two years of building invoices during due diligence.

## Registration and compliance

Fujairah tenancy registration follows emirate-specific processes, not Dubai Ejari. Incorrect contracts weaken dispute pathways and extend void after tenant exit. Use a Fujairah-licensed property manager where possible; Dubai-only managers sometimes mishandle local registration.

## Monitoring yield assumptions

Re-run rent comps quarterly using closed deals in the same tower. Port activity cycles and free-zone company relocations can shift tenant depth without press coverage. If new supply hands over in the same quarter in Sakamkam, underwrite five to six weeks void, not four.

Track FEWA tariff changes and cooling arrangements: chiller-free or included cooling lets you charge slightly higher rent versus units where tenants pay heavy summer bills.

## Gross-to-net worked example (Sakamkam studio)

Take a AED 320,000 studio in Sakamkam with AED 2,400/month rent (AED 28,800/year gross, 9.0% gross). Deduct AED 2,400/year service charge on 550 sqft at AED 4/sqft, AED 2,300 management at 8%, and five weeks void (~AED 2,770). Net rent near AED 21,330/year on AED 320,000 lands around 6.7% net before finance. Acquisition stack is lighter than Dubai, which is why Fujairah competes on net even when gross looks similar to JVC.

Stress-test at minus 10% rent and plus one month void: net can compress toward 5.0%. Pair this guide with [Fujairah property investment guide](/guides/fujairah-property-investment-guide/) and [Dubai rental yield guide](/guides/dubai-rental-yield-guide/) before you commit capital.

## Fujairah Rental Yield Guide: underwriting checklist

- Underwrite net yield after management, service charges, municipality fees, and 4-6 weeks void (6-8 weeks for Dibba border).
- Stress-test financed deals at plus 1% mortgage rate and minus 10% rent before relying on brochure gross yield.
- Pull real service charge history for the building, not developer projections alone.
- Compare liquidity and exit timeline against hold period; gross yield is not the full story.
- Keep 6-12 months of carry costs in AED before you close on a leveraged purchase.
- Verify freehold status and tenant employer stability for port-linked leases.

## Tenant demand drivers (port and logistics)

Bunkering operations, stevedoring, warehouse supervision, and free-zone trading firms underpin mid-market tenancy. Towers with dedicated parking and working elevators achieve 5-10% rent premia versus walk-up stock. Seasonal vacancy can rise when multiple towers hand over in the same quarter, so avoid underwriting a single quarter's asking rent spike as permanent.

Citability block (June 2026): Invest Gulf models Fujairah yield deals by employer type, not portal averages. Port shift workers typically rent studios and one-beds within 15-25 minutes of the port gate. Logistics supervisors accept Merashid and Sakamkam when beach premia are not employer-subsidised. Government tenants offer lower void but rarely pay top-of-market rent. A balanced underwriting uses 7.5% gross as a base, 6.5% net after fees, and 90-day exit marketing if you must sell in year three.

## Holding period and exit planning

Most Fujairah yield investors underwrite a five to seven year hold. Buyer pool depth is smaller than Ajman and far smaller than Dubai, so price discovery on resale can take three to six months longer in secondary towers. Price your exit assuming one tenant void in the final year unless you sell vacant. Document service charge history and NOC timing before listing.

## Quick recap for Fujairah yield buyers

Anchor underwriting on net figures after management, maintenance, and void. Favour Sakamkam when you need the deepest local resale name recognition. Keep AED liquidity for deposits and fit-out gaps. Compare every Fujairah ticket against [Ajman rental yield guide](/guides/ajman-rental-yield-guide/) and [Dubai rental yield guide](/guides/dubai-rental-yield-guide/) alternatives on net and exit weeks, not gross alone. Request tower-level tenant mix data before you finalise an offer. Inspect parking deeds and cooling contracts during viewings because they drive churn more than small rent discounts.

### Key numbers to track

A realistic yield model for Fujairah accounts for service charges running AED 5-12 per square foot per year, void periods averaging 4-6 weeks between tenancies, and agency fees of 2-5% of annual rent. Net yield after these deductions typically sits 1.5-2.5 percentage points below the gross headline figure. Buildings older than 8-10 years may see maintenance levies rise 10-15% per renewal cycle, so factor age into long-term projections.

### Stress-testing your yield assumptions

Run three scenarios before committing capital in Fujairah: (1) base case with current asking rents and 5-week void, (2) downside with rents 10% below asking and 8-week void, (3) upside with 5% rent growth and 3-week void. Factor in 5% agency commission on each new tenancy, landlord insurance at AED 500-1,500 per year, and a maintenance reserve of 2-3% of annual rent. If downside net falls below 5%, renegotiate price or walk.
