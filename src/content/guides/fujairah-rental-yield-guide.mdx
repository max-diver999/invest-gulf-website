Fujairah is the UAE's only east coast emirate: Indian Ocean coastline, Hajar Mountains, and employment anchored in the Port of Fujairah, logistics free zones, and local government rather than Dubai commuter flows. For yield investors, low AED per sqft against achievable local rents creates headline gross returns of 7-10% on apartments, with the trade-off that secondary liquidity is among the thinnest in the country.

This guide uses the same framework as our [Dubai rental yield hub](/guides/dubai-rental-yield-guide/): area tables, gross-to-net math, tenant analysis, Northern Emirates comparison, and exit risks. **Insider tip:** Prefer inland Sakamkam or Merashid units with two closed leases in the same tower over beach stock marketed at Dubai-like premiums, because Fujairah yield is an east coast employment story, not a lifestyle flip.

## What rental yields does Fujairah offer in 2026?

Fujairah typically delivers about 7 to 10% gross on mid-market Sakamkam and Merashid apartments, with newer waterfront towers nearer 6 to 8% and net yields of 5.5 to 8% when void stays near one month. We treat the area as a port-linked yield satellite, not a Dubai commute play, on tickets near AED 250K to 800K.

Yield checklist:
- Mid-market inland: 7 to 10% gross
- Newer waterfront: 6 to 8% gross
- Net after fees: 5.5 to 8%

Insight: Fujairah often matches or beats Dubai gross percentages because prices lag rents, not because absolute rents exceed Dubai. A one-bed that rents for AED 32K to 42K/year against AED 400K to 520K ticket produces the 7 to 10% band investors quote in broker decks.

## Which Fujairah areas deliver the best yields?

Sakamkam typically leads Fujairah gross at about 7.5 to 10% on studios and one-beds priced AED 250K to 550K, Merashid runs nearer 7 to 9%, and Dibba border stock can touch 7.5 to 9% on logistics tenants. Invest Gulf's worked net on AED 480,000 with AED 38,000 rent lands near 6.0% after about AED 9,200 costs.

Area checklist:
- Sakamkam: peak gross, stronger agent name recognition
- Merashid: industrial adjacency, verify truck routes
- Dibba border: logistics tenants, longer void base case

### Sakamkam

Core yield corridor for studios and one-beds. Port staff, government employees, and retail workers dominate tenancy. Gross 7.5 to 10% on AED 250K to 550K tickets when fit-out is functional.

### Merashid corridor

Similar yield math with more industrial adjacency. Logistics firms, quarry contractors, and free-zone back-office staff rent here for affordability versus beach premiums. Yields of 7 to 9% gross are common on 2010-era mid-rises.

| Factor | Sakamkam | Merashid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gross yield | 7.5-10% | 7-9% |
| Tenant mix | Port, gov, retail | Logistics, industrial |
| Building age | Mixed | Often 2005-2015 |
| Resale depth | Slightly better | Similar thin |
| Noise / truck routes | Lower | Verify location |

Choose Merashid when price per sqft undercuts Sakamkam by 8% or more on comparable size; choose Sakamkam when resale to end-users matters because name recognition is stronger among local agents.

### Dibba border districts

Cross-border trade, fishing support, and industrial suppliers drive tenancy. Gross yields can touch 7.5 to 9% on units priced AED 280K to 450K. Underwrite conservatively: model 5 to 6 weeks void, inspect truck traffic, confirm freehold status project by project.

### Beach and waterfront stock

Gross yields typically compress to 6 to 7.5% because entry prices carry a lifestyle premium. Pure yield investors often prefer inland Sakamkam unless they buy at a discount to portal asking prices.

Net example: AED 480,000 purchase, AED 38,000 rent, AED 9,200 costs (service charge, management, one month void) yields AED 28,800 net, about 6.0% net. Push void to two months in a poorly managed tower and net can fall toward 5.2%.

## Who are the port and logistics tenants?

Fujairah tenancy typically means Port of Fujairah staff, bunkering firms, stevedoring contractors, and free-zone logistics operators within a 20-minute drive on 12-month leases with 1 to 4 cheques. Our underwriting pulls rent evidence from closed broker deals in the same building, not portal asks alone, for the area.

Tenant checklist:
- Port and bunkering employers
- Free-zone logistics operators
- Government and industrial contractors

Fujairah buy-to-let underwriting should assume a locally employed tenant, not a Dubai commuter saving on rent. Median one-bedroom rent in Sakamkam runs roughly AED 30,000 to 42,000 per year against purchase prices of AED 350,000 to 520,000, producing 7 to 9% gross before fees. Vacancy of four to six weeks per year is a prudent base case; two months is appropriate for border industrial stock or towers with deferred maintenance. Net yield after 5 to 8% management, AED 5 to 12/sqft service charges, and void typically lands at 5.5 to 7.5% on mid-market apartments. Lease norms stay at 12 months with one to four cheques and a one-month deposit in most closed deals. Invest Gulf keeps Ejari-style depth assumptions out of Fujairah models because registration depth is shallow versus Dubai, so closed tower leases remain the evidence standard.

## How do Fujairah yields compare to Dubai and Ajman?

Fujairah typically matches or beats Dubai JVC on headline gross at about 7 to 10% because entry per sqft is lower, while Ajman posts similar 7 to 9% gross with deeper Dubai-commuter tenancy. Invest Gulf picks Fujairah for east coast diversification and Ajman when commute gravity matters more in the area stack.

| Market | Gross headline | Liquidity | Tenant depth |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fujairah Sakamkam | 7 to 10% | Thin (90 to 180 days) | Local employment |
| Dubai JVC | 6 to 8% | Deep | Ejari-backed |
| Ajman border | 7 to 9% | Moderate | Dubai commuters |

Compare checklist:
- Fujairah: local employment stability
- Ajman: Dubai border tenant depth
- Dubai: Ejari data and fastest exit

Fujairah versus Ajman versus Dubai is a portfolio design choice rather than a single winner. Fujairah suits investors who want 7 to 10% gross on AED 250K to 550K Sakamkam tickets backed by port and logistics payroll, with resale marketing of 90 to 180 days as the liquidity cost. Ajman often posts similar percentages with more Dubai-commuter depth, while Dubai JVC trades 6 to 8% gross for Ejari evidence and faster exits. Net after 5 to 8% management and AED 5 to 12/sqft service charges typically lands at 5.5 to 8% when void stays near four to six weeks. Beach-adjacent Fujairah stock compresses toward 6 to 7.5% gross because lifestyle pricing lifts entry tickets without matching rent. We consistently model Fujairah as a five-year-plus hold satellite beside Ajman or Dubai core positions, not as a twelve-month flip.

## What costs turn gross yield into net?

Fujairah gross-to-net modelling typically subtracts 5 to 8% management, AED 5 to 12/sqft service charges, 4 to 6 weeks void, and a 2 to 3% maintenance reserve before quoting pocket yield. We use the [how to calculate rental yield Dubai](/guides/how-to-calculate-rental-yield-dubai/) formulas with Fujairah inputs for the area.

| Cost line | Typical range |
| --- | --- |
| Property management | 5 to 8% of rent |
| Service charge | AED 5 to 12/sqft |
| Void | 4 to 6 weeks base case |
| Agent renewal | 5% common on new tenant |
| Maintenance reserve | 2 to 3% of annual rent |

Cost checklist:
- Gross above 8% often nets 6 to 7%
- Gross above 10% without inspection is a red flag
- Re-run at two months void

## What freehold checks are mandatory for foreign buyers?

Fujairah freehold typically requires project-by-project title verification because foreign ownership is narrower than Dubai, and a Sakamkam tower marketed as freehold may still fail Municipality registration before any 7 to 10% gross model is bankable. Our underwriting confirms SPA freehold language and Fujairah Municipality registration before modelling rent on AED 250K to 550K tickets in the area.

Freehold checklist:
- Freehold title on SPA matches marketing name
- Municipality registration confirmed within 30 days of SPA
- Foreign eligibility checked per project before deposit

## How thin is Fujairah resale?

Fujairah resale typically means marketing periods of 90 to 180 days, so the 7 to 10% gross premium only compensates on 5+ year holds with exit pricing set below last comps. We reject sub-24 month flip plans across the area yield book and prefer RAK when tourism branding matters more.

| Hold horizon | Realistic exit |
| --- | --- |
| under 24 months | High discount risk |
| 3-5 years | Moderate discount |
| 5-7 years | Yield carried return |
| 7+ years | Appreciation optional |

Liquidity checklist:
- Plan 90 to 180 day marketing
- Hold 5+ years as base case
- Compare RAK for deeper tourism resale

## What risks should you model?

Fujairah yield risks typically include older towers without documented maintenance reserves, illegal partitioning, FEWA summer spikes, and single-employer concentration in border stock that can push void past six weeks and cut a 7% gross story toward 5% net. Our research treats any gross more than 1.5 points above neighbouring towers as a red flag until three live leases prove rent.

Risk checklist:
- Maintenance reserve documentation for buildings older than 10 years
- Illegal partitioning near the unit
- FEWA summer bill history above AED 1,500/month
- Employer concentration in border stock

## When should you pick Fujairah over Ajman or Dubai?

Choose Fujairah typically when east coast port tenancy and 7 to 10% gross on AED 250K to 550K tickets matter more than Dubai Ejari depth, and you can hold 5+ years through 90 to 180 day exits. We fit Fujairah as a satellite beside Ajman or Dubai core positions in the area mandate.

Choose Fujairah if:
- East coast exposure fits your portfolio
- Tenants are locally employed, not Dubai commuters
- You accept 5+ year hold and thin resale

Choose Dubai instead if:
- Resale within 12 months is possible
- You need Ejari-backed rent comps
- Golden Visa qualifying stock matters

## What does a three-year Sakamkam cash flow look like?

A three-year Sakamkam model typically uses AED 450,000 purchase, AED 36,000 rent, and about AED 8,800 costs for roughly AED 27,200 net (6.0% net), with IRR improving on hold rather than flip. Our underwriting stress-tests at minus 10% rent and plus one month void, which can compress net toward 5.0% in the area.

Cash-flow checklist:
- Base net near 6.0% on mid-market tickets
- Treat appreciation as optional
- Stress void to two months

## What registration and compliance steps apply?

Fujairah tenancy registration typically runs through Fujairah Municipality and FEWA rules that differ from Dubai Ejari, so copying DET short-term assumptions can invalidate a 5.5 to 8% net model. We confirm landlord registration with the property manager before first move-in across the area file.

Compliance checklist:
- Municipality tenancy registration
- FEWA utility setup
- Emirate-specific STR rules only

## How should you stress-test yield assumptions?

Fujairah stress tests typically run three scenarios: current asks with 5-week void, rents 10% below ask with 8-week void, and 5% rent growth with 3-week void, plus 5% agency commission and landlord insurance of AED 500 to 1,500 per year. Our underwriting walks when downside net falls below 5% in the area.

Stress checklist:
- Base, downside, and upside void cases
- Maintenance reserve 2 to 3% of rent
- FEWA and cooling arrangement check

Track FEWA tariff changes and cooling arrangements: chiller-free or included cooling lets you charge slightly higher rent versus units where tenants pay heavy summer bills.

## What furnishing level maximises Fujairah yield?

Fujairah furnishing typically means functional beds, sofas, and appliances capped at 8 to 12 months of gross rent, because port tenants rarely pay Marina-grade finish premia on Sakamkam studios. We reject over-furnishing that raises ticket price without moving achievable rent.

Furnishing checklist:
- Cap fit-out at 8 to 12 months rent
- Target corporate lets only if leases exist
- Avoid Marina-spec spend on studios

## How do school and family demand affect yields?

Family demand near SPEA schools typically adds only a modest premium on 2BR units versus Dubai KHDA corridors, so investors should verify bus routes and employer housing allowances before underwriting rent above the AED 38K to 48K family band. Our analysis keeps school-driven yield assumptions thinner than Dubai family belts for the area.

Family checklist:
- Confirm school bus routes within 20 minutes
- Check employer housing allowances on the lease file
- Avoid Dubai-style school rent premia above 5%

## What monitoring should you run after purchase?

Post-purchase monitoring typically means reviewing achieved rent versus underwrite every 12 months, tracking void days, and treating portal listings as an upper bound rather than market mid. Invest Gulf reassesses exit early if two consecutive renewals require rent cuts, because Fujairah resale discounts can exceed one year of lost yield.

Monitoring checklist:
- Annual rent versus underwrite
- Void-day tracking
- Early exit review after two rent cuts

## Who should not buy Fujairah yield stock?

Skip Fujairah typically if you need Dubai Ejari depth for bank leverage, assume Sharjah-Dubai commuters as tenants, or must exit within 24 months against 90 to 180 day marketing norms. Our underwriting also skips border industrial micro-locations without an in-person truck-route and dust inspection in the area.

Skip checklist:
- Bank models that need Ejari depth
- Commuter-tenant strategies
- Sub-24 month exit requirements
