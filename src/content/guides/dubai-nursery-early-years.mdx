# Dubai Nursery Fees 2026: Complete KHDA Guide to Early Years Costs

**TL;DR:** Dubai nursery fees run **AED 18,000–55,000 per year** (AED 1,500–4,500/month). KHDA regulates all private nurseries and publishes ratings. Budget nurseries offer solid care at the lower end; Outstanding-rated EYFS and Montessori settings in premium areas command top rates. Plan 3–6 months ahead for popular spots, infant rooms fill especially fast.

**Related:** [Moving to Dubai with family](/guides/moving-to-dubai-with-family/) · [Dubai cost of living](/guides/dubai-cost-of-living-guide/) · [Dubai relocation guide](/guides/dubai-relocation-guide/)

> **Disclaimer:** Nursery fees change each academic year. Verify current rates directly with the nursery before committing.

---

## What parents need to know first

Dubai nurseries operate under KHDA (Knowledge and Human Development Authority) licensing, which means every private nursery is inspected, rated, and accountable to a regulatory framework, the same body that oversees [Dubai's private schools](/guides/british-schools-dubai/).

That oversight matters for two reasons. First, it gives parents a transparent quality signal rather than marketing claims. Second, KHDA inspection reports are publicly available, so you can read evaluators' exact comments on a nursery's safeguarding, teaching quality, and learning environment before ever visiting.

What KHDA does not do is cap nursery fees. Unlike school fees, which are linked to annual KHDA-set increase limits, nurseries price independently. This makes comparative research more important, and the range wider.

## Dubai nursery fee bands: 2025–26 benchmarks

Dubai nursery fees vary by area, rating, curriculum, and session type. The figures below reflect full-time (five days, full day) annual fees at the 2025–26 rates for each band.

| Band | Annual fee | Monthly equivalent | Typical profile |
|---|---|---|---|
| Budget | AED 18,000–26,000 | AED 1,500–2,200 | Smaller independent nurseries; solid care, basic facilities |
| Mid-range | AED 26,000–38,000 | AED 2,200–3,200 | KHDA-rated Good; EYFS framework; decent outdoor space |
| Premium | AED 38,000–55,000 | AED 3,200–4,600 | Outstanding-rated; international brand or specialist curriculum; purpose-built facilities |

**Registration and setup costs** add AED 1,000–2,500 as a one-time fee. Uniform and resource packs typically cost AED 500–1,200. Some nurseries also charge a refundable deposit of one month's fees.

**Part-time sessions** (three half-days or five mornings) reduce annual fees by 30–45% compared to full-time rates, typically ranging from AED 10,000–22,000 per year.

## How KHDA rates nurseries

KHDA inspects nurseries using four ratings: Outstanding, Good, Acceptable, and Unsatisfactory. The inspection framework evaluates:

- **Children's achievement**, progress in communication, physical development, literacy, and numeracy
- **Quality of learning and teaching**, lesson structure, adult interaction, how well staff extend children's thinking
- **Curriculum**, breadth, relevance, coverage of the chosen framework (EYFS, Montessori, IB PYP, etc.)
- **Health, safety, and well-being**, safeguarding, hygiene, indoor/outdoor environment quality
- **Leadership and management**, governance, staff qualifications, continuous improvement

Ratings are refreshed typically every two years. See the full explanation of how these frameworks work in the [KHDA school ratings guide](/guides/khda-school-ratings-explained/), the same core criteria apply to nurseries.

**Practical point:** An Outstanding nursery may charge 30–50% more than a Good-rated neighbour. The ratings reflect real quality differences, but a Good nursery with strong staff stability often outperforms an Outstanding nursery that has had leadership or staffing changes since inspection.

## Curriculum options in Dubai nurseries

### EYFS (Early Years Foundation Stage)

The dominant framework across Dubai, EYFS is the UK government's statutory curriculum for children aged 0–5. It covers seven areas of learning: communication and language, physical development, personal social and emotional development, literacy, mathematics, understanding the world, and expressive arts and design.

EYFS prepares children directly for British curriculum primary schools (Year 1), which makes it the default choice for families planning to use the [British school system](/guides/british-schools-dubai/) as children get older. The framework is play-based at ages 1–3 and becomes progressively more structured toward the FS2 year.

### Montessori

Around 40 Montessori nurseries operate in Dubai, ranging from authentic AMI/AMS-accredited settings to nurseries that use the brand loosely. True Montessori environments use mixed-age groupings (3–6 years typically), child-led activity cycles, and specialist materials. Montessori feeds well into IB PYP primary schools and some British schools with flexible Year 1 approaches.

Fees at accredited Montessori nurseries tend to sit in the mid-to-premium band: AED 30,000–50,000 full-time annually.

### IB PYP Early Years

A small number of nurseries offering IB Primary Years Programme early years programming have opened in Dubai in recent years, primarily as feeder settings to IB World Schools. Fees are typically in the premium band (AED 42,000–55,000).

### American/NAEYC-aligned

A handful of nurseries use a US developmental approach aligned to NAEYC (National Association for the Education of Young Children) standards. These suit families intending to move to American curriculum schools. Fees vary but sit in the mid-range (AED 28,000–42,000).

## Area-by-area nursery overview

Where you live in Dubai will largely determine which nurseries are practical. The nursery run, often at 7:15–8:00 AM through school-adjacent traffic, should not be longer than 15 minutes one way.

**Jumeirah / Umm Suqeim:** Highest concentration of premium and mid-range EYFS nurseries. Strong Outstanding-rated options; fees at the top of the market (AED 38,000–52,000). Good choice for families near Beach Road.

**Downtown Dubai / Business Bay:** Premium nurseries within towers or nearby; convenient for DIFC/Downtown professionals. Limited outdoor space in most buildings.

**Dubai Hills Estate / Al Barsha:** Strong cluster of mid-range and premium EYFS nurseries. Families in Dubai Hills often shortlist two or three nurseries within a 10-minute drive. Good for families planning to feed into [British primary schools](/guides/british-schools-dubai/) in this corridor.

**Arabian Ranches / Damac Hills / Reem:** Family community profile means high nursery density. Mix of budget-to-mid-range with a few premium options. Less traffic pressure than inner Dubai at drop-off.

**JVC / JVT / Sports City:** Budget and mid-range nurseries dominate. Good option for families prioritising value; Outstanding ratings achievable at AED 20,000–30,000 in this zone.

**Mirdif / Muhaisnah:** Affordable end of the market with solid KHDA-regulated nurseries. Well-suited for families in Mirdif villa communities.

**Dubai Marina / JBR:** Apartment-oriented areas; fewer nurseries than villa communities but growing supply as families move into Marina Shores and neighbouring towers.

## What's included in Dubai nursery fees: and what isn't

Understanding what the headline fee covers saves budget surprises at registration.

**Usually included:**
- Full-day childcare and learning sessions
- Meals and snacks (verify this, some nurseries are snacks-only)
- Core curriculum resources and learning materials
- Teacher-led activities and outings within the nursery

**Usually excluded:**
- Registration/admission fee (AED 1,000–2,500, one-time)
- Uniform (AED 300–800)
- Resource or activity packs (AED 200–600 per term)
- Field trips and external activities (AED 50–200 per trip)
- School bus if available (AED 600–1,200/month depending on distance)
- After-care / extended-day premium (AED 500–1,500/month above standard hours)

At a premium nursery running AED 45,000/year, all-in first-year costs including registration and supplies often reach AED 50,000–53,000. Budget an additional 10–15% above headline fees for the realistic total.

## How to choose a Dubai nursery: 6-step process

### Step 1: Fix your start date and your child's age band

Dubai nurseries use three broad room groups: Infant (45 days–18 months), Toddler (18 months–3 years), and Pre-school/FS1 (3–5 years). Knowing which room your child needs on start date immediately filters your options and wait-list timelines.

### Step 2: Set a fee ceiling

Before visiting a single nursery, decide your maximum monthly fee. Premium settings are genuinely good, but so are several Good-rated nurseries at AED 2,200–2,800/month. Fixing a budget prevents emotional upselling on the visit.

### Step 3: Draw a 15-minute radius

Open Google Maps, drop a pin on your home (or likely home area), and draw a 15-minute drive time at 7:30 AM. Only consider nurseries inside that radius. Outside it, daily logistics erode quickly.

### Step 4: Check KHDA ratings

Search the nursery name on khda.gov.ae or ask for the last inspection report. Look specifically at comments on *quality of teaching*, *children's achievement*, and *health and safety*. Ratings alone are not enough, read the inspector's narrative.

### Step 5: Visit and observe, not just tour

Schedule a visit during activity time, not between sessions. Watch how staff interact with children who are distressed, bored, or disruptive. The ratio of genuine conversation to crowd control tells you more than any brochure.

### Step 6: Ask the feeding-school question

Where do most children go at age 4–5? A nursery that feeds into consistently strong primary schools is a good cultural and academic signal. This is especially relevant if you're targeting [British curriculum primaries](/guides/british-schools-dubai/), nurseries embedded in the same community tend to have informal relationships with local school admissions teams.

## Registration and documentation checklist

Once you've chosen a nursery, the registration process in Dubai requires:

- **Passport copies** for child and sponsoring parent
- **Child's UAE visa / Emirates ID** (or proof of pending application)
- **Birth certificate**, attested and translated if not in English or Arabic
- **Vaccination record**, UAE mandatory immunisation schedule compliance
- **Medical fitness certificate**, from a MOHAP-registered clinic (some nurseries have a preferred clinic)
- **Emergency contact and medical consent forms** (provided by the nursery)
- **Signed fee agreement** and registration fee payment

If you're still in the residency visa process, most nurseries will provisionally hold a place against a receipt of visa application and request documentation within 30 days of joining.

## Employer allowances and cost-sharing

**Childcare allowances** appear in roughly 20–30% of senior expat packages in Dubai. They are rarely itemised in the initial offer but are frequently negotiated before joining. Common structures:

- Flat monthly allowance: AED 1,500–3,000/month
- School/nursery fee reimbursement up to a cap: AED 25,000–40,000/year
- Voucher schemes at employer-affiliated nurseries (particularly in DIFC and Dubai Internet City free zones)

If your employer does not list a childcare benefit, ask your HR contact or PRO whether a housing or family support allowance can be structured to include childcare. Many multinational employers have a flexible benefits framework that accommodates this.

## Transitioning from nursery to school

The standard transition path in Dubai:

| Age | Stage | Setting |
|---|---|---|
| 45 days–3 years | Infant/Toddler | KHDA nursery |
| 3–4 years | FS1 (Kindergarten 1) | Nursery or school's own early years |
| 4–5 years | FS2 (Kindergarten 2) | Nursery or school's own FS2 |
| 5–6 years | Year 1 | Private school |

most families move children at FS1 (age 3) into a primary school's own foundation stage unit rather than staying in a standalone nursery. This secures the school place a year early and aligns the child with peers who will continue through the same school.

Outstanding British curriculum schools often have their own FS1/FS2 units with EYFS-trained staff. These units charge full school fees, but entry at FS1 is significantly easier than mid-school entry and removes the Year 1 application pressure entirely.

Understanding how [KHDA rates schools vs nurseries](/guides/khda-school-ratings-explained/) helps frame which inspection signals to prioritise at each transition stage.

## Key questions to ask at a nursery visit

Before making a final choice, get direct answers to:

1. What is your staff-to-child ratio by age group? (KHDA minimum: 1:4 for infants, 1:6 for toddlers, 1:8 for pre-school)
2. What is your staff turnover rate over the last 12 months?
3. How do you handle settling-in and separation anxiety?
4. What does a typical daily schedule look like for a child in [your child's room]?
5. How do you communicate with parents: daily summary, app, in-person at pickup?
6. Where do most children go at age 4–5? Which schools do you have the strongest feed into?
7. What is your policy on sick children, and how do you handle minor medical incidents?
8. Is the registration fee refundable if we need to withdraw before the term starts?

These questions distinguish a nursery that can answer with specific, confident detail from one that relies on glossy marketing.

## Planning your Dubai move with young children

Nursery choice sits inside a larger family relocation decision. The sequence that saves the most rework:

1. **Identify your target area** based on commute and school pipeline.
2. **Shortlist nurseries** in that area and check wait-list availability.
3. **Sign a lease with Ejari**: most nurseries need this for enrolment documentation.
4. **Register with nursery** and process family visa in parallel.
5. **Review school pipeline**: know which primary schools your target nursery feeds and whether those schools align with your child's eventual curriculum path.
