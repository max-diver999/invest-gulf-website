## What do parents need to know first about Dubai nurseries?

KHDA inspects every licensed private nursery in Dubai and publishes Outstanding to Unsatisfactory ratings, so parents can compare safeguarding and teaching quality instead of marketing alone. Fees are not capped the way school fees are linked to KHDA increase limits. Our research treats KHDA PDFs as the first filter before any tour.

Parent-first checklist:
- Download the latest KHDA inspection PDF
- Compare fees only after rating and staffing comments
- Tour during live activity hours, not marketing slots

Dubai nursery fees for full-time enrolment typically range from AED 18,000 to AED 55,000 per year in 2026, with budget settings near AED 1,500 per month and premium EYFS or Montessori nurseries in Jumeirah or Dubai Hills charging about AED 3,500 to 4,500 per month. Registration fees of AED 1,000 to 2,500 plus uniform and resource packs of AED 500 to 1,500 commonly lift first-year cash needs by another 10 to 15% above headline tuition. Part-time sessions can cut annual fees by 30 to 45%, often landing between AED 10,000 and 22,000. Invest Gulf recommends downloading the latest KHDA inspection PDF before touring, because an Outstanding label that is two years old may not reflect current staffing stability. Ask what the fee covers beyond session time, since meals, transport and settling-in weeks are frequently billed separately and can add 15 to 25% to the advertised figure.

Insider tip: Tour between 9:00 and 11:00 on a weekday during free-flow activity, not the Sunday marketing slot, so you see real staff ratios rather than a staged circle time.

What KHDA does not do is cap nursery fees. Unlike school fees, which are linked to annual KHDA-set increase limits, nurseries price independently. Comparative research matters more because the fee range is wider.

## What Dubai nursery fee bands apply in 2026?

Dubai nursery fees typically range from AED 18,000 to AED 55,000 per year for full-time places in 2026, or about AED 1,500 to 4,500 per month. Premium EYFS or Montessori settings sit at the top of the band. Invest Gulf budgets registration and kits as extra first-year cash.

| Band | Annual fee | Monthly equivalent | Typical profile |
|---|---|---|---|
| Budget | AED 18,000 to 26,000 | AED 1,500 to 2,200 | Smaller independent nurseries; solid care, basic facilities |
| Mid-range | AED 26,000 to 38,000 | AED 2,200 to 3,200 | KHDA-rated Good; EYFS framework; decent outdoor space |
| Premium | AED 38,000 to 55,000 | AED 3,200 to 4,600 | Outstanding-rated; international brand or specialist curriculum; purpose-built facilities |

Registration and setup costs add AED 1,000 to 2,500 as a one-time fee. Uniform and resource packs typically cost AED 500 to 1,200. Some nurseries also charge a refundable deposit of one month's fees.

Part-time sessions (three half-days or five mornings) reduce annual fees by 30 to 45% compared to full-time rates, typically ranging from AED 10,000 to 22,000 per year.
## How does KHDA rate Dubai nurseries?

KHDA rates nurseries on learning quality, curriculum, safeguarding, and leadership, then assigns an Outstanding to Unsatisfactory band similar to schools. Inspection cycles typically refresh every two years on the public KHDA portal. We read the latest PDF date before treating an Outstanding label as current for fee premiums of 30 to 50%.

Rating checklist:
- Confirm inspection date is within two years
- Read safeguarding and staffing comments, not only the headline grade
- Compare fee premium versus a Good-rated neighbour

Inspectors focus on:

- Children's achievement, progress in communication, physical development, literacy, and numeracy
- Quality of learning and teaching, lesson structure, adult interaction, how well staff extend children's thinking
- Curriculum, breadth, relevance, coverage of the chosen framework (EYFS, Montessori, IB PYP, etc.)
- Health, safety, and well-being, safeguarding, hygiene, indoor/outdoor environment quality
- Leadership and management, governance, staff qualifications, continuous improvement

Ratings are refreshed typically every two years. See the full explanation of how these frameworks work in the [KHDA school ratings guide](/guides/khda-school-ratings-explained/), the same core criteria apply to nurseries.

Practical point: An Outstanding nursery may charge 30 to 50% more than a Good-rated neighbour. The ratings reflect real quality differences, but a Good nursery with strong staff stability often outperforms an Outstanding nursery that has had leadership or staffing changes since inspection.
## Which curriculum path fits: EYFS, Montessori, or other?

EYFS is the dominant early-years framework across Dubai nurseries and typically prepares children for British curriculum Year 1, while Montessori and IB PYP early years suit other primary paths. Accredited Montessori fees often sit at AED 30,000 to 50,000 annually. We match curriculum to the intended primary school.

Curriculum checklist:
- EYFS for British Year 1 pathways
- Montessori for IB PYP or flexible British entries
- Confirm AMI/AMS accreditation if paying Montessori premiums

EYFS is the UK government's statutory curriculum for children aged 0 to 5. It covers seven areas of learning: communication and language, physical development, personal social and emotional development, literacy, mathematics, understanding the world, and expressive arts and design.

EYFS prepares children directly for British curriculum primary schools (Year 1), which makes it the default choice for families planning to use the [British school system](/guides/gulf-schools-comparison/) as children get older. The framework is play-based at ages 1 to 3 and becomes progressively more structured toward the FS2 year.

### Montessori

Around 40 Montessori nurseries operate in Dubai, ranging from authentic AMI/AMS-accredited settings to nurseries that use the brand loosely. True Montessori environments use mixed-age groupings (3 to 6 years typically), child-led activity cycles, and specialist materials. Montessori feeds well into IB PYP primary schools and some British schools with flexible Year 1 approaches.

Fees at accredited Montessori nurseries tend to sit in the mid-to-premium band: AED 30,000 to 50,000 full-time annually.

### IB PYP Early Years

A small number of nurseries offering IB Primary Years Programme early years programming have opened in Dubai in recent years, primarily as feeder settings to IB World Schools. Fees are typically in the premium band (AED 42,000 to 55,000).

### American/NAEYC-aligned

A handful of nurseries use a US developmental approach aligned to NAEYC (National Association for the Education of Young Children) standards. These suit families intending to move to American curriculum schools. Fees vary but sit in the mid-range (AED 28,000 to 42,000).

## Which Dubai areas have the strongest nursery options?

Nursery options by Dubai area typically cluster premium EYFS stock in Jumeirah at AED 38,000 to 52,000, with value Outstanding options in JVC often at AED 20,000 to 30,000. Dubai Hills and Al Barsha sit in the mid-to-premium corridor. We shortlist by commute at morning peak, not brochure distance.

Area checklist:
- Jumeirah / Umm Suqeim for premium Outstanding density
- Dubai Hills / Al Barsha for mid-to-premium British feeders
- JVC / Sports City for value Outstanding bands

Jumeirah / Umm Suqeim: Highest concentration of premium and mid-range EYFS nurseries. Strong Outstanding-rated options; fees at the top of the market (AED 38,000 to 52,000). Good choice for families near Beach Road.

Downtown Dubai / Business Bay: Premium nurseries within towers or nearby; convenient for DIFC/Downtown professionals. Limited outdoor space in most buildings.

Dubai Hills Estate / Al Barsha: Strong cluster of mid-range and premium EYFS nurseries. Families in Dubai Hills often shortlist two or three nurseries within a 10-minute drive. Good for families planning to feed into [British primary schools](/guides/gulf-schools-comparison/) in this corridor.

Arabian Ranches / Damac Hills / Reem: Family community profile means high nursery density. Mix of budget-to-mid-range with a few premium options. Less traffic pressure than inner Dubai at drop-off.

JVC / JVT / Sports City: Budget and mid-range nurseries dominate. Good option for families prioritising value; Outstanding ratings achievable at AED 20,000 to 30,000 in this zone.

Mirdif / Muhaisnah: Affordable end of the market with solid KHDA-regulated nurseries. Well-suited for families in Mirdif villa communities.

Dubai Marina / JBR: Apartment-oriented areas; fewer nurseries than villa communities but growing supply as families move into Marina Shores and neighbouring towers.

- Optional bus and activity fees

## Which Dubai nursery fees evidence should exist before you commit?
Document checklists before signing a Dubai nursery place typically include passports, Emirates ID or visa proof, attested birth certificate, vaccination record, and a medical fitness certificate from a MOHAP-registered clinic. Many nurseries hold places 30 days against visa-in-progress proof. Invest Gulf gathers scans before the first tour.

KHDA inspects all private nurseries in Dubai and rates them Outstanding, Good, Acceptable, or Unsatisfactory on learning quality, curriculum, safeguarding, and leadership, with cycles typically every two years. Infant rooms often accept children from 45 days old, toddler rooms from about 18 months, and FS1 from age 3 to 3.5 depending on intake policy. Popular Outstanding nurseries in Jumeirah, Downtown, and Dubai Hills commonly need applications 3 to 6 months ahead, while infant places can wait 2 to 4 months year-round across the city. We also check whether employers offer AED 1,500 to 3,000 monthly childcare allowances, which are rarely advertised in offer letters but frequently granted when negotiated at the offer stage for senior expat packages at multinationals and government-adjacent employers in Dubai. Sibling discounts are usually per family rather than per child, so confirm how a second enrolment is treated.

- **Passport copies** for child and sponsoring parent
- **Child's UAE visa / Emirates ID** (or proof of pending application)
- **Birth certificate**, attested and translated if not in English or Arabic
- **Vaccination record**, UAE mandatory immunisation schedule compliance
- **Medical fitness certificate**, from a MOHAP-registered clinic (some nurseries have a preferred clinic)
- **Emergency contact and medical consent forms** (provided by the nursery)
- **Signed fee agreement** and registration fee payment

If you're still in the residency visa process, most nurseries will provisionally hold a place against a receipt of visa application and request documentation within 30 days of joining.

Employer childcare allowances in Dubai typically range from AED 1,500 to 3,000 per month on multinational packages, but the benefit is rarely listed in the offer letter. Ask during negotiation and confirm home-payroll tax treatment before relying on it. Our research treats allowance as negotiable, not automatic.

Allowance checklist:

## How does the move from nursery to primary school work?

Moving from nursery to primary school in Dubai typically happens at FS1 around age 3, when many families enter a school's own foundation unit rather than staying in a standalone nursery. FS2 follows at age 4 to 5, then Year 1 at 5 to 6. We prefer FS1 school entry when the target school has capacity.

Transition checklist:
- Confirm FS1 capacity at the target school
- Compare nursery vs school early-years fees
- Align curriculum continuity before leaving nursery

| Age | Stage | Setting |
|---|---|---|
| 45 days to 3 years | Infant/Toddler | KHDA nursery |
| 3 to 4 years | FS1 (Kindergarten 1) | Nursery or school's own early years |
| 4 to 5 years | FS2 (Kindergarten 2) | Nursery or school's own FS2 |
| 5 to 6 years | Year 1 | Private school |

most families move children at FS1 (age 3) into a primary school's own foundation stage unit rather than staying in a standalone nursery. This secures the school place a year early and aligns the child with peers who will continue through the same school.

Outstanding British curriculum schools often have their own FS1/FS2 units with EYFS-trained staff. These units charge full school fees, but entry at FS1 is significantly easier than mid-school entry and removes the Year 1 application pressure entirely.

Understanding how [KHDA rates schools vs nurseries](/guides/khda-school-ratings-explained/) helps frame which inspection signals to prioritise at each transition stage.

Visit checklist:
- Staff turnover last 12 months
- Qualified teacher versus assistant ratios
- FS1 transition process in writing
