An Integrated Tourism Complex is sold as a place and lived in as a jurisdiction. Inside the gate, the rules that govern what you pay, what you may do with your own unit and who decides both are written by a developer and a management company, not by a legislature.

That arrangement is normal in the Gulf. What makes Oman different is the absence of the statute that usually sits behind it. There is no strata law here setting a budget procedure, an audit standard or a dispute forum for jointly owned property, so the community documents attached to your sale agreement are not an implementation of a regime. They are the regime.

## What binds you before you sign?

The community documents, in full, from the moment the sale agreement is executed. There is no cooling-off window in which the rules are provisional and no later vote at which you accept them.

This inverts the order most buyers expect. In a purchase, price is negotiated and terms are accepted; here a substantial part of the terms is a separate document set that governs the next decade of ownership and is presented as an annex. Owners read the sale agreement carefully and the annex quickly, which is the wrong way round: the sale agreement governs one transaction, and the annex governs everything that happens afterwards. The title question that precedes all of this, meaning whether you can lawfully own the unit at all, is a different enquiry and sits on the [Oman title classes page](/guides/oman-property-foreigner-living/).

## Which documents tell you what ownership costs?

Five, and the point of asking for all five is that any one alone is answerable in a way that conceals the pattern.

| Document | What it reveals | What a single year hides |
|---|---|---|
| Service-charge history, 5 years | The trend and how costs were absorbed | Whether this year's rate is a low point |
| AGM minutes, last 2 | What owners raised and what was answered | Disputes settled before they reached a budget |
| Reserve fund balance | Capacity to absorb a large repair | Whether the reserve was recently drained |
| Special-levy record, 5 years | What arrives outside the annual budget | The existence of levies at all |
| House rules, in full | Letting, guests, pets, vehicles, gate | Restrictions that change your investment case |

A current-year rate is the figure a seller volunteers, and it answers a question no owner has: what will I pay next year. The question an owner has is what this manager does when a cost arrives, and only the series answers it. A rate flat for five years with a levy in year three is a different asset from a rate that rose steadily and never levied, even where both arrive at the same number today.

## Why does the language of the minutes matter?

Because Arabic is the working language of many Omani community associations, and translation is offered as a courtesy rather than owed as an entitlement.

The consequence is not inconvenience. An owner who cannot read the minutes has no visibility into the period between the decision and the invoice, which is exactly the period in which a levy is proposed, debated and approved. They learn about it when it is billed, at which point the argument they might have made is a year old and the vote has happened. Foreign owners in this position often assume the association is opaque; frequently it is not, and the information was available in a language they did not read. Budget translation as a standing cost of ownership rather than a one-off, and treat the first set as part of the purchase enquiry.

## What does salt water add to the budget?

Two cost centres that inland developments do not carry, and both are funded by levy when the reserve is short: marine infrastructure and salt-exposed building envelopes.

Jetties, seawalls, breakwaters and pontoons deteriorate on a schedule set by the sea rather than by use, and the works are specialist, marine-contracted and expensive per metre. Facades and render on buildings within reach of salt air degrade faster than the same specification a few kilometres inland. Neither appears in an annual service charge until the year the work is scheduled, and neither is visible to a buyer walking a well-maintained promenade. The diagnostic is the age of the marine works against the reserve balance, not the age of the buildings: a complex whose towers are eight years old may be sitting on a jetty installed at masterplan stage a decade earlier. For yields and entry pricing across the complexes, see the [ITC zones comparison](/guides/oman-itc-zones-property/). The reason a coastal reserve fund has to be read against the sea rather than the buildings belongs here.

## Which house rules change the investment case?

The letting clause, first and by a distance. A restriction on short-let converts a unit modelled on nightly income into a long-let asset with a different yield, a different tenant and a different exit, and the rule sits in a document most investors read after they have modelled the return.

The others worth extracting before signing:

- Guest registration at the gate, which decides how a short-let handover actually works in practice.
- Pet limits, which narrow the long-let tenant pool in a family market.
- Vehicle and parking allocation, which in dense phases is the difference between one space and two.
- Alteration consent, which governs whether a refurbishment before letting needs association approval and how long that takes.

Each is small in isolation. Together they describe the operating envelope of the asset, and an investor who models income without reading them has modelled a property that may not be the one on offer. Daily life inside the mature complexes is covered on the [Al Mouj living page](/guides/living-muscat-al-mouj/).

## Where does an owner go when the charge is wrong?

To the dispute mechanism in the community documents, and after that to the Omani courts. There is no regulator sitting between those two steps.

This is the practical meaning of the missing statute, and it is worth stating without drama. Dubai owners disputing a charge have a regulated route with a filing procedure and an authority that reads the budget. An Omani ITC owner has a contract, a counterparty, and a court. The court works, and it is slower and more expensive than a complaint form, so the economics of disputing a AED-scale annual charge rarely justify the path. That asymmetry is the argument for reading the documents before signing rather than after an invoice arrives, because the leverage available at the point of purchase is the only cheap leverage in the relationship.

## What changes when the developer hands the association over?

Control, and with it the identity of the person setting your budget. Early in a complex's life the developer runs the association and carries costs it has an interest in keeping invisible while units are still selling.

The handover to owner control is the moment a subsidised budget meets its real cost base, and it produces the sharpest single-year rise most ITC owners experience. Nothing improper needs to have happened. A developer maintaining its own showpiece absorbs landscaping, security and marine upkeep as a sales expense; an owners' committee inheriting the same complex discovers those lines have to be funded from the charge. A buyer purchasing in a phase still under developer management is therefore reading a rate that has a known future step in it, and the useful question is when the transfer is scheduled rather than what the charge is today.

## How does governance show up in the resale price?

Through the buyer pool rather than through the rate. Two complexes with identical service charges sell differently when one produces an audited history in a week and the other cannot locate its minutes.

A cautious buyer, which after handover is most of the resale market, will ask for the same five documents this page opened with. A complex that answers quickly is transactable; one that does not narrows its market to buyers who do not ask, and price follows the narrower pool. This is why governance quality belongs in an acquisition model rather than in a lifestyle assessment: it is a liquidity characteristic. The hold periods and market depth that frame it are on the [Oman investment guide](/guides/oman-property-investment-guide/), and the non-ITC baseline for comparison sits on the [Muscat cost of living page](/guides/muscat-cost-of-living/).
