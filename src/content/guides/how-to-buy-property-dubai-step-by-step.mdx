Buying property in Dubai is one of the more transparent processes in the Gulf, but it has its own terminology, regulatory bodies, and sequencing that trips up first-time buyers every year. This guide covers the full process for both ready (secondary market) and off-plan purchases, with a separate section for buyers who cannot travel to Dubai.

No fluff. Just the steps, the paperwork, and the mistakes that cost people money.

## What should you prepare before making an offer in Dubai?

Pre-offer prep in Dubai typically means fixing a budget that includes about 5-6% above purchase price for cash buyers (DLD 4%, trustee, NOC AED 500-5,000, admin), plus mortgage extras if financed. Get bank pre-approval first so Invest Gulf buyers know their ceiling before negotiating.

| Cost item | Typical range |
| --- | --- |
| DLD transfer fee | 4% of purchase price |
| DLD admin fee (title deed) | AED 250-580 |
| Trustee office fee | AED 2,000-4,000 |
| NOC fee (developer) | AED 500-5,000 |
| Broker commission | 2% (buyer side, if applicable) |
| Mortgage registration fee | 0.25% of loan amount |
| Valuation fee (mortgage) | AED 2,500-3,500 |

Non-resident LTV caps are often tighter than resident caps; know your ceiling before you negotiate. Mortgage buyers should add roughly 0.5-1% for loan-related fees.

## How do you buy a ready (resale) property step by step?

A ready Dubai resale purchase typically means Form F with about 10% deposit, developer NOC in 1-7 business days (AED 500-5,000), then DLD trustee transfer with manager's cheques for price and 4% DLD fee. Cash deals often close in 2-5 business days after NOC for Invest Gulf buyers.

1. Agree price and sign Form F (transfer deadline often 30 days cash / 60 days mortgage)
2. Seller obtains developer NOC; clear service charges
3. Book DLD trustee; bring passports, Form F, NOC, manager's cheques
4. Transfer completes in 1-2 hours; title deed same day
5. Post-transfer: DEWA, owners' association, Ejari if tenanted

Do not skip Form F even if the seller pushes for an informal agreement. Without it, deposit protection is weak. Electronic signing via Dubai REST is accepted.

Buying a ready Dubai property step by step for a cash foreign buyer typically means locking Form F with a deposit near 10% of price, waiting 1-7 business days for the developer NOC at AED 500-5,000, then attending a DLD trustee appointment with manager's cheques for the purchase price and a separate cheque for the 4% Dubai Land Department transfer fee plus admin near AED 250-580. Trustee fees commonly run AED 2,000-4,000, and the appointment itself often finishes in 1-2 hours with a same-day title deed print. Mortgage paths add about 2-4 weeks for valuation and liability letters, stretching end-to-end timelines toward 4-8 weeks. Invest Gulf process checks treat missing original NOC, mismatched unit numbers across Form F and title, and informal deposits without Form F as the top three cash-deal failure modes before anyone debates yield or community brand.

## How do you buy off-plan property step by step?

An off-plan Dubai purchase typically means verifying RERA registration and escrow, paying a 5-20% reservation to the escrow account, signing the SPA, then registering Oqood at 4% of price. Milestone payments must stay in escrow through handover, which we check on every receipt before the next instalment.

1. Verify RERA project number, Trakheesi licence, and escrow via Dubai REST
2. Reserve with 5-20% to the registered escrow account only
3. Sign SPA; scrutinise milestones, delay clauses, variation, snagging
4. Register Oqood (4% fee); keep the certificate for any pre-handover resale
5. Pay construction milestones via escrow; snag before handover acceptance

Snagging periods often cover structural defects for about 12 months post-handover; never sign acceptance without a written defect list.

## Can you buy Dubai property remotely with a POA?

Remote Dubai purchases typically mean a notarised, apostilled, Arabic-translated Power of Attorney that lets a representative sign Form F, apply for NOC, and attend DLD trustee transfer. POA execution abroad usually takes 7-14 days, so we start attestation before Form F transfer deadlines.

- Powers: Form F, NOC, trustee transfer, SPA, Oqood
- Steps: local notary, apostille, UAE Embassy if required, Arabic translation, UAE notary validation
- Funds: wire to UAE account or escrow; manager's cheques still needed at trustee

## How long does each stage of a Dubai purchase take?

Dubai purchase timelines typically mean 2-5 business days for cash title transfer after NOC, plus 1-7 business days for NOC itself, while mortgages add 2-4 weeks for valuation and liability letters. End-to-end cash often lands in 2-3 weeks and mortgage in 4-8 weeks for our clients.

| Stage | Typical timing |
| --- | --- |
| Form F to NOC | 1-7 business days |
| Cash trustee transfer | 2-5 business days after NOC |
| Mortgage valuation / liability | 2-4 weeks |
| Off-plan Oqood after SPA | Days to a few weeks |
| Remote POA from abroad | 7-14 days |

## What document checklist runs before signing?

The pre-signing document checklist typically means passports, Form F or SPA, DLD fee funding for 4%, purchase funds or escrow proof, and trustee booking for ready deals. Add original NOC and seller title for resale, or Trakheesi, RERA, and escrow details for off-plan for Invest Gulf files.

- Ready: NOC, seller title deed, liability letter if mortgaged, Ejari if tenanted
- Off-plan: Trakheesi, RERA registration, escrow account, Oqood after SPA
- Remote: notarised and attested POA

## Which mistakes cost Dubai buyers the most money?

Costly Dubai buyer mistakes typically include informal deposits without Form F, attending trustee without the original NOC, skipping snagging, and missing Form F deadlines that can forfeit a 10% deposit. Currency moves of about 10% on long off-plan plans can rival transaction fees for our clients.

1. No Form F / weak deposit protection
2. Verbal NOC promises at the trustee door
3. Off-plan payment schedule risk without exit clauses
4. Signing handover without written snagging
5. Unit-number mismatch across Form F and title/Oqood
6. Missing Form F deadline without written extension
7. Ignoring service-charge history and special levies
8. Ignoring FX risk on long payment plans

## Which regulators oversee Dubai property transfers?

Dubai property transfers typically mean Dubai Land Department (DLD) for title and Oqood, RERA for broker licensing, Form F standards, and off-plan escrow rules, plus Dubai Municipality completion certificates at handover. Dubai REST is the buyer-facing app we use for RERA, escrow, and licence checks.

- DLD: title deeds, Oqood, trustee network, 4% transfer/Oqood fee
- RERA: Form F, escrow, Trakheesi oversight
- Municipality: completion certificate before title conversion on new builds

## How do buyer scenarios change the step-by-step sequence?

Buyer scenarios typically change fee stacks and underwriting: cash resale stresses Form F, NOC, and 4% DLD; mortgaged resale adds 2-4 weeks of bank steps; buy-to-let needs real service-charge filings, not brochure yields. We map each path and fee stack before any offer letter goes out.

- Cash resale: 5-6% closing stack, 2-3 weeks common
- Mortgage resale: LTV limits + valuation; 4-8 weeks common
- Buy-to-let: underwrite net yield with filed service charges and void assumptions

## What final checks run before you sign Form F or an SPA?

Final checks before Form F or SPA typically mean confirming freehold designation, active Trakheesi, RERA escrow for off-plan, clear title or Oqood, clear service charges, and SPA delay/rescission clauses. Remote buyers need a UAE-authorised POA within the 7-14 day attestation window for Invest Gulf deals.

- Freehold zone and seller/developer name match on DLD/REST
- Escrow account number on every off-plan receipt
- Unit number identical on Form F and title/Oqood

Insider tip: Photograph the DLD unit number on the title or Oqood beside the Form F unit field before you transfer funds; Dubai buildings often use marketing names that do not match the land department code.

Dubai's property market rewards buyers who verify RERA escrow and DLD records rather than take a developer's or broker's word for it. The digitised trustee process is fast when documents are complete, and slow when NOC, unit numbers, or manager's cheques are wrong.

An off-plan Dubai purchase checklist for international buyers typically starts with Dubai REST verification of the RERA project number, active Trakheesi licence, and escrow account before any 5-20% reservation payment leaves your bank. The SPA should link payment milestones to construction stages, state delay remedies, and describe snagging; Oqood registration then triggers a 4% fee equivalent to the ready-property DLD transfer charge and creates your pre-handover ownership record. Construction payments must stay in the same escrow account referenced in the SPA, and handover acceptance should wait until a written snagging list is filed, with structural defect windows often near 12 months. Invest Gulf remote-buyer files usually run a 7-14 day POA attestation track in parallel so a representative can sign and attend trustee steps without freezing a Form F deadline that might otherwise put a 10% deposit at risk.
