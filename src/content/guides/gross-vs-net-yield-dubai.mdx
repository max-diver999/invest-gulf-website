Every Dubai property listing quotes gross yield. Almost none quote net yield, the number that actually reaches your bank account. This is not accidental. The gap between gross and net in Dubai is real, consistent, and large enough to turn what looks like an 8% investment into a 5.5% one.

Understanding this gap, and knowing how to calculate it for any specific property, is the single most valuable thing you can do before buying.

## What is gross rental yield in Dubai?

Gross rental yield in Dubai means annual Ejari-registered rent divided by purchase price, so an AED 900,000 apartment collecting AED 80,000 rent shows about 8.9% gross before ownership costs of roughly AED 15,000 to 25,000 per year that never appear on listing cards for foreign buyers.

If a AED 900,000 apartment generates AED 80,000 in annual rent, the gross yield is 8.89%. This is the number on the listing. It is also the number that does not account for the approximately AED 15,000 to 25,000 per year in ownership costs that come off that rental income before anything reaches you.

Gross yield is not a lie. It is a useful starting point for comparing properties on a like-for-like basis. The problem is that it is universally used as if it were the end point, as if service charges, management fees, vacancy, and maintenance do not exist.

## Which costs reduce gross yield to net yield in Dubai?

Net yield in Dubai means Ejari rent minus service charges, management, vacancy, and maintenance before dividing by price, and mid-market towers commonly lose 2 to 3 percentage points as AED 12 to 25 per sq ft service charges alone can remove AED 13,500 yearly on a 750 sq ft unit for foreign buyers evaluating the same numbers.

Service charges are annual fees paid to the building's Owners Association to fund common area maintenance, building management, and the reserve fund. They are non-optional and non-negotiable.

| Community type | Service charge range (AED/sq ft/year) |
|---|---|
| Discovery Gardens, Sports City | AED 11 to 16 |
| JVC (established towers) | AED 14 to 18 |
| JVC (newer towers) | AED 16 to 22 |
| Business Bay | AED 18 to 24 |
| JLT | AED 14 to 22 |
| Dubai Marina | AED 20 to 28 |
| Downtown Dubai | AED 22 to 32 |
| Palm Jumeirah (apartments) | AED 25 to 40 |
| Palm Jumeirah (villas) | AED 30 to 50 |

Worked example: A 750 sq ft apartment in a mid-tier JVC tower with AED 18/sq ft service charges pays AED 13,500 per year in service charges. On AED 90,000 annual rent, that is a 15% reduction in revenue before any other cost.

### Property Management Fees

If you use a property management company to handle tenant sourcing, Ejari registration, rent collection, and maintenance coordination, expect to pay 5 to 10% of annual rent. At 8% of AED 90,000, that is AED 7,200 per year.

Self-managing from abroad is possible but requires reliable local contacts for maintenance calls and a Dubai bank account for rent collection. The management fee is often worth paying for non-resident investors.

### Vacancy

The standard citywide vacancy allowance for Dubai is 7 to 8% of the year, roughly 4 weeks per year. On AED 90,000 annual rent, a 7% vacancy allowance reduces effective annual income by AED 6,300.

Communities with strong tenant demand (Dubai Marina, Downtown, well-located JVC buildings) may experience only 2 to 3 weeks between tenancies on correctly priced units. Communities with supply pressure may see 6 to 10 weeks of vacancy per year. Apply a realistic figure for your specific building based on rental history data, not optimistic projections.

### Maintenance and Minor Works

Budget AED 3,000 to 8,000 per year for a standard apartment: fixing appliances, repainting between tenancies, minor plumbing. Higher for older stock or cheaper mid-market buildings where finishes are lower quality. Lower for newer handovers in their first 2 to 3 years (snag corrections are typically developer-covered under warranty).

### Short-Let Specific Costs

If operating under a DET Holiday Home Permit:
- Holiday Home Permit: AED 1,520/year (apartments and studios), AED 3,570/year (villas and townhouses)
- Platform fees: 15 to 20% of collected revenue (Airbnb, Booking.com, Vrbo)
- Tourism Dirham: approximately AED 15 per room per night, remitted to DET
- Municipality fee: 7% of accommodation value, applied per booking
- STR management company: 15 to 25% of revenue (if not self-managing)

## Is short-term rental more profitable than long-term tenancy in Dubai?

Short-term rental in Dubai tourist corridors can mean 30% to 50% higher gross revenue than long-term Ejari rent, yet DET permits of AED 1,520 per year, Tourism Dirham near AED 15 per room night, 7% municipality fees, and 15% to 25% operator cuts often shrink the net advantage to 0% to 20% for foreign buyers evaluating the same numbers.

| Metric | Long-term tenancy | Short-term rental (STR) |
|---|---|---|
| Annual gross revenue | AED 100,000 | AED 140,000 (30 to 40% premium) |
| Management cost | 8% = AED 8,000 | 20% platform + 20% operator = AED 56,000 |
| Vacancy / off-season | 7% = AED 7,000 | 20 to 25% effective vacancy |
| Permit and compliance costs |, | AED 1,520/year + Tourism Dirham |
| Net revenue (approx.) | AED 70,000 | AED 70,000 to 84,000 |
| Net advantage of STR |, | 0 to 20% |

The gross revenue advantage of STR (30 to 50%) compresses to 0 to 20% net advantage after management fees, platform commissions, and higher operating costs. The advantage is real, but requires a well-located unit, active management, and consistent occupancy. JVC in a non-tourist suburb with a Holiday Home permit will not generate the same STR premium as a Marina canal-view apartment with JBR beach access.

## Where should you pull rent and service charge data before you buy?

Rent and service charge data before purchase should come from Ejari transacted comps and Mollak building charges, not portal asking rents, because a 1 to 2 percentage point error on AED 18 per sq ft charges can flip an 8% gross story into a sub-6% net outcome for foreign buyers.

Yield claims that should pause a Dubai purchase include guaranteed yields lasting only 1 to 3 years, gross above 10% on standard apartments, and models that assume zero service charges or 100% occupancy, because citywide vacancy of 7% to 8% alone cuts AED 90,000 rent to about AED 82,800 collected for foreign buyers evaluating the same numbers.

## How does net yield differ by community tier in Dubai?

Net yield by Dubai community tier typically means mid-market JVC or Sports City stock netting about 5.2% to 7.8% while premium Marina and Downtown towers often net 3.8% to 6.0% after higher service charges of AED 12 to 18-plus per sq ft and longer void periods for foreign buyers.

### Yield analysis by community tier

| Community tier | Price range/sqft | Service charge/sqft | Typical gross yield | Typical net yield |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Premium (Downtown, Marina) | AED 1,200 to 2,000+ | AED 12 to 18 | 5.5 to 7.5% | 3.8 to 6.0% |
| Mid-tier (JVC, Sports City) | AED 800 to 1,200 | AED 8 to 14 | 7.0 to 9.5% | 5.2 to 7.8% |
| Entry-level (International City, DSO) | AED 450 to 700 | AED 6 to 12 | 8.5 to 12.0% | 6.5 to 10.2% |

Yield compression factors at premium tier:
- Higher absolute service charges (AED 15+ per sqft on luxury towers)
- Premium property management fees (8 to 12% vs 6 to 8% standard)
- Longer void periods due to higher rent expectations (45+ days average vs 30 days)

Yield enhancement factors at entry tier:
- Lower service charges on older or simpler buildings
- Faster tenant placement due to affordable rent points
- Higher tenant demand relative to supply in budget categories

## Why do service charges swing net yield more than management fees?

Service charges swing Dubai net yield more than management fees because they are fixed per sq ft whether the unit is empty or tenanted, so AED 15-plus per sq ft on luxury towers and major works years that can double charges hit cash flow even during 45-day voids for foreign buyers.

Variable charge risks:
- Major maintenance years: Elevator replacements, façade work, HVAC overhauls can double annual charges
- Seasonal utility spikes: Summer cooling costs can add AED 2 to 4 per sqft in peak months
- **Insurance increases:** Building insurance rates have increased 15 to 25% annually 2023 to 2026 due to regional climate risks

### Service charge optimization strategies

**At purchase stage:**
- Review 3-year service charge history for target building
- Compare charges against similar buildings in same community
- Verify if any major maintenance is deferred and will impact future charges
- Check building reserve fund health (should be 10 to 15% of annual charges)

**Post-purchase management:**
- Attend building OA meetings to influence service provider contracts
- Monitor common area utility consumption patterns
- Advocate for energy efficiency improvements that reduce operating costs
- Review management company performance annually

## How much vacancy should you model by apartment type?

Vacancy modelling in Dubai should start at 7% to 8% citywide, with studios often empty 20 to 30 days and family 3-bedroom stock 35 to 50 days between tenancies, while summer exodus from June to August can add several percent to voids for foreign buyers underwriting annual cash flow.

### Dubai vacancy rates by property category (2024 to 2026 data)

| Property type | Average vacancy rate | Seasonal variation | Typical void period |
|---|---|---|---|
| Studios (entry level) | 5 to 8% | +2% summer | 20 to 30 days |
| 1BR apartments | 6 to 9% | +3% summer | 25 to 35 days |
| 2BR apartments | 7 to 10% | +2% summer | 30 to 45 days |
| 3BR+ apartments | 8 to 12% | +1% summer | 35 to 50 days |
| Villas | 6 to 10% | +1% summer | 45 to 75 days |

**Seasonal factors affecting vacancy:**
- **Summer exodus (June to August):** Many expat families return home, increasing available stock
- **School year timing:** Family tenants prefer September starts, creating Q3 inventory buildup
- **Ramadan period:** Reduced viewing activity can extend void periods by 10 to 15 days

### High-vacancy risk properties

Properties experiencing above-average vacancy rates share common characteristics:

**Location factors:**
- No metro connectivity (adds 15 to 20 minutes commute vs metro-connected alternatives)
- Limited parking (under 1.2 spaces per unit in areas requiring car ownership)
- No community amenities (gym, pool, retail) in family-targeted buildings

**Building factors:**
- Poor maintenance resulting in outdated common areas
- Unreliable elevators or utilities
- High service charges relative to competing buildings
- Restrictive building rules (pet policies, guest restrictions)

**Unit factors:**
- Below-standard fit-out quality compared to community average
- Inadequate storage space (crucial for family tenants)
- Poor natural light or ventilation
- Unrealistic rent pricing relative to Ejari comparables

## Does UAE tax change net yield for overseas landlords?

UAE tax does not reduce individual rental net yield inside the country because personal rental income tax is 0%, yet overseas landlords must still model home-country tax on AED rents after management and service charges, or an 5% UAE net can shrink materially once foreign reporting rules apply Build the same assumptions into your SPA timeline.

**Tax efficiency strategies:**
- Structure ownership through UAE company to potentially defer home country taxation
- Maximize deductible expenses (mortgage interest, management fees, maintenance)
- Consider timing of property disposal to optimize capital gains treatment
- Verify tax treaty benefits for dividend/rental income repatriation

## What checklist should you run before you accept a quoted yield?

A quoted-yield checklist means pulling Mollak service charges on the exact building, adding DEWA or chiller of AED 800 to 1,500 per month on many 1-beds, budgeting 5% to 8% vacancy plus management, and using Ejari tower comps so an AED 1 million unit at AED 70,000 rent nets near 4.3% before mortgage for foreign buyers evaluating the same numbers.

1. **Pull Mollak SC** on the exact building: not the community average.
2. **Add DEWA/chiller**: district cooling can add AED 800 to 1,500/month on a 1BR.
3. **Budget 5 to 8% vacancy + management** on mid-market stock; 10% on heavy investor towers.
4. **DLD + agent + mortgage registration** on acquisition: 6 to 8% all-in on ready buys.
5. **Ejari transacted rent** on same tower: ignore Property Finder asking rents.
6. **Compare net to home-currency return** after tax if you are non-UAE resident.

| Line item | Typical 1BR (AED 1M, AED 70K gross rent) |
|---|---|
| Gross rent | AED 70,000 |
| Service charge (AED 18/sqft × 700 sqft) | −AED 12,600 |
| DEWA + chiller | −AED 6,000 |
| Management + vacancy (7%) | −AED 4,900 |
| Maintenance reserve | −AED 3,500 |
| **Net before mortgage** | **~AED 43,000 (4.3% on price)** |

If net falls below your target, either **negotiate price**, **pick a lower-SC building**, or **switch community**, dashboards do not fix a bad acquisition price.
