The UAE Federal Authority for Identity, Citizenship, Customs and Port Security (ICP) set AED 15,000 per month as the minimum salary for the skilled employee track of the Green Visa when the programme launched in April 2022. At mid-2026 exchange rates, AED 15,000 is approximately USD 4,085 or EUR 3,780 per month, a figure that sits comfortably within the compensation range for mid-level professionals in sectors such as technology, finance, engineering, healthcare, and law in the UAE.

The threshold functions as a proxy for two things: financial self-sufficiency (the holder should not be dependent on government assistance) and skill level validation (higher salaries correlate with more specialised roles). It is not an arbitrary income filter, it is anchored to the broader ISCO classification system that defines which occupations qualify at all.

The AED 15,000 floor applies to the **monthly salary as documented in UAE employment records**. It is not an annual income test, and it is not assessed against offshore or overseas earnings. You must be employed by a UAE-registered entity with a valid work permit and receiving UAE payroll at or above this level.

**Important caveat:** Visa rules in the UAE are periodically revised by ministerial decree. Always verify the current threshold directly with the ICP Smart Services portal or a registered immigration consultant before submitting an application.

### ISCO level 1: Managers and executives

This group includes chief executives, general managers, functional directors (finance, operations, marketing, technology), and senior administrators in both private and public organisations. Qualifying roles are leadership and strategic decision-making positions that typically command compensation well above the AED 15,000 threshold.

Examples: Chief Financial Officer, Operations Director, Regional General Manager, Head of Department in a listed company, Senior Government Official.

### ISCO level 2: Professionals

The largest qualifying group. ISCO level 2 covers roles requiring a body of specialist knowledge typically acquired through tertiary education, though the UAE does not mandate a degree certificate for Green Visa eligibility in statute, the ISCO classification of your role is what matters.

Examples include:
- **Engineering:** Civil, mechanical, electrical, structural, and software engineers; project engineers; systems architects
- **Finance:** Accountants, financial analysts, auditors, actuaries, investment managers
- **Healthcare:** Physicians, dentists, pharmacists, specialist nurses, physiotherapists
- **Legal:** Licensed lawyers, legal counsel, compliance officers
- **Technology:** Software developers, data scientists, cybersecurity specialists, network architects
- **Education:** University lecturers, research professionals
- **Architecture and design:** Registered architects, urban planners

### ISCO level 3: Technicians and associate professionals

This level covers roles requiring technical knowledge and practical skills, typically applying scientific or engineering principles in support of professionals at level 2.

Examples include:
- Engineering technicians, draftspersons, quality control inspectors
- IT support supervisors, network administrators, database administrators
- Healthcare associates: radiographers, laboratory technicians, operating theatre technicians
- Finance associates: loan officers, securities and finance clerks in supervisory roles
- Legal associates: paralegal supervisors, senior case managers

**What does not qualify:** ISCO skill level 4 (clerical support) and below, administrative assistants, data entry operators, receptionists, call centre agents, and unskilled or semi-skilled workers. A clerical role paying AED 20,000 per month does not qualify under this track regardless of salary; the ISCO classification must be met independently.

When in doubt about your role's classification, your MOL (Ministry of Labour) registered occupation code on your work permit is the primary reference. Consulting an immigration lawyer for borderline cases, skilled trades, hybrid roles, or non-standard job titles, is worth the cost before investing in an application.

## What counts as "salary": basic vs. total package

Gulf investors reviewing what counts as "salary": basic vs. total p typically require AED 1,200/month carry proof, 4% DLD transfer fee awareness, and 6% net yield modeling before contingencies lapse, because Invest Gulf files average AED 2M turnaround when title deed and Oqood packs arrive before offer signature.


UAE employment contracts typically split compensation into components: basic salary, housing allowance, transport allowance, and sometimes meals, utilities, or other benefits. The question of which figure ICP uses to assess the AED 15,000 threshold matters significantly for applicants in the AED 14,000–17,000 total package range.

**ICP's standard reference** is the salary figure on the employer-issued **salary certificate**, which is the official HR document submitted with the application. Most UAE employers write total monthly remuneration, basic plus contractual allowances, on salary certificates because this is the figure they report to the MOL and the Social Security system.

In practice, applicants with a total package at or above AED 15,000 (where basic salary alone might be lower) have generally been accepted. However:

- **Discretionary bonuses and commissions are not counted.** These are not guaranteed monthly income and will not appear on a standard salary certificate.
- **In-kind benefits (accommodation provided by employer, company car) are not counted** unless converted to a documented monthly cash equivalent in the contract.
- **Overtime and variable pay are not counted** for threshold purposes.

The practical test: if your UAE salary certificate, signed and stamped by HR, shows a monthly total of AED 15,000 or above, and your payslips confirm this figure is being deposited consistently, you meet the salary element of the threshold.

For applicants where total remuneration sits close to the AED 15,000 line, a clear, well-structured salary certificate from HR removes ambiguity. Ask HR to itemise all components and show the monthly total explicitly.

The practical difference is in which authority issues the work permit:

- **Mainland employment:** work permit issued by the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation (MOHRE). The labour card shows the employer's mainland trade licence.
- **Free zone employment:** work permit issued by the relevant free zone authority (JAFZA, TECOM, DIFC, ADGM, and so on). Each free zone maintains its own registration and HR systems.

ICP processes Green Visa applications for both categories through the same Smart Services portal. The key point is that your salary certificate and work permit should reflect the legal entity employing you, not a parent company or a related entity in another jurisdiction.

**DIFC and ADGM employees** (who operate under English common law frameworks) follow a slightly different labour authority path, but their employment records are fully accepted by ICP for Green Visa applications. Salary documentation from DIFC HR or ADGM-registered employers meets the same standards as mainland documents.

On a standard UAE work permit, leaving an employer triggers a visa cancellation process. The new employer must re-sponsor the visa from scratch. During gaps between jobs, the individual may need to exit the country or apply for a status change.

On the Green Visa, residency is **self-sponsored and not employer-linked**. ICP policy provides a grace period, currently up to **180 days**, after employment ends before residency is affected. During this period you can:

- Interview and negotiate a new role without visa pressure
- Complete a notice period with the old employer while onboarding a new one
- Establish a freelance licence and transition to the freelancer Green Visa track if self-employment becomes your direction

When you secure new employment, you update your employment record and work permit through the relevant labour authority, your Green Visa status continues uninterrupted as long as the new role meets the ISCO and salary criteria.

This continuity is particularly valuable in sectors with high employer turnover, technology, finance, and consulting, where professionals frequently move between firms for career advancement. It also makes the UAE more competitive as a destination for senior international talent who need to know that a company reorganisation or acquisition does not automatically end their residency.

## What should buyers verify on summary?

The UAE Green Visa skilled employee track is the most accessible income-based route to five-year self-sponsored UAE residency. The AED 15,000 monthly salary threshold is well within reach for mid-career professionals in technology, finance, engineering, healthcare, legal, and related fields. The ISCO level 1–3 requirement means the track is targeted at roles with genuine skill content, not simply high-paying jobs in any occupation.

The practical requirements are straightforward: a valid UAE work permit in a qualifying occupation, a salary certificate confirming AED 15,000 or above per month, three to six months of payslips and bank statements, and the standard residency document set. Processing typically takes two to four weeks. Government fees total AED 1,500–1,800.

For those currently below the threshold, the standard UAE employment visa remains available with no salary floor. The skilled employee Green Visa becomes an upgrade worth pursuing once your compensation and role align with the criteria.
