Every Dubai property investment brochure quotes gross yield. Net yield, what actually reaches your bank account, is gross yield minus a list of costs that rarely appear in the same PDF. This guide itemises every cost line involved in running a Dubai rental property in 2026, so you can build a realistic net yield model before you buy.

## What is the full running cost stack for a Dubai rental?

Expect service charges, management fees, Ejari, maintenance, insurance, and vacancy to trim roughly 2.5 to 3 percentage points off gross yield on a typical mid-market apartment. Service charges alone often exceed management fees, so verify the building rate on RERA Mollak or Dubai REST before you model rent.

| Cost line | Typical annual range (mid-market 1BR) | Who pays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Service charges | AED 10,000 to 18,000 | Owner |
| Property management | 5 to 8% of rent collected | Owner |
| Ejari registration | AED 220 per lease | Owner or tenant (by agreement) |
| Maintenance reserve | 0.5 to 1% of property value | Owner |
| Vacancy allowance | 5 to 8% of gross rent | Owner (lost income) |
| Landlord insurance | AED 1,000 to 2,500 | Owner |

On an 800 sqft mid-market apartment at AED 15/sqft, service charges equal AED 12,000 per year. The same unit in a Downtown tower at AED 28/sqft equals AED 22,400. That AED 10,400 gap is often the difference between two listings that look similar on a portal.

How to verify before buying: RERA Mollak publishes approved service charge rates by building. Your broker should provide this; if they do not, ask specifically. The Dubai REST app allows direct lookup by building name. Never rely only on developer-provided service charge estimates; compare against Mollak data for existing comparable buildings.

## How much do property management fees cost in Dubai?

Full long-term management typically runs 5 to 8% of collected rent per year; short-term specialists charge 15 to 20% of revenue because turnover, cleaning, and platform compliance add labour. A one-time tenant-sourcing fee of 5% of annual rent or one month's rent is common if you self-manage after move-in.

| Service type | Typical cost |
| --- | --- |
| Tenant sourcing only (one-time) | 5% of annual rent, or 1 month's rent |
| Full management, long-term let | 5 to 8% of collected rent per year |
| Full management, short-term let (STR) | 15 to 20% of revenue |
| Setup / onboarding fee | AED 1,000 to 3,000 (one-time) |

What "full management" should include at minimum:

- Tenant screening and selection
- Tenancy agreement drafting
- Ejari registration
- Rent collection and follow-up
- Routine maintenance coordination
- Annual property inspection

What it often does not include without separate agreement:

- Major repairs above a defined threshold (typically AED 500 to 1,000)
- Renewal negotiations beyond standard increment
- Legal proceedings for non-payment

Always get a written scope of services. A management fee of 5% that excludes tenant sourcing and Ejari is not comparable to one that includes them. See the [Dubai rental yield guide](/guides/dubai-rental-yield-guide/) for how management fits net yield math.

## What does Ejari registration cost and why does it matter?

Ejari costs about AED 220 per lease registration and is required for every long-term tenancy. Without it, tenants cannot connect DEWA or complete many visa steps, and landlords have weaker standing at the Rental Disputes Centre.

- Cost: approximately AED 220 per registration
- Frequency: once per lease (typically annual renewal)
- Paid by: landlord or tenant depending on agreement; often absorbed by the manager
- Consequence of non-registration: utility and visa delays for the tenant; dispute risk for the landlord

The fee is small. The operational consequence of missing registration is large: a tenant in an unregistered tenancy will hit connectivity and visa problems that create conflict and potential liability for the landlord.

## Who pays DEWA and cooling charges on a Dubai rental?

On long-term residential leases the tenant pays DEWA consumption after registering in their name. The landlord pays reconnection during void periods and may carry district cooling deposits or chiller accounts depending on the building.

For residential long-term lets, the landlord's direct costs are usually limited to:

- DEWA reconnection fee (void period): AED 110 to 130 when a new tenant moves in
- Consumption during void periods: landlord pays DEWA while the unit is empty
- Chiller or district cooling deposits: some buildings use centralised plants (Emicool, Empower, DEWA Cooling). The cooling account may be tenant-registered but often requires an AED 1,000 to 2,000 security deposit per tenancy

For short-term rentals, the landlord maintains the DEWA account. Electricity consumption is absorbed into operating costs and priced into nightly rates. The [DEWA utility guide](/guides/dubai-utility-bills-deewa/) walks through tariff tiers if you want bill-level detail.

## How much should you budget for maintenance and snagging?

Budget 0.75% of property value per year as a conservative maintenance reserve, higher in the first two years after handover when snagging is common. AC servicing is the most frequent cash call.

| Category | Typical annual cost |
| --- | --- |
| Routine maintenance (appliances, minor plumbing) | AED 3,000 to 8,000 |
| Air conditioning servicing | AED 500 to 1,500 |
| Snagging / first-year defects (new builds) | AED 5,000 to 20,000 (partly under developer warranty) |
| Major maintenance (periodic, 5 to 10 years) | AED 10,000 to 30,000 per cycle |

New-build properties come with a developer warranty that should cover structural defects for 10 years and materials or fittings defects for 1 year. Snagging, identifying and documenting defects before or shortly after handover, is critical so the developer rectifies issues under warranty rather than you paying out of pocket.

## Do landlords need insurance in Dubai?

Building shell insurance is usually funded through service charges. Contents, fixtures, and liability for furnished or STR units are optional but cheap relative to exposure.

- Landlord contents insurance: AED 1,000 to 2,500 per year depending on coverage and value
- Liability insurance: some policies include landlord liability for tenant injuries

For a furnished unit or STR operation where furniture, appliances, and guest liability matter, include insurance in your net yield model even if it is not legally mandatory.

## How does vacancy affect net yield?

Vacancy is lost revenue, not a line item on a manager's invoice, but it belongs in every honest model. Citywide averages sit near 7 to 8%; prime towers can run lower; oversupplied pockets run higher.

| Location type | Typical vacancy rate |
| --- | --- |
| Prime (Marina, Downtown, Palm) | 4 to 5% annually |
| Citywide average | 7 to 8% annually |
| Supply-heavy sub-markets | 8 to 12% annually |

On AED 90,000 annual rent, 7% vacancy equals AED 6,300 in lost income per year. Deduct that from gross yield before you compare communities.

## What does a worked net yield example look like?

On a AED 1,400,000 purchase with AED 84,000 annual rent (6% gross), realistic running costs often land near AED 36,000 to 38,000, producing roughly 3.3 to 3.5% net before tax or finance. Purchase price and service charge rate move that number more than a 1% change in rent.

| Cost item | Annual cost (example) |
| --- | --- |
| Service charge (AED 15/sqft x 850 sqft) | AED 12,750 |
| Property management (7% of rent) | AED 5,880 |
| Ejari registration | AED 220 |
| Maintenance reserve (0.75%) | AED 10,500 |
| Insurance | AED 1,500 |
| Vacancy (7% of rent) | AED 5,880 |
| Total costs | AED 36,730 |
| Net annual income | AED 47,270 |
| Net yield | 3.38% |

The gross yield was 6%. The net yield is 3.38%. If the same property were purchased at AED 1,100,000 with the same rent (gross yield 7.6%), net yield would be approximately 4.3%. Entry price and service charge rate dominate outcomes.

## How do long-term and short-term management costs compare?

STR gross revenue can run 30 to 50% above equivalent long-term rent in STR-friendly locations, but higher management fees, cleaning, platform costs, and licensing eat much of the gap. A unit at 60% annual occupancy rarely beats a stable long-term tenancy on net income.

| Factor | Long-term let | Short-term let |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Management fee | 5 to 8% of rent | 15 to 20% of revenue |
| Utilities | Tenant DEWA | Owner DEWA |
| Turnover cost | Low | Cleaning, linen, restocking |
| Licensing | Ejari only | DTCM holiday home permit required |
| Vacancy shape | Long voids | Daily occupancy risk |

Read the [short-term rental Dubai license guide](/guides/short-term-rental-dubai-license/) before you assume STR management quotes are comparable to long-let fees.

## How do you choose a property manager in Dubai?

Ask for references from current landlord clients, confirm Ejari and RERA compliance processes, and define maintenance approval limits in writing. Managers running 50 to 200 units often balance responsiveness with systems; solo brokers can be flexible but create single-point-of-failure risk.

Questions to put in the contract review:

1. Fee basis: percentage of collected rent or gross achievable rent? Do you pay during void months?
2. Tenant sourcing: included or charged separately?
3. Maintenance threshold: maximum repair cost without owner approval?
4. Reporting: monthly statements and annual summaries in what format?
5. Ejari and compliance: who registers and renews?
6. Exit terms: notice period and handover of tenant files?

Tier 1 institutional managers (large portfolios, 4 to 6% fees) suit multi-unit investors. Tier 2 community specialists (6 to 10%) suit single-building focus. Tier 3 broker-managers (5 to 8%) suit hands-on owners who want a direct contact. Match tier to how often you visit Dubai.

## How do service charges evolve as buildings age?

Years 1 to 3 often stay near developer projections. Years 4 to 7 bring lift, pool, and facade work as warranties expire. Years 8 to 15 show steadier increases of 3 to 8% annually if the owners' association is well run; neglected buildings spike faster.

| Building age band | Service charge pattern | Investor note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 to 3 years | Developer-influenced rates | Watch snagging and warranty use |
| 4 to 7 years | First major capex cycle | Ask for 3-year OA budgets |
| 8 to 15 years | Predictable inflation | Reputation affects rent and resale |
| 15+ years | Facade and MEP cycles | Special assessments possible |

Developer track records differ: Emaar and Nakheel buildings often stabilise in a narrower band than boutique projects where initial charges were underpriced. Factor trajectory, not just today's AED/sqft, into net yield.
