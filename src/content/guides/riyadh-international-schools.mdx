## Why school choice drives your address in Riyadh

Riyadh is car-dependent, full stop. Ring-road traffic between 06:45 and 07:45 will stress your family more than compound pool size or Diplomatic Quarter views ever will.

Get the triangle right:

- Home, legacy north/west compounds, Diplomatic Quarter fringe, newer gated communities, or city towers near KAFD/Olaya
- School, often clustered north and west; a few campuses serve central and east corridors  (confirm current official rules)
- Office, government ministries, bank HQs, Vision 2030 project offices, hospital campuses

A 20-minute run at 07:15 can become 45–55 minutes at 07:40. Metro expansion may help some corridors by late 2020s  (confirm current official rules), do not lease on renderings alone.

Get it wrong and you pay twice: SAR 70,000+ tuition per child plus an hour each way, five days a week, for a three-year assignment. That is not a commute, it is a second unpaid job.

## School system overview: no KHDA dashboard

Dubai has KHDA, one portal, ratings, fee QR codes. Riyadh has nothing equivalent. You piece together quality from Ministry registration, school PDFs, parent WhatsApp, and campus tours.

What exists instead:

- Ministry of Education framework for private schools  (confirm current official rules)
- School-published fee schedules (PDF, request annually, not cached blog posts)
- Parent WhatsApp and compound groups (verify gossip against registrar letters)
- Accreditation bodies (**BSO, CIS, NEASC, MSA, per school (confirm current official rules)**)
- Employer HR preferred-school lists (often outdated, cross-check)

Academic year: typically September–June, confirm per school; some offer January entry or rolling nursery intake  (confirm current official rules).

Language: English instruction in most internationals; Arabic mandatory hours vary by curriculum, grade and MoE policy  (confirm current official rules).

Who attends: employer-sponsored expat families dominate; a growing share of Premium Residency and long-stay investor households, confirm sponsor documentation each school accepts  (confirm current official rules).

## School map: how to research without hype

Riyadh school marketing still references pre-2016 lore. Ignore alumni gossip; verify MOE licence, seat availability and your compound bus route in writing.

Riyadh's pool is smaller than Dubai's 200+ KHDA-regulated options but deeper than it was five years ago. Names change after mergers; always confirm legal entity on the invoice matches the campus you toured.

Research method (repeat for each shortlist school):

1. Request 2026–27 fee PDF and refund policy in writing
2. Ask for accreditation certificates (BSO/CIS/etc.)  (confirm current official rules)
3. Confirm exam boards (Cambridge vs Edexcel vs College Board)
4. Request waiting-list status by year group: not generic "limited places"
5. Tour at pick-up time (heat, traffic, bus chaos)
6. Ask for university destinations list (last three cohorts), not logo wall

Categories (illustrative, not exhaustive ranking):

| Tier | Profile | Planning notes |
|---|---|---|
| Premium British / IB | Long-established expat demand, senior-year pressure | Apply 9–12 months ahead for Years 7, 10, 12  (confirm current official rules) |
| Mid British / American | Solid academics, more mid-year entry | Bus routes from major compounds  (confirm current official rules) |
| Indian value | Fee-efficient, high workload | Popular with South Asian professional families |
| Bilingual / national-plus | Arabic-English blend | Different university path, confirm fit |

Do not treat social-media "best school" lists as fact. Vision 2030 marketing sometimes conflates announced campuses with operating seats, ask for first day of term headcount in your child's year.

## Waiting lists: the norm at premium years

Waiting lists are structural, not exceptional, at popular British and American years (Reception, Year 7, Year 10, Grade 9, DP1).

Typical timeline:

| Action | When |
|---|---|
| Email 4–6 schools for fee PDF + availability | 12 months before September start |
| Submit applications + pay assessment fee | 9–10 months ahead |
| Placement tests / interviews | 8–9 months ahead |
| Deposit to hold seat | Immediately on offer, often non-refundable (confirm current official rules) |
| Backup school deposit | Same window, do not rely on one favourite |

Mid-year arrival: possible in Indian tiers and some mid British years; hard in IGCSE Year 11 or DP1 without subject clash.

Saudi-specific wrinkle: iqama and employer letter timing can delay final enrolment even after academic offer, coordinate HR and registrar in parallel. See [Saudi family visa](/guides/saudi-family-visa/) *(planned)* and [Saudi iqama process](/guides/saudi-iqama-process/) *(planned)*.

Deposit discipline: many families pay two deposits (primary + backup) totalling SAR 10,000–40,000 at risk, read clawback if assignment ends early.

## Compound vs apartment: school run reality

This is the core Riyadh family decision tied to schools.

### Compounds (gated villa / townhouse communities)

Pros:

- School bus pickup at compound gate, standard for major internationals  (confirm current official rules)
- Security, pools, compound social calendar
- Children play in controlled environment, aligns with conservative outdoor norms
- Legacy communities have established bus contracts

Cons:

- Rent premium, SAR 10,000–22,000/month for 3BR villa is common  (confirm current official rules)
- Older stock, maintenance variability
- Far from some new office clusters without ring-road pain

Best for: families with two+ children, both parents commuting, first KSA posting, preference for on-site community.

### City apartments (towers, non-compound villas)

- Closer to KAFD, Olaya, Diplomatic Quarter work nodes
- Lower rent possible for 2BR in older districts  (confirm current official rules)
- Faster access to restaurants, malls, new entertainment districts

- No compound bus, parent drop-off or private driver
- School run not negotiable at peak, one traffic incident ruins the morning
- Less outdoor play space; heat limits street activity six months/year
- Parking and security vary by building

Best for: single-campus plan with tested sub-30-minute run, older children, or one parent not working.

### Decision matrix

| Factor | Favour compound | Favour apartment |
|---|---|---|
| Children under 10 | ✓ | |
| Two working parents | ✓ | |
| Premium British + backup school in different compass | ✓ | |
| One parent handles school run | | ✓ if run tested |
| Short 18-month assignment | | ✓ (flex lease) |
| Diplomatic Quarter / KAFD single-site job | | ✓ |

Rule: Run the Sunday morning test before Ejar signature. Repeat after Ramadan schedule shift if you arrive mid-year, traffic patterns change.

## Admissions process: step by step

Typical international school flow:

1. Online enquiry: child passport, previous reports, iqama copy (if available)
2. Assessment fee: SAR 500–2,500 non-refundable  (confirm current official rules)
3. Placement test: English, maths; sometimes CAT4 or writing sample
4. Family interview: expectations on behaviour, Arabic policy, fee payment
5. Conditional offer: subject to documents and deposit
6. Registration deposit: SAR 5,000–25,000+  (confirm current official rules); often forfeited on withdrawal
7. Final enrolment: iqama, medical form, insurance certificate, sponsor letter

Documents employers should prep early:

- Attested transcripts and withdrawal letter from previous school
- Vaccination record (Arabic/English)
- Medical insurance meeting school minimum network
- Sponsor letter confirming fee payer

Timing for September 2027 start: begin September 2026 enquiries for premium years.

## Hidden costs, levies and employer allowance gaps

Tuition is half the story, registration, capital levies, transport and uniform cycles hit year one hardest. Model allowance caps against secondary years, not KG.

| Cost type | SAR (indicative) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Registration / re-enrolment | 5,000–25,000/year  (confirm current official rules) | Often excluded from "tuition" headline |
| Capital / development levy | 10,000–50,000 one-off  (confirm current official rules) | Ask if depreciates on exit |
| Bus deposit | 1,000–3,000 refundable  (confirm current official rules) | Late pickup fines apply |
| Late payment surcharge | % per term  (confirm current official rules) | HR direct billing avoids personal cash flow pain |
| SEN support | 15,000–60,000+/year  (confirm current official rules) | Not always covered by allowance |
| Learning support assistant | Hourly  (confirm current official rules) | Get written policy before move |

Employer schooling allowance: many Vision 2030 packages cap at SAR 40,000–60,000/child/year, premium British exceeds cap from day one. Negotiate top-up or curriculum tier before accepting transfer.

HR one-page ask list:

1. Allowance SAR cap per child: net or gross?
2. Covers registration, bus, exams, levy?
3. Mid-year join: full year tuition liability?
4. Clawback if leaving before 24 months?
5. Direct billing to employer vs reimbursement lag?

## Arabic, Islamic studies and conservative campus norms

Saudi private schools operate inside Kingdom norms even when instruction is English.

- Arabic classes mandatory, intensity varies  (confirm current official rules)
- Islamic studies required for Muslim students; non-Muslim exemption process varies by school  (confirm current official rules)
- Dress code, modest uniform standards; respect national expectations on campus trips
- Gender segregation may apply in upper grades at some schools  (confirm current official rules)

Discuss with children before move, culture shock is real for UAE-raised kids.

## SEN, EAL and learning support

Support is thinner and less standardized than UAE Outstanding schools.

- Request written SEN policy and recent case examples (anonymised)
- Ask in-house therapist vs outsourced, waiting lists common  (confirm current official rules)
- EAL provision for non-native English, critical for mid-year arrivals
- Do not rely on tour-day assurances; speak to SENCO by video call

If your child has an EHCP/IEP equivalent, bring it translated and ask what can be honoured under local MoE rules  (confirm current official rules).

## Age-band advice (2–18)

### Early years (2–5)

Priority: proximity, heat management, toilet training, feeder priority into primary  (confirm current official rules).

- Fees often SAR 45,000–80,000/year even in nursery sections  (confirm current official rules)
- Cut-off birthday: commonly 31 August, verify per school
- Visit at pick-up to see heat protocols (indoor play, shaded areas)

### Primary (6–11)

Priority: literacy/numeracy, homework load, Arabic hours, bullying policy.

- British KS2 sets IGCSE readiness
- Class size, ask actual numbers, not brochure caps

Switching window: Years 3–5 least damaging for curriculum change.

### Lower secondary (12–14)

Priority: subject breadth before IGCSE/MYP choices.

- IGCSE options meeting usually Year 8, attend even if mid-year arrival

Avoid moving if child starts IGCSE cohort next year unless unavoidable.

### IGCSE (15–16) and sixth form (17–18)

Priority: exam board, subject blocks, university counsellor ratio.

- Fees peak SAR 90,000–120,000/year premium tier  (confirm current official rules)
- Sixth form seat not guaranteed from IGCSE, ask criteria early

### IB Diploma (17–18)

Priority: HL availability, TOK/EE support, CAS scheduling in heat.

- Not every child suits DP intensity, ask about A-Level or alternative paths

## Moving from UAE: curriculum mapping

British Year 6 in Dubai is not automatically British Year 7 in Riyadh, assessment gaps cost mid-year transfers. Request placement tests before you resign in UAE.

| UAE situation | Riyadh likely placement | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| British Outstanding Year 5 | Same year British | Low if reports strong |
| American Grade 6 | American or British Year 7 | Medium, maths alignment |
| IB MYP Year 2 | IB MYP or IGCSE bridge | Medium, coordinate early |
| Indian CBSE Class 8 | CBSE or British Year 9 | High if switching to British |

Assessment: expect English + maths; UAE "Outstanding" does not waive testing.

Social: children may miss Dubai extracurricular density, set expectations on clubs, summer camps and gendered activity norms.
