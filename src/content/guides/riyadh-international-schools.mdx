## How does the Riyadh school calendar and language mix work?

Riyadh schools typically run September to June calendars spanning about 9 to 10 months of instruction, with English in most internationals and mandatory Arabic hours that vary by curriculum, grade, and MoE policy, so Invest Gulf tells foreign relocators to confirm January-entry rarity before assuming mid-year flexibility in the area (confirm current official rules).

Who attends:

- Employer-sponsored expat families (majority share)
- Growing Premium Residency and long-stay households
- Sponsor documentation differs by registrar within 30 to 60 days of offer

Language and culture expectations on campus still reflect Saudi norms even when the curriculum is British or IB, and a SAR 70,000+ tuition line does not waive those campus rules.

## What does the admissions process require step by step?

Admissions typically require online enquiry, SAR 500 to 2,500 assessment fees, English and maths placement tests, a family interview, then registration deposits of SAR 5,000 to 25,000+ that are often forfeited on withdrawal under rules Invest Gulf treats as cash at risk for 12 months (confirm current official rules).

1. Online enquiry with passport and prior reports
2. Assessment fee payment
3. Placement test (sometimes CAT4 or writing)
4. Family interview on behaviour, Arabic policy, fees
5. Conditional offer
6. Registration deposit
7. Final enrolment with iqama, medical, insurance, sponsor letter

Documents to prep early: attested transcripts, vaccination record, medical insurance meeting school minima, sponsor letter naming the fee payer.

## How should employer school allowances be verified?

Employer allowances typically need a one-page HR confirmation covering SAR caps per child, whether bus and exams are included, mid-year join liability, and clawback if you leave before 24 months, because a 10% misunderstanding becomes tens of thousands of riyals on Invest Gulf family models in this market.

HR ask list:

1. Cap per child: net or gross?
2. Covers registration, bus, exams, levy?
3. Mid-year join: full-year tuition liability?
4. Clawback window (often 24 months)?
5. Direct billing versus reimbursement lag?

Age-band disruption typically stays lowest in Years 3 to 5, rises through IGCSE subject locks, and peaks in Diploma or A-Level years when premium fees hit SAR 90,000 to 120,000 and university predictions drive the next 2 years that Invest Gulf will not treat as casual in this market.
