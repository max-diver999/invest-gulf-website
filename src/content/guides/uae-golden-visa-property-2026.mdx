Many Dubai purchases are **partly motivated by residency** — not just rent. The UAE **Golden Visa** offers long-term residence for qualifying investors, but property marketing often oversimplifies approval.

## Property route (overview)

The well-known threshold is **AED 2 million** in qualifying property investment. Implementation details (single vs combined assets, off-plan treatment, mortgage vs cash) depend on **current Federal Authority for Identity and Citizenship (ICA) rules** and emirate processing.

> Always confirm with a licensed immigration consultant at the time of application — not a developer brochure dated last year.

## What the visa gives you

- Long-term UAE residence (commonly cited as 10-year renewable for investors)
- Stability for family sponsorship (subject to rules)
- Easier banking and business setup — but not automatic

It does **not** automatically optimise your global tax position.

## Off-plan and payment plans

Developers frequently bundle "Golden Visa eligible" into payment-plan launches. Key questions:

- Is **AED 2M gross value** or **paid-to-date** counted?
- Is the project registered and recognised for visa processing?
- What happens if handover delays?

Get written confirmation from both **immigration counsel** and **developer compliance** — not sales gallery staff alone.

## Process snapshot

1. Purchase property and obtain title / qualifying documentation
2. Apply through approved channels (often GDRFA in Dubai)
3. Medical and background checks
4. Visa stamping and Emirates ID

Timelines vary by nationality, existing status, and document completeness.

## Investment vs visa-only buying

If residency is the primary goal, still underwrite the **property as an investment**:

- Net yield after charges
- Exit liquidity if visa rules change
- Concentration risk (AED 2M+ in one tower)

A bad unit with a visa stamp is still a bad unit on resale.
