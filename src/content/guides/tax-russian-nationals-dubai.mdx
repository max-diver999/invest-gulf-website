## The Russia-UAE Double Tax Treaty: Current Status (2026)

The Convention between the Russian Federation and the UAE for the Avoidance of Double Taxation was signed in 2011 and entered into force in 2013. It covers taxation of income and capital.

In August 2023, Russia issued a decree suspending application of its double tax treaties with 38 countries it classified as "unfriendly states", primarily EU member states, the UK, the US, Canada, Australia, and others that imposed sanctions. **The UAE is not on Russia's unfriendly-states list.** As of mid-2026, the Russia-UAE DTT continues to operate.

What the treaty covers:
- **Income from immovable property**: may be taxed in the country where the property is situated (the UAE), which has zero personal income tax
- **Capital gains** from sale of immovable property: taxable in the country of location (UAE)
- **Dividends** from UAE companies
- **Residence tiebreaker**: for individuals simultaneously tax resident in both countries

The treaty's allocation of taxing rights to the UAE for property income and capital gains, combined with the UAE's zero-tax environment, can significantly reduce, or potentially eliminate, Russian tax on Dubai-source property income and gains for qualifying Russian nationals. However, treaty application depends on correctly establishing residence tiebreaker rules and income characterisation under both domestic legal systems. Specialist Russian tax advice is mandatory before relying on treaty benefits.

## Russian Tax Residency: Who Pays on Worldwide Income

Russia defines tax residency based on physical presence. Under Article 207 of the Russian Tax Code:

**Russian tax resident** = individual spending **183 or more days** in Russia during a 12-month calendar year.

Russian tax residents are subject to Russian personal income tax (NDFL, Nalog na Dokhody Fizicheskikh Lits) on **worldwide income**, including:
- Rental income from Dubai property
- Dividends from UAE or other foreign companies
- Interest income
- Capital gains from property sales

Russian **non-residents** (who spent fewer than 183 days in Russia) are taxed only on Russian-source income at flat 30% rate. A Russian national living full-time in Dubai and spending fewer than 183 days per year in Russia may become a Russian non-resident, eliminating Russian personal income tax obligations on UAE-source income, while also becoming potentially eligible for UAE tax residency via the 183-day rule.

The year you relocate and the year following are particularly important for day-counting. Russian passports and border crossing records are the primary documentation the Russian tax authority (Федеральная налоговая служба, FNS) uses.

## Russian Personal Income Tax Rates (2026)

Russia introduced a progressive personal income tax scale starting 2025, with further changes proposed. The key rates for 2026:

| Annual Income (RUB) | Tax Rate |
|---|---|
| Up to 2,400,000 | 13% |
| 2,400,001 – 5,000,000 | 15% |
| 5,000,001 – 20,000,000 | 18% |
| 20,000,001 – 50,000,000 | 20% |
| Above 50,000,000 | 22% |

For Russian tax residents declaring UAE rental income, the applicable rate depends on total annual income from all sources. At prevailing Dubai rental yields of 6–9% gross on mid-market apartments, the Russian income tax liability on UAE rental profits, absent treaty exemption, could run 13–18% of net rental income.

The investment thesis for Dubai property from a Russian tax perspective still often holds: even with Russian income tax applied to UAE rental income, the absence of UAE capital gains tax (versus potential Russian taxation on gains) and zero recurring UAE property taxes make Dubai assets efficient compared to domestic Russian alternatives.

## Controlled Foreign Company (CFC) Rules for Russian Owners

Russian CFC legislation (Chapter 3.4, Russian Tax Code) requires Russian tax residents to declare and potentially pay tax on undistributed profits of foreign companies they control. Key thresholds:

- **25% individual ownership** in a foreign company triggers CFC rules
- **10% individual ownership** where total Russian resident ownership exceeds 50%
- The controlling person must declare the CFC and its financial results annually to FNS

If a Russian tax resident holds Dubai property through a UAE free zone company or mainland LLC, that company is a CFC for Russian purposes. Undistributed profits of the UAE entity may be taxed in Russia at the individual's personal income tax rate, with certain exemptions for active businesses and smaller profit thresholds.

CFC exemptions available:
- **Active foreign company exemption**: If over 80% of revenue comes from active business operations (not passive rental/investment income), the CFC may qualify for exemption, difficult to meet for a pure property-holding vehicle
- **Low-profit exemption**: CFC profits below RUB 10 million per year are exempt from CFC taxation in Russia

For many Russian investors holding a single Dubai apartment personally (not through a company), CFC rules do not directly apply. The CFC framework becomes critical when structuring portfolios through UAE holding companies, free zone SPVs, or multi-property vehicles. Russian legal advice specific to your structure is essential.

## Currency Controls: The Practical Barrier for Russian Buyers

Post-February 2022 Russian currency control regulations (Federal Law No. 173-FZ on Currency Regulation, and numerous subsequent decrees) impose complex restrictions on Russian residents conducting cross-border financial transactions. Key issues affecting Dubai property investors:

**Receiving rental income abroad**: Russian residents may have restrictions on receiving foreign currency rental income into foreign bank accounts without appropriate authorisation or notification. Permitted transactions and required procedures have changed multiple times since 2022 and vary by counter-party country. The UAE is not a "friendly country" for all Russian currency control purposes, precise rules depend on the specific transaction structure.

**Receiving sale proceeds abroad**: Proceeds from foreign property sales denominated in foreign currency face reporting requirements and, in some cases, mandatory repatriation rules or special permissions. Practical enforcement has been uneven, but the regulatory risk is real.

**Transferring funds from Russia to buy Dubai property**: Capital outflows from Russian bank accounts for foreign property purchases are subject to restrictions. Many Russian buyers use funds already held outside Russia (e.g. savings accumulated pre-February 2022, or income earned while abroad) to fund UAE property purchases.

**UAE bank account for Russians**: Some UAE banks impose heightened compliance screening for Russian national clients following FATF and international guidance. Emirate-to-emirate variation exists, consult [Gulf Banking Comparison for Expats](/guides/gulf-banking-comparison-expats/) for practical banking options. FAB, ENBD, Mashreq, and various digital banks have different Russian-client policies that have shifted over 2022–2026.

Currency control violations in Russia carry fines of 20–100% of the transaction value. Obtaining proper authorisations and structuring transactions correctly from the outset is critical. This requires a Russian lawyer specialising in currency regulation.

## UAE Tax Position: What Russian Nationals Enjoy

On the UAE side of the ledger, the picture is straightforward. Russian nationals purchasing Dubai freehold property face:

| UAE Tax | Applicable? |
|---|---|
| Personal income tax on rental income | No, UAE has no personal income tax |
| Capital gains tax on property sale | No, UAE has no capital gains tax |
| Annual property tax or wealth tax | No, UAE has no annual property or wealth tax |
| Municipal housing fee (Dubai) | Yes, 5% of annual rent, typically paid by tenant via DEWA utility bill |
| DLD transfer fee at purchase | 4%, paid once at transaction |

The UAE's zero-personal-income-tax environment means Russian nationals who successfully establish UAE tax residency (183+ days in UAE, break Russian residency) can legally receive Dubai rental income and property sale proceeds entirely free of both Russian and UAE personal income tax. UAE tax residency certificate from the Federal Tax Authority can then be used to claim DTT benefits where applicable.

## Establishing UAE Tax Residency: Russian Nationals

Russian nationals who want to use the UAE's zero-tax environment effectively need to break Russian tax residency and establish UAE tax residency. The requirements:

**Break Russian residency**: Spend fewer than 183 days in Russia in the calendar year. This is straightforward in principle but requires discipline in travel patterns. Russian tax authority monitoring of cross-border movements has become more active.

**Establish UAE tax residency**: Meet UAE Federal Tax Authority criteria, primarily 183+ days physically present in the UAE within a calendar year, with supporting documentation: Emirates ID, UAE tenancy agreement or property title deed, bank statements showing UAE activity, utility connections.

**Obtain UAE Tax Residency Certificate**: Apply through the FTA online portal with supporting documents. This certificate enables claiming Russia-UAE DTT benefits for Russia-source income (e.g. dividends from Russian companies you still hold) and documents UAE tax status.

**Practical planning note**: Many Russian nationals relocate initially on a standard Dubai work visa or investor visa, later upgrading to Golden Visa via AED 2M+ property. The Golden Visa does not itself establish tax residency but facilitates the long-term UAE presence needed for both UAE and Russian residency status management.

## Dubai Property as a Diversification Asset for Russian Nationals

Beyond the tax dimension, Dubai property has specific appeal for Russian nationals as an asset class:

**Geopolitical diversification**: Dubai freehold property is a non-Russian, non-sanctioned, legally robust asset outside Russian jurisdiction, protected by UAE property law and RERA regulation.

**Currency diversification**: UAE dirham is pegged to the USD at 3.6725 since 1997, providing effectively USD-denominated assets outside the ruble zone. For Russian nationals whose wealth is predominantly ruble-denominated, Dubai property offers USD exposure.

**Rental income in USD**: Dubai rental contracts are denominated in AED (pegged to USD), providing stable foreign currency income streams for Russian nationals.

**Liquidity**: Dubai's active secondary market, transparent DLD registration system, and online price indices provide liquidity that some comparable asset classes (offshore accounts, gold) do not.

**Inheritance planning**: Dubai property held by a non-Muslim Russian national can be secured via DIFC Will registration, ensuring the asset passes according to the owner's wishes rather than UAE personal status law default rules.

## Practical Next Steps for Russian Nationals

1. **Consult a Russian tax lawyer** (not just a UAE broker) on your specific residency and income situation before purchasing. Treaty application, CFC, and currency control are all fact-specific.
2. **Document your UAE presence** meticulously: entry/exit stamps, Emirates ID records, UAE bank statements, utility bills. Russian FNS challenges to claimed non-residency require contemporaneous evidence.
3. **Structure ownership carefully**: Personal ownership (your name on Title Deed) avoids CFC complexity. Company ownership adds flexibility but triggers Russian CFC reporting.
4. **Understand currency paths** before committing: what account will receive rent? How will sale proceeds be repatriated or reinvested? Route planning with a currency specialist avoids penalties.
5. **Register a DIFC Will** if you are a non-Muslim to protect the Dubai asset for your chosen beneficiaries regardless of which country your estate is managed from.
