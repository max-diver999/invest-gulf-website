For tourist visas and short-term visit permits, fines begin accumulating on day 11 after expiry. There is no upper daily cap, fines accumulate every calendar day until the overstay is resolved by departing, paying at the airport, or regularising status.   - question: "Is there a grace period before UAE visa overstay fines start?"
    answer: "Yes. The UAE provides a 10-day grace period after a visa expires before the daily fine of AED 50 begins. If you depart within this 10-day window, no overstay fine is assessed. The grace period applies to tourist visas, visit visas, and most short-term entry permits. Residency visa holders have a 30-day grace period after visa cancellation or expiry to either renew their residency, change status, or depart. After the respective grace period ends, daily fines begin."
  - question: "What happens if you overstay a UAE visa at the airport?"
    answer: "At departure, UAE immigration will flag the overstay on your passport record. The total fine, AED 50 per day multiplied by the number of days overstayed, must be paid before you are permitted to board. Payment is made at the immigration counter. Once paid and cleared, you can depart without further immediate action. However, your travel history is recorded in the UAE immigration database (GDRFA/ICP systems), and a pattern of overstays or a very long overstay may result in a re-entry ban of varying duration."
  - question: "Can you get a UAE visa overstay ban and how long does it last?"
    answer: "Yes. UAE authorities can impose a re-entry ban on individuals with significant overstays, typically those who overstay by more than 6 months or have repeated violations. Short overstays of days to weeks are usually resolved by paying the fine at departure, with no automatic ban. For longer overstays, the GDRFA has discretion to impose bans of 1 year, 5 years, or permanent bans in the most severe cases. There is no published fixed threshold, decisions are made case by case. Voluntary departure and prompt fine payment typically result in a more lenient outcome than being caught without valid status."
  - question: "How do you pay a UAE visa overstay fine?"
    answer: "Overstay fines can be paid at the airport immigration counter on departure, through the GDRFA (General Directorate of Residency and Foreigners Affairs) smart services portal, or through the ICP Smart Services app. Payment is accepted by card. If you are still in the country and want to resolve the situation before travelling, the GDRFA Dubai office and ICP service centres can process fine payments and provide a clearance before your departure appointment. Some authorised typing centres can also facilitate payment. Always obtain a receipt confirming full payment and clearance."
  - question: "Does a UAE visa overstay affect future visa applications?"
    answer: "A single short overstay resolved with fine payment typically does not permanently bar future UAE visa applications, but it is recorded. Subsequent visa applications, for new tourist visas, residency, or employment visas, go through the same GDRFA and ICP systems that hold your travel history. Officers reviewing your application can see past overstays. A pattern of violations, or a long unresolved overstay, significantly reduces the likelihood of visa approval. Keeping a clean record is always preferable to relying on the discretionary nature of post-overstay applications."
  - question: "What is the overstay grace period for UAE residency visa holders?"

This applies to employment visa cancellations, company liquidations, and expired residency permits. During the grace period, you are technically out of status but protected from fines. After day 30, the standard AED 50 per day fine applies. Residency holders who become unemployed but want to remain in the UAE should initiate a status change or apply for a new permit well before the 30-day window closes."

Every person entering the UAE receives a visa with a fixed validity period. For most tourist and visit visas, this is either 30 or 60 days, which counts from the date of entry, not from the date the visa was issued. Once you have used your permitted days, the visa expires. The overstay clock begins the moment the visa lapses, subject to the applicable grace period.

The UAE takes visa compliance seriously. The country hosts over 200 nationalities, processes tens of millions of annual arrivals, and runs one of the world's most active air transit hubs. Enforcement is systematic, not discretionary. Your entry and exit dates are stamped and digitally recorded at every port of entry, and departure records are matched against arrival records at the border.

This guide covers every visa category and scenario: tourist visas, visit visas, residency visas, employment visas, and the specific rules for residence cancellations, which operate differently from simple tourist overstays. "I got an entry stamp but my visa wasn't in my passport"

Visitors from many nationalities receive visa-on-arrival or ETA (Electronic Travel Authorisation) status. The permitted stay duration is embedded in the border system, not on a physical sticker. Your permitted days begin from your physical entry date. Check your entry stamp or GDRFA portal record to confirm your exact departure deadline.

"I entered a free zone, does that reset my stay?"

No. Free zone areas in the UAE are still within UAE territory. Moving between the mainland and a free zone, or spending time in a free zone, does not constitute a border crossing that resets your visa duration.

"I overstayed but left by land to Oman, does that count?"

Yes. All UAE land border crossings (Hatta, Wadi Helo, Khatmat Malaha, and others) are staffed by UAE immigration and connected to the same GDRFA/ICP systems. Overstays are flagged at land borders exactly as they are at airports. You cannot exit through a land border to avoid fine payment.
