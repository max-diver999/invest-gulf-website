## How safe is Dubai compared with other global cities?

Dubai is among the safest large cities for expats in 2026, with a Numbeo Crime Index near 15.2 and about 165,000 CCTV cameras citywide. Violent crime remains rare versus peer metros. Invest Gulf clients typically budget risk around roads and scams for the first 12 months, not street violence.

| Index | Dubai/UAE position (2026) | What it measures |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Economist Safe Cities Index | 7th globally (2025) | Infrastructure, personal, digital, health security |
| Numbeo Crime Index | 15.2 (very low) | Citizen perception of crime levels (lower = safer) |
| Global Peace Index (UAE) | 34th out of 163 countries | Conflict, safety, militarisation |
| Mercer Quality of Living | UAE ranks 74th globally | Safety as one component |

Dubai operates approximately 165,000 CCTV cameras across the emirate as of 2026, creating one of the world's highest surveillance densities at roughly 1 camera per 20 residents. These rankings reflect real conditions for the resident population. The main caveats: expatriates in certain industries (construction, hospitality) face different exposure than office and professional workers in established residential communities.

Dubai safety for relocating expats in 2026 means a city where violent street crime is uncommon, while measured indices still show a Numbeo Crime Index near 15.2 and an Economist Safe Cities ranking inside the global top 10. Roughly 165,000 CCTV cameras operate across the emirate, supporting rapid police response when residents dial 999 for emergencies or 998 for ambulance and fire. Invest Gulf relocation files show most newcomer incidents cluster around phone snatching in tourist crowds, rental deposit fraud, and road collisions rather than home invasions. Family communities such as Arabian Ranches and The Springs report very low violent crime, whereas Deira and Naif markets need bag and phone discipline. Practical baseline: save emergency numbers on day one, install the Dubai Police app, and treat cyber impersonation calls as seriously as physical risk during the first 12 months.

## What are the main safety risks for expats in Dubai?

Petty theft, rental scams, and road incidents are the three risks we see most often for new residents, not violent street crime. Tourist volume creates more scam vectors than quieter Gulf hubs. Road exposure includes AED 800 mobile-phone fines and frequent high-speed motorway driving each week.

The shift from Abu Dhabi to Dubai in crime exposure: Dubai's higher tourist volume and international connectivity create more scam vectors. Abu Dhabi's quieter profile means slightly fewer petty theft incidents in practice.

Tourist-dense Deira and Naif typically show higher pickpocketing risk than gated family communities such as Arabian Ranches, where violent crime stays rare across 12 months. The area around Marina and JBR needs phone awareness after dark. Invest Gulf briefings treat those promenades as caution zones for the first 90 days.

| Deira / Naif markets | Higher petty theft | Watch bags and phones in crowds |
| JBR / Marina promenade | Phone snatching at night | Use lit routes, app taxis |
| Arabian Ranches / Springs | Very low violent crime | Standard home security still wise |

Phone snatching clusters after 10 pm on busy promenades; family villa communities often go 12 months without a violent crime report that reaches newcomers' WhatsApp groups. Keep bags zipped and phones off café tables for the first 30 days while you learn crowd patterns.

## Is Dubai safe for women living alone?

Dubai is one of the safer global cities for women living alone, with criminal penalties for public harassment and women-only metro carriages in daily use across this market. The Dubai Foundation hotline operates 24 hours for urgent support. Our clients typically save that number in the first 7 days. For family area context, see [Dubai neighbourhoods for families](/guides/dubai-neighbourhoods-for-families/).

Legal protections:

- Harassment in public space is a criminal offence, report to 999 or Dubai Police app
- Workplace harassment covered under UAE Labour Law, HR and Labour Ministry channels available
- Domestic violence is illegal, Dubai Foundation for Women and Children operates a 24-hour hotline: **800 988**
- Metro women-only carriages run on the Red and Green lines during peak hours most weekdays

Practical tips for women new to Dubai:

- Save the Dubai Foundation for Women and Children hotline: 800 988
- Know your building security desk and neighbours
- In tourist areas, use headphones and maintain phone awareness
- Trust your instincts, the same awareness rules apply here as in any city

## How safe are Dubai roads for new expat drivers?

Dubai roads require defensive driving from day one because motorway limits run 100-140 km/h, tailgating is common, and roughly 40% of accidents occur after sunset. Mobile phone use draws an AED 800 fine. Our checklists put dashcams ahead of neighbourhood crime worry for new drivers.

| Factor | Situation |
| --- | --- |
| Speed | UAE motorways have 100-140 km/h limits; tailgating common |
| Fines | Mobile phone use AED 800 fine, seat belt violations AED 400 |
| Tourist driver mix | Approximately 2.8 million rental car days annually in Dubai |
| Weather | Sand haze and rare rain on polished roads creates sudden hazard |
| Night driving | Some outer roads poorly lit; 40% of accidents occur after sunset |

Defensive driving practices:

- Maintain double the following distance you would at equivalent speeds in Europe or North America
- Move to the middle lane on SZR during peak hours to avoid merging conflicts
- Do not drive in the outer right lane of SZR near interchanges, merge conflicts highest there
- Avoid driving immediately after rain, roads become slippery before water clears

Accident procedure:

- Minor collision with no injuries: move cars to side, use Dubai Police app to file e-accident report
- Injury or serious damage: call 999, do not move vehicles
- Your insurer should receive notification within 24 hours of any incident

## What scams target new expats in Dubai?

Rental deposit fraud, fake investment schemes, and government-impersonation calls are the scam patterns we flag first for newcomers, ahead of street robbery. Fake Dubizzle deposits remain common each quarter across tourist areas. Protection means Ejari-linked payments only and DFSA checks before any AED 1,000 transfer.

Property and rental scams:

- Fake listings on Dubizzle or WhatsApp asking for deposit transfers before viewing
- "Agents" asking for holding fee via bank transfer with no contract
- Landlords misrepresenting community or building facilities

**Protection:** Only pay via Ejari-linked transaction or official channel. Never transfer money before physically viewing and signing a contract. See [Dubai relocation guide](/guides/dubai-relocation-guide/) for renting safely.

Investment fraud:

- Unsolicited calls or WhatsApp messages offering crypto, forex, or property investment guarantees
- Fake Dubai government investment scheme branding
- "Golden Visa" processors requesting upfront fee for processing before meeting

**Protection:** Verify any investment offer via Dubai Financial Services Authority (DFSA) or Securities and Commodities Authority (SCA) registration check.

Phone impersonation scams:

| Scam type | Warning signs |
| --- | --- |
| "Dubai Police" calling about your visa | Real police do not call about visa status |
| "Customs" package scam | Real customs send official mail |
| Bank fraud team | Call your bank's official number to verify |
| Lottery winnings | UAE does not operate surprise foreign lotteries |

If you receive a suspicious call: hang up, call the official number from the organisation's real website, report via ecrime.ae.

New expat scam exposure in Dubai concentrates on three cash paths that our underwriting reviews before relocation: fake rental deposits on Dubizzle or WhatsApp, unsolicited crypto or property "guarantees," and callers claiming to be Dubai Police, customs, or your bank. Violent street crime is rare, yet a single rushed transfer of AED 5,000 to 50,000 can erase months of savings in under 10 minutes. Ejari-linked tenancy payments, DFSA or SCA registration checks, and hanging up on visa scare calls are the practical controls. Lottery messages and Golden Visa processors asking for fees before a meeting are almost always fraud. Report attempts via ecrime.ae and keep the Dubai Police app installed so a non-emergency file can be opened within minutes rather than waiting days for a station visit.

Urban ambulance targets in Dubai are typically under 8 minutes for life-threatening calls, with roughly 180 ambulances across about 40 stations and a stated aim of under 6 minutes for 90% of calls. Private transport can cost AED 150-250. Our clients carry insurance cards from week one.

Ambulance dispatch: 998. Dubai Hospital's emergency department handles over 200,000 cases annually. For non-life-threatening transport, private ambulances or taxi may be faster in some suburban areas.

Public intoxication, bounced cheques, and social media defamation create more expat legal pain than random street crime, and drug possession remains zero tolerance even for traces. Cheque cases can stretch for 6 months or longer. Invest Gulf briefings treat consulate numbers as part of the 30 days checklist.

| Situation | Dubai law |
| --- | --- |
| Alcohol-related public incident | Serious, even with valid licence, public intoxication is illegal |
| Social media complaints about local business | Can be considered defamation, use official complaint channels |
| Photography of people without consent | May constitute privacy breach, ask before photographing locals |
| Debt and bounced cheques | Cheque fraud is a criminal matter, manage finances carefully |
| Drug possession | Zero tolerance, even trace amounts |
| Relationship outside marriage | Complex legal area, understand before any dispute |

If you are detained or arrested: Contact your home country's consulate or embassy immediately. Consulates in Dubai include:

Document retention matters: keep police report PDFs for at least 12 months for insurance, and notify your insurer within 24 hours of any vehicle claim. Social media posts that name a local business can become a defamation file within days, so use official complaint channels instead of public threads.

## What safety habits matter most in your first year?

Digital hygiene, app-based transport, and controlled home access typically prevent most incidents newcomers face in the first 12 months. Enable two-factor authentication before arrival and avoid public Wi-Fi for transfers above AED 500. Our checklists also ban lending your UAE SIM to others. Budget context: [Dubai monthly budget for expat families](/guides/dubai-monthly-budget-expat-family/).

- Enable two-factor authentication on all financial apps
- Do not use public Wi-Fi for banking
- Register UAE SIM under your name only, do not lend SIM to others
- Change passwords on email and banking apps before arriving

**Home safety:**

- Know your building's security contact
- Do not share exact address publicly on social media
- Confirm contractor identity before letting anyone into your flat
- Keep valuables in a safe or locked cabinet

Street and social safety:

- Avoid public intoxication, legal exposure beyond just embarrassment
- Do not leave valuables visible in parked cars, common target at tourist beaches
- In markets and souks, carry bag in front; keep phone in pocket
- Use authorised taxi stands or app-based transport, avoid unofficial taxis

**Vehicle safety:**

- Lock car before walking away, even briefly
- Do not leave engine running unattended
- Park in lit areas at night
- Dashboard camera is recommended, useful for insurance and fine disputes

Insider tip: Save 999, 998, and 800 988 in favourites on day one, then walk your night route home once with an app taxi so the first late return is not also your first navigation test.
