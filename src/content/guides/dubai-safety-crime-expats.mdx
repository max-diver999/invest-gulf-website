# Dubai Safety for Expats 2026: Crime, Women's Safety & Emergency Guide

Quick answer: Dubai is one of the world's safest cities by measured crime indices. Violent crime is rare; petty theft, scams, and road accidents are the realistic risks. Emergency: call 999 (police), 998 (ambulance/fire).

TL;DR: Dubai operates under strict law enforcement with extensive CCTV coverage, making street crime and violent incidents uncommon compared to most global cities. Expats' real risks are scams targeting newcomers, traffic accidents, and digital fraud. This guide covers safety data, neighbourhood assessments, women's safety, emergency contacts, and practical tips to protect yourself in the first year. For broader relocation context, see [Dubai relocation guide](/guides/dubai-relocation-guide/) and [Dubai culture guide](/guides/dubai-culture-etiquette-expats/).

## Dubai safety rankings: what the data says

Dubai regularly appears in the top tier of global safety indices. The UAE ranks 34th out of 163 countries on the Global Peace Index 2026, placing it in the most peaceful quartile globally. Understanding what these rankings measure helps calibrate expectations.

| Index | Dubai/UAE position (2026) | What it measures |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Economist Safe Cities Index | 7th globally (2025) | Infrastructure, personal, digital, health security |
| Numbeo Crime Index | 15.2 (very low) | Citizen perception of crime levels (lower = safer) |
| Global Peace Index (UAE) | 34th out of 163 countries | Conflict, safety, militarisation |
| Mercer Quality of Living | UAE ranks 74th globally | Safety as one component |

Dubai operates approximately 165,000 CCTV cameras across the emirate as of 2026, creating one of the world's highest surveillance densities at roughly 1 camera per 20 residents. These rankings reflect real conditions for the resident population. The main caveats: expatriates in certain industries (construction, hospitality) face different exposure than office and professional workers in established residential communities.

## Women's safety in Dubai

Dubai is considered one of the more welcoming major cities globally for women living and working independently.

**Daily life safety:**

- Street harassment is uncommon, authorities take harassment reports seriously
- Women-only metro carriages available on all Red and Green Line trains (front and rear carriages)
- Pink taxis operated by female drivers available via app (Dubai Taxi app has filter)
- Solo walking at night in established areas is generally safe

**Legal protections:**

- Harassment in public space is a criminal offence, report to 999 or Dubai Police app
- Workplace harassment covered under UAE Labour Law, HR and Labour Ministry channels available
- Domestic violence is illegal, Dubai Foundation for Women and Children operates a 24-hour hotline: **800 988**

**Practical tips for women new to Dubai:**

- Save the Dubai Foundation for Women and Children hotline: 800 988
- Know your building security desk and neighbours
- In tourist areas, use headphones and maintain phone awareness
- Trust your instincts, the same awareness rules apply here as in any city

## Road safety: the overlooked risk

Traffic accidents are the most statistically likely safety event for Dubai expats, with approximately 350-400 road fatalities annually across the UAE (as of 2026 statistics). Dubai Police respond to roughly 140,000 traffic incidents per year, with response times averaging 4-6 minutes in central areas. The combination of high speeds, lane-changing norms, mobile phone use despite AED 800 fines, and mixed driver experience creates genuine risk.

| Factor | Situation |
| --- | --- |
| Speed | UAE motorways have 100–140 km/h limits; tailgating common |
| Fines | Mobile phone use AED 800 fine, seat belt violations AED 400 |
| Tourist driver mix | Approximately 2.8 million rental car days annually in Dubai |
| Weather | Sand haze and rare rain on polished roads creates sudden hazard |
| Night driving | Some outer roads poorly lit; 40% of accidents occur after sunset |

**Defensive driving practices:**

- Maintain double the following distance you would at equivalent speeds in Europe or North America
- Move to the middle lane on SZR during peak hours to avoid merging conflicts
- Do not drive in the outer right lane of SZR near interchanges, merge conflicts highest there
- Avoid driving immediately after rain, roads become slippery before water clears

**Accident procedure:**

- Minor collision with no injuries: move cars to side, use Dubai Police app to file e-accident report
- Injury or serious damage: call 999, do not move vehicles
- Your insurer should receive notification within 24 hours of any incident

## Scams targeting new expats: know before you arrive

The most frequent reports from new Dubai arrivals involve financial scams rather than physical crime.

**Property and rental scams:**

- Fake listings on Dubizzle or WhatsApp asking for deposit transfers before viewing
- "Agents" asking for holding fee via bank transfer with no contract
- Landlords misrepresenting community or building facilities

**Protection:** Only pay via Ejari-linked transaction or official channel. Never transfer money before physically viewing and signing a contract. See [Dubai relocation guide](/guides/dubai-relocation-guide/) for renting safely.

**Investment fraud:**

- Unsolicited calls or WhatsApp messages offering crypto, forex, or property investment guarantees
- Fake Dubai government investment scheme branding
- "Golden Visa" processors requesting upfront fee for processing before meeting

**Protection:** Verify any investment offer via Dubai Financial Services Authority (DFSA) or Securities and Commodities Authority (SCA) registration check.

**Phone impersonation scams:**

| Scam type | Warning signs |
| --- | --- |
| "Dubai Police" calling about your visa | Real police do not call about visa status |
| "Customs" package scam | Real customs send official mail |
| Bank fraud team | Call your bank's official number to verify |
| Lottery winnings | UAE does not operate surprise foreign lotteries |

If you receive a suspicious call: hang up, call the official number from the organisation's real website, report via ecrime.ae.

## Cybercrime and digital safety

Dubai Cybercrime department (TDRA) handles digital fraud reports at ecrime.ae. Common cases:

| Type | How to avoid |
| --- | --- |
| WhatsApp scam asking for "OTP" | Never share OTP, not even to "bank staff" |
| Social media investment groups | Verify all investment platforms via DFSA |
| Fake job offers requiring fee | Legitimate employers never charge fees |
| Romance scams | Do not send money to online contacts you have not met |
| SIM swap fraud | Enable SIM swap PIN via Etisalat/du |

**Reporting:** ecrime.ae or Dubai Police cybercrime unit. File a report immediately, time is critical for financial recovery.

## Practical safety habits for year one

**Digital safety:**

- Enable two-factor authentication on all financial apps
- Do not use public Wi-Fi for banking
- Register UAE SIM under your name only, do not lend SIM to others
- Change passwords on email and banking apps before arriving

**Home safety:**

- Know your building's security contact
- Do not share exact address publicly on social media
- Confirm contractor identity before letting anyone into your flat
- Keep valuables in a safe or locked cabinet

**Street and social safety:**

- Avoid public intoxication, legal exposure beyond just embarrassment
- Do not leave valuables visible in parked cars, common target at tourist beaches
- In markets and souks, carry bag in front; keep phone in pocket
- Use authorised taxi stands or app-based transport, avoid unofficial taxis

**Vehicle safety:**

- Lock car before walking away, even briefly
- Do not leave engine running unattended
- Park in lit areas at night
- Dashboard camera is recommended, useful for insurance and fine disputes

## FAQ

**Should I be worried about terrorism in Dubai?**
The UAE's security apparatus is extensive and threat levels are consistently managed. Exercise awareness in crowded public spaces as you would in any major city. Follow government travel advisories from your home country.

**Are there areas in Dubai I should avoid at night?**
No absolute no-go zones. Exercise the same street awareness you would in any large city. Al Quoz industrial areas and poorly lit side streets in older Deira are best avoided late at night without reason.

**Can I trust Dubai taxis?**
RTA-licensed Dubai Taxis and app-based Careem/Uber are trustworthy. Avoid unlicensed drivers offering rides outside airports or venues. Women prefer booking app taxis for the trip record and driver ID.

**What happens if I lose my passport in Dubai?**
Report to Dubai Police first, get a police report. Then contact your consulate for emergency travel document. Do not travel without a valid travel document, report immediately.

**Is Dubai safe during Ramadan?**
Yes, Ramadan often reduces traffic and changes social patterns but does not affect physical safety. Avoid eating or drinking in public during daylight during Ramadan out of respect. See [Dubai culture guide](/guides/dubai-culture-etiquette-expats/).

**How do I report a scam I fell victim to?**
File immediately at ecrime.ae and at the nearest Dubai Police station. If financial fraud, notify your bank simultaneously to attempt transaction reversal. Keep all communications with the scammer as evidence.

**Is the healthcare system equipped for emergencies?**
Yes, Dubai's hospital system handles trauma and emergency medicine to international standards. Government and private hospitals both have 24-hour emergency departments. Carry your insurance card at all times. See [Dubai healthcare guide](/guides/gulf-healthcare-comparison/) for detail.
