Not every UAE resident can sponsor a domestic worker. The following requirements apply:

Residency status: The sponsor must hold a valid UAE residency visa. Tourists, visit visa holders, and individuals without residency status cannot sponsor domestic workers.

Income threshold: GDRFA requires sponsors to demonstrate financial capacity to support a domestic worker. The commonly applied informal minimum is AED 6,000/month in confirmed salary. This is typically evidenced through an employment contract, bank statements, or a salary certificate from the employer. Self-employed individuals and company owners demonstrate income through trade licence documents and bank statements.

Accommodation standard: The sponsor must be able to provide adequate accommodation for the domestic worker, a private room within the sponsor's residence. This is assessed informally; there is no mandatory inspection, but inadequate accommodation can be grounds for complaint-based intervention by MOHRE if reported.

No labour violations: Employers with outstanding MOHRE labour violations or blacklisting may not be eligible to sponsor new domestic workers. Clear status can be checked through MOHRE smart services.

One worker per 3 residents: There is a guideline ratio limiting the number of domestic workers per household relative to family size. In practice, this is rarely a binding constraint for typical families, but large households or those requesting multiple domestic workers may face review.

### Route 2: Transfer From Another Employer Within UAE

If the worker is already inside the UAE on a valid domestic worker visa and wishes to change employer, the transfer route applies.

Eligibility for transfer:
- Worker is on a valid, unexpired domestic worker visa
- Current employer provides a No Objection Certificate (NOC), OR worker has completed at least 6 months of employment and has grounds for transfer under MOHRE rules
- No ongoing labour dispute registered against the worker

1. Confirm worker's current visa status through GDRFA system (worker provides visa copy, Emirates ID)
2. Apply for MOHRE approval for transfer through the smart services portal
3. New employment contract drafted and registered with MOHRE
4. GDRFA updates residency sponsorship to new employer
5. New Emirates ID issued under new sponsor
6. MOHRE domestic worker card updated

Transfer processing time: 1–3 weeks, significantly faster than overseas recruitment.

All domestic worker employment must be formalised through an MOHRE-registered contract. The standard MOHRE contract template is available through the MOHRE smart services portal (smartservices.mohre.gov.ae) and must include:

- Full identification details of employer and worker
- Job title and full description of duties
- Monthly salary (in AED, with payment method)
- Working hours and weekly rest day
- Accommodation and meal provision details
- Annual leave entitlement
- Annual flight entitlement
- Contract start date and duration (typically 2 years)
- Notice period for termination

**Important:** Oral or informal contracts are not recognised for visa processing. The MOHRE registration generates a contract ID that is referenced in subsequent GDRFA applications.

### GDRFA Entry Permit (For Overseas Recruitment)

The GDRFA entry permit is the immigration document that allows a domestic worker to enter the UAE on the domestic worker visa category. It is applied for by the employer (or their PRO service) through the GDRFA smart services portal.

**Processing time:** 3–7 working days for standard applications, 1–3 days with express service (additional fee applies)
**Validity:** 60 days from issue; the worker must enter the UAE within this window
**Electronic format:** Entry permit is issued electronically and sent to both employer and the worker's source country

### Medical Fitness Test

All domestic workers must pass a UAE Medical Fitness Test at a MOHRE-approved medical centre. The test screens for:

- Tuberculosis (chest X-ray)
- Communicable diseases (including HIV)
- Fitness for work in Dubai conditions

A negative result issues the Medical Fitness Certificate required for Emirates ID and visa processing. Workers who test positive for certain conditions are not eligible for UAE residency, this is a non-negotiable requirement.

**Cost:** AED 300–700 depending on the medical centre and any additional tests required
**Processing time:** Results typically within 2–5 working days; express results available at some centres for an additional fee

Workers who completed pre-departure medical screening in their source country through a UAE-approved panel physician may have reduced testing requirements upon arrival, but UAE medical clearance is still mandatory.

### Emirates ID

Once the medical fitness certificate is obtained, the employer submits an Emirates ID application through an ICA service centre (formerly EIDA). Biometrics (fingerprint and photo) are captured in person.

**Emirates ID fee:** AED 370 (standard 2-year ID)
**Processing time:** 3–7 working days; express processing available

### Residency Visa Stamping

The final step in the visa process is residency visa stamping in the worker's passport, performed by GDRFA. This can be done at a GDRFA service centre or through authorised typing centres and PRO services.

**Cost:** AED 500–1,200 depending on visa category and service charges

### What nationalities are eligible for domestic worker visas from Dubai?

The UAE has bilateral labour agreements with Philippines, Sri Lanka, India, Indonesia, Ethiopia, Kenya, and several other countries. Workers from these countries are the most common source. ### Can I hire two domestic workers?

Yes, provided you meet income requirements (typically AED 12,000–15,000+/month for two workers) and have adequate accommodation for both. Each worker requires their own visa, contract, and MOHRE registration.

### What if the domestic worker wants to leave before the contract ends?
