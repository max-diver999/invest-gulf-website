# Dubai Domestic Worker Visa: Complete 2026 Application Guide

TL;DR: Sponsoring a domestic worker visa in Dubai costs AED 5,000–8,500 all-in for the first issue and requires you to be a UAE resident earning approximately AED 6,000+/month. The process involves MOHRE contract registration, GDRFA entry permit, source-country or in-country medical clearance, Emirates ID, and residency visa stamping, typically 4–8 weeks for an overseas recruit. The process is simpler and faster for workers already inside the UAE (transfer route). Once active, the visa is valid for 2 years and renewal is straightforward.

> Legal disclaimer: UAE domestic worker visa regulations are updated periodically by GDRFA (General Directorate of Residency and Foreigners Affairs) and MOHRE (Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation). This guide reflects the process as of June 2026. Verify current fees and steps through the official MOHRE smart services portal or GDRFA Dubai before initiating an application.

## Who Can Sponsor a Domestic Worker Visa

### Eligibility Requirements for Employers

Not every UAE resident can sponsor a domestic worker. The following requirements apply:

Residency status: The sponsor must hold a valid UAE residency visa. Tourists, visit visa holders, and individuals without residency status cannot sponsor domestic workers.

Income threshold: GDRFA requires sponsors to demonstrate financial capacity to support a domestic worker. The commonly applied informal minimum is AED 6,000/month in confirmed salary. This is typically evidenced through an employment contract, bank statements, or a salary certificate from the employer. Self-employed individuals and company owners demonstrate income through trade licence documents and bank statements.

Accommodation standard: The sponsor must be able to provide adequate accommodation for the domestic worker, a private room within the sponsor's residence. This is assessed informally; there is no mandatory inspection, but inadequate accommodation can be grounds for complaint-based intervention by MOHRE if reported.

No labour violations: Employers with outstanding MOHRE labour violations or blacklisting may not be eligible to sponsor new domestic workers. Clear status can be checked through MOHRE smart services.

One worker per 3 residents: There is a guideline ratio limiting the number of domestic workers per household relative to family size. In practice, this is rarely a binding constraint for typical families, but large households or those requesting multiple domestic workers may face review.

## The Two Main Routes

### Route 1: Sponsoring From Overseas

The most common route when hiring a new worker who is currently outside the UAE. The process involves source-country recruitment and pre-entry processing.

Steps:

1. Contract a licensed recruitment agency in the source country (Philippines, Sri Lanka, India, Indonesia, Ethiopia, Kenya, and others)
2. Worker vetting and pre-screening by the source country agency
3. Employment contract drafted and attested by the source country Ministry of Labour / POLO (Philippines Overseas Labour Office) or equivalent
4. MOHRE contract registration in UAE: register the approved employment contract through MOHRE's smart services portal
5. Apply for GDRFA entry permit: the immigration entry permit that allows the worker to enter the UAE on the domestic worker visa category
6. Worker undergoes medical test in source country (for certain nationalities) or upon arrival in UAE
7. Worker travels to UAE on entry permit
8. Medical fitness test in UAE at an approved MOHRE medical centre
9. Emirates ID biometrics at an ICA (Identity, Citizenship, Customs and Ports Authority) service centre
10. Residency visa stamped in the worker's passport through GDRFA
11. Domestic worker card issued through MOHRE

### Route 2: Transfer From Another Employer Within UAE

If the worker is already inside the UAE on a valid domestic worker visa and wishes to change employer, the transfer route applies.

Eligibility for transfer:
- Worker is on a valid, unexpired domestic worker visa
- Current employer provides a No Objection Certificate (NOC), OR worker has completed at least 6 months of employment and has grounds for transfer under MOHRE rules
- No ongoing labour dispute registered against the worker

Steps:

1. Confirm worker's current visa status through GDRFA system (worker provides visa copy, Emirates ID)
2. Apply for MOHRE approval for transfer through the smart services portal
3. New employment contract drafted and registered with MOHRE
4. GDRFA updates residency sponsorship to new employer
5. New Emirates ID issued under new sponsor
6. MOHRE domestic worker card updated

Transfer processing time: 1–3 weeks, significantly faster than overseas recruitment.

## Step-by-Step: Key Stages Explained

### MOHRE Contract Registration

All domestic worker employment must be formalised through an MOHRE-registered contract. The standard MOHRE contract template is available through the MOHRE smart services portal (smartservices.mohre.gov.ae) and must include:

- Full identification details of employer and worker
- Job title and full description of duties
- Monthly salary (in AED, with payment method)
- Working hours and weekly rest day
- Accommodation and meal provision details
- Annual leave entitlement
- Annual flight entitlement
- Contract start date and duration (typically 2 years)
- Notice period for termination

**Important:** Oral or informal contracts are not recognised for visa processing. The MOHRE registration generates a contract ID that is referenced in subsequent GDRFA applications.

### GDRFA Entry Permit (For Overseas Recruitment)

The GDRFA entry permit is the immigration document that allows a domestic worker to enter the UAE on the domestic worker visa category. It is applied for by the employer (or their PRO service) through the GDRFA smart services portal.

**Processing time:** 3–7 working days for standard applications, 1–3 days with express service (additional fee applies)
**Validity:** 60 days from issue; the worker must enter the UAE within this window
**Electronic format:** Entry permit is issued electronically and sent to both employer and the worker's source country

### Medical Fitness Test

All domestic workers must pass a UAE Medical Fitness Test at a MOHRE-approved medical centre. The test screens for:

- Tuberculosis (chest X-ray)
- Communicable diseases (including HIV)
- Fitness for work in Dubai conditions

A negative result issues the Medical Fitness Certificate required for Emirates ID and visa processing. Workers who test positive for certain conditions are not eligible for UAE residency, this is a non-negotiable requirement.

**Cost:** AED 300–700 depending on the medical centre and any additional tests required
**Processing time:** Results typically within 2–5 working days; express results available at some centres for an additional fee

Workers who completed pre-departure medical screening in their source country through a UAE-approved panel physician may have reduced testing requirements upon arrival, but UAE medical clearance is still mandatory.

### Emirates ID

Once the medical fitness certificate is obtained, the employer submits an Emirates ID application through an ICA service centre (formerly EIDA). Biometrics (fingerprint and photo) are captured in person.

**Emirates ID fee:** AED 370 (standard 2-year ID)
**Processing time:** 3–7 working days; express processing available

### Residency Visa Stamping

The final step in the visa process is residency visa stamping in the worker's passport, performed by GDRFA. This can be done at a GDRFA service centre or through authorised typing centres and PRO services.

**Cost:** AED 500–1,200 depending on visa category and service charges

## Using a PRO or Typing Service

The domestic worker visa process involves multiple government portals, service centres, and document requirements across MOHRE, GDRFA, ICA, and approved medical centres. Many employers, particularly those doing this for the first time, use a PRO (Public Relations Officer) service or licensed typing centre to manage the process.

**PRO service cost:** AED 500–1,500 per application, all-in
**What they handle:** Portal submissions, document checking, appointment scheduling, physical attendance at service centres on your behalf, status tracking

PRO services save time and reduce the risk of application rejection due to document errors. For employers managing their first domestic worker sponsorship, the cost is generally worthwhile.

## Ongoing Employer Obligations

Once the visa is active, UAE law imposes ongoing employer obligations:

**Monthly salary payment:** Must be paid on time through the UAE Wage Protection System (WPS). Delayed payment triggers MOHRE alerts and potential fines.

**Medical insurance:** Employer must maintain valid medical insurance for the domestic worker throughout the visa period. Lapsing insurance is a labour violation.

**Annual leave and flights:** Workers are entitled to 30 calendar days of annual leave after each completed year of service, plus a return flight to their home country. These must be provided when requested and cannot be withheld as a control mechanism.

**Rest day:** One full rest day per week is mandatory. Many domestic workers prefer Friday as their rest day; some prefer other days aligned with their community activities. The specific day is agreed in the employment contract.

**Safe and adequate accommodation:** The worker must be accommodated in the sponsor's residence with adequate privacy, ventilation, and space. Workers can file complaints with MOHRE regarding accommodation that does not meet basic standards.

## Domestic Worker Rights and Dispute Resolution

UAE domestic workers have legal rights and access to dispute resolution mechanisms:

**MOHRE hotline:** 800 60, workers can call to report labour violations, late salary, withholding of documents, or physical mistreatment

**MOHRE smart services portal:** Workers can file formal complaints online

**Shelter centres:** MOHRE operates approved shelter centres where domestic workers in distress can be accommodated during dispute resolution

**Early termination:** Workers can request early termination and transfer to a new employer if the employment contract is violated by the employer. MOHRE mediates disputes.

Employers who confiscate passports (a violation of UAE law), withhold salary, or prevent workers from contacting family face MOHRE sanctions and criminal liability in serious cases.

## Visa Cancellation

When employment ends, whether the contract term expires, the worker returns home, or you leave the UAE, the domestic worker visa must be cancelled through GDRFA. Steps:

1. **MOHRE contract termination registration** through smart services
2. **GDRFA visa cancellation** application
3. **Emirates ID cancellation** through ICA
4. **Exit visa stamped** if the worker is leaving the country (or 30-day overstay window applies automatically post-cancellation in some cases)
5. **End-of-service gratuity payment** calculated and processed

Delays in cancellation after employment ends expose employers to ongoing legal obligations and MOHRE fees.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I hire a domestic worker on a family visitor visa rather than a domestic worker visa?

No. Domestic workers who are employed must be on domestic worker residency visas. Having a worker on a family or tourist visa while employed is a violation of UAE labour law and immigration regulations. Penalties apply to both employer and worker.

### What nationalities are eligible for domestic worker visas from Dubai?

The UAE has bilateral labour agreements with Philippines, Sri Lanka, India, Indonesia, Ethiopia, Kenya, and several other countries. Workers from these countries are the most common source. Workers from some nationalities may face additional processing requirements or restrictions at certain periods, check current MOHRE guidance.

### Can I hire two domestic workers?

Yes, provided you meet income requirements (typically AED 12,000–15,000+/month for two workers) and have adequate accommodation for both. Each worker requires their own visa, contract, and MOHRE registration.

### What if the domestic worker wants to leave before the contract ends?

Either party can terminate the contract with the required notice period (usually 30–60 days as specified in the contract). If the employer has violated contract terms, the worker may terminate immediately. MOHRE mediates disputes where the parties disagree. End-of-service gratuity is payable on a pro-rata basis for service periods of less than one year.
