> Legal disclaimer: UAE domestic worker visa regulations are updated periodically by GDRFA (General Directorate of Residency and Foreigners Affairs) and MOHRE (Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation). Sponsoring a domestic worker in Dubai is an immigration and labour process, not a property transaction. You need valid UAE residency, a registered MOHRE employment contract, GDRFA approvals, medical clearance, and Emirates ID before the worker can live and work legally in your household. Total first-year government and medical costs typically run AED 5,000-8,500 plus any recruitment agency fees overseas.

## Who can sponsor a domestic worker visa?

Not every UAE resident can sponsor. Dubai employers (household sponsors) must meet MOHRE and GDRFA criteria:

**Residency status:** Valid UAE residency visa. Tourists, visit visa holders, and individuals without residency cannot sponsor domestic workers.

**Income threshold:** GDRFA expects financial capacity to support a domestic worker. The commonly applied informal minimum is AED 6,000/month in confirmed salary, shown via employment contract, salary certificate, or bank statements. Self-employed sponsors use trade licence documents and bank statements.

**Accommodation:** Adequate accommodation for the worker, typically a private room within the sponsor's residence. Inadequate housing can trigger MOHRE complaints if reported.

**Labour standing:** Employers with outstanding MOHRE violations or blacklisting may not sponsor new workers until cleared.

**Household ratio:** Guidelines limit domestic workers relative to household size. Typical families rarely hit the cap; large households requesting multiple workers may face review.

Property ownership is not required. Renters with valid residency and income evidence can sponsor.

## Overseas recruitment vs transfer within the UAE

### Route 1: Hire from abroad

1. Contract a licensed recruitment agency in the source country (Philippines, Sri Lanka, India, Indonesia, Ethiopia, Kenya, and others).
2. Worker vetting and pre-screening in the source country.
3. Employment contract drafted and attested by the source country labour ministry or POLO equivalent.
4. MOHRE contract registration in UAE through smart services.
5. GDRFA entry permit application.
6. Medical screening (source country for some nationalities, UAE upon arrival for all).
7. Worker travels on entry permit (usually 60-day validity).
8. UAE medical fitness test at approved centre.
9. Emirates ID biometrics at ICA.
10. Residency visa stamping through GDRFA.
11. MOHRE domestic worker card issued.

Timeline: 8-16 weeks including source country processing.

### Route 2: Transfer from another UAE employer

Eligibility:

- Valid, unexpired domestic worker visa.
- Current employer NOC, or minimum service period met under MOHRE transfer rules.
- No active labour dispute on the worker file.

Steps: confirm GDRFA status, MOHRE transfer approval, new contract registration, sponsorship update, new Emirates ID if required, card update.

Timeline: 1-3 weeks, significantly faster than overseas recruitment.

## Step-by-step: MOHRE contract and GDRFA entry permit

**MOHRE contract:** Oral agreements are not recognised. Registration generates a contract ID referenced in all GDRFA steps. Standard fields include salary (must meet UAE minimums for domestic workers), rest day, annual leave, and return flight entitlement.

**GDRFA entry permit (overseas hires):** Applied through GDRFA smart services or an authorised typing centre.

- Processing: 3-7 working days standard, express available for extra fee.
- Validity: typically 60 days; worker must enter UAE within the window.
- Delivery: electronic permit to employer and worker.

## Medical fitness test and Emirates ID

All domestic workers pass a UAE medical fitness test at a MOHRE-approved centre:

- Tuberculosis screening (chest X-ray).
- Communicable disease panel including HIV.
- Fitness for employment in UAE conditions.

Cost: AED 300-700. Results usually within 2-5 working days.

Negative result issues the medical certificate required for Emirates ID and visa stamping. Positive results for certain conditions block residency; this is non-negotiable under current health policy.

Emirates ID: AED 370 for standard 2-year ID. Biometrics captured in person at ICA. Processing often 3-7 working days.

## Residency visa stamping

Final step: residency stamp in passport via GDRFA service centre or authorised PRO.

Cost: AED 500-1,200 depending on category and service charges.

## Using a PRO or typing service

PRO cost: AED 500-1,500 per application typical.

PROs handle portal submissions, document checks, appointments, and physical attendance at service centres. First-time sponsors often save weeks and avoid rejection for formatting errors.

## Ongoing employer obligations

**Monthly salary:** Pay on time through UAE Wage Protection System (WPS). Late payment triggers MOHRE alerts and fines.

**Medical insurance:** Valid insurance for the worker throughout the visa period. Lapsed coverage is a labour violation.

**Annual leave and flights:** 30 calendar days annual leave after each completed year of service, plus return flight home when entitled under contract. Cannot be withheld as leverage.

**Rest day:** One full rest day per week mandatory. Day is contractual (often Friday, but negotiable).

**Accommodation:** Safe private room with ventilation and privacy. Workers may file MOHRE complaints over substandard housing.

## Domestic worker rights and dispute resolution

MOHRE mediates contract disputes. Either party may terminate with notice per contract (often 30-60 days). Material contract breach can allow immediate termination. End-of-service gratuity may apply on shorter service periods under UAE domestic worker law updates; confirm current MOHRE calculator at termination.

## Visa cancellation when you leave Dubai

Before your own residency ends:

1. Cancel MOHRE employment contract.
2. Cancel GDRFA visa for the worker.
3. Settle final salary, leave, and ticket entitlements.

Failure to cancel leaves sponsor liable for overstay fines and continued employer obligations.

## Hiring two workers or changing employers mid-contract

Two workers: typically requires higher demonstrated income (often AED 12,000-15,000+/month) and adequate accommodation for both. Each worker needs separate visa, contract, and MOHRE registration.

Early exit: notice periods apply unless MOHRE finds employer breach. Worker-initiated absconding cases create separate legal tracks; document all payments and contract compliance.

## Common mistakes that delay files

- Marriage or birth certificates not attested through the full chain before travel.
- Salary certificate showing total package but not basic pay where MOHRE asks for basic.
- Entry permit expiring before worker travels.
- Medical booked before entry permit category is correct.
- Name spelling mismatch between passport MRZ and MOHRE contract.

## Nationality and bilateral labour agreements

The UAE maintains bilateral labour frameworks with Philippines, Sri Lanka, India, Indonesia, Ethiopia, Kenya, and others. Workers from these countries dominate the domestic sector. Check current MOHRE nationality guidance before recruiting, as temporary restrictions can apply.

## Practical scenarios

**New expat family arriving:** Secure your employment visa and housing first, then start MOHRE contract while recruitment runs abroad. Do not book worker travel until entry permit PDF is issued.

**Transfer from another Dubai household:** Confirm NOC or eligibility, then run transfer route to save 6-10 weeks versus overseas hire.

**Leaving UAE:** Cancel worker visa before cancelling your own sponsorship to avoid fines.

## WPS salary rules and minimum pay

Domestic workers fall under UAE domestic worker law and MOHRE WPS rules. Salaries must be paid through approved WPS channels on the dates in the registered contract. Minimum salary floors apply by worker category and nationality schedule; underpaying blocks renewal and exposes the sponsor to fines.

| Topic | What sponsors should verify |
| --- | --- |
| Contract salary | Matches MOHRE registration and WPS file |
| Payment date | Fixed monthly date, no informal cash-only deals |
| Overtime | Only if contract and law allow; document extras |
| End of service | Use MOHRE calculator at termination |

## Renewals and absconding risk

Renewal starts 30-60 days before visa expiry. Book medical and Emirates ID renewal early; summer appointment queues in Dubai can add two weeks.

If a worker stops reporting without formal cancellation, sponsors must follow MOHRE absconding procedure rather than withholding passport (passport retention by employer is prohibited). Document communication attempts and contact MOHRE before assuming the file is closed.

## Ejari, tenancy, and household size

Renters sponsor domestic workers daily in Dubai. Ejari-registered tenancy is not always requested for domestic visa files, but your lease should reflect household occupancy if municipality or building rules limit unit capacity. A studio lease plus two live-in workers can trigger building management pushback even when immigration files proceed.

## Coordination with family visas

Domestic worker sponsorship is separate from [spouse and children residence](/guides/moving-to-dubai-with-family/). Many families process the maid file after primary visas clear so PRO bandwidth is not split across too many parallel applications.

## Document pack sponsors should prepare once

Keep one folder (digital and paper) that your PRO can reuse at renewal:

| Document | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Sponsor passport and visa copy | Every submission |
| Sponsor Emirates ID | Identity match |
| Salary certificate (30-day fresh) | Income proof |
| Ejari or tenancy contract | Address consistency |
| Worker passport copy | Permit and ID steps |
| Signed MOHRE contract PDF | Legal baseline |

Refreshing the salary certificate before each renewal avoids the common rejection where HR issues a letter dated six months ago.

## Language and orientation expectations

MOHRE contracts specify rest days, leave, and ticket entitlements in Arabic and English on standard templates. Walk the worker through house rules separately from the legal contract: guest policies, children pickup permissions, and phone use. Disputes that reach MOHRE are judged against the registered contract, not informal WhatsApp rules.
