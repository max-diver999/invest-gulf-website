  - question: "Can you sell off-plan property in Dubai before handover?"
    answer: "Yes, through an assignment sale processed with DLD, typically requiring a No Objection Certificate (NOC) from the developer. The buyer takes over your SPA obligations. Developer NOC fees, minimum construction completion thresholds, and restrictions vary by project. Some developers limit pre-handover sales until 30–50% construction completion."
  - question: "How much does it cost to flip off-plan in Dubai?"
    answer: "Costs include developer NOC fee (often AED 500–5,000 or percentage of price), DLD assignment/transfer fees (commonly 4% on the transfer value to the assignee in many structures), broker commission if used (2%), and any unpaid instalments to clear title for the buyer. Model all lines, not just capital gain headline."
  - question: "Is off-plan flipping still profitable in 2026?"
    answer: "The 2021–2023 cycle produced strong launch-to-assignment gains. In 2026, supply-heavy communities and normalised pricing make flipping harder, many assignments trade near par or below purchase price plus instalments paid. Flips still work in undersupplied micro-locations and Tier 1 launches with genuine launch discount, not as a default strategy."
  - question: "What is an NOC and why do developers control off-plan resale?"
    answer: "A No Objection Certificate is the developer's written approval for you to assign your off-plan contract to a new buyer. Developers use NOC policy to manage secondary supply, collect fees, and ensure the assignee meets their KYC standards. Without NOC, DLD will not process the assignment."
  - question: "What are the biggest mistakes off-plan flippers make?"
    answer: "Buying in oversupplied districts assuming automatic appreciation; ignoring post-handover payment tails that block clean assignment; missing instalments during hold period; underestimating NOC delays; and failing to verify assignee demand before launch day purchase. Flipping is a liquidity bet, treat it as trading, not passive investing."
