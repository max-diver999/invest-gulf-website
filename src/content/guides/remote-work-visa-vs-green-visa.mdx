The UAE Remote Work Visa, officially the Dubai Virtual Working Programme, is a 1-year renewable residence permit for foreign nationals who work remotely for an employer or run a business registered outside the UAE. Dubai's Department of Economy and Tourism launched it in October 2020 as one of the world's first government-formalised remote work visa schemes.

The premise is narrow but clear: your income comes from abroad, the UAE provides you legal residency and full expat infrastructure, and the relationship costs approximately USD 852 in fees. You do not need a UAE job offer, a UAE employer sponsor, or a local business licence. You also cannot generate income from UAE-source clients or employers while on this permit.

Abu Dhabi has a comparable framework through ADIO. Other emirates have informal equivalents, but the Dubai programme is the most structured and widely used. See the full [UAE Remote Work Visa guide](/guides/qatar-vs-uae-remote-work-visa/) for step-by-step documentation details.

### Green Visa

The UAE Green Visa is a 5-year self-sponsored residence permit introduced under the UAE's 2022 visa reform package. Unlike employer-sponsored visas that tie your residency to a single company, the Green Visa makes you your own sponsor, your right to live in the UAE is attached to your qualification category, not to a specific employer's continued patronage.

Three primary tracks exist:

- Skilled employee track: UAE-based employment with a monthly salary of at least AED 15,000, a bachelor's degree or higher, and skill level classification of 1, 2, or 3 under the UAE Standard Classification of Occupations.
- Freelancer and self-employed track: A valid UAE freelance permit or business licence with demonstrated annual income of at least AED 360,000 (AED 30,000 per month), supported by bank statements or contracts.
- **Investor track:** A minimum investment of AED 500,000 in a UAE company, verified through official commercial registration.

Outstanding university graduates from UAE or top global universities, and certain humanitarian or scientific contributions, qualify under additional sub-categories, but the three above cover the vast majority of applicants. Full qualification details are in the [UAE Green Visa guide](/guides/uae-green-visa-freelancer/).

You need one of the following:

**Employed category:** Active employment contract with a foreign company, minimum 12 months continuous employment with that employer, monthly salary of USD 3,500 or more shown on the most recent 3 months of bank statements. The employer must be registered outside the UAE.

**Self-employed or business-owner category:** Average monthly revenue of USD 3,500+ over the preceding 3 months, bank statements showing consistent inflows, and ownership documentation (incorporation certificate or equivalent) for a business registered outside the UAE.

The hard constraint in all three categories is the **foreign income source requirement**. The moment your income originates from a UAE-based entity, you need different documentation, and likely a different visa category.

### Green Visa eligibility

The Green Visa requires you to contribute economically to the UAE rather than simply reside here while earning elsewhere.

**Skilled employee track:** UAE-based employment, monthly salary of AED 15,000 or more, bachelor's degree minimum, and a role classified at skill level 1, 2, or 3 by the UAE Standard Classification of Occupations (ISCO-aligned). Most professional and managerial roles qualify at these levels. This track does require a UAE employer, but the key difference from a standard work visa is that the residency is issued in your name, not tied to your employer's continued sponsorship.

**Freelancer and self-employed track:** Valid UAE freelance permit or trade licence, AED 360,000 annual income demonstrated through bank statements or contracts, and optionally a UAE-based bachelor's degree or equivalent. The income threshold is the most common qualification challenge, AED 30,000 per month is a meaningful bar that eliminates casual or emerging freelancers.

**Investor track:** Investment of AED 500,000 or more in an existing UAE company, verified through commercial documentation. This is separate from property investment, property at AED 2 million qualifies for the 10-year Golden Visa, not the Green Visa. See the [UAE residency visa types guide](/guides/golden-visa-vs-dubai-residence-visa/) for the full landscape across investor and property-based pathways.

### Green Visa total cost

Government fees for the Green Visa itself are typically **AED 700–1,000**. However, the full cost stack for a first-time applicant includes:

- Medical fitness test: AED 320–370 (required at approved DHA/DOH facility)
- Emirates ID issuance: AED 370
- Biometrics and service charges: AED 150–300
- Typing centre or consultant: AED 200–500 if used

**Total first-year cost: approximately AED 1,500–1,800** without consulting support. The Green Visa is renewable every 5 years at broadly the same fee structure, meaning over a 5-year period, the annualised cost is around AED 300–360 per year in government fees alone, compared to the Remote Work Visa's annual renewal.

On a pure per-year basis, the Green Visa is more cost-efficient once you factor in the 5-year duration, but the Remote Work Visa does not require a UAE freelance licence (which costs AED 1,500–7,500 depending on free zone) or proof of AED 30,000 monthly income from UAE sources.

### Remote Work Visa: permitted activities

- Live in Dubai (or the issuing emirate) full-time
- Continue working for your foreign employer remotely
- Run your overseas-registered business from Dubai
- Open a UAE bank account
- Obtain a UAE driving licence
- Lease property, enrol children in school, access healthcare
- Sponsor family members under the same framework
- Purchase freehold property in designated zones

### Remote Work Visa: prohibited activities

- Taking UAE-source employment (requires a UAE work permit)
- Working for UAE-registered companies or clients on a commercial basis
- Generating invoiced income from UAE entities without a UAE business licence
- Operating a UAE-registered business on this visa

The prohibition on UAE-source income is not a technicality. If you are a remote developer who starts taking contracts from a Dubai startup, or a consultant who begins billing UAE clients, you have moved outside the terms of the Remote Work Visa. This is a meaningful constraint for anyone whose client base evolves toward the UAE market.

### Green Visa: permitted activities

The Green Visa is broader. Depending on your track:

- **Skilled employee:** Full UAE employment rights with your registered employer
- **Freelancer:** Issue invoices to UAE and international clients, operate under your UAE freelance permit, expand client base freely
- **Investor:** Operate and draw income from your UAE-registered company

Green Visa holders on the freelancer or investor track can build a UAE-client business, hire staff under their licence, enter UAE government contracts, and scale operations, activities that are entirely outside the scope of the Remote Work Visa.

For people seriously considering [part-time or flexible UAE living](/guides/dubai-property-investment-guide/), the Green Visa's freelance track gives substantially more commercial freedom than the Remote Work Visa even if you do not plan to work full-time from the UAE immediately.

Under the **Remote Work Visa**, you can sponsor a spouse and dependent children. Each requires health insurance and their own processing fees. Accommodation requirements apply, you need a rental agreement in your name that satisfies minimum space standards.

Under the **Green Visa**, sponsorship follows the same UAE family visa framework. Spouse and children under 18 (or unmarried daughters of any age in most cases) qualify. Financial capacity is assessed at the time of application, a bank statement or income evidence showing you can support dependants is standard documentation.

The main practical difference: the Remote Work Visa's family sponsorship renews annually alongside the main permit, adding a recurring administrative cycle. The Green Visa's 5-year validity means family members' visas can be aligned to the same 5-year window, reducing renewal overhead.

## What should buyers verify on tax residency implications?

Physical presence under either visa counts toward UAE tax residency under the **183-day rule**, spending more than 183 days per year in the UAE can establish you as a UAE tax resident, subject to meeting additional criteria set by the Federal Tax Authority.

This matters for people relocating from high-tax jurisdictions. UAE personal income tax on employment income is 0% for most expat structures (verify with a tax adviser for your specific situation). Establishing UAE tax residency can legally reduce your home-country tax obligations if you break residency there first.

The **Green Visa has a structural advantage for tax residency** purposes: its 5-year duration provides continuous residency that home-country tax authorities and UAE authorities can both verify over multiple years. The Remote Work Visa's annual renewal cycle creates annual gaps in documentation, not a legal problem, but a practical one when assembling tax residency evidence.

The UAE also issues a **Tax Residency Certificate (TRC)** through the Federal Tax Authority for residents who meet the criteria, this is the document most commonly required by foreign tax authorities to accept your UAE tax residency claim. Both visa holders can apply for a TRC, but FTA may require evidence of economic substance in the UAE beyond just accommodation and days present.

**You are employed abroad and not ready to change that.** If you have a well-paying remote role with a non-UAE company and no immediate plans to change, the Remote Work Visa lets you live in Dubai without restructuring your employment. Your employer does not need to establish any UAE presence.

**You want to test Dubai before committing.** The 1-year format is deliberately low-commitment. If after 12 months you decide Dubai is not for you, non-renewal is straightforward with no business licence to wind down, no UAE freelance permit to maintain, and no minimum-stay obligations.

**Your income is comfortably above USD 3,500/month and clearly foreign.** Clean documentation from a well-established foreign employer makes processing fast, typically 5–7 working days. The simpler the paper trail, the faster and smoother the Remote Work Visa experience.

**You want minimal UAE administrative overhead.** No UAE business licence, no ISCO classification of your role, no local degree attestation. The Remote Work Visa is genuinely simpler to obtain than the Green Visa for people who qualify cleanly.

### Choose the Green Visa if:

**You want to work for UAE clients or employers.** This is the clearest decision trigger. If your business model involves serving the UAE market or you are considering a UAE-based role, the Green Visa is mandatory, not optional.

**You want residency certainty beyond 1 year.** Five-year validity means less annual administrative friction, more stable banking relationships, longer lease agreements, and a more straightforward tax residency paper trail. For families, it also means aligning everyone's residency cycles.

**You are building a freelance or consulting business.** The Green Visa freelancer track, requiring a UAE freelance permit and AED 360,000 annual income, is purpose-built for independent professionals who want to operate commercially in the UAE. This is the most direct path for digital professionals, consultants, and creative industry workers relocating to Dubai.

**You are in the medium-to-long term in the UAE.** If your plan involves 3+ years in the UAE, the Green Visa's cost-per-year advantage compounds. You also reduce the risk of a failed annual renewal disrupting your residency at an inconvenient time.

**You are employed by a UAE company.** If you have or are considering a UAE-based job offer paying AED 15,000+ monthly with appropriate qualifications, the skilled employee track of the Green Visa is the natural fit, it gives you self-sponsored residency rather than employer-dependent status.
