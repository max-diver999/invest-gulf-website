## Can women expats drive legally in Saudi Arabia in 2026?

Women expats can drive legally in Saudi Arabia once iqama is issued and a Saudi licence is obtained, because reforms effective from June 2018 opened licensing to women and our underwriting treats guardian consent as unnecessary for standard adult iqama holders who complete Moroor conversion or school within about 1 to 4 weeks after medical clearance for foreign buyers relocating with family cars.

## How do licensing pathways work for expat women?

Licensing for expat women typically means foreign-licence conversion when nationality sits on the eligible list, or full driving school when conversion is closed, and we budget SAR fees in the low hundreds for swap visits versus SAR 2,000 to 4,500 plus 4 to 10 weeks for school routes for foreign buyers.

### Path A: Foreign licence conversion

Most expats with a valid home-country or GCC licence skip beginner school entirely.

Typical documents (confirm current official rules):
- Valid foreign licence (English/Arabic or certified translation)
- Iqama + passport copies
- Medical fitness certificate
- Passport photos
- Fees (SAR low hundreds)

GCC swap: UAE licence holders often use conversion route, still confirm eligible nationality table at appointment; US/UK/EU/Indian rules differ. Bring original licence; photocopies alone fail.

### Path B: Full Saudi driving school

If conversion unavailable: register at an approved school, complete theory and practical hours, pass traffic tests, and plan women-only training slots in Riyadh, Jeddah, or Dammam.

### Path C: International permit, short visit only

An International Driving Permit on a visit visa is not a substitute for a resident Saudi licence once iqama is issued. Convert promptly to avoid insurance void arguments.

Women expats planning a Saudi licence should treat conversion eligibility as a nationality-specific checklist, not a friend anecdote from two years ago.
