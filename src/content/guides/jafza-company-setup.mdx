## What is JAFZA and who should use it?

JAFZA is Dubai's Jebel Ali Free Zone for trading, manufacturing, and logistics companies that import, store, re-export, or distribute physical goods across the GCC, Africa, and Asia. Founders who move containers, bonded stock, or fleets typically shortlist JAFZA first for the next 12 to 36 months; solo consultants and DFSA finance usually should not, per Invest Gulf research.

Why trading and logistics founders choose JAFZA:

- Jebel Ali Port access: customs, re-export, and maritime logistics cluster
- Warehouse and industrial plots: scalable space for inventory and assembly
- Visa quota tied to facility size: plan headcount alongside square metres
- 100% foreign ownership: standard for JAFZA establishments and companies
- Established trade finance ecosystem: banks understand import/export KYC
- Long operating history: counterparties recognise JAFZA on invoices and LC documents

JAFZA is rarely the best fit for solo consultants, DFSA-regulated finance, or e-commerce sellers with no UAE stock. Compare [IFZA company setup](/guides/ifza-company-setup/) for low-cost digital trading and [DMCC company setup](/guides/dmcc-company-setup/) for JLT services and commodities. ## Which business activities fit JAFZA best?

JAFZA activities typically fit import/export trading, freight forwarding, manufacturing, assembly, packaging, automotive parts, FMCG distribution, and heavy equipment. Pure consulting, fintech, and DFSA-regulated finance belong in [DIFC](/guides/difc-company-setup/) or ADGM; e-commerce without UAE stock often costs less in IFZA or DMCC under AED 20000 year-one bands in Invest Gulf activity maps.

Activity fit checklist:

- Strong: containers, bonded warehouse, industrial assembly
- Medium: B2B distribution with light office front
- Weak: remote consulting only, DFSA finance, dropship with zero UAE inventory

Regulated activities (financial services, certain healthcare, hazardous materials) require additional approvals.

## When should you choose JAFZA over DMCC or IFZA?

JAFZA over DMCC or IFZA typically makes sense when goods move in containers through Jebel Ali and you need warehouse visas at industrial density. Choose DMCC for precious metals, tea, coffee, or energy paper on DMCC infrastructure; choose IFZA when goods never touch UAE soil and year-one budgets sit under AED 20000 in Invest Gulf comparisons.

Rule of thumb for foreign buyers of setup packages:

- Containers and bonded stock: JAFZA
- JLT client meetings and commodities desks: DMCC
- Digital trading without UAE inventory: IFZA

For mainland access rules shared by both zones, see [UAE free zone vs mainland](/guides/uae-free-zone-vs-mainland/).

## Which JAFZA legal structures are available?

JAFZA legal structures typically include free zone establishments and free zone companies with 100% foreign ownership, plus branch structures of foreign parents that retain overseas liability. No UAE national shareholder is required inside the free zone for standard trading and industrial licences.

Structure notes from our research:

- FZE / FZCO for most trading and warehouse operators
- Branch of foreign parent when head-office liability must stay overseas
- Activity licence must match bills of lading and HS codes from day one
- Facility lease is part of the legal pack, not an afterthought

## How do JAFZA visa quotas link to office and warehouse space?

JAFZA visa quotas typically link to leased office or warehouse area, often planning near one visa per 9 to 15 sqm of office, with warehouse plots earning higher headcount per square metre by activity. Flexi-desk packages may allow only 1 to 3 visas; a 200 to 300 sqm warehouse might support 15 to 30 visas depending on lease category in Invest Gulf planning.

Contrast with DMCC: a standard flexi-desk often includes three visas without dedicated square metres. JAFZA trades lower land cost per visa for industrial users but offers fewer visas on entry-level service packages. Always obtain written visa quota on the lease summary before payment.

Visa quota checklist:

- Written quota on lease before deposit
- Flexi tier realism at 1 to 3 seats
- Warehouse math for 15 to 30 seats on 200 to 300 sqm
- No verbal "20 visas" promises on flexi desks

## What are the JAFZA setup steps?

JAFZA setup steps typically run lease selection, name reservation, shareholder KYC, fee payment, licence collection, then banking and visas. Flexi-office tiers often cost AED 15000 to AED 25000 per year, serviced offices AED 40000 to AED 80000, and warehouses AED 80000 to AED 250000+ depending on sqm and power.

### Step 1 to 2: Facility and lease

Options include:

- Flexi-office / business centre: AED 15000-25000 per year, limited visas
- Serviced office: AED 40000-80000 per year, moderate visa quota
- Warehouse: AED 80000-250000+ per year depending on sqm, racking, and power
- Industrial land / built unit: custom pricing for manufacturing

### Step 3: Reserve company name

Follow UAE naming rules. Approval typically within 1 to 2 business days.

### Step 4: Submit shareholder KYC and application

Individual shareholders provide passport, proof of address under three months, bank reference or source of funds, and trade experience summary. Corporate shareholders add incorporation certificates, good standing, board resolution, and full UBO disclosure.

### Step 5 to 7: Fees, licence, bank, visas

Pay licence and facility fees on approval in principle, collect trade licence and MOA, register customs codes if importing through Jebel Ali, then open the bank account and sponsor visas up to leased quota. ## What port and customs advantages does JAFZA offer?

JAFZA port advantages typically mean customs clearance, bonded storage, and re-export inside the Jebel Ali cluster subject to licence and customs codes. Mismatch between licence, warehouse type, and HS codes delays clearance and trade finance for 2 to 6 weeks; budget a licensed customs broker in year one even if you self-file later.

Port advantage checklist from our research:

- Licence activity aligned with bill of lading descriptions
- Bonded storage sized to the first 12 months of inventory
- Customs broker retained before the first import
- DMCC ruled out when warehouse scale is the actual need

## Do JAFZA companies pay UAE corporate tax?

JAFZA companies typically pay UAE corporate tax at 9% on profits above AED 375000 from June 2023 unless they qualify as Qualifying Free Zone Persons with 0% on Qualifying Income under strict conditions. Mainland retail sales and certain passive categories often fail QFZP treatment, so model each stream before year-end in Invest Gulf tax notes.

Tax checklist:

- Default plan: 9% above AED 375000
- QFZP 0% only with evidenced qualifying income
- Mainland retail risk to free-zone treatment
- Transfer pricing on related-party imports

Common JAFZA setup mistakes typically include leasing flexi space while promising warehouse volumes, assuming 0% tax automatically, buying visa headcount without square metres, and invoicing mainland retail without a distributor or DED path. Year-one budgets under AED 20000 usually belong in IFZA, not an empty Jebel Ali unit.

Mistake checklist from our analysis:

- Activity licence that does not match HS codes
- Visa promises above contracted quota
- Skipping customs broker budget in month one
- Choosing JAFZA for prestige when goods never arrive

## Is JAFZA the right free zone for your business?

JAFZA is typically right when physical goods, bonded storage, or industrial headcount sit at the centre of revenue for the next 12 to 36 months. Skip JAFZA if you sell only digital services, need a JLT address, trade DMCC commodities, need DFSA regulation, or must stay under AED 20000 year one per Invest Gulf fit checks.

Decision list:

- You sell digital services or remote consulting only: consider IFZA/DMCC
- You need JLT address for client meetings: consider DMCC
- Budget is under AED 20000 year one: consider IFZA
- You trade commodities on DMCC infrastructure: consider DMCC
- You need DFSA-regulated financial services: consider DIFC
