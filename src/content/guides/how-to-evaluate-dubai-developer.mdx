Buying off-plan in Dubai means your primary counterparty is the developer, often for three to five years before you receive keys. Marketing renders buildings in perfect light. The SPA defines what actually happens if construction slips, costs rise, or you need to exit early.

This guide is a 12-point due diligence checklist you can run on any Dubai developer before signing. It complements the broader [Dubai Developers Guide](/guides/dubai-developers-guide/) and the [Off-Plan Property Dubai Guide](/guides/off-plan-property-dubai-guide/).

Dubai developer evaluation for off-plan buyers typically starts with Trakheesi registration, a DLD-regulated escrow account, and a contractual Oqood timeline before non-refundable funds move. Tier 1 names such as Emaar, Nakheel, and Sobha often show on-time delivery near 88% to 95%, while thinner players can sit nearer 65% to 82%. Independent SPA review commonly costs AED 5000 to AED 15000 and pays for itself when service-charge underestimates of 20% to 30% appear. On a 900 sqft apartment, an AED 5 per sqft miss equals AED 4500 per year of yield drag. Vacancy bands in Invest Gulf underwriting run near 4% to 5% prime and 8% to 12% in supply-heavy districts. Survey costs of AED 2000 to AED 5000 stay minor versus multi-year exposure, and penalties of 1% to 2% monthly without caps remain a hard walk-away red flag.

## What checklist should run before you sign a Dubai developer SPA?

A Dubai developer SPA checklist typically requires twelve checks in order: Trakheesi listing, DLD-regulated escrow, contractual Oqood registration, delivery history, corporate backing, completed build quality, service-charge reality, payment-plan stress test, SPA penalties, pre-handover resale rules, micro-market supply, and independent legal review. Missing any of the first three puts foreign buyers outside the standard protection framework.

### 1. Trakheesi registration

Project name, developer legal entity, registration status (active), and permitted sales phase.

Red flag: Sales meetings that proceed without a Trakheesi reference, or "registration pending" as a permanent state.

### 2. Escrow account verification

Off-plan buyer funds must sit in a DLD-regulated escrow account. The developer receives releases only against certified construction milestones.

Escrow account number, bank name, and that your payment schedule aligns with milestone triggers.

Tools: Dubai REST app, Trakheesi project page, direct request to developer sales admin.

Red flag: Requests to wire to a corporate operating account, "temporary" non-escrow routing, or discounts for bypassing escrow.

### 3. Oqood registration commitment

Your SPA must be registered on Oqood (DLD's off-plan contract system) within the statutory period. Oqood is your legal standing as buyer, equivalent to title for a completed unit.

SPA clause specifying Oqood registration timeline and who pays the 4% DLD fee.

Red flag: Vague language like "registration upon request" or delays beyond standard windows.

### 4. Historical on-time delivery rate

Delivery rate separates Tier 1 (~88% to 95%) from riskier volume players (~65% to 82%).

Completion status of the developer's last 3 to 5 projects in similar segments (tower vs villa, mid vs premium).

Practical test: Visit the most recent handover building. Talk to owners about snagging resolution speed and service-charge reality.

### 5. Financial backing and corporate structure

Privately held developers require more scrutiny than ADX-listed groups with quarterly audited accounts.

Parent company backing (government-linked, conglomerate, or standalone private), active project count versus cash-flow capacity, and whether the developer is simultaneously launching too many projects for stated completion capacity.

Red flag: Developer with 15 active launches and no completed tower in the target community.

### 6. Build quality on completed stock

Renderings are not build quality. Inspect completed units.

Check: Fit-out standard vs brochure, common-area maintenance, lift performance, parking allocation accuracy, sound insulation between units.

### 7. Service charge estimate vs reality

Developers often quote low estimated charges at launch.

Mollak filings on prior buildings by the same developer; RERA Service Charge Index for the district.

Model impact: On a 900 sqft apartment, AED 5/sqft underestimate = AED 4500/year permanent yield drag.

### 8. Payment plan stress test

Model three scenarios:

| Scenario | What to model |
|---|---|
| On-time handover | Full payment schedule as written |
| 12-month delay | Calendar-linked instalments still due? |
| 24-month delay | Penalties, financing gap, opportunity cost |

Red flag: Calendar-linked payments that continue during construction delays without adjustment clauses.

### 9. SPA penalty and termination clauses

Read before signing, not after a missed payment.

Check: Late payment penalty rate (1% to 2% monthly is common); termination deduction cap after default; developer delay remedies (compensation, exit rights).

### 10. NOC and pre-handover resale rules

If you may sell before handover, understand NOC policy now.

Minimum construction completion percentage for NOC issuance; NOC fees; restrictions on assignee nationality or financing.

See [How to Flip Off-Plan in Dubai](/guides/how-to-flip-off-plan-dubai/) for resale mechanics.

### 11. Community supply and tenant demand

Developer quality does not override micro-market oversupply.

Competing handovers in the same community over your hold period; Ejari transacted rents vs listing asks; vacancy band for the district (prime 4% to 5%, citywide 7% to 8%, supply-heavy 8% to 12%).

### 12. Independent legal and snagging planning

Before signing: Independent solicitor reviews SPA (AED 5000 to AED 15000).

Before handover: Book professional snagging inspection; budget DEWA connection, service-charge deposit, and Ejari setup for rental intent.

Pre-signature checklist rule: capture Trakheesi, escrow bank, and service-charge estimate in writing before any non-refundable transfer.

## How can you score a developer quickly?

A quick developer score typically means ranking Trakheesi status, escrow clarity, and on-time delivery evidence before brochure amenities. Award higher marks when the last 3 to 5 handovers match the promised segment, and cut scores when 15 active launches lack a completed tower in the same community.

Scoring grid used in Invest Gulf research:

| Signal | Strong | Weak |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trakheesi | Active listing | Pending indefinitely |
| Escrow | Named DLD bank | Operating account request |
| Delivery | 88% to 95% on-time band | Below 70% or opaque |
| SPA review window | Allows 5 to 10 days | Sign tonight pressure |

## How do Tier 1 and Tier 2 developers differ?

Tier 1 and Tier 2 developers typically differ on delivery evidence and SPA leverage: Tier 1 names often land near 88% to 95% on-time handover bands, while Tier 2 names such as Danube, Binghatti, Azizi, Samana, and Ellington require all 12 checks without exception. Payment-plan flexibility is not a substitute for completed-building proof.

- Tier 1: deeper completed stock to inspect, tighter escrow norms, slower "launch discount" games
- Tier 2: wider payment-plan creativity, wider delivery dispersion, mandatory snagging budget
- Either tier: walk away if escrow cannot be verified in writing

## What extra checks do remote buyers need?

Remote buyers typically require a longer SPA review window of 5 to 10 days, video walkthroughs of the developer's last completed building, and escrow bank confirmation emailed before funds leave the home-country account. Budget AED 5000 to AED 15000 for independent counsel and AED 2000 to AED 5000 for surveyor support when price exceeds AED 3 million.

Remote checklist from our analysis:

- Escrow letter on bank letterhead, not sales WhatsApp screenshots
- Oqood timeline named in the SPA with the 4% DLD fee payer stated
- Snagging booked before keys, not after furniture delivery
- NOC resale rules read before you assume a pre-handover exit

## Which Dubai portals should you bookmark?

Dubai portals you should bookmark typically include Trakheesi for registration status, Dubai REST for Mollak and title tools, and DLD project pages for completion signals before the first viewing call. Our methodology treats brochure PDFs as marketing, not evidence, until those portals corroborate the story within 30 days of your offer.

Bookmark list:

- Trakheesi project search
- Dubai REST for Mollak service-charge filings
- DLD / Oqood registration status tools
- Dubai Courts public search for related-party litigation flags

## When should you hire an independent surveyor?

An independent surveyor typically becomes essential when the developer is Tier 2 with no completed tower in the community, the project is a first phase on novel infrastructure, purchase price exceeds AED 3 million, or you plan immediate short-term rental needing Civil Defence compliance. Survey costs of AED 2000 to AED 5000 remain minor versus multi-year exposure.

Hire triggers in our checklist:

- No completed comparable building within a short drive
- Price above AED 3 million with asymmetric defect downside
- STR intent requiring compliance evidence
- Reclaimed or novel infrastructure first phase

## How do you stress-test developer delivery risk?

Developer delivery stress tests typically model 12-month and 24-month delay paths against calendar-linked instalments, financing gaps, and opportunity cost. Track Trakheesi updates on all developer projects from the past 3 years and compare DLD handover dates with promises in our underwriting.

Delayed project concentration risk rises when multiple slips stack; subsequent launches may prioritise delayed inventory over new projects. Our underwriting also reviews DED commercial registration, registered office, authorised capital, and Dubai Courts litigation flags for privately held groups.

Corporate structure checks:

- Parent backing versus standalone private balance sheets
- Active launch count versus completed capacity
- Land freehold versus instalment lease complexity
- Building permit and utility NOC status before SPA

## How does due diligence differ by project type?

Due diligence typically differs by project type: family communities need school and amenity delivery evidence, investment towers need Ejari yield and resale depth, and mixed-use schemes need commercial pre-leasing clarity. Vacancy assumptions of 4% to 5% prime versus 8% to 12% supply-heavy districts should change your hold model before you sign in our checklists.

| Project type | Extra checks |
| --- | --- |
| Family community | School operators, traffic, retail delivered not planned |
| Investment tower | Achieved rents, STR policy, resale volumes |
| Mixed-use | Anchor pre-leases, service-charge split methodology |
| Hotel / serviced | Operator agreement and tourist-area compliance |

## Which developer sales tactics are red flags?

Developer sales red flags typically include pressure to sign during the first presentation, "limited time" pricing that extends for weeks, refusal to share an SPA draft for legal review, and guaranteed rental yield promises without escrow documentation. Payment schedules that take more than 50% before construction milestones, or wires outside regulated escrow, are structural exits in the checklist.

Construction red flags to document:

- Subcontractor payment delays reported publicly
- Main contractor changes mid-build without explanation
- Snagging backlogs past 6 months on prior handovers
- Service-charge actuals far above launch estimates

## What is a practical due diligence workflow?

A practical due diligence workflow typically runs across 10 to 21 days: portal checks on day one, completed-building visit within the first week, SPA legal review over 5 to 10 days, then escrow and Oqood confirmation before any non-refundable transfer. Compressing that into a same-day reservation is how foreign buyers lose leverage.

Workflow stages:

1. Trakheesi + escrow evidence pack
2. Delivery and Mollak comps
3. Independent SPA markup
4. Payment-plan delay stress test
5. Sign only after written answers

## How do you verify regional reputation?

Regional reputation checks typically require UAE-specific delivery evidence even when an international brand is strong at home: local partner control, UAE construction management, and contractual smart-building warranties matter more than overseas brochures. LEED or Estidama targets count only when certification is tied to handover within a stated number of months in our research.

For international branches, verify:

- Local partner arrangements and control
- UAE completed stock you can walk within 30 days of offer
- Named suppliers and warranty terms in the SPA
- Service-charge impact of smart systems in Mollak comps over 12 months

## How do SPA dispute clauses work?

SPA dispute clauses typically choose Dubai courts or DIAC arbitration and control timeline, cost, and enforceability if you need deposit recovery after a delay fight. Buyer protection options such as performance bonds or third-party completion insurance remain uncommon and must be read as contract text, not sales verbal promises over a 30 day negotiation window.

Dispute checklist from our analysis:

- Forum named: courts versus DIAC
- Language and evidence translation needs
- RERA complaint routes for registered brokers
- Whether delay compensation is capped or illusory

## How does developer risk fit your portfolio?

Developer risk in a portfolio typically means limiting concentration across one name, one community, and one handover year while keeping cash for 12 to 24 months of delay. Align UAE bank pre-approval for mortgage at handover with exit liquidity for similar unit types before you stack a second off-plan ticket above AED 3 million.

Portfolio rules we use:

- Cap single-developer exposure across holdings
- Stagger handover years intentionally
- Prefer ready stock when vacancy already sits at 8% to 12%
- Model resale marketing time by brand, not brochure absorption claims

## What is the final rule before you sign?

The final rule before you sign typically means walking away when escrow cannot be verified, Oqood is not contractually guaranteed, Trakheesi is missing, legal review is refused, or penalties exceed 2% monthly without caps after a 5 to 10 day SPA window. Guaranteed ROI slides never replace the 12-point checklist in our underwriting.
