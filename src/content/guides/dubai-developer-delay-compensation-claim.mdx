A delayed handover is the most common thing that goes wrong on a Dubai off-plan purchase, and it is the situation buyers are least prepared for. The brochure gave a quarter, the SPA gave a date, and the developer has now given a third answer. What you can do about it depends far less on how late the project is than on what your contract says and on whether the regulator still lists the project as live.

This page sets out the position honestly, including where the answer is that a buyer has no claim worth bringing. It is general information about how the process works, not legal advice on your contract, and any actual claim needs a UAE-qualified lawyer who has read your SPA.

Insider tip: the date buyers remember is almost never the date the contract enforces, because the anticipated completion date, the grace period and the authority-approval carve-out stack on top of each other, and a project that feels eighteen months late is often only two months past the enforceable deadline.

## What counts as a delay in a Dubai off-plan contract?

A delay only becomes actionable once the contractual deadline has passed, and that deadline is rarely the date printed at the front of the sale and purchase agreement. Most Dubai SPAs state an anticipated completion date and then attach a grace period, commonly six or twelve months, during which late delivery is expressly not treated as a breach.

On top of the grace period sit the carve-outs, and they do most of the work. Typical clauses extend the date for force majeure, for delays in obtaining authority approvals or utility connections, and sometimes for any cause outside the developer's reasonable control, which is drafted broadly enough to cover a great deal. A buyer who bought expecting handover in the second quarter of one year may find that the enforceable deadline, after a 12 months grace period and a documented approvals delay, sits 18 months later. This is why the first step in any delay situation is not a complaint to the developer but a careful reading of three clauses together: the completion date, the grace period, and the extension carve-outs. Until those combine to a date that has passed, there is no breach to compensate, however frustrating the wait has been.

| Contract element | What it typically says | Effect on your position |
|---|---|---|
| Anticipated completion date | A stated quarter or date | Starting point only, rarely the deadline |
| Grace period | Commonly 6 or 12 months | No breach until it expires |
| Force majeure clause | Events outside developer control | Extends the deadline, often without a cap |
| Authority approval carve-out | Permits, utilities, connections | Extends where documented |
| Delay compensation clause | Fixed sum or rate per month | The main source of any entitlement |
| Termination trigger | Extended delay threshold | Sets when refund becomes arguable |

## What compensation can an off-plan buyer actually claim?

Compensation for handover delay in Dubai is primarily contractual. Where the SPA contains a delay clause, that clause sets the entitlement, typically as a fixed sum or a monthly rate against the amount paid. Where the SPA is silent, the buyer is not without remedy but must prove actual loss rather than point to an agreed figure.

The honest position is that many standard developer SPAs are drafted so that the buyer's delay entitlement is modest and the developer's default remedies against the buyer are extensive. That asymmetry is normal in off-plan contracts and it is one reason the delay clause deserves attention before signing rather than after. Where a claim is viable, the realistic heads of loss are replacement accommodation cost, rent the completed unit would have produced over the delay period, which on a unit yielding 6.5% gross is roughly AED 65,000 a year per AED 1 million of price, and financing cost incurred on funds already released. Speculative losses, such as the appreciation a buyer says they would have captured elsewhere, do not travel well. Under the UAE Civil Transactions Law a court may also adjust an agreed compensation figure so that it matches loss actually suffered, on the application of either party, which cuts both ways: a generous clause can be reduced, and a thin one can in principle be revisited.

## How does the SPA decide your position?

The SPA decides almost everything, which is why two buyers in the same tower can have materially different remedies for the same delay. Read the delay clause, the termination clause and the payment schedule as one document, because they interact.

The payment schedule matters more than buyers expect. A construction-linked schedule ties your instalments to verified progress, so a stalled project stops taking your money; a date-linked schedule keeps demanding payments whether or not the concrete is moving. If your remaining exposure is small because milestones have not been reached, your practical position in a delay is far stronger than a buyer on a time-based plan who has already released 70% of the price. Payment plan structures and their real costs are set out on the [off-plan payment plans page](/guides/off-plan-payment-plans-dubai/), and the escrow protections that sit behind them on the [escrow and Oqood page](/guides/escrow-oqood-dubai-explained/).

## What happens when RERA cancels the project instead?

Project cancellation is a different track from delay, and it changes both the remedy and the forum. Where the Dubai Land Department audits a project and formally cancels it, the matter moves out of the ordinary contractual dispute route and into a supervised liquidation of the escrow account, where recovery is capped by whatever share of the 100% of instalments that should have been escrowed actually remains.

Dubai established a dedicated judicial committee to handle the liquidation of cancelled real estate projects and the settlement of the rights arising from them. In that process, buyer claims are made against the escrow funds and any other recoverable assets rather than pursued as an ordinary damages claim against the developer. The practical consequence for a buyer is that recovery depends on what is left in escrow, which is why escrow compliance is worth checking at purchase rather than at cancellation. Before choosing any route, confirm the project's current registered status with the Land Department, because a project that a buyer assumes is merely late may already have been cancelled, and a claim filed in the wrong forum loses time that a limitation period does not give back.

## Which forum hears an off-plan delay claim in Dubai?

Off-plan sale disputes go to the Dubai Courts real estate division. They do not go to the Rental Disputes Centre, which handles landlord and tenant matters and has no jurisdiction over a sale contract, and this is the most common procedural mistake buyers make.

| Situation | Where it goes | What you are claiming |
|---|---|---|
| Late handover, project still live | Dubai Courts real estate division | Contractual delay compensation or damages |
| Project formally cancelled by DLD | Special judicial committee for cancelled projects | A share of escrow and recoverable assets |
| Dispute over a completed unit's condition | Contractual route, snagging and defects liability | Rectification, not delay compensation |
| Landlord and tenant matters | Rental Disputes Centre | Rent, eviction, contract terms |
| Contract with a DIFC or arbitration clause | The forum the contract names | As the clause provides |

Where the SPA names arbitration or a DIFC forum, that clause governs and filing in the wrong place wastes both time and fees. The wider map of Dubai property dispute routes is on the [dispute resolution page](/guides/dubai-property-dispute-resolution/).

## What evidence do you need before filing?

Evidence decides delay claims more often than argument does, and most of it is documentation the buyer already has but has never organised. Assemble it before taking advice, because a lawyer reading a complete file gives a far more useful answer than one reading a summary.

- The signed SPA with the completion date, grace period, carve-outs and delay clause identified by number.
- The Oqood registration certificate confirming the unit is on the Interim Real Estate Register.
- Every payment receipt, matched line by line to the contractual payment schedule.
- All written notices from the developer revising the completion date, with dates received.
- Evidence of actual loss: tenancy contracts for replacement accommodation, comparable rents for the unit type, financing statements.
- The project's current status as registered with the Dubai Land Department.

Move every exchange with the developer to email. A phone call in which a sales manager promises a new date is worth nothing in a file, and the same statement in writing is worth a great deal.

## How long does a delay claim take and what does it cost?

Cost and time are the reason many viable claims are not worth bringing. Dubai court fees are calculated as a percentage of the claim value subject to a cap, legal representation is charged separately, and a first-instance judgment on a contested real estate matter is typically measured in months rather than weeks, with appeal routes beyond that.

Set the arithmetic out before filing. If the SPA delay clause produces an entitlement of AED 40,000 and the combined court fee and legal cost to pursue it approaches that figure, the claim is a negotiating position rather than a commercial action. Where the entitlement is a meaningful fraction of a seven-figure purchase, or where several buyers in the same project share the same clause and the same facts, the economics change substantially, and 20 buyers instructing one firm spread a fixed cost across a claim value 20 times larger, which is the single most reliable way a thin individual claim becomes a viable collective one. We have seen the same delay produce a rational claim for one buyer and an irrational one for another in the same tower, purely because of how much each had paid and what their clause provided.

## When is walking away better than claiming?

Sometimes the right answer is to complete, take handover and move on, even when a delay claim exists. A buyer whose unit has appreciated 15% against the contract price on an AED 1.5 million purchase has captured AED 225,000, which usually dwarfs any delay entitlement, and litigation against a developer whose cooperation you still need at handover and at resale carries a cost that never appears on the invoice.

Weigh three things: the size of the entitlement against the cost of pursuing it, the market position of the unit today against the contract price, and whether the developer is one you would buy from again. Where the unit has fallen in value and the delay is severe, termination and refund becomes the question instead of compensation, and that is a higher bar requiring either a fundamental breach established through the courts or a formal cancellation. The narrower statutory cooling-off and cancellation position is covered on the [cooling-off period page](/guides/dubai-cooling-off-period-off-plan/), and the handover process you are heading toward on the [handover checklist](/guides/dubai-property-handover-checklist/).

## What should you check in the SPA before you sign?

Every problem on this page is cheaper to prevent than to litigate, and the prevention takes about 1 hour of careful reading. Before signing an off-plan SPA, extract these 6 answers in writing and keep them with the contract, because each one determines whether a delay two years from now is an inconvenience or an unrecoverable loss.

1. The anticipated completion date, the grace period in months, and every carve-out that can extend them.
2. Whether the SPA contains a buyer-side delay compensation clause, and what it actually pays.
3. The delay threshold at which you gain a termination and refund right, if any.
4. Whether the payment schedule is construction-linked or date-linked.
5. The escrow account details and confirmation that the project is registered and funds are held under the regulated escrow regime.
6. The dispute forum the contract names, and whether it is the Dubai Courts, arbitration or a DIFC route.
