## Which Gulf city has the deepest school market?

Dubai typically offers the deepest Gulf international school market with about 220 KHDA-regulated private schools and fee bands from AED 12,000 CBSE seats to AED 130,000 elite campuses, and Invest Gulf family checklists start there before Abu Dhabi, Doha, or Riyadh comparisons for the same curriculum across Dubai Abu Dhabi Doha and Riyadh shortlists.

Dubai international schools regulated by KHDA number roughly 220+ campuses serving over 300,000 students, with published Outstanding, Very Good, Good, and Acceptable ratings parents can read in full at khda.gov.ae before paying deposits. Fee bands in the emirate typically run from AED 12,000 to 25,000 for budget Indian CBSE options, AED 30,000 to 60,000 for mid-range British or American schools, and AED 90,000 to 130,000 for elite campuses such as top GEMS and British names. Employer education allowances of AED 40,000 to 100,000 per child are common in senior packages, yet families still need bus budgets of AED 5,000 to 12,000 and registration deposits of AED 500 to 2,000. Invest Gulf relocation models treat school choice and housing district as one spreadsheet, not two separate decisions, especially when FS1, FS2, and Year 7 wait lists stretch 6 to 24 months.

Insider tip: read the full KHDA subject and wellbeing narrative, not only the overall grade, because a Good school with Outstanding welfare often beats a Very Good campus with Acceptable inclusion for younger children.

### Abu Dhabi: Strong Quality, Smaller Pool

Abu Dhabi has approximately 200 private schools regulated by ADEK, including Al Ain campuses, with Taaleem inspections mirroring KHDA methods but thinner choice in any single neighbourhood.

### Qatar (Doha): Significant Government Investment

Doha serves roughly 150,000+ students under MOEHE private-school rules, with Education City university branches anchoring premium pathways after Qatar National Vision 2030 and World Cup-era campus builds.

### Saudi Arabia (Riyadh): Fastest-Growing Market

Riyadh is the fastest-growing Gulf school market under Vision 2030 hiring, though public inspection detail still lags Dubai; Jeddah remains the longer-established Red Sea expat school base.

| City | Private scale signal | Inspection transparency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dubai | 220+ KHDA schools | Full public reports |
| Abu Dhabi | ~200 ADEK schools | Published Taaleem cycle |
| Doha | 150,000+ students metro | Less granular MOEHE |
| Riyadh | Fastest growth | MoE less public |
