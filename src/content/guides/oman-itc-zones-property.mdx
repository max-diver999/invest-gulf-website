Oman foreign property ownership is not a Muscat-wide freehold market. It is a zone-specific ITC system where non-GCC nationals can acquire freehold only inside Integrated Tourism Complexes, master developments combining residential, tourism, retail, and leisure infrastructure under Royal Oman Police and Ministry of Housing supervision.

Al Mouj and Muscat Bay dominate investor attention. Newer complexes enter the list periodically. The investment thesis is stability in a USD-pegged currency, lower density lifestyle, and a potential ROP investor residency pathway at roughly OMR 250,000 (confirm current official rules), not Dubai-scale liquidity or 8% gross yields.

This guide covers ITC zone mechanics, qualifying developments, yields, costs, residency linkage, and the diligence gaps that cost buyers money after the SPA is signed.

### What are Oman ITC zones and who can buy freehold?

Oman's ITC ownership law permits both Omani and non-Omani natural and juridical persons to own land or built units in a licensed Integrated Tourism Complex. The key legal test is the licensed ITC and the individual property record, not a general claim that all Muscat property is available to foreign buyers.

Oman's Integrated Tourism Complex ownership framework is defined by the Royal Decree on real-estate ownership in licensed ITCs. The decree distinguishes 2 owner categories: natural persons and juridical persons. It permits Omani and non-Omani persons in both categories to own land or built units within a licensed ITC. That wording is narrower than a city-wide freehold right and wider than a rule limited to completed apartments. A buyer should therefore identify the exact project, confirm that it is a licensed ITC, and obtain the unit's title and registration documents before transferring any deposit. The legal source establishes the ownership permission, but it does not prove a property's rental income, resale liquidity, visa eligibility, or financing terms. Those are separate commercial and immigration questions. [Royal Decree: Real Estate Ownership Act for Integrated Tourism Complexes](https://www.housing.gov.om/cmsapi/files/content/Royal%20Decree%20-%20Real%20Estate%20Ownership%20Act%20for%20the%20Integrated%20Tourism%20Complexes.pdf) is the primary source to review with an Omani lawyer.

Critical rule: ITC status applies to the development and the specific unit, not to a city-wide map in a broker PDF. Your Omani lawyer pulls the official title register entry before you wire a deposit.

Al Mouj and Muscat Bay dominate foreign ownership with the deepest resale history; newer zones require extra title verification.

Al Mouj remains the deepest ITC market, established rental history, golf and marina infrastructure, Carrefour retail anchor, and the largest foreign-owner community in Oman. Muscat Bay offers newer waterfront product with thinner resale comparables.

## What is the regulatory framework for foreign ITC ownership?

Oman's ITC Act allows Omani and non-Omani natural or juridical persons to own land or built units in a licensed ITC. This permission applies to the project asset only. It does not grant residence, establish yield, secure finance, or allow buying outside a licensed ITC. MORE Group Insider tip: verify title and ROP's 2-year owner visa rule.

The Omani Royal Decree on Integrated Tourism Complex ownership is a primary legal source for foreign ITC buyers. It permits 2 owner groups, natural persons and juridical persons, to own land or built units in a licensed ITC. The permission applies to both Omani and non-Omani owners. A buyer should therefore confirm 3 things before paying a deposit: the project holds an ITC licence, the contract identifies the exact land or built unit, and the buyer's ownership and registration documents match that asset. The decree establishes a right to own within a licensed ITC, but it does not state a minimum investment amount, a rental-return range, a mortgage limit, or an immigration entitlement. Separately, Royal Oman Police states that the property-owner visa is valid for 2 years and renewable while ownership continues. [Royal Decree on ITC ownership](https://www.housing.gov.om/cmsapi/files/content/Royal%20Decree%20-%20Real%20Estate%20Ownership%20Act%20for%20the%20Integrated%20Tourism%20Complexes.pdf).

For a non-Omani buyer, an ITC purchase is a title-and-licence exercise rather than a general Muscat freehold permission. The Royal Decree allows 2 legal owner types, natural persons and juridical persons, to acquire land or built units only within a licensed Integrated Tourism Complex. That wording makes 4 documents central to diligence: evidence of the ITC licence, the sale contract, the unit or land description, and the registration record. A marketing map cannot replace those documents because it may describe a development without proving the legal status of a particular asset. The Act does not define an automatic visa, financing, price, or return outcome. Royal Oman Police lists a property-owner visa valid for 2 years, renewable while ownership continues. [Oman ITC ownership Act](https://www.housing.gov.om/cmsapi/files/content/Royal%20Decree%20-%20Real%20Estate%20Ownership%20Act%20for%20the%20Integrated%20Tourism%20Complexes.pdf) is the starting point.

The Royal Decree supplies a practical diligence sequence for an ITC purchase. First, verify that the development is licensed as an Integrated Tourism Complex. Second, confirm whether the buyer will acquire a land interest or a built unit, because the decree expressly covers both. Third, identify the buyer category: the law permits Omani and non-Omani natural persons as well as juridical persons. Fourth, match the sale contract, project approval, title record, and registration process to that exact asset. This 4-step sequence is more reliable than a marketing map or a generic statement that “foreigners can buy in Muscat.” The decree does not publish a minimum purchase price, mortgage limit, rental return, or visa threshold. Those items should be verified separately with the relevant authority, lender, developer, and licensed adviser before a buyer relies on them. [Official Royal Decree PDF](https://www.housing.gov.om/cmsapi/files/content/Royal%20Decree%20-%20Real%20Estate%20Ownership%20Act%20for%20the%20Integrated%20Tourism%20Complexes.pdf) is the legal starting point.

**Insider tip:** obtain the ITC licence reference and the unit-specific title or registration evidence before paying a reservation fee. A development name, a brochure, and a broker statement do not establish the same thing as the record for the unit you are buying.

### What financing options exist for ITC property?

Omani banks may lend 60% to 80% LTV depending on residency; cross-border UAE financing is limited and usually expensive.

Typical financing terms (2026):
- Loan-to-value: 70-80% for Omani residents, 60-70% for non-residents
- Interest rates: 4.5-6.5% depending on relationship and profile
- Loan tenure: Up to 25 years for property purchases
- Income requirements: Minimum OMR 1,000 monthly for basic eligibility

Documentation requirements:
- Salary certificate and bank statements (6 months)
- Property valuation by bank-approved valuer
- ITC zone confirmation and developer approval
- Insurance coverage for property and loan protection
