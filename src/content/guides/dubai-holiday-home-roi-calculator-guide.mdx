## What is the Dubai holiday home ROI formula?

Dubai holiday home ROI typically means annual net STR income after DET fees, management, and service charges, divided by total acquisition cost including 4% DLD and furnishing. Gross Airbnb screenshots are not the formula for the market. Our analysts model still reject 85% occupancy decks that ignore summer vacancy.

Formula checklist before any SPA:

1. Build 12-month nightly blend, not peak season only.
2. Use 68% to 72% occupancy unless operator P&L proves higher.
3. Subtract DET, Tourism Dirham, 7% municipality, and management.

Dubai holiday-home ROI for a Marina one-bedroom typically starts from total acquisition near AED 1.1 million to AED 1.5 million, then subtracts DET permit fees of AED 1,520, Tourism Dirham near AED 15 per bedroom night, municipality tax of 7%, and management of 15% to 20%. Professional operators often land annual occupancy around 68% to 72%, or about 250 to 263 booked nights, not the 85% brochure figure. Furnishing of AED 35,000 to AED 65,000 belongs in the denominator with the 4% DLD charge, because unfurnished stock cannot launch short stays. Cleaning across 40 to 60 turnovers at AED 150 to AED 250 per visit is another line owners forget. Net yield only beats long-term Ejari when the building permits holiday homes and the nightly blend supports the premium after those operating costs. A model built without those three inputs is a forecast, not an underwriting. Occupancy assumptions above 75% deserve particular scepticism unless a real operator produced them for that building.

Insider tip: If OA permission is verbal only, price the deal as long-term rental until you hold written approval.

### Gross revenue checklist

**Gross STR revenue = Average nightly rate × Booked nights per year**

Booked nights = 365 × Occupancy rate − Owner use days − Maintenance blocks

### Net operating income (NOI)

**NOI = Gross revenue − Operating expenses**

### ROI / Net yield

**STR net yield = NOI ÷ Total acquisition cost**

Total acquisition cost = Purchase + DLD (4%) + Trustee + Furnishing + Setup + Contingency

## What acquisition cost belongs in the ROI denominator?

Acquisition cost in the ROI denominator typically means purchase price plus 4% DLD, trustee fees, furnishing, and a contingency near 5%. On a AED 1,350,000 Marina one-bedroom, that stack reaches about AED 1,525,500. Invest Gulf still treats purchase-only denominators as a 10% to 15% ROI overstatement.

| Line item | 1BR Marina example (AED) |
|---|---|
| Purchase price | 1,350,000 |
| DLD 4% | 54,000 |
| Trustee / admin | 4,000 |
| Furnishing & setup | 50,000 |
| Contingency (5%) | 67,500 |
| **Total acquisition** | **1,525,500** |

Using purchase price alone as denominator **overstates ROI by 10 to 15%**.

## Step 2: revenue inputs, nightly rate and occupancy

Revenue inputs typically mean a 12-month blended nightly rate of AED 320 to AED 380 for a standard Marina one-bedroom, not a peak AED 500 screenshot. Professional occupancy assumptions sit near 68% to 75%, or about 255 nights at 70%. Invest Gulf still discounts marketing decks that annualise December pricing across the market.

| Season | Months | Rate (1BR Marina) | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| Peak (events, Q4 to Q1) | 5 | AED 420 to 550 | 40% of revenue |
| Shoulder | 4 | AED 300 to 380 | 35% |
| Summer low | 3 | AED 220 to 280 | 25% |

**Blended average:** AED 320 to 380/night for well-furnished standard 1BR, not AED 500 every night.

### Occupancy assumptions

| Management level | Annual occupancy | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Self-managed, inexperienced | 50 to 58% | Learning curve, review gaps |
| Professional operator | 68 to 75% | Industry benchmark |
| Premium branded | 72 to 80% | Top Marina/JBR only |
| Marketing deck | 85%+ | Peak months only, reject |

**Booked nights at 70% occupancy:** 365 × 0.70 = **255 nights**

## Which operating expenses belong in STR ROI?

Operating expenses in Dubai STR typically include the AED 1,520 apartment DET permit, Tourism Dirham near AED 15 per bedroom night, 7% municipality fee, and management of 15% to 20% of gross. Cleaning at AED 150 to AED 250 across 40 to 60 turnovers often exceeds owner expectations. Invest Gulf expense stacks still add DEWA, Mollak service charges, and insurance of AED 1,500 to AED 3,000.

## Worked example: marina 1br STR vs LTR

A Marina STR versus LTR worked example typically compares AED 340 blended nights at 70% occupancy against an Ejari lease on the same acquisition stack near AED 1,525,500. Many mid-priced units still net under 3% on STR after full costs. STR only wins when net beats LTR by about 1 percentage point or more after all fees.

### Assumptions

- Acquisition: AED 1,525,500 (table above)
- Blended nightly rate: AED 340
- Occupancy: 70% (255 nights)
- 1 bedroom → Tourism Dirham ~AED 15/night

### STR model

| Line | AED/year |
|---|---|
| Gross revenue (255 × 340) | 86,700 |
| Less: Management 18% | (15,606) |
| Less: Cleaning 50 × 200 | (10,000) |
| Less: DET permit | (1,520) |
| Less: Tourism Dirham (255 × 15) | (3,825) |
| Less: Municipality 7% | (6,069) |
| Less: DEWA landlord share | (5,000) |
| Less: Service charge (750 sq ft × 20) | (15,000) |
| Less: Maintenance | (13,500) |
| Less: Consumables / misc | (4,000) |
| **NOI** | **~12,180** |

**STR net yield on acquisition:** ~0.8%, **this example shows STR failing**

*Why?* Purchase price at AED 1.35M with only AED 340 blended rate underperforms. Let's recalculate with stronger inputs.

### Revised STR model (optimised unit)

- Purchase: AED 1,100,000 (better entry)
- Total acquisition: AED 1,245,000
- Nightly blend: AED 380
- Occupancy: 72% (263 nights)

| Line | AED/year |
|---|---|
| Gross (263 × 380) | 99,940 |
| Operating costs (same structure, scaled) | (72,400) |
| **NOI** | **~27,540** |
| **Net yield** | **~2.2%** |

Still below strong LTR, now compare LTR on same unit.

### LTR comparison (same AED 1.1M unit)

| Line | AED/year |
|---|---|
| Ejari rent | 78,000 |
| Service charge | (15,000) |
| Management 6% | (4,680) |
| Vacancy 7% | (5,460) |
| Maintenance | (11,000) |
| **NOI** | **~41,860** |
| **Net yield** | **~3.4%** |

On this unit, **LTR wins**. STR premium requires either lower purchase basis, higher nightly rate (premium fit-out + view), or 75%+ occupancy.

### STR-winning scenario

| Line | AED/year |
|---|---|
| Purchase + costs | 950,000 acquisition |
| Gross STR (275 nights × 400) | 110,000 |
| Total operating costs | (62,000) |
| **NOI** | **48,000** |
| **Net yield** | **~5.1%** |
| LTR Ejari on same unit | 72,000 rent → ~38,000 NOI → 4.0% |

Here STR adds **~1.1 percentage points net**, the realistic premium when operation is tight.

## Which Dubai areas deliver the best holiday home ROI?

Tier-1 Dubai holiday home areas typically mean Marina, JBR, and Downtown, where blended one-bedroom nights run about AED 350 to AED 550 and pro-managed occupancy sits near 65% to 78%. JVC and Sports City usually lose to Ejari after full STR costs. We still verify OA permission building by building.

| Area | Blended nightly (1BR) | Occ (pro-managed) | STR viable? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dubai Marina | AED 350 to 450 | 68 to 76% | Yes, verify OA |
| JBR | AED 380 to 480 | 70 to 78% | Yes |
| Downtown | AED 400 to 550 | 65 to 74% | Yes, premium entry |
| Business Bay | AED 280 to 380 | 62 to 72% | Growing, mid-term mix |
| Palm (apartment) | AED 500 to 800 | 60 to 70% | Premium capital |
| JVC | AED 180 to 260 | 55 to 65% | Weak vs LTR usually |
| Sports City | AED 160 to 240 | 50 to 60% | LTR preferred |

Marina, JBR, Downtown, Palm apartments, and canal-side Business Bay concentrate most investable STR demand, with professional occupancy often between 62% and 78% when OA rules allow short stays. Nightly blends of AED 350 to AED 550 in those corridors can still fail after 15% to 20% management, AED 1,520 DET fees, and summer occupancy drops of 15 to 25 points. JVC and Sports City nights near AED 160 to AED 260 rarely clear a full cost stack versus Ejari leases already near 5.5% net. Roughly 20% to 30% of buildings restrict holiday homes entirely, which turns any spreadsheet into a long-let model overnight. Diligence should pull tower-level comps, Mollak service charges, and written OA approval before reserving a unit or wiring any holding deposit. Occupancy assumptions above 75% deserve particular scepticism unless a real operator produced them for that specific building rather than for the district.

## What sensitivity factors move STR ROI most?

Sensitivity on Dubai STR typically means a 10% cheaper purchase adds about 0.8 to 1.2 percentage points of net yield, while five occupancy points add 0.6 to 1.0 points. An unmodelled summer drop of 20% can erase 1.5 to 2.5 points. Invest Gulf priority order still starts with OA permission in the market.

| Variable | Impact on net yield |
|---|---|
| Purchase price −10% | +0.8 to 1.2 pp |
| Occupancy +5 points | +0.6 to 1.0 pp |
| Nightly rate +AED 30 | +0.7 to 1.0 pp |
| Self-manage vs 18% mgmt | +1.0 to 1.5 pp (time cost) |
| OA bans STR | Deal killer |
| Summer occ −20% unmodelled | −1.5 to 2.5 pp surprise |

**Priority order for diligence:** OA permission → occupancy data → purchase basis → nightly rate comps.

## When is STR more profitable than long-term rental?

STR is typically more profitable than long-term rental when OA permission is written, trailing occupancy clears 68%, and net STR beats Ejari by at least 1 percentage point after 15% to 20% management. LTR wins when Ejari already nets 5.5% or the building bans short stays. We still default to LTR when that premium is thinner than AED 10,000 a year.

Choose **STR** when:

- [ ] OA written permission confirmed
- [ ] Location in tier-1 STR area (Marina, JBR, Downtown, BB canal)
- [ ] Trailing occupancy data supports 68%+
- [ ] Net STR exceeds LTR by 1+ pp after full costs
- [ ] You accept active management or 18% fee

Choose **LTR** when:

- [ ] OA restricts STR
- [ ] Ejari net yield already 5.5%+
- [ ] Passive income priority
- [ ] Building has high STR supply (competition)
- [ ] Purchase price does not support nightly premium

## What common holiday home ROI errors should you avoid?

Common holiday home ROI errors typically include annualising December rates across 365 days, omitting AED 50,000 furnishing, and skipping the AED 1,520 DET permit. Cleaning for 40 to 60 turnovers is a real cash cost. We still compare against Ejari, not asking rents, in [Dubai Rental Yield Guide](/guides/dubai-rental-yield-guide/).

1. Peak-season annualisation: December nightly rate × 365.
2. Ignoring owner blocks and personal-use nights.
3. Zero cleaning budget on 40 to 60 turnovers.
4. Furnishing excluded from capital (AED 35,000 to AED 65,000).
5. LTR comparison uses listing rent instead of Ejari.
6. No permit cost line at AED 1,520 minimum.

## What actually moves holiday home ROI in Dubai?

Holiday home ROI in Dubai typically moves with building permission and realistic occupancy more than headline nightly rates of AED 400+. DET approval and OA rules decide whether the model is investable at all. We still verify both in writing before revenue assumptions.

| Driver | What to verify | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Building permission | DET permit plus OA approval | Without both, the STR model is not investable |
| Comparable ADR | Active listings in the same tower | Marina-wide averages overstate weak buildings |
| Occupancy | Low-month occupancy, not peak only | Summer vacancy can erase winter gains |
| Furnishing cost | Full setup quote | Linen, kitchenware, and photography add capital |
| Management fee | Percent of gross plus cleaning | Cheap managers can raise vacancy |

Two rules protect most investors. First, compare net STR income with a conservative 12-month Ejari lease in the same building. If STR only beats long-term rent by AED 10,000 to 15,000 per year, the extra operational risk is often not worth it. Second, do not buy for holiday-home use until building approval is confirmed in writing.

## When is short-term rental the wrong strategy?

Short-term rental is typically the wrong strategy when OA rules block holiday homes, when Ejari already nets 5.5% or more, or when purchase price cannot support nightly premiums after AED 35,000 to AED 65,000 of furnishing. Peak-season calendars are not underwriting. We still walk from deals that only work at 85% occupancy.

## What checklist should run before you reserve an STR unit?

A pre-reservation checklist typically requires written OA and DET permission, 68% to 72% occupancy assumptions, furnishing inside acquisition cost, and a summer stress test against Ejari net yield. Undocumented Mollak schedules after week one are a hard stop. We still compare net STR premium in percentage points before any reservation fee.
