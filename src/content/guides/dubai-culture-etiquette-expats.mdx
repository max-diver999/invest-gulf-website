## What shapes Dubai's cultural landscape for expats?

Dubai culture typically blends an Islamic emirate framework with a workforce where expats are about 68% of the population, so successful newcomers adapt behaviour by context rather than assuming one Western default. Invest Gulf relocation briefings start with modest dress, Ramadan daylight rules, and relationship-first business habits. Emiratis are a minority locally but set public norms.

## How should expats show Islamic and mosque awareness?

Islamic customs awareness typically means respecting five daily prayer windows and Friday Jumu'ah around 12:00 to 13:30 without needing to practise Islam yourself. Invest Gulf workplace notes treat prayer-room access in malls and offices as normal infrastructure across a 5 day office week. Mosque visits require modest dress, removed shoes, and permission before photos in the first 30 days of touring.

- Remove shoes before entering a mosque
- Long pants, long sleeves, and head covering for women on mosque tours
- Remain quiet during prayer times
- Cultural sites such as Dubai Museum, Heritage Village, Al Seef, and traditional souks reward the same modesty defaults

Sheikh Mohammed Centre for Cultural Understanding offers tours that many newcomers complete inside their first 30 days.

## What changes during Ramadan for work and public life?

Ramadan in Dubai typically reshapes about 30 days of the calendar (2026 planning window roughly March 10 to April 8, verify exact dates), with shorter work days such as 9:00 to 15:00 or 10:00 to 16:00 and daylight bans on public eating, drinking, and smoking. Invest Gulf teams move lunches into hotels or private rooms. Non-Muslims may eat in designated hotel areas.

| Time | Normal Dubai | During Ramadan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sunrise to sunset | Regular public eating | Restricted in public spaces |
| Work hours | 9:00-18:00 typical | Often 6 to 7 hour days |
| Iftar | Normal dinner | Breaking fast; family priority |
| Evening | Normal social mix | Extended family and social time |

- Offices often provide private areas for non-fasting staff
- Conference calls with overseas clients continue
- Iftar invitations are relationship opportunities; dates and water come first
- Hotel iftar buffets need advance booking at popular venues

## What dress codes apply in malls, offices, and beaches?

Dress codes in Dubai typically require covered shoulders and knees in malls, government offices, and traditional areas, while beachwear stays at beaches and pools and should be covered when you leave the sand. Invest Gulf packing lists treat Ramadan as one notch more conservative for about 30 days each year. Free-zone offices are more casual than DIFC client meetings, but shorts still fail most office floors across a normal 5 day week.

| Setting | Expectation |
| --- | --- |
| DIFC / Business Bay meetings | Suits common; modest necklines |
| Media/Internet City | Smart casual; shorts still rare |
| Dubai Mall / Mall of the Emirates | Shoulders and knees covered |
| Gold Souk / Deira | More conservative; loose layers help |
| Beach / pool | Swimwear OK on site only |
| Hotel bars | Smart casual; international zones looser |

Summer heat from May through September still does not cancel modesty expectations in public malls.

## How do greetings, hospitality, and public behaviour work?

Social etiquette typically means offering "Marhaba" as a safe greeting, waiting for a woman to extend her hand first, and exchanging business cards with the right hand or both hands. Invest Gulf hosts treat Arabic coffee and at least one small cup as hospitality you should accept within the first 30 days of networking. Public kissing remains restricted outside private or hotel contexts every day of the year.

| Situation | Safer default |
| --- | --- |
| Holding hands | Usually acceptable |
| Passionate PDA | Avoid in public |
| Photography | Ask first; skip sensitive sites |
| Metro dress | Cover shoulders and knees |
| Ramadan daylight | No public eating or drinking |

## What alcohol and nightlife rules should residents know?

Alcohol rules typically allow licensed venues such as hotels, clubs, and authorised restaurants, while home consumption for residents requires a licence many households budget around AED 270 per year. Invest Gulf compliance notes keep drunk driving at zero tolerance every day of the year. Public intoxication is illegal and seriously penalised.

| Topic | Practical rule |
| --- | --- |
| Where to drink | Licensed hotels, restaurants, clubs |
| Home licence | Emirates ID + salary evidence; ~AED 270/year |
| Peak nightlife | Thursday-Friday weekend nights |
| Networking venues | Golf clubs, beach clubs, business councils |

Dating in expat circles often follows Western norms, but marriage certificates still matter for family visa sponsorship. Conservative expectations rise in traditional neighbourhoods.

## How does Dubai business etiquette actually differ?

Business etiquette in Dubai typically privileges wasta and face-to-face relationship building over purely transactional email, and decisions can take multiple meetings across 2 to 4 weeks. Invest Gulf negotiation prep assumes Friday afternoon meetings are poor form and Ramadan's ~30 days need buffer time. Present cards respectfully; bilingual Arabic/English cards help in the first 90 days.

| Business habit | Practical rule |
| --- | --- |
| Hierarchy | Age and title get visible respect |
| Feedback | Public praise, private correction |
| Disagreement | Indirect; read between lines |
| Contracts | Personal trust still matters |
| Timeline | Budget 2 to 4 weeks for multi-meeting deals |

## Which cultural mistakes and family habits matter most?

Cultural mistakes typically cluster around rushed meetings without rapport, left-hand-only greetings, soles of shoes pointed at others, public PDA, and eating in public during Ramadan daylight. Invest Gulf family notes add school National Day events and Arabic classes as low-friction integration paths across the first 12 months. Sharjah is more conservative than Dubai; adjust when you cross emirates.

| Mistake | Better habit |
| --- | --- |
| Rushing to business | Spend the first meeting on people |
| Beachwear in malls | Cover up after the beach |
| Photos without consent | Ask first, especially families |
| Ignoring prayer windows | Buffer 12:00-13:30 on Fridays |
| Assuming one dress code | Match venue: souk vs hotel vs beach |
