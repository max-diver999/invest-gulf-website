Royal Decree Law M/14 took effect on 22 January 2026 and created the first nationwide legal framework for non-Saudi property ownership in designated zones supervised by the Real Estate General Authority (REGA). This is not nationwide freehold. It is a zone-by-zone, project-by-project permission system where the REGA designated zone list is the first document every foreign buyer must verify.

Outside designated zones, general residential property in Riyadh, Jeddah, and Dammam remains restricted for non-Saudis. Inside designated zones, ownership is possible, but only in REGA-registered projects with the correct deed type (confirm current official rules).

## How does law m/14 compare for Gulf buyers in 2026?

Law M/14 effective 22 January 2026 grants non-Saudi ownership only inside REGA-designated zones. It is not nationwide freehold. Implementing regulations from REGA define fees, property types, and registration mechanics that must be read alongside the decree.


Law M/14 establishes the right. REGA implementing regulations define the mechanics, fees, property types, registration process, and zone boundaries. Both are required reading.
## How designated zones work: three layers

Layer one is the published zone boundary on REGA. Layer two is developer registration for the specific project. Layer three is deed type and foreign eligibility on the title register. All three must align before SPA; Vision 2030 branding alone proves nothing.


## How does published and cited zones compare for Gulf buyers in 2026?

Commonly cited zones include NEOM, Red Sea Project, Qiddiya, Diriyah Gate, select ROSHN phases, and phased Riyadh and Jeddah districts (confirm current official rules). The list expands in phases, so save dated REGA screenshots during diligence.


The list will grow. Monitor REGA publications monthly if you are in active diligence.

## What foreigners cannot buy

General residential stock in central Riyadh, Jeddah, and Dammam outside designated zones remains restricted for non-Saudis. Agricultural land, Makkah and Madinah holy precincts, and unapproved commercial plots are off limits regardless of marketing.

## How does this comparison stack up for Gulf investors?

Zone property ownership does not automatically grant Premium Residency (~SAR 4M cited separately). Treat investment, residency, and exit liquidity as three independent tracks verified with REGA and immigration counsel.


A foreigner can own zone property without Premium Residency, and potentially qualify for Premium Residency without zone property (confirm current official rules). Plan each track independently.

## How does due diligence workflow compare for Gulf buyers in 2026?

Match project name to REGA zone list, verify developer registration, review SPA penalties and escrow, model a five-year zero-liquidity hold, and obtain Saudi lawyer written approval before deposit. Expect 2-4 weeks minimum; developers often pressure faster.


## How does eastern province compare for Gulf buyers in 2026?

Eastern Province designated pockets near Khobar and Dammam attract energy-sector tenants but remain thinner on resale depth than UAE freehold cores. Underwrite longer marketing periods and verify zone status per project.


## Comparison: Saudi zones vs UAE freehold

UAE offers decades of DLD data, deep resale, and Golden Visa linkage at AED 2M. Saudi designated zones offer lower entry tickets on giga-projects but higher rule-change risk and limited secondary pools. Saudi suits patient capital with counsel on the ground.


## What red flags should pause this Gulf purchase?

Pause on missing REGA designation letter, unregistered developer, off-plan without verified escrow, or sales decks that cite Dubai-style DLD fees. Refusal to provide zone proof in writing is a walk-away signal.
