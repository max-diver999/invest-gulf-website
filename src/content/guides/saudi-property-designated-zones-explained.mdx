Outside designated zones, general residential property in Riyadh, Jeddah, and Dammam remains restricted for non-Saudis. Inside designated zones, ownership is possible, but only in REGA-registered projects with the correct deed type (confirm current official rules).

## What the June 2026 Executive Regulation actually settled

Saudi Arabia's Council of Ministers approved the Executive Regulation of the Law of Real Estate Ownership by Non-Saudis on 23 June 2026, together with the geographic map of designated areas. That replaced the "phased list, check back later" position that applied through the first half of the year, and it is the document a buyer should now be working from.

The headline numbers are concrete. Riyadh carries nine designated zones, including King Abdullah Financial District, Diriyah Gate, New Murabba, Qiddiya, King Salman Park, SEDRA and Sports Boulevard, plus transit-oriented development sites. Jeddah carries a far larger set at 57 designated areas, Jeddah Central among them. Makkah and Madinah remain restricted to Muslim buyers. Outside the designated map, a foreign resident may hold one residential unit for personal use, which is a personal allowance rather than an investment route. Transfer charges on non-Saudi disposals are capped at 5%, and total acquisition cost commonly models near 10% once fees and taxes are stacked.

The practical consequence is that "can foreigners buy in Riyadh?" now has a precise answer rather than a cautious one: yes, inside nine named zones, and no elsewhere on freehold terms. Verify the specific project against the REGA register rather than against a developer's Vision 2030 branding, because zone eligibility and project-level registration are separate gates and only the second one lets a transaction complete.
