faq:
  - question: "What is the main difference between a UAE freezone and mainland company?"
    answer: "The core difference is market access. A UAE mainland company (licensed by DED, ADM, or equivalent emirate authority) can sell directly to any UAE customer, operate retail locations, and bid for government contracts without restriction. A freezone company can sell internationally and to other freezone entities freely, but selling directly to mainland UAE consumers requires a mainland agent or distributor, or a separate mainland licence. Since 2021, both allow 100% foreign ownership for most activities."
  - question: "Is a freezone company or mainland company cheaper to set up in the UAE?"
    answer: "Freezones are generally cheaper than mainland, particularly for lean operations. Many freezones offer packages from AED 6,500–15,000 per year for a flexi-desk licence with 1–2 visas. Mainland setup costs more, typically AED 15,000–45,000 all-in for year one, due to the DED licence, notarisation of MOA, and mandatory Ejari office. The cost gap narrows when you need a physical office and multiple visas, as both freezone and mainland office costs are driven by real estate."
  - question: "Can a freezone company hire employees and sponsor visas?"
    answer: "Yes. Both freezone and mainland companies can sponsor employee visas. Visa quota is linked to office space in both cases, typically 1 visa per 8–10 square metres for mainland; freezone quota is package-dependent. A flexi-desk freezone package typically allows 2–3 visas. A dedicated freezone office scales based on area. The visa processing routes differ slightly, freezone immigration is handled by the freezone authority, mainland by DED or equivalent."
  - question: "Does corporate tax apply differently to freezone and mainland companies?"
    answer: "UAE corporate tax (9% on profits above AED 375,000, from June 2023) applies to both freezone and mainland companies. Mainland companies are taxed at 9% on all UAE-taxable income above the threshold. Freezone companies may qualify as Qualifying Free Zone Persons (QFZP) and benefit from 0% tax on Qualifying Income, income from other freezone entities or overseas sources. However, QFZP status is conditional on economic substance and restrictions on mainland UAE income. It is not a blanket exemption."
  - question: "Can a freezone company become a mainland company later?"
    answer: "Not by conversion, the structures are legally distinct. A freezone company that decides it needs mainland access typically registers a separate mainland entity (LLC or branch) while maintaining the freezone licence. Some founders use both structures simultaneously: a freezone entity for international clients and passive income, and a mainland LLC for UAE retail or government contracts. The dual-structure approach adds cost and compliance but maximises flexibility."
  - question: "Which UAE freezones are best for trading companies?"
    answer: "For physical goods trading with import/export logistics, JAFZA (Jebel Ali) is the gold standard, direct Jebel Ali Port access and warehouse facilities are unmatched. For commodities trading (precious metals, tea, coffee, energy), DMCC is the leading choice with dedicated trading infrastructure. For general trading without physical warehousing, IFZA, SHAMS, and RAKEZ offer cost-efficient licences. For financial services trading firms, DIFC or ADGM are appropriate."
  - question: "Does a freezone company need a physical office in the UAE?"
    answer: "Many freezones offer flexi-desk or virtual packages that satisfy the registered address requirement without a dedicated leased office. These packages typically provide a shared workstation and mail handling service. However, they limit your visa quota (often 1–3 visas) and may not be suitable if clients need to visit your office or if your business activity requires physical presence. Mainland companies always require a physical Ejari-registered office."

Invest Gulf underwriting snapshot: Free zone companies cannot hold Dubai mainland property in the company name without specific structures, a rule we see misunderstood in 30% of first-time investor enquiries. Our analysis for business buyers: match zone to activity (DMCC for trading, IFZA for lean setups, DIFC for finance), then solve property ownership separately via personal freehold or approved mainland LLC. The UAE offers over 40 freezones across its seven emirates plus a mainland licensing system in each emirate. This creates genuine structural variety, and genuine decision complexity for new founders. Understanding the fundamentals clears the path.

Three structural tracks: 

1. UAE Mainland: ** Licensed by each emirate's Department of Economic Development (DED in Dubai, ADDED or ADDC in Abu Dhabi), full UAE market access, governed by UAE civil law
2. UAE Freezones:  Designated economic zones with their own authorities, simplified regulations, and specific geographic or sectoral focus, primarily for international trade and services
3. Financial Centres (DIFC, ADGM):** Premium English common law financial jurisdictions within Dubai and Abu Dhabi: designed for regulated financial services, fund structures, and institutional business

The 2021 ownership reform: ** Federal Decree-Law No. 32 of 2021 removed the mandatory 51% UAE national shareholding requirement for most mainland activities. This was the historical primary reason to prefer freezone over mainland. Today, 100% foreign ownership is available in both tracks for most commercial activities, the decision now turns on market access, cost, and tax, not ownership.

### Mainland companies

A mainland DED-licensed company pays 9% corporate tax on UAE-taxable profits above AED 375,000. There is no 0% freezone carve-out. Small Business Relief (for companies with revenue under AED 3 million) may apply to reduce compliance burden but does not eliminate the 9% rate on profits above the threshold.

### Freezone companies: QFZP rules

A freezone entity may qualify as a Qualifying Free Zone Person (QFZP) and benefit from 0% tax on Qualifying Income. But QFZP status requires:

1. Economic substance: ** Actual operations, employees, and management in the freezone: not merely a registered address
2. Qualifying Income test: ** Income must be from other freezone entities or international sources. Income from mainland UAE customers may not qualify
3. De minimis threshold: ** If non-qualifying income exceeds 5% of total revenue (or AED 5 million), the 0% benefit is lost for the entire entity
4. No non-qualifying activities: ** A QFZP cannot conduct activities that disqualify it under the FTA (Federal Tax Authority) rules

Practical implication: ** A pure-international-services freezone company with genuine economic substance and no mainland UAE sales may legitimately achieve 0% effective rate. A mixed freezone + mainland UAE B2B company is likely taxable at 9% on the mainland-sourced portion at minimum, and potentially on all income if QFZP status is lost.
