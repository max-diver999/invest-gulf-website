**Quick answer:** UAE freezone gives you fast setup, 100% foreign ownership, fixed annual costs, and international trade freedom — at the cost of direct UAE market access. Mainland gives you unrestricted UAE market access and government contract eligibility — at higher first-year cost and with more regulatory complexity. Both allow 100% foreign ownership for most activities since 2021.

**TL;DR:** If your revenue comes primarily from international clients or other freezone entities, a freezone is simpler and cheaper. If you need to sell to UAE consumers directly, win government tenders, or operate retail locations, you need a mainland licence. ## The UAE Business Setup Landscape — Key Context

The UAE offers over 40 freezones across its seven emirates plus a mainland licensing system in each emirate. This creates genuine structural variety — and genuine decision complexity for new founders. Understanding the fundamentals clears the path.

**Three structural tracks:**

1. **UAE Mainland:** Licensed by each emirate's Department of Economic Development (DED in Dubai, ADDED or ADDC in Abu Dhabi) — full UAE market access, governed by UAE civil law
2. **UAE Freezones:** Designated economic zones with their own authorities, simplified regulations, and specific geographic or sectoral focus — primarily for international trade and services
3. **Financial Centres (DIFC, ADGM):** Premium English common law financial jurisdictions within Dubai and Abu Dhabi — designed for regulated financial services, fund structures, and institutional business

**The 2021 ownership reform:** Federal Decree-Law No. 32 of 2021 removed the mandatory 51% UAE national shareholding requirement for most mainland activities. This was the historical primary reason to prefer freezone over mainland. Today, 100% foreign ownership is available in both tracks for most commercial activities — the decision now turns on market access, cost, and tax, not ownership.

## Market Access — The Key Decision Variable

**When you need mainland:**

- Selling to UAE retail consumers directly (restaurants, shops, clinics, schools)
- Supplying goods or services to UAE government entities or ministries
- Operating a DED-licensed real estate brokerage (RERA requirement)
- Running a manufacturing or industrial operation in Dubai or Abu Dhabi
- Any activity where the client base is primarily UAE-based individuals or companies not in freezones

**When freezone works:**

- Providing services to international clients (no UAE market sales)
- B2B services to multinational companies (which often have their own UAE entities)
- Commodities trading with international counterparties
- Technology, SaaS, or digital services with global customer base
- Holding company structure for international assets
- Professional services for other freezone companies

**The grey zone — B2B services to mainland UAE companies:** Many founders provide consulting, IT, or professional services to UAE mainland companies from a freezone entity. This is generally permissible, but the corporate tax treatment of this income under QFZP rules needs verification — income from mainland UAE companies may qualify as non-qualifying income and attract 9% tax regardless of the freezone structure.

## Visa and Immigration — Freezone vs Mainland

Both structures allow you to sponsor employee and investor visas. The key practical differences:

**Freezone visas:**
- Processed by the freezone authority (faster in most cases — 2–3 weeks end-to-end)
- Quota is package-based: flexi-desk typically 2–3; dedicated office scales based on space
- Premium freezones (DIFC, ADGM) issue employment visas with relevant freezone affiliation noted
- UAE Golden Visa is accessible to founders, senior executives, and investors through both freezone and mainland entities

**Mainland visas:**
- Processed by DED establishment card and GDRFA (General Directorate of Residency)
- Quota based on office area: approximately 1 per 9–10 square metres
- Investor visa available to LLC shareholders from AED 72,000 share value (indicative — verify current DED rules)
- Full family sponsorship (spouse, children, domestic staff) available to both freezone and mainland employees

## Corporate Tax — The Most Misunderstood Dimension

The introduction of UAE corporate tax in June 2023 fundamentally changed the freezone vs mainland cost calculation. Many founders still assume "freezone equals zero tax" — this is no longer straightforward.

### Mainland companies

A mainland DED-licensed company pays 9% corporate tax on UAE-taxable profits above AED 375,000. There is no 0% freezone carve-out. Small Business Relief (for companies with revenue under AED 3 million) may apply to reduce compliance burden but does not eliminate the 9% rate on profits above the threshold.

### Freezone companies — QFZP rules

A freezone entity may qualify as a Qualifying Free Zone Person (QFZP) and benefit from 0% tax on Qualifying Income. But QFZP status requires:

1. **Economic substance:** Actual operations, employees, and management in the freezone — not merely a registered address
2. **Qualifying Income test:** Income must be from other freezone entities or international sources. Income from mainland UAE customers may not qualify
3. **De minimis threshold:** If non-qualifying income exceeds 5% of total revenue (or AED 5 million), the 0% benefit is lost for the entire entity
4. **No non-qualifying activities:** A QFZP cannot conduct activities that disqualify it under the FTA (Federal Tax Authority) rules

**Practical implication:** A pure-international-services freezone company with genuine economic substance and no mainland UAE sales may legitimately achieve 0% effective rate. A mixed freezone + mainland UAE B2B company is likely taxable at 9% on the mainland-sourced portion at minimum, and potentially on all income if QFZP status is lost.

## Banking — Freezone vs Mainland

Both structures give you access to UAE corporate banking. Practical differences:

**Mainland LLCs:** Generally the easiest to bank. All major UAE banks actively offer accounts to DED-licensed mainland entities. The Ejari-registered office address and MOA structure are familiar to UAE bank compliance teams.

**Standard freezones:** Most major UAE banks accept DMCC, IFZA, JAFZA, RAKEZ, and similar established freezone entities. Some smaller or newer freezones face more scrutiny. DMCC entities, in particular, have strong bank relationships due to the zone's scale and reputation.

**DIFC and ADGM:** Easiest banking of all categories — the English common law structure, DFSA or FSRA regulatory oversight, and institutional character of these zones make them low-risk for UAE banks. International banks prefer these structures.

**Challenges:** Some UAE banks apply enhanced due diligence to freezone entities with no UAE market sales, particularly those with international shareholders and offshore income. Demonstrating genuine business operations and providing transaction documentation helps.

## Special Cases — When Neither Standard Structure Fits

**Holding company:** If your primary goal is holding assets (property, company shares, IP) rather than active trading, consider a DIFC Prescribed Company, ADGM Special Purpose Company, or a RAK ICC (International Corporate Centre) offshore company. These are leaner, cheaper holding vehicles than an operating freezone LTD or mainland LLC.

**Freelance / solo operator:** If you are a sole professional providing services and do not need to employ staff or have complex B2B contracts, a UAE freelance permit (available in most emirates, costs AED 6,000–12,000 per year) is a simpler and cheaper alternative to a full company structure. See [UAE freelance permit guide](/guides/uae-freelance-permit-dubai/).

**Dual-entity structure:** Some businesses operate a freezone entity for international revenue and a mainland LLC for UAE domestic sales — maximising tax efficiency on international income while maintaining direct local market access. This adds cost and compliance complexity but can be optimal for businesses with genuinely split revenue streams.

**Real estate holding:** Using a UAE company (freezone or mainland) to hold property has specific DLD or DMT registration and corporate tax implications. For detailed analysis, see the [buying property through a UAE company guide](/guides/buy-property-through-uae-company/).

## Summary

The freezone vs mainland decision has become more nuanced since 2021's ownership reforms and 2023's corporate tax introduction. The old simple rule ("freezone for foreigners, mainland requires a local partner") no longer applies. Today the decision is:

- **Market access:** UAE retail and government contracts need mainland
- **Tax position:** QFZP 0% treatment is conditional and only available to genuine freezone operators with international or freezone-entity revenue
- **Cost:** Freezones are cheaper at entry level; the gap narrows with office and visa needs
- **Legal system:** DIFC and ADGM offer English common law; all others operate under UAE civil law

For most international founders establishing a services business, a well-chosen freezone is the right first step. For founders building a UAE-market-facing business from day one, the mainland LLC is the right tool. And for financial services, holding structures, and family offices, the financial centres are worth the premium.
