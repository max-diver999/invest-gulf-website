# Dubai Alcohol Rules for Expats 2026: Licences, Venues & Legal Lines

> Disclaimer: Alcohol regulations are subject to change by emirate decree. Verify current rules before arrival. This is not legal advice.


<TldrBlock text={"Alcohol is legal for non-Muslim adults in Dubai, in licensed venues and at home with a permit. Zero tolerance for drinking and driving. Purchase from MMI or African+Eastern stores only."} />

## The basic legal framework

Dubai permits alcohol within a structured framework set by the Dubai government:

- Emirate-level permission: Dubai (unlike Sharjah) permits alcohol in licensed venues
- Personal licence requirement: Residents technically need a personal alcohol permit to purchase for home use
- Licensed venue system: Hotels, certain restaurants, licensed clubs can serve alcohol to non-Muslim customers
- Age minimum: 21 years
- Religion restriction: Muslims may not purchase or consume alcohol under UAE law

What is legal:

- Drinking at a licensed hotel bar or restaurant
- Drinking at a licensed beach club or brunch venue
- Purchasing from MMI or African+Eastern with valid licence (or as a resident in practice)
- Drinking in a private residence
- Receiving alcohol as a gift

What is illegal:

- Public drinking or visible intoxication in any public space
- Drinking on beaches outside licensed beach clubs
- Drinking in parks, streets, car parks, or non-licensed premises
- Drinking in a vehicle (even as a passenger)
- Driving with any detectable blood alcohol
- Purchasing without a licence (technically, enforcement varies)
- Minors (under 21) consuming alcohol
- Muslims purchasing or consuming alcohol

## Personal alcohol licence: what it is and how to get one

The personal alcohol licence (also called a "home licence" or "liquor licence") is issued by the Dubai Government and allows non-Muslim UAE residents to purchase alcohol from licensed retailers.

Who can apply:

- Non-Muslim UAE residents with valid Emirates ID
- Aged 21 and above
- Dubai residents only (licence is emirate-specific)

Application process:

Since 2023, the personal alcohol licence system has been simplified and operates primarily through MMI (Maritime and Mercantile International).

| Step | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Visit MMI store | Bring Emirates ID and passport |
| Complete application | Non-Muslim declaration, ID details |
| Set monthly spend limit | You specify; can be adjusted |
| Receive licence | Digital and/or physical card |
| Use at MMI/A+E | Swipe or show at purchase |

Cost: The application fee has historically been nominal or free as part of retailer registration. Check current MMI website (mmi.ae) for 2026 fee structure.

Monthly purchase limits: The licence allows you to set a monthly spending limit. This can be adjusted at the retailer. There is no minimum spend requirement.

## Where to buy alcohol in Dubai

Only two licensed retail chains operate in Dubai:

| Retailer | Short name | Coverage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maritime and Mercantile International | MMI | Widespread, JBR, Al Quoz, Deira, Jumeirah |
| African+Eastern | A+E | Multiple locations, Business Bay, Jumeirah |

**Locations:** Both chains have multiple stores across Dubai. Neither operates in Deira's traditional retail areas or near mosques. Locations concentrate in newer commercial and residential zones.

**No supermarket sales:** Carrefour, Spinneys, and all other supermarkets in Dubai do not sell alcohol. This is a common surprise for new arrivals.

**Online delivery:** Both MMI and African+Eastern offer home delivery through their own platforms and third-party delivery apps (where permitted). Check current availability at mmi.ae or africaneastern.com.

**Duty-free:** Dubai Duty Free at both Dubai Airport terminals sells alcohol to arriving passengers in the standard international duty-free format. Arrivals allowance: 4 litres of spirits or 2 cartons of beer for non-Muslim travellers aged 18 and over.

## Licensed venue types: where to drink out

Dubai's hospitality licensing system means alcohol service is tied to the venue's operating licence.

| Venue type | Alcohol service |
| --- | --- |
| International hotel bars and restaurants | Yes, primary venue type |
| Licensed standalone restaurants | Yes, specific licences held |
| Beach clubs | Yes, most major ones licensed |
| Licensed brunch venues | Yes, most hotels and some restaurants |
| Nightclubs / rooftop bars | Yes, hotel-based primarily |
| Supermarkets and convenience stores | No |
| Unlicensed restaurants | No, any restaurant not licensed may not serve |
| Public beaches | No, unless within beach club perimeter |
| Sports venues | Variable, some DIFC and licensed event spaces |

**How to tell if a venue is licensed:** Alcohol is visibly on the menu. If in doubt, ask staff. Licensed venues display the licence prominently or can show it on request.

**DIFC (Dubai International Financial Centre):** Operates under its own legal framework with its own licensing system. The DIFC has a dense cluster of licensed restaurants and bars, popular with finance professionals. Access is open to the public; you do not need to work in DIFC to visit.

## Alcohol at home: practical reality

Many Dubai expats purchase from MMI or African+Eastern without holding a formal personal licence, relying on residency proof at the counter. The legal position requires the licence; enforcement varies.

**Recommended approach:** Obtain the personal licence through MMI. The process is straightforward, takes one visit, and puts you on the right side of the legal framework. This becomes more relevant if there is ever a police presence at a gathering.

**Bringing alcohol to social events:**

- Transporting sealed, purchased alcohol between licensed purchase and a private residence is legal
- Open containers in vehicles are illegal
- Alcohol at private parties in residential buildings is within the private residence framework

**Homeowners and rental tenants:** Whether you own or rent, the same rules apply. Your building management company has no legal right to confiscate alcohol that is within your private residence, but some building common areas have house rules against alcohol in lifts or lobbies.

## Ramadan alcohol rules

Ramadan introduces additional restrictions across all venues:

| Period | Venue type | Alcohol service |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ramadan daytime (before iftar) | Hotel bars | Typically suspended or limited to discreet areas |
| Ramadan daytime | Licensed restaurants | Usually suspended |
| Ramadan post-iftar | Licensed hotel venues | May resume after sunset (varies by venue) |
| MMI / African+Eastern | Retail stores | Typically closed daytime; limited hours overall |
| Eid Al Fitr (end of Ramadan) | Venues | Normal service resumes |

**Year-to-year variation:** Ramadan alcohol rules have relaxed somewhat in recent years, particularly for hotel venues serving international tourists. Verify specific venue and retailer hours each Ramadan rather than assuming, the rules have shifted.

**General principle during Ramadan:** The respectful approach is to drink discreetly, if at all, in designated spaces. Visible public alcohol consumption during Ramadan carries more legal risk than at other times.

## Drunk driving: absolute zero tolerance

This is the most important legal section for every expat who owns a car:

**UAE law:** Any measurable blood alcohol while operating a vehicle is illegal. The limit is **zero**. Not 0.08 as in the UK or US. Not 0.05 as in Germany. Zero.

**Penalties:**

| Consequence | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Immediate arrest | Roadside test, arrested on the spot |
| Vehicle confiscation | Up to 60 days minimum |
| Licence cancellation | |
| Criminal prosecution | Court case, not traffic fine |
| Fines | AED 20,000 and above |
| Potential imprisonment | Depending on case circumstances |
| Potential deportation | Non-nationals may be deported |

**This includes the morning after.** A night of drinking can leave measurable alcohol the following morning. If you are driving in the morning after an evening of drinking, you are taking a legal risk.

**Safe transport options on drinking nights:**

- Careem or Uber, both widely available, app-based, tracked
- Dubai Taxi (book via app for account)
- Hotel concierge taxi desk
- Designated driver arrangement
- Some venues offer courtesy buses or arrangements with taxi apps

There is no acceptable reason to drive after any alcohol consumption in Dubai.

## Public intoxication: a separate offence

Being visibly drunk in public is a criminal offence in Dubai, separate from driving.

| Situation | Risk |
| --- | --- |
| Stumbling or loud behaviour on a public street | Police arrest |
| Altercation while drunk in public | Serious legal consequences |
| Drunk behaviour in hotel lobby or lift | Hotel security may involve police |
| Drunk at airport (departures) | Can result in boarding denial or arrest |
| Drunk behaviour at beach (non-licensed area) | Criminal offence |

**What "public" means:** Anywhere outside your private residence and outside the perimeter of a licensed venue. The pavement outside a bar is public. The hotel lobby is borderline. The street is definitely public.

**Practical guideline:** Drink in the venue, finish there, travel home in a sober manner or via taxi.

## FAQ

**Can I have alcohol delivered to my home in Dubai?**
Yes, MMI and African+Eastern both offer home delivery. Some third-party apps also partner with licensed retailers. Confirm the delivery service requires licence or residency proof.

**What should I do if I am stopped by police after drinking at a hotel?**
If walking (not driving): remain calm, be cooperative, and explain clearly that you were at a licensed hotel. If any doubt, contact your embassy. Do not be combative. The most common resolution for non-driving incidents is a warning.

**Can I have alcohol at a beach barbecue?**
Only if the beach is within the perimeter of a licensed beach club. Public beaches in Dubai do not permit alcohol consumption. Kite Beach, Jumeirah Open Beach, and JBR Beach are public, no alcohol.

**Is wine at a licensed restaurant different from wine at a hotel?**
No legal difference for the drinker. Both are licensed venues. The licence is held by the venue. Your experience is the same.

**Can I bring wine from France in my checked luggage?**
Non-Muslim arrivals aged 18+ may bring up to 4 litres of alcohol (wine, spirits) duty-free. Declare above this limit at customs. Bring as checked baggage, not hand luggage, same rules as most countries.

**What if I am pulled over and have had one drink three hours ago?**
Any measurable blood alcohol is illegal. There is no safe amount. Use taxis if you have consumed any alcohol.

**Does a personal liquor licence limit what I can buy?**
The licence sets a monthly spending limit which you specify and can adjust. There is no restriction on type of product (beer, wine, spirits).

**Can I find alcohol-free alternatives in Dubai supermarkets?**
Yes, non-alcoholic beers (Heineken 0.0, Bavaria 0.0, etc.) and non-alcoholic wines are widely available in supermarkets. These are popular options during Ramadan and for Muslim colleagues at social events. See [Dubai relocation guide](/guides/dubai-relocation-guide/) for grocery shopping context.
