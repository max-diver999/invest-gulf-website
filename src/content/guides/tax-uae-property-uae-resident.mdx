**Personal Income Tax:**
The UAE does not levy personal income tax. This is federal law, there is no emirate-level override. For a UAE tax resident individual who receives rental income from UAE property, that income is not subject to any income tax in the UAE.

**Capital Gains Tax:**
The UAE does not levy capital gains tax on individuals. If you sell a UAE property and realise a gain, that gain is not taxed in the UAE. No withholding tax applies to the sale proceeds.

**Corporate Tax (for context):**
The UAE Federal Corporate Tax (9% on taxable income above AED 375,000, effective for financial years starting on or after 1 June 2023) applies to juridical persons, companies. Individual natural persons owning investment property in their personal name are generally not subject to Corporate Tax on rental income under the current legislative framework. UAE Ministry of Finance guidance confirms individual residential property rental is outside the Corporate Tax scope for natural persons.

**VAT:**

**Two Pathways to UAE Tax Residency for Natural Persons:**

**Pathway 1: The 183-Day Rule**
A natural person is a UAE tax resident if they are physically present in the UAE for 183 days or more in any 12-month period. Days of presence are counted including entry and exit days. This is the simplest quantitative test.

**Pathway 2: UAE as Domicile**
A natural person is also a UAE tax resident if:
- They have a permanent home in the UAE (not just a property, a home they genuinely use as their principal residence); AND
- Their centre of vital interests (financial and personal) is in the UAE; OR
- They are habitually resident in the UAE

This pathway allows people who split their time between countries to establish UAE tax residency even without crossing 183 days, if the UAE is genuinely their primary home base.

**What Is Not Sufficient Alone:**
- Owning property in UAE without a valid residence visa
- Having a UAE residence visa without physical presence
- Being physically present for fewer than 90 days without other strong ties

**Misconception 1: "Owning property in Dubai makes me a UAE tax resident"**

False. Property ownership provides a pathway to a UAE investor visa, a residency visa, not automatic tax residency. Tax residency requires meeting the 183-day presence test or the domicile criteria under UAE tax law. A UAE investor visa without physical presence does not establish tax residency.

**Misconception 2: "The UAE has no taxes at all"**

Partially false. The UAE has no personal income tax, no capital gains tax, and no personal inheritance tax on property. It does have 5% VAT (introduced 2018), UAE Corporate Tax (9% on business profits above AED 375,000, introduced 2023), and excise taxes on specific goods. For individual residential property investors, the practical personal tax liability in UAE is zero.

**Misconception 3: "I can move to Dubai and stop paying UK/Australian tax immediately"**

False. Simply moving to Dubai does not end home-country tax obligations. You must formally exit your home-country tax system, filing the relevant cessation notifications, which may have a lag period. Some countries (Germany, for example) have extended limited liability provisions that apply for years post-departure.

**Misconception 4: "US citizens are fine once they move to Dubai"**

False. The USA taxes its citizens on worldwide income regardless of residence. Moving to Dubai does not eliminate a US citizen's IRS obligations. US persons must report foreign assets (FBAR), maintain FATCA compliance (Form 8938), and declare worldwide income. The only true exit from US worldwide taxation is citizenship renunciation, an irreversible and consequential step.
