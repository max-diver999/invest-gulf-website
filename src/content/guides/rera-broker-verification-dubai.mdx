# RERA broker verification Dubai: Trakheesi, BRN, Form B

Dubai recorded more than 205,000 property transactions in 2024, with foreign nationals accounting for a large share. Every mediated deal should run through a **RERA-licensed** broker, yet unlicensed intermediaries still drive a disproportionate share of DLD fraud complaints. This guide shows how to verify a broker in under five minutes before you tour a unit or transfer a deposit.

| Check | Tool | Pass criteria |
|---|---|---|
| Broker BRN active | Trakheesi / Dubai REST | Status: Active, matching name |
| Brokerage licence valid | Trakheesi office search | Office licence not expired |
| Listing permit displayed | Bayut / Property Finder ad | Trakheesi permit number visible |
| Form B signed | RERA-mandated contract | Before any viewing or deposit |
| Commission agreed | Form B clause | 2% + 5% VAT market norm on secondary |

## Why does RERA license brokers in Dubai?

RERA licenses individual brokers through the DREI exam, continuing education, and annual renewal tied to a **brokerage office licence**. The office, not only the agent, carries accountability for escrow routing on off-plan introductions. If an agent trades outside an active BRN or a revoked office licence, DLD cannot restore money sent to personal accounts.

High transaction volume attracts new entrants marketing on WhatsApp and Instagram without registration. For overseas buyers, the Trakheesi check is the highest-return fraud filter before any emotional commitment to a unit.

## How do you run the five-minute Trakheesi check?

**Step 1:** Open trakheesi.ae or the Dubai REST app and choose the broker search tool.

**Step 2:** Enter the **BRN** (Broker Registration Number) from the business card, not only a mobile number.

**Step 3:** Confirm four fields: status **Active** (not Expired or Cancelled), name matching the card, brokerage name matching the firm they cite, and expiry date in the future.

**Step 4:** Search the **office licence** and confirm the agent's BRN is listed under that active firm. A personal BRN under a revoked office is not a legal engagement.

**Step 5:** On Bayut, Property Finder, or Dubai REST listings, locate the **Trakheesi permit number**. Ads without permits are unverified until the owner authorisation is proven.

If all five pass, you are dealing with a regulated intermediary. Deposits sent to **DLD-regulated escrow** on off-plan or formal secondary processes carry structurally different recovery options than cash to an individual.

## What is Form B and how does it differ from Form A?

**Form A** is the seller-broker agreement registering a listing with RERA, setting exclusivity (often three to six months), price, and co-broking splits. Sellers should insist on Form A before marketing begins.

**Form B** is the buyer-broker agreement defining search mandate, duration, termination, liability cap, and **written commission**. Market norm on secondary sales is **2% plus 5% VAT**, but RERA does not statutorily cap fees; only the signed Form B binds the number.

| Form | Who signs | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Form A | Seller + listing broker | Registers listing, exclusivity, commission split |
| Form B | Buyer + buyer broker | Search mandate, commission, engagement term |

On off-plan primary sales, developer-paid commission should leave the buyer line at **zero** when buying genuine developer inventory.

## Where do commission disputes come from?

RERA's real estate disputes channel sees repeated cases: verbal fee promises, no Form B, buyer completes privately, broker claims introduction fee. Courts have sided with brokers who can prove they supplied the listing trail even without Form B in some cases. Fix: negotiate commission in Form B **before** the first viewing and keep WhatsApp promises subordinate to the signed contract.

## What changes for off-plan purchases?

Ask for the developer **authorisation letter** for that project, not a generic developer logo on a card. Unit price should match the developer's official schedule whether you buy direct or via broker; deep "discounts" may signal assignment sales with separate risks.

Deposits must hit the **escrow IBAN** listed on Dubai REST for that project. Match the IBAN character by character to the SPA. Contract should commit to **Oqood** registration within a defined window after payment.

## How should remote buyers use brokers safely?

Remote buyers should add an independent UAE property lawyer (often AED 5,000 to 15,000) who reviews the SPA separately from the broker. Do **not** grant power of attorney to the selling broker. Verify title on Dubai REST by video before authorising transfer.

## What belongs in a Dubai secondary purchase cost stack?

Beyond broker commission, buyers budget DLD transfer fee at **4%** on typical secondary transfers, trustee and registration charges, NOC fees if applicable, and mortgage costs if leveraged. These are transaction mechanics separate from broker licensing but shape total cash need at completion.

## When should you walk away from a broker?

**Hard stops:** BRN missing or expired; deposit to a personal account; refusal to sign Form B; Trakheesi permit absent on ads; same-day pressure without cooling time; cash commission without VAT invoice; broker holding seller POA without disclosure.

**Proceed with caution:** dual agency (disclosed but negotiate independently); BRN active under a different office than the card states; no developer authorisation letter for the named off-plan tower.

## How do you report an unlicensed broker?

Document WhatsApp, receipts, and marketing. File via dubailand.gov.ae RERA services. If money already moved to a personal account, add Dubai Police cybercrime reporting and bank fraud recall immediately. Recovery inside UAE escrow is far stronger than chasing overseas personal transfers.



## How does a licensed broker fit your wider due diligence?

RERA registration covers **the intermediary**, not the asset. Parallel checks buyers still run: title on Dubai REST, service charge statements on Mollak for apartments, owners association rules, snagging on ready stock, and mortgage offer validity if financed. The broker coordinates viewings and document flow; your lawyer signs off on the SPA and transfer mechanics.

| Layer | Broker role | Your independent check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity | Must show active BRN | Trakheesi + office licence |
| Listing | Marketing with permit | Permit number on portal ad |
| Contract | Form B commission | Lawyer on SPA and transfer |
| Payment | Routes to escrow | IBAN match on REST |

Secondary buyers often underestimate **NOC** timing from developers or master communities; brokers can chase status but cannot waive legal prerequisites. Off-plan buyers should track construction milestones separately from broker enthusiasm.

## What mistakes do international buyers repeat?

Skipping Trakheesi because an agent "works with a big brand office" without verifying the individual BRN. Paying a "reservation fee" to a personal account to "hold" off-plan inventory. Signing Form B after the viewing but with blank commission boxes. Accepting WhatsApp PDFs as substitute for REST title snapshots. Each shortcut removes a recoverable audit trail if the deal fails.

Treat broker warmth as unrelated to regulatory status: friendly agents can still be unlicensed or selling without owner mandate. The fix remains the same five-step Trakheesi sequence at the start of every new agent relationship, even referrals from friends.



## How do brokers interact with mortgages and transfers?

Mortgage brokers and RERA sales brokers are different licences. If one person plays both roles, disclose conflicts and confirm which entity earns which fee. Bank valuation delays can push MOU dates; Form B should not auto-trigger full commission if the deal fails for financing outside your control unless you explicitly agree that clause.

On transfer day, the broker often coordinates with the trustee office but the buyer still verifies cheque amounts, seller NOC status, and DLD appointment slots. Licensed status does not remove your obligation to read the final transfer summary before signing.


## How often should you re-check Trakheesi?

Licences renew annually. Re-run the BRN search on the day you pay a reservation or sign an MOU, not only at first WhatsApp contact. Agents sometimes move between brokerages; an active BRN under Firm A yesterday may sit inactive under Firm B today if they changed jobs without updating you.

Keep screenshots of Trakheesi results dated the same day as any deposit. If a dispute arises, DLD expects evidence that you verified licensing before payment.

## Quick reference before you pay any broker fee

| Stage | Minimum action |
| --- | --- |
| First contact | Trakheesi BRN and office licence |
| Before viewing | Form B signed with commission filled |
| Before deposit | Escrow IBAN or transfer path confirmed |
| Before MOU | Listing Trakheesi permit verified |

Overseas buyers should save PDF exports from Trakheesi and Dubai REST in a dated folder shared with their lawyer. When a deal spans several weeks, repeat the BRN search before each new cheque tranche so you are not relying on a stale screenshot from the first viewing week.

If you use a buyer's agent introduced by a developer, still verify that person's BRN and the developer authorisation letter. Developer sales galleries and external brokers share inventory but not always the same compliance paperwork.

Dual-language marketing is common in Dubai; Arabic permit text on Trakheesi should still match the English listing ID you see on portals. When numbers disagree, pause until the listing broker reconciles with RERA records.

Share your Trakheesi screenshots with your lawyer before signing an MOU so commission and escrow clauses align with the same regulated party you verified online. That single habit prevents most identity mismatches we see in dispute files.

*Commission figures reflect market convention through Q1 2026, not statutory caps. Verify BRN status on the transaction date. This guide is information only, not legal advice.*
