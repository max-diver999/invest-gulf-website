  - question: "Do I pay income tax in Qatar as an expat?"
    answer: "No, Qatar has zero personal income tax for residents and non-residents. Your Qatar employment income, rental income from Qatar property, and local investment gains are not subject to Qatar income tax. However, you may still have home country tax obligations."
  - question: "Do I still need to file taxes in my home country while working in Qatar?"
    answer: "This depends on your nationality and home country tax laws. US citizens must file regardless of residence. UK residents may need to file if they maintain UK tax residency or have UK income. Germany has exit tax rules. Consult a qualified tax advisor for your specific situation."
  - question: "What is CRS and how does it affect me in Qatar?"
    answer: "CRS (Common Reporting Standard) means Qatar banks report your account details and balances to your tax-resident country automatically. If you're tax resident in a CRS country, your home tax authority receives information about your Qatar bank accounts and investment income."
  - question: "Do US citizens in Qatar need to report Qatar bank accounts?"
    answer: "Yes, US citizens must report foreign bank accounts over $10,000 via FBAR filing, and substantial foreign assets via Form 8938. Qatar accounts count as foreign accounts for US tax purposes, even though you live and work in Qatar."
  - question: "Is there capital gains tax on Qatar property sales?"
    answer: "No, Qatar doesn't impose capital gains tax on property sales by individuals. However, if you're tax resident in another country, you may owe capital gains tax there on Qatar property profits under their worldwide income rules."
  - question: "Should I change my tax residency when moving to Qatar?"
    answer: "Consider it carefully. Qatar residency may break home country tax residency if you qualify. This can reduce global tax burdens but may affect future benefits, pensions, or healthcare access. Rules vary significantly by country, so get professional tax advice before making changes."
