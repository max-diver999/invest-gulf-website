## Why is UAE inheritance different for non-Muslim property owners?

UAE inheritance for non-Muslims typically means courts may apply home-country law, but that discretion is not guaranteed without a registered UAE will, and Invest Gulf treats 12 to 36 months of freeze risk as the default path because foreign buyers who rely on an English or US will alone leave Dubai freehold under uncertain personal-status litigation.

The key problem is that "may apply" is not "shall apply." Without a DIFC instrument, estates face legal uncertainty and extended court timelines.

| DIFC Will type | What it covers |
|---|---|
| Property Will | Dubai freehold property, Emirates-based bank accounts |
| Business Will | DIFC-incorporated company shares |
| Financial Assets Will | DIFC-domiciled financial assets, brokerage accounts |
| Guardianship Will | Appointment of guardian for minor children in UAE |
| Full Will | All UAE sited assets, property, business, financial, personal |

Important limitation: A DIFC Will covers Dubai and Northern Emirates freehold property. Abu Dhabi property requires a separate ADGM Will.

## How does the DIFC Wills Service Centre work?

The DIFC Wills Service Centre typically allows non-Muslim foreigners to register English-law wills for Dubai freehold, DIFC shares, financial assets, and guardianship, and Invest Gulf budgets AED 4,000 to 12,000 all-in because foreign buyers who skip drafting often face DIFC Court probate delays beyond the usual 4 to 12 week Grant window.

| Item | Indicative 2026 figure |
|---|---|
| Property Will registration (single) | AED 950 |
| Mirror / couple Property Will | AED 1,600 |
| Full Will registration | AED 2,500 to 5,000 |
| Professional drafting | AED 2,000 to 8,000 |
| Straightforward processing | 3 to 10 business days |

Insider tip: Register the Property Will within 30 days of title transfer, then diary an annual review after any marriage, divorce, birth, or Abu Dhabi purchase that may require an ADGM instrument.

## What is an ADGM Will and when do you need one?

An ADGM Will typically covers Abu Dhabi freehold and ADGM company assets under a separate English-law registry, and we require both instruments when foreign buyers hold stock in Dubai and Abu Dhabi because a DIFC Will alone leaves Abu Dhabi title outside the 4 to 12 week DIFC probate path.

ADGM registration fees commonly run AED 950 to 3,500. Cross-check beneficiary language so the two wills do not contradict universal estate wishes.

- Register a DIFC Will for Dubai property
- Register an ADGM Will for Abu Dhabi property
- Keep executor names and shares consistent
- Use a lawyer who files both registries

## What happens under Sharia default inheritance rules?

Sharia default distribution typically awards a wife 1/8 with children and a husband 1/4 with children, with male children taking double female shares, and we flag this for foreign buyers who want 100% spousal transfer because without a DIFC Will the intended 100% spouse outcome fails under the fallback split.

- Spouses: wife 1/8 (with children); husband 1/4 (with children)
- Children: male receives double the share of female (with variations)
- Parents: fixed shares if children exist
- Non-Muslim beneficiaries may face barriers if the deceased is Muslim

A registered DIFC Will overrides this default for covered assets.

## How does Dubai probate work with and without a DIFC Will?

Dubai probate without a DIFC Will typically runs 18 to 36 months from death notice to DLD title transfer, while a DIFC Grant path often finishes in 8 to 20 weeks total, and we model that gap because foreign buyers underestimate frozen-account months when mortgage and service charges still fall due.

**Without a registered DIFC Will:**
1. Family notifies UAE authorities and DLD of death
2. UAE bank accounts and property ownership freeze
3. Apostilled death certificate translated to Arabic
4. File in Dubai Courts / Personal Status Court
5. Court determines applicable law (potentially 6 to 18 months)
6. Succession certificate issued
7. DLD transfer on court order (3 to 12 months more)
8. Total realistic timeline: 18 to 36 months

**With a registered DIFC Will:**
1. Executor obtains UAE death certificate
2. Files for Grant of Probate in DIFC Courts
3. DIFC Courts process under common law (4 to 12 weeks)
4. Grant issued
5. DLD transfers Dubai property (4 to 8 weeks after Grant)
6. Total realistic timeline: 8 to 20 weeks

## What freezes when a Dubai property owner dies?

Death notification typically freezes sole-name UAE bank accounts immediately, and DLD will not transfer title until a Dubai court succession order or DIFC/ADGM Grant appears, so we require foreign buyers to pre-fund 6 to 12 months of service charges and mortgage payments outside the deceased's sole account.

- Joint accounts may stay accessible depending on the bank
- Rental collection needs an authorised representative with legal standing
- Appoint an executor who can be UAE-present during probate
- Consider mortgage life cover payable to a UAE-accessible account

## What estate planning mistakes do Dubai owners make?

Estate planning mistakes typically include relying on a home-country will alone, skipping ADGM after an Abu Dhabi purchase, and leaving executors uninformed, and we see foreign buyers lose 12 to 36 months when a DIFC Will exists on paper but nobody knows how to file DIFC Courts probate.

1. Assuming an English/US will governs Dubai freehold automatically
2. Registering DIFC but not ADGM after buying Abu Dhabi property
3. Drafting without a DIFC-approved draftsman (ambiguous beneficiaries break probate)
4. Not updating after marriage, divorce, births, or new purchases
5. Ignoring DIFC company shares that need a Business Will
6. Never briefing the named executor

DIFC Will registration in 2026 typically costs AED 950 for a single Property Will or AED 1,600 for a mirror couple filing, with Full Wills at AED 2,500 to 5,000 plus drafting of AED 2,000 to 8,000, and we compare that AED 4,000 to 12,000 stack with AED 30,000 to 150,000+ litigation risk for foreign buyers who delay.

Processing for straightforward wills often finishes in 3 to 10 business days after documents land.

## What does a DIFC guardianship Will cover?

A Guardianship Will typically names who cares for minor children living in the UAE if both parents die, and Invest Gulf pairs it with a Property or Full Will because foreign buyers who cover assets alone still leave UAE courts to decide guardianship for 12+ months of uncertainty.

| Cover item | Without Guardianship Will | With Guardianship Will |
|---|---|---|
| Minor children in UAE | UAE court decides | Named guardian applies |
| Timeline uncertainty | Often 12+ months | Aligns with 4 to 12 week DIFC probate |
| Asset coordination | Separate court track | Pair with Property/Full Will (AED 4,000 to 12,000 stack) |

Without it, UAE courts decide guardianship. Keep guardian details consistent across DIFC filings and brief the named guardian within 30 days of registration.

## What cross-border tax issues affect UAE estates?

Cross-border estate tax typically sits outside DIFC probate itself, yet home-country domicile, trusts, and life insurance timing still reshape net outcomes, and we coordinate UAE counsel with home advisers because foreign buyers who optimise only DIFC fees can still trigger unexpected home-estate reporting across 10-year Golden Visa stays.

|---|---|

## How do company structures affect Dubai estate planning?

Company-held Dubai property typically needs a Business Will alongside a Property Will when shares sit in DIFC or free-zone entities, and we map tenants-in-common versus joint tenancy because foreign buyers who assume survivor rights alone still need a will for the second death within 8 to 20 weeks of probate planning.

| Ownership structure | DIFC Will strategy |
|---|---|
| Joint tenants | Survivor takes automatically; will covers survivor's death |
| Tenants in common | Will must specify each owner's share disposition |
| Corporate plus individual | Separate Business and Property Wills required |

## Who should you appoint as executor for UAE assets?

An UAE-capable executor typically needs authority to appear in DIFC Courts, manage local banks, and instruct DLD within the 4 to 12 week Grant window, and we prefer joint executors (one UAE-resident, one family) because foreign buyers who name only overseas relatives stall probate when flights and visas slip.

Executor powers should include:
1. Represent the estate in DIFC Courts and DLD
2. Access and manage UAE bank accounts during probate
3. Coordinate with home-country executors
4. Make property management decisions during probate
5. Engage valuers, agents, and local counsel

## How do mortgages and off-plan units affect inheritance?

Mortgaged or off-plan estates typically remain subject to the lender charge and unfinished SPA payment duties, and we budget life cover equal to the outstanding balance because foreign buyers who inherit title still face 18 to 36 month freezes if mortgage payments lapse from frozen accounts.

| Purchase stage | Estate planning complexity |
|---|---|
| SPA signed, pre-handover | Will must address incomplete property rights |
| Oqood registered | Standard property will coverage applies |
| Title deed issued | Full property ownership transferable |

- Confirm mortgage life insurance exists
- Plan refinancing or assumption by beneficiaries
- Keep liquid funds for payments during probate

DIFC versus onshore Dubai probate typically separates English-law Grants of about 4 to 12 weeks from Personal Status Court paths of 18 to 36 months when no UAE will exists. Our underwriting for foreign buyers prices registration near AED 950 to 1,600 for Property Wills and AED 4,000 to 12,000 with drafting, against estate legal spend of AED 30,000 to 150,000+ without one. Guardianship Wills cover minor children living in the UAE; ADGM Wills cover Abu Dhabi at roughly AED 950 to 3,500. Sole-name bank freezes begin at death notification, so 6 to 12 months of service-charge and mortgage liquidity should sit in a separate UAE-accessible account. Joint tenancy delays but does not erase the need for a will on the surviving owner's death.

## What checklist should you complete before registering a will?

A pre-registration checklist typically covers title schedule, executor contacts, guardianship, life cover, and ADGM gaps, and we run an annual review because foreign buyers who file once then buy Abu Dhabi stock leave 12 to 36 months of unprotected Northern-versus-Abu Dhabi mismatch.

**Before filing:**
- List every Dubai freehold folio and DIFC company share
- Name UAE-capable executors and backups
- Align guardianship with Property or Full Will
- Confirm mortgage life cover beneficiaries
- Decide whether ADGM is also required

**Annual review:**
- Update after marriage, divorce, children, or new purchases
- Re-check executor suitability
- Coordinate with home-country estate changes
