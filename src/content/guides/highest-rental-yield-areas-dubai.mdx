Dubai's yield story is built on a genuine foundation: 4 million residents, 68% foreign-buyer transactions, zero income tax, and a regulated rental market that produces mid-market gross yields that most comparable cities cannot match. Gross yields of 7 to 9% in the right communities are real and documented in DLD transaction data.

The gap between that headline number and what an investor actually receives is where analysis matters. Service charges, management costs, vacancy, and rising running expenses compress gross yield to a number that looks quite different from the brochure. This guide works through the real numbers, gross, net, and what drives the gap, for every significant investor community in Dubai.

## How do you read gross vs net yield correctly in Dubai?

Net yield is what you keep after Mollak service charges, management, vacancy, Ejari and maintenance, while gross yield is only annual rent divided by purchase price. Budget DLD's 4% transfer fee on capital deployed. Our analysts model a 1.5 to 3 point gap as the normal starting assumption.

| Cost layer | Typical range | DD note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DLD transfer (resale) | 4% of price | Plus admin and trustee |
| Service charges | AED 11 to 32/sqft/yr | Building-specific on Mollak |
| Management | 5 to 8% of collected rent | Higher for short-let |
| Vacancy | 4 to 10% by community | Never model zero |

Annual costs to include in net models:

- Service charges (from RERA Mollak, not developer estimate)
- Property management (5 to 8% of collected rent)
- Vacancy allowance (4 to 10% depending on community)
- DEWA / utility connections (minor recurring)
- Ejari registration (AED 520/year)
- Short-let licensing if applicable (AED 1,520/year for apartments)

**Return on capital deployed = net income ÷ (purchase price + acquisition costs)**

Transaction costs of 6 to 9% are a permanent capital drag. A property that earns 6% net yield on its purchase price earns approximately 5.6% net on total capital deployed including acquisition costs.

The yield table below uses a consistent net yield methodology across all communities.

## Which tier-1 communities lead on net yield in 2026?

Tier-1 net yield leaders for 2026 are Dubai Sports City and well-managed JVC towers, typically printing 5.5 to 7.4% net on gross bands of 7.5 to 9.5%. Discovery Gardens follows with AED 11 to 16 per sq ft charges. Our analysis ranks Mollak rates above community averages.

Dubai Sports City and Jumeirah Village Circle lead 2026 gross rental yields at roughly 7.8 to 9.5% and 7.5 to 9.2% respectively, typically compressing to 5.5 to 7.4% net after Mollak service charges, management, and vacancy. One-bedroom entry tickets often sit at AED 500,000 to 800,000 in Sports City and AED 550,000 to 1,000,000 in JVC, with service charges commonly AED 12 to 18 per sq ft in Sports City and AED 13 to 20 per sq ft across JVC towers. Discovery Gardens follows at 7.5 to 8.8% gross and 5.6 to 6.9% net with lower charges of AED 11 to 16 per sq ft. Citywide vacancy near 7 to 8% should sit in every model; prime Marina and Downtown units often run closer to 4 to 5%. Building-level Mollak rates matter more than community averages when two towers sit 200 metres apart.

### Dubai Sports City

Sports City yield checklist for foreign buyers:

- Target gross 7.8 to 9.5% and net 5.7 to 7.4%
- Prefer towers with Mollak charges near AED 12 to 18 per sq ft
- Model 6 to 8% vacancy on mid-income long-let demand

Gross yield: 7.8 to 9.5%
Net yield: 5.7 to 7.4%
Entry price (1-bed): AED 500,000 to 800,000
Service charges: AED 12 to 18/sqft

Dubai Sports City consistently produces the strongest net yield performance in the Dubai market. The combination of high gross yield, mid-range service charges, and strong tenant demand from mid-income professionals creates a compelling income investment.

What drives the yield: Sports City's tenant base is dominated by mid-income professionals, hospitality workers, and skilled tradespeople who value the community infrastructure at accessible rent levels. Average 1-bed rents of AED 60,000 to 80,000/year on units priced at AED 650,000 to 800,000 produce gross yields around 8.5 to 9%.

Service charge levels are manageable: AED 12 to 18/sqft on well-managed towers. On an 800 sqft apartment, that is AED 9,600 to 14,400/year, a drag of 13 to 18% on gross rental income.

Key risk: Short-let optionality is limited. Sports City's tenant profile is long-term, not tourist-facing. The investment thesis is pure yield, not yield plus short-let uplift.

### Jumeirah Village Circle (JVC)

Gross yield: 7.5 to 9.2%
Net yield: 5.4 to 7.1%
Entry price (1-bed): AED 550,000 to 1,000,000
Service charges: AED 13 to 20/sqft (wide building variance)

JVC is Dubai's highest-volume mid-market community and produces consistent gross yields among the city's best. The community houses hundreds of towers at varying quality levels, which creates a wider yield spread than almost any other Dubai location.

The best JVC buildings for net yield have service charges at AED 13 to 15/sqft, strong management, and established rental histories. The worst have service charges at AED 20+/sqft on relatively low rents, compressing net yield significantly. The community average masks this variance.

Critical approach: Never buy in JVC based on community-level yield data. Pull the specific building's Mollak service charge rate and compare actual Ejari transaction rents for that building. Two towers 200 metres apart can produce a 1.5% net yield difference.

Entry price range: From AED 450,000 for older studios to AED 1,100,000+ for newer, branded 2-bed units. The strongest net yield typically comes from 1-bed units in towers with AED 13 to 15/sqft service charges priced at AED 600,000 to 800,000.

### Discovery Gardens

Gross yield: 7.5 to 8.8%
Net yield: 5.6 to 6.9%
Entry price (1-bed): AED 480,000 to 680,000
Service charges: AED 11 to 16/sqft

Discovery Gardens is one of Dubai's most established affordable residential communities, a Nakheel development from the late 2000s that has benefited from 15 years of rental market normalisation. It is not new or glamorous, which is exactly why the yield numbers are strong: prices reflect age, and rents reflect genuine demand from a stable, cost-conscious tenant base.

Service charges in Discovery Gardens are among the lowest in the freehold market, AED 11 to 16/sqft, reflecting lower-specification common areas and older but functional infrastructure. The net yield benefit is tangible.

**Key consideration:** Older building stock means higher maintenance frequency. Reserve fund adequacy in older Nakheel communities varies. Check for any outstanding special assessments before purchase.

## Which tier-2 communities balance yield and supply risk?

Tier-2 communities such as IMPZ, Dubai South and Business Bay typically gross 6.5 to 9% and net about 4.5 to 6.8% after charges. Entry one-beds often start near AED 420,000 to 1.4 million. Our team underwrites extra vacancy for 12 to 18 months after clustered handovers.

### IMPZ and Dubai Production City

Gross yield: 7 to 8.5%
Net yield: 5.2 to 6.8%
Entry (1-bed): AED 420,000 to 650,000
Service charges: AED 10 to 16/sqft

IMPZ attracts studio and 1-bed investors seeking lower tickets with commute-linked tenancy. Gross yields stay elevated because capital values lag Marina and Business Bay, but new handovers can soften rents for 12 to 18 months after cluster completions.

### Dubai South and Expo corridor

Gross yield: 7 to 9%
Net yield: 5 to 6.5%
Entry (1-bed): AED 450,000 to 750,000
Service charges: AED 12 to 18/sqft

Aviation and logistics employment supports mid-market rents near Al Maktoum Airport. Underwrite extra vacancy when several towers hand over in the same quarter. See [Dubai South property investment](/areas/dubai-south-property-investment/) for location context.

### Business Bay

Gross yield: 6.5 to 8%
Net yield: 4.5 to 6.5%
Entry (1-bed): AED 900,000 to 1.4M
Service charges: AED 18 to 28/sqft (high variance)

Business Bay can match mid-market gross on paper, but service charge spread between towers is extreme. Always pull Mollak for the exact building before you compare to JVC.

## Which premium communities trade yield for liquidity?

Premium communities such as Marina and Downtown typically net 3.5 to 5.5% after AED 18 to 32 per sq ft service charges, trading yield for liquidity and vacancy near 4 to 5%. Our research keeps short-let uplift as a scenario, not the base case.

### Dubai Marina

Gross yield: 5.5 to 7%
Net yield: 4 to 5.5%
Entry (1-bed): AED 1.1M to 1.8M
Service charges: AED 18 to 26/sqft

**Short-let uplift in Marina:** Licensed holiday homes in Marina with good waterfront or Marina view access can generate 30 to 40% more revenue than equivalent long-term rental rates. A 1-bed that earns AED 100,000/year on long-term rental may generate AED 130,000 to 140,000/year on short-let with professional management. Deduct management fees of 15 to 20% of revenue, however, and the net uplift narrows to 10 to 15% over long-term.

The Marina investment thesis is: moderate net yield (4 to 5.5%) plus capital value stability plus residency optionality (AED 2M+ units qualify for Golden Visa) plus short-let flexibility if needed.

### Downtown Dubai

Gross yield: 5.0 to 6.5%
Net yield: 3.5 to 5.0%
Entry (1-bed): AED 1,800,000 to 3,500,000
Service charges: AED 22 to 32/sqft

Downtown delivers the lowest net yield among major Dubai investment communities, but consistently produces the highest occupancy rates and strongest resale liquidity. Vacancy in Downtown runs 4 to 5% versus 7 to 8% citywide. Rent reductions during market softening are modest because supply is constrained.

Net yield of 3.5 to 5% is not the thesis for Downtown. Capital value preservation, Golden Visa qualification, and asset quality suitable for high-net-worth tenants make the economics work for a specific buyer profile, not the yield-maximiser.

## When does short-let beat long-term rent?

Short-let is more profitable mainly in tourist-facing Marina, Downtown, JBR and Palm stock, where gross revenue can rise 30 to 50% before DET permits of AED 1,520 per year and 15 to 20% management fees. We treat short-let as a location strategy.

| Strategy | Best areas | Net note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Long-term | JVC, Sports City, Discovery Gardens | Stable Ejari tenants |
| Short-let | Marina, Downtown, JBR, Palm | DET permit + 15 to 20% mgmt |
| Hybrid | Marina 1-beds | Model both scenarios |

## How should you build a yield-focused portfolio?

A yield-focused portfolio often splits about 70% into mid-market income stock in Sports City, JVC and Discovery Gardens, and 30% into a Marina or Downtown liquidity anchor when Golden Visa or exit speed matters. Invest Gulf methodology keeps citywide vacancy near 7 to 8% in the base model.

## What does a net yield calculation look like on a real unit?

A worked net yield example on AED 70,000 rent and a AED 720,000 purchase prints about 9.7% gross but nearer 6.1% net after 7% vacancy, AED 11,520 service charges, 6% management, Ejari and maintenance. Invest Gulf shows both numbers so brochure gross is never mistaken for cash yield by foreign buyers.

Net yield maths on a mid-market Dubai apartment shows why brochure gross figures mislead. Take annual rent of AED 70,000 on a AED 720,000 purchase: gross yield prints near 9.7%, yet a 7% vacancy allowance removes AED 4,900, service charges at AED 16 per sq ft on 720 sq ft remove AED 11,520, management at 6% of rent removes AED 4,200, Ejari near AED 520 and a AED 2,000 maintenance allowance leave about AED 46,860 net income, or roughly 6.1% on price and closer to 5.6% once 6 to 9% acquisition costs including the 4% DLD transfer fee sit in the denominator. Short-let in Marina or Downtown can lift gross revenue 30 to 40% before 15 to 20% platform management fees, while Sports City and JVC remain long-term Ejari plays.

| Income/cost item | Annual AED |
| --- | --- |
| Gross annual rent (Ejari-based) | 70,000 |
| Less: vacancy 7% | (4,900) |
| Less: service charges (16/sqft × 720) | (11,520) |
| Less: property management 6% of rent | (4,200) |
| Less: Ejari registration | (520) |
| Less: minor maintenance allowance | (2,000) |
| **Net annual income** | **46,860** |
| **Net yield on capital deployed** | **6.08%** |
