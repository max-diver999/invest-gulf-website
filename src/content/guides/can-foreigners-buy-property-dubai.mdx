  - question: "Do foreigners need a UAE visa to buy property in Dubai?"
    answer: "No. You can purchase on a tourist visa or entirely remotely using a notarised Power of Attorney. Residency is optional and separate, though qualifying purchases may support Golden Visa applications afterward."
  - question: "Which areas in Dubai are freehold for foreigners?"
    answer: "Over 60 designated zones include Dubai Marina, Downtown Dubai, Palm Jumeirah, Business Bay, JVC, Dubai Hills Estate, Arabian Ranches, Creek Harbour, and Dubai South. Outside designated zones, foreigners may only hold leasehold (usufruct) rights, not full freehold title."
  - question: "How is buying in Dubai different from buying in Abu Dhabi?"
    answer: "Dubai uses DLD and RERA with 60+ freehold zones and deep transaction volume. Abu Dhabi uses DMT with fewer designated investment zones under Law 13/2019.   - question: "What is RERA escrow and why does it matter?"
    answer: "RERA requires off-plan buyer payments into DLD-regulated escrow accounts, released to developers only against certified construction milestones. Never pay off-plan deposits to a developer's general corporate account. Verify escrow via Trakheesi and the Dubai REST app."
  - question: "Can foreigners get a mortgage in Dubai?"
    answer: "Yes. UAE banks lend to non-residents, typically up to 50% LTV on properties under AED 5 million. Residents may access higher LTV on first purchases. Down payment, salary documentation, and bank-specific criteria apply."
  - question: "Does buying Dubai property give UAE citizenship?"
    answer: "No. Property ownership may support residency applications such as the Golden Visa at AED 2 million registered value, but citizenship remains a separate discretionary process. See UAE Residency vs Citizenship for the full distinction."
  - question: "Can I buy Dubai property through a company?"
    answer: "Yes, via UAE mainland or free-zone entities, but corporate ownership introduces UAE corporate tax considerations and home-country substance rules. Most individual investors buy in personal name for simplicity. Get cross-border tax advice before using an SPV."
