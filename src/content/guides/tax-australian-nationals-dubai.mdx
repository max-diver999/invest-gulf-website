faq:
  - question: "Do Australian tax residents pay tax on Dubai rental income?"
    answer: "Yes. Australian tax residents are taxed by the ATO on worldwide income, including rental income from Dubai property. There is no Australia-UAE double tax treaty, so no treaty relief applies. Rental income from Dubai is declared on your Australian tax return and taxed at your marginal rate (up to 45% plus 2% Medicare Levy). You may deduct property management fees, mortgage interest, depreciation, and other qualifying expenses against this income."
  - question: "Is there a capital gains tax on Dubai property for Australians?"
    answer: "For Australian tax residents, UAE property is a CGT asset. If you sell Dubai property at a profit, the capital gain forms part of your Australian assessable income in the year of sale. The 50% CGT discount applies if you held the property for 12 months or more, effectively halving the taxable gain. There is no CGT on the UAE side, the UAE imposes zero capital gains tax."
  - question: "Can Australians negatively gear Dubai property?"
    answer: "Yes, if you are an Australian tax resident and your Dubai property generates a loss (mortgage interest and deductible expenses exceed rental income), the net loss can offset other Australian income, including salary, under negative gearing rules. This is one of the few ways Australian investors can extract an Australian tax benefit from Dubai property ownership. Verify current ATO guidance on foreign property negative gearing with a registered tax agent."
  - question: "Is there an Australia-UAE double tax treaty?"
    answer: "No. Australia and the UAE have no double tax agreement (DTA). This means Australian tax residents receive no treaty-based relief from Australian taxation on UAE-source income. Every dollar of Dubai rental income is taxable in Australia at marginal rates with no offset mechanism from UAE (which taxes zero anyway). This absence of a treaty also means no formal UAE tax residency certificate pathway is available to claim ATO exemption, ATO determines Australian residency based on domestic tests."
  - question: "How do I break Australian tax residency if I move to Dubai?"
    answer: "Breaking Australian tax residency is notoriously difficult and requires genuinely and permanently abandoning Australian domicile. The ATO's statutory residence test (ITAA 1997) and common law domicile test both apply. Leaving Australia with family remaining, keeping a family home, maintaining close employment ties, or holding a return ticket all support continuing Australian residency. Courts have consistently found Australians to remain tax residents despite years abroad. A Tax File Number (TFN) holder living overseas should not assume they have broken residency without a detailed analysis."

No treaty relief on UAE-source income: Australian tax residents cannot use a DTA to exempt or credit Dubai rental income against ATO obligations. Every dollar of net rental income from Dubai is assessable in Australia at your full marginal rate.

No treaty-based residency tiebreaker: DTAs typically contain tiebreaker clauses to resolve dual-residency situations. Without one, if both Australia and the UAE consider you tax resident simultaneously, there is no international treaty framework to resolve the conflict, Australian domestic rules apply regardless of UAE residency status.

The practical implication is stark: an Australian national living in Dubai on a UAE Golden Visa, earning AED 120,000 per year in Dubai rental income, and still qualifying as an Australian tax resident must declare and pay Australian income tax on that rental income at marginal rates, without any UAE tax credit to offset the liability (since UAE taxes nothing).

Compare this to UK nationals, who have a UK-UAE DTA providing some treaty structure, or French nationals with the France-UAE DTT from 1989. Australians are among the most exposed to home-country taxation on Dubai property income due to the treaty gap.
