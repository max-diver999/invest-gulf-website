## No Australia-UAE Double Tax Treaty: The Key Starting Point

Australia has negotiated double tax agreements (DTAs) with 45+ countries. The UAE is not among them. This has two practical consequences for Australian nationals owning Dubai property:

**No treaty relief on UAE-source income**: Australian tax residents cannot use a DTA to exempt or credit Dubai rental income against ATO obligations. Every dollar of net rental income from Dubai is assessable in Australia at your full marginal rate.

**No treaty-based residency tiebreaker**: DTAs typically contain tiebreaker clauses to resolve dual-residency situations. Without one, if both Australia and the UAE consider you tax resident simultaneously, there is no international treaty framework to resolve the conflict — Australian domestic rules apply regardless of UAE residency status.

The practical implication is stark: an Australian national living in Dubai on a UAE Golden Visa, earning AED 120,000 per year in Dubai rental income, and still qualifying as an Australian tax resident must declare and pay Australian income tax on that rental income at marginal rates — without any UAE tax credit to offset the liability (since UAE taxes nothing).

Compare this to UK nationals, who have a UK-UAE DTA providing some treaty structure, or French nationals with the France-UAE DTT from 1989. Australians are among the most exposed to home-country taxation on Dubai property income due to the treaty gap.

## Australian Tax Residency: Harder to Break Than You Think

The ATO applies four tests to determine Australian tax residency, and any one of them can establish residency:

**1. Resides test (common law)**: The ordinary meaning of "residing" — where you actually live, assessed by physical presence, intention, family, social and economic ties, and lifestyle continuity. This is a thorough facts-and-circumstances test.

**2. Domicile test**: You are a resident if your domicile (in the legal sense) is Australia, unless your permanent place of abode is outside Australia. "Domicile" is a legal concept, not merely where you live — changing domicile requires specific legal intent and action.

**3. 183-day test**: If you are physically present in Australia for 183+ days in a year, you are deemed a resident (unless your usual domicile is abroad and you intend to take up permanent residence elsewhere). Note this test establishes residency — it does not require 183 days to avoid residency.

**4. Commonwealth Superannuation Fund test**: A narrow test for Commonwealth government employees.

The ATO's track record in asserting continuing Australian residency for apparent expatriates is extensive. Common mistakes:
- Keeping a family home available for personal use in Australia
- Spouse or dependants remaining in Australia
- Maintaining Australian employer relationships
- Having a lease or property in Australia
- Failing to establish a permanent home abroad with long-term commitment

Australian nationals should obtain a formal **ATO private ruling** or advice from a registered Australian tax agent before assuming they have broken Australian tax residency. The private ruling process provides some certainty before committing to major transactions.

## Negative Gearing Dubai Property from Australia

Negative gearing is a uniquely Australian tax concept that allows rental property losses to offset other Australian income. It applies to foreign property held by Australian tax residents.

**How it works for Dubai property**:
If your Dubai mortgage interest payments, property management fees, maintenance costs, depreciation, and other deductible expenses **exceed** your Dubai rental income, the net loss can be deducted from your other Australian income (salary, wages, business income).

**Example**: Australian resident earns AUD 200,000 salary. Dubai apartment rental income: AUD 35,000. Total deductible expenses (interest on Dubai mortgage: AUD 50,000 + management fees: AUD 5,000 + depreciation allowance): AUD 55,000. Net rental loss: AUD 20,000. This loss reduces Australian taxable income to AUD 180,000, saving approximately AUD 8,600 in Australian income tax (at 43% effective rate on the margin).

**Important constraints**:
- Depreciation on UAE-constructed buildings (Division 43 capital works deduction) and fixtures (Division 40 plant and equipment) must be claimed at ATO-specified rates. Some overseas construction costs qualify; others do not. An Australian quantity surveyor's report for the Dubai property helps establish the deductible base.
- If the property eventually sells at a profit, all depreciation deductions previously claimed reduce the cost base, increasing the taxable capital gain.
- Negative gearing provides immediate tax cash flow benefit but requires ongoing management of ATO compliance for a foreign property.

Negative gearing Dubai property is used by high-income Australian professionals in Dubai who are still Australian tax residents, turning a UAE investment into an Australian tax efficiency tool. It is one of the few structural advantages Australians have versus other nationalities investing in Dubai.

## Capital Gains Tax on Dubai Property for Australians

When an Australian tax resident sells Dubai property at a profit, the gain is a **capital gains event (CGT event A1)** under Australian tax law.

**50% CGT discount**: If the property was held for 12 months or more, only 50% of the capital gain is included in assessable income. This effectively halves the CGT rate from your marginal rate. At a 45% marginal rate, the effective CGT rate on a qualifying long-held Dubai property is approximately **22.5%** on the actual gain.

**Cost base**: The cost base includes the original purchase price, DLD transfer fees (4%), conveyancing, mortgage costs (where applicable), capital improvement costs (renovation), and any selling costs. Accurate records of all AED-denominated costs, converted to AUD at the rate prevailing at each transaction date, are essential.

**Currency movements**: AUD/AED exchange rate movements affect the Australian dollar gain calculation even if the AED-denominated value of the property is unchanged. The AED is USD-pegged; AUD/USD has historically been volatile. An Australian who bought Dubai property when AUD was strong and sells when AUD is weaker will record a larger Australian dollar capital gain even if the AED value was flat.

**Capital loss on Dubai property**: If sold at a loss, the capital loss can be offset against other Australian capital gains in the same year or carried forward to future years. It cannot offset ordinary income (unlike negative gearing losses from rental).

**Partial main residence exemption**: For Australian residents who lived in the Dubai property as a main residence for part of the ownership period, a pro-rata main residence CGT exemption may apply — unusual for investment property but theoretically available if the property served as the owner's principal home during a period of UAE residence while remaining Australian tax resident.

## Superannuation Considerations for Australian Expats in Dubai

Australian superannuation (super) continues to accumulate for many Australian expats in Dubai, particularly those who retain Australian employer relationships or contribute voluntarily. Key points:

**Super is not accessible until preservation age**: typically 60, or in cases of permanent incapacity or severe financial hardship. Dubai relocation does not trigger access.

**Super contributions while in UAE**: Employees of Australian entities seconded to Dubai often remain in the Australian super system under their employer's default arrangements. Super contributions from an overseas employer are not compulsory unless the employer is an Australian-based entity subject to the Superannuation Guarantee.

**Taxation**: Super earnings are taxed at 15% in Australia within the fund. Upon withdrawal from age 60, super is generally tax-free for Australian residents. For non-residents, a withholding tax on super lump sums may apply at different rates.

**SMSF while in Dubai**: Running a Self-Managed Super Fund (SMSF) from Dubai as a non-resident creates complex ATO residency and trustee rules for the fund itself. An SMSF is typically required to maintain Australian residency at the fund level. Expats should obtain specific SMSF legal advice.

## Banking for Australian Nationals in Dubai

No specific Australia-UAE financial reporting treaty creates automatic information exchange issues, but the UAE participates in the OECD Common Reporting Standard (CRS). UAE bank accounts held by Australian tax residents will be reported to Australian tax authorities via automatic exchange.

Australian residents with foreign financial accounts must report account details on their Australian tax return if the total foreign assets exceed AUD 50,000 (via the Foreign Financial Assets disclosure). Rental income from Dubai and proceeds from property sales must be declared regardless of account location.

## Breaking Australian Tax Residency: What Actually Works

For Australian nationals who want to fully exit Australian tax obligations on Dubai income, genuine residency cessation requires:

**Permanent departure intent**: You must form an intention to permanently reside outside Australia. A fixed-term work contract in Dubai (say, three years) typically does not establish permanent departure for ATO purposes.

**Dispose of or rent out (not use) your Australian home**: If you keep a property available for personal use in Australia, it strongly suggests continuing Australian domicile. Leasing the property to arm's-length tenants on a market-rate, ongoing basis is preferable.

**Family relocation**: Moving your entire family (spouse, children) to Dubai is the strongest evidence of life-centre transfer. A spouse remaining in Sydney while you work in Dubai almost universally results in continued Australian residency.

**Australian income source severance**: Resigning from Australian employers, converting investments to non-Australian assets, and reducing Australian income streams weakens ATO's case for continued residency.

**File a final Australian resident return**: For the year of departure, file a return as a resident for the part of the year you were in Australia, and as a non-resident for the remainder. Engage an Australian-qualified tax agent to manage this transition return correctly.

The ATO's **Tax Residency Review** process may request evidence years after departure if you later become a significant Australian income taxpayer again (e.g. through Australian capital gains). Maintain contemporaneous records of UAE life for at least seven years.

## Scenario Comparison: Australian National Tax Outcomes

| Scenario | ATO Tax on Dubai Rental | CGT on Dubai Property Sale | Key Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Australian tax resident, individual ownership | Marginal rate (19–45%) + 2% Medicare | 50% discount then marginal rate | High ongoing compliance cost |
| Australian tax resident, negatively geared | Net loss offsets Australian income | 50% discount reduced by prior depreciation | Good cash flow but compliance |
| Former Australian resident (genuinely departed) | No ATO tax on UAE-source income | No ATO CGT (if non-resident at sale date) | ATO may assert residency wasn't broken |
| Non-resident Australian (less than 183 days in AU) | 10.5% withholding on AU rent only | No 50% CGT discount as non-resident; rate 32.5% flat | Loss of CGT discount as non-resident |

Note: Non-residents for Australian tax purposes lose the CGT 50% discount — gains are taxed at a flat 32.5% rate for non-residents without the discount. This creates a dilemma: retaining Australian residency preserves the CGT discount but imposes income tax; breaking residency removes income tax but potentially increases effective CGT on sale.

## Practical Recommendations for Australian Nationals

- **Engage an Australian-registered tax agent** familiar with overseas property before your first Dubai rental income year. The ATO penalties for undeclared foreign income are significant.
- **Keep AED transaction records in AUD equivalent** from day one. Property purchase costs, rent received, and expenses all need historical AUD conversion rates for accurate tax returns. The ATO typically accepts RBA exchange rate data.
- **Consider a quantity surveyor's depreciation report** for the Dubai property to maximise allowable Australian deductions on the cost of fixtures, fittings, and building works.
- **Do not confuse UAE Golden Visa with Australian residency cessation.** Your UAE Golden Visa does not break Australian tax residency. Only genuine permanent departure with full tie severance does.
- **Review every two to three years.** Your Australian tax residency status and property portfolio efficiency should be reviewed as circumstances change — family situation, Dubai presence, Australian income levels, and property values all affect the optimal structure.
