## Is there an Australia-UAE double tax treaty for Dubai property income?

Australia and the UAE typically have no comprehensive double tax agreement, so Australian tax residents cannot exempt Dubai rent through a DTA, and our research treats every AED of net rent as ATO-assessable at marginal rates up to 45% plus 2% Medicare Levy.

DTAs usually include tiebreaker clauses for dual residency. Without one, Australian domestic rules still govern your Australian return regardless of a UAE residency certificate or Golden Visa status.

Australian tax residents typically declare Dubai rent at marginal rates up to 45% plus a 2% Medicare Levy because Australia has no UAE double tax treaty and FITO has nothing to offset when UAE income tax is zero. An Australian on a UAE Golden Visa collecting AED 120,000 per year in Dubai rent while remaining an ATO resident must still report that rent in Australia each year. The 50% CGT discount can apply after 12 months if residency continues at sale, but negative gearing only helps while the ATO still classifies you as resident. Breaking residency usually needs family relocation, long-term UAE housing, and severed Australian income ties, not a visa stamp alone. Model after-tax yield with Australian brackets before treating Dubai rent as tax-free cash flow for lifestyle planning.

## How does the ATO decide if you remain an Australian tax resident in Dubai?

The ATO typically applies ITAA 1997 statutory tests plus domicile principles, and our underwriting treats a kept Australian family home, dependants left behind, or 183 days spent in Australia as a red flag that preserves residency.

1. Resides test: where you live day to day and where your permanent home is located
2. Domicile test: whether Australia remains your permanent home even while abroad
3. 183-day test: 183+ days in Australia in an income year can deem residency
4. Commonwealth superannuation test: narrow category for certain government employees

ATO files often review 7 years of ties. Common mistakes that keep Australians resident while working in Dubai:

- Keeping a family home available in Australia
- Spouse or dependants remaining in Australia
- Maintaining Australian employer or director ties
- Short-term Dubai leases without permanent relocation evidence

## What Australian income tax rates apply to Dubai rental income?

Dubai rent is typically ordinary assessable income for Australian residents and stacks on salary at marginal rates up to 45% plus 2% Medicare Levy, and we model FITO as zero benefit when UAE income tax paid is AED 0.

| Taxable income (AUD) | Tax on this income |
| --- | --- |
| $0 - $18,200 | Nil |
| $18,201 - $45,000 | 19c per $1 over $18,200 |
| $45,001 - $120,000 | $5,092 + 32.5c per $1 over $45,000 |
| $120,001 - $180,000 | $29,467 + 37c per $1 over $120,000 |
| $180,001+ | $51,667 + 45c per $1 over $180,000 |

## Can Australians negatively gear Dubai property against local salary?

Yes, Australian tax residents can typically negatively gear Dubai property when deductible expenses exceed rent and the net loss offsets other Australian income across a full year, and Invest Gulf still requires Division 40 and Division 43 compliance.

Constraints:

- Division 43 and Division 40 depreciation on foreign buildings must follow ATO rates
- A quantity surveyor report on the Dubai asset helps establish the base
- Depreciation claimed earlier reduces the CGT cost base on sale
- Compliance requires Australian-year reporting even when rent is collected in AED across 12 months

## How does Australian CGT apply when you sell Dubai property?

UAE property is typically a CGT asset for Australian residents, with a 50% discount after 12 months for individuals, and Invest Gulf notes non-residents lose that discount and may face a 32.5% flat rate on taxable gains.

Keep FX records and convert AED proceeds and costs using ATO-approved exchange rates for the relevant dates. There is zero UAE capital gains tax on the disposal.

| Status at sale | CGT treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Australian resident, 12+ months held | 50% CGT discount available |
| Australian resident, under 12 months | No discount |
| Australian non-resident | No discount; flat rate risk (verify) |

## What superannuation rules affect Australian expats living in Dubai?

Superannuation typically stays locked until preservation age around 60 years, and Invest Gulf warns that a Dubai move does not unlock early release while fund earnings remain taxed at 15% inside the fund for foreign buyers holding Australian super balances.

- Employees seconded from Australian entities may stay in default super funds
- Pure UAE employers with no Australian nexus generally do not trigger Superannuation Guarantee
- Withdrawals from age 60 are often tax-free for Australian residents
- SMSFs from Dubai create trustee residency risk without specialist advice

## How hard is it to break Australian tax residency when you move to Dubai?

Breaking Australian tax residency typically requires a genuine centre-of-life shift abroad, and our research treats family left in Australia or a retained home as a red flag that can keep residency alive across 7 years of evidence review.

Stronger evidence includes:

- Spouse and children relocating with you
- Selling or leasing the Australian home on arm's length terms
- Resigning from Australian employment rather than remote work
- Filing a part-year resident return for the departure year

## Should you stay an Australian tax resident while owning Dubai property?

Staying resident typically preserves the 50% CGT discount after 12 months but taxes Dubai rent at full marginal rates up to 45% plus 2% Medicare, and we compare that with non-resident status that stops rent tax but removes the discount.

| Strategy | Dubai rent | CGT on Dubai sale | Complexity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Remain Australian resident | Taxed at marginal rate | 50% discount if 12+ months held | Lower residency dispute risk |
| Become non-resident | Not taxed in Australia | No CGT discount | Harder to achieve, ATO scrutiny |

Australians typically need a residency opinion, after-tax yield model at marginal rates up to 45% plus 2% Medicare Levy, and CGT exit planning before SPA signing, and our underwriting treats UAE zero tax alone as a red flag assumption.
