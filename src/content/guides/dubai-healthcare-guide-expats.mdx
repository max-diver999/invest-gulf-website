## Is health insurance mandatory for Dubai residents?

Yes. DHA enforces insurance for all Dubai residents, and employers sponsor most employees while dependants must also be covered for foreign buyers on family visas. Self-sponsored residents buy individual DHA-compliant policies before visa stamping. Pre-signature checklist timing ties policy start dates to Emirates ID and employment processes within the first 30 days.

Operating without cover risks fines and blocks visa renewal. Tie policy start date to [Emirates ID after property purchase](/guides/emirates-id-after-property-purchase/) or employment visa stamping.

## Who regulates healthcare in Dubai?

Healthcare regulation in Dubai is led by DHA for licensing and mandatory insurance, with MoHAP at federal level and DHCA for Dubai Healthcare City free zone entities used by foreign buyers. JCI accreditation is a practical filter many expats apply when shortlisting hospitals within the first 30 days. Checklist diligence verifies provider licensing on DHA portals before first visits.

| Body | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Dubai Health Authority (DHA) | Licenses facilities; mandatory insurance framework and EBP |
| Ministry of Health and Prevention (MoHAP) | Federal regulator; northern emirates and some dual-licensed facilities |
| Dubai Healthcare City Authority (DHCA) | Regulates DHCC free zone entities |
| Joint Commission International (JCI) | International accreditation filter used by many expats |

Practical rule: confirm licensing on at least 2 portals (DHA plus free-zone where relevant) before paying cash for a specialist visit above AED 500.

## What does the Essential Benefits Plan cover?

EBP is the minimum package for workers earning under AED 4,000 per month and covers emergency care, GP visits, limited specialist access, basic diagnostics, and maternity within network restrictions. Premium is roughly AED 500 to 650 per year, usually employer-paid for eligible staff. Invest Gulf notes for foreign buyers treat EBP as a floor, not a family plan.

EBP excludes most dental, optical, and elective care. Higher earners and families need enhanced plans with broader networks.

| Included | Often excluded |
| --- | --- |
| Emergency and primary care | Dental and optical |
| Basic diagnostics | Elective procedures |
| Maternity within network rules | Broad out-of-network choice |

Yes. DHA-operated public hospitals accept all residents, and non-citizens pay higher tariffs than UAE nationals while still usually beating private cash prices. Private GP visits without insurance often cost AED 200 to 500, with specialists AED 400 to 900. We shortlist mix one public emergency option such as Rashid with a private network hospital near home.

## What is Dubai Healthcare City and who uses it?

Dubai Healthcare City clusters specialists, JCI hospitals, and labs near central Dubai for day-to-day specialist care, not medical tourism alone. Foreign buyers often choose DHCC for oncology, fertility, ophthalmology, orthopaedics, and dentistry within 15 to 30 minutes of DIFC or Bur Dubai. Invest Gulf area matching checks DHCC drive time against a 12 months school calendar.

## How do insurance networks, co-pays, and cash prices work?

Insurance networks decide whether foreign buyers get direct billing or pay cash first, and pre-authorisation is required for most specialists, admissions, and elective procedures. In-network GP co-pays of AED 20 to 50 are common, while uninsured MRI scans can run AED 1,200 to 3,000. Checklist reading always starts from the policy schedule, not the brochure.

| Out-of-network | Pay upfront; partial reimbursement |
| Pre-authorisation | Required for most specialists and admissions |

Maternity pathways need sub-limit review before conception because waiting periods of 6 to 12 months appear on many plans. Invest Gulf school files also require vaccination records before term.
