## What happens after DLD registers your Dubai property?

DLD registration typically produces a title deed within 2 to 4 weeks after final payment on ready stock, and that deed anchors Golden Visa, Ejari, and resale for Russian buyers. Off-plan buyers hold an Oqood certificate until handover issues the final deed for the same AED asset. Our checklist:

1. **Investment:** Owning a Dubai freehold asset that appreciates and/or generates yield with no annual property tax
2. **Relocation:** Using the property (or the Golden Visa it qualifies for) as the platform for a genuine Dubai residency

Most buyers start with objective one and evolve to objective two. This guide is for those who have purchased, or are close to purchasing, and want a clear roadmap for making Dubai a functioning home, not just an investment holding.

The dedicated [Dubai property for Russian buyers guide](/guides/dubai-property-for-russian-buyers/) covers the purchasing process itself. This guide starts from registration and moves forward.

## Step 1: How do you secure and store your DLD title deed?

Title deeds typically download from the DLD app or arrive as certified trustee copies, and lenders may hold the deed until a mortgage clears. Cloud PDF backup plus paper copies are required for Golden Visa agents and banks within the same 60 to 90 days visa window after filing starts. Pre-signature checklist:

Keep these originals securely. You will need the title deed for the Golden Visa application, mortgage releases, and any future sale or transfer.

## Step 2: Which UAE visa route fits your property purchase?

Property above AED 2,000,000 registered equity typically qualifies for a 10-year Golden Visa, while company or employment routes run 2 to 3 years and still need separate filing. Processing from deed to stamp commonly takes 60 to 90 days once medical and biometrics clear. Our checklist:

| Property Value | Applicable Visa Route | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| AED 2,000,000+ (registered net equity) | UAE Golden Visa | 10 years, renewable |
| AED 750,000 to 2,000,000 (historically) | Investor residence visa | Note: verify current minimum with DLD/GDRFA |
| Company formation (any property value) | Freelance/investor visa via company | 2-3 years |
| Employment by UAE entity | Employment visa | 2-3 years |

The Golden Visa at AED 2M is the most stable and recommended route for buyers meeting the threshold. It is independent of employment, self-renewable, and covers family sponsorship for spouse and children under 25 in full-time education. See [Golden Visa AED 2M explained](/guides/uae-golden-visa-property/) for current eligibility details.

### Golden Visa Application Process

Application body: GDRFA (General Directorate of Residency and Foreigners Affairs, Dubai) or ICP (Federal Identity Authority) for federal processing.

Typical timeline from title deed to stamped visa: 60-90 days

Required documents (current standard, verify before applying):
- Valid passport with sufficient remaining validity
- Passport-size photograph
- DLD title deed or GDRFA-approved property valuation certificate
- Proof of property value meeting AED 2M threshold (valuation report if below asking)
- Health insurance proof
- Medical fitness test result (taken at approved UAE clinic)

**Fees:** Government fees are approximately AED 3,000-5,000 for the Golden Visa processing, plus AED 1,000-1,500 for medical test and Emirates ID. Agent-assisted service costs AED 3,000-8,000 additionally. See the [Golden Visa application step-by-step](/guides/golden-visa-application-step-by-step/) for the complete fee schedule.

## Step 3: How do you complete medical tests and Emirates ID?

Medical fitness tests typically return within 2 days at approved clinics, and Emirates ID biometrics commonly take 5 to 10 days after the ICA appointment. Families should book children together so school enrolment is not blocked waiting for cards ahead of a September intake, and AED 1,000 to 1,500 usually covers medical plus Emirates ID fees. Our checklist:

- Book an approved clinic medical (blood test and chest X-ray for adults)
- Complete ICA biometrics with passport and entry permit ready
- Collect Emirates ID before KHDA school enrolment deadlines

You need the stamped entry permit, passport, photos, and tenancy contract or property ownership proof for many appointment slots. Families should schedule children together to align school enrolment deadlines. See the [Emirates ID application guide](/guides/emirates-id-application-guide/) for document checklists.

## Step 4: How do Russian buyers open UAE bank accounts?

Russian buyers typically open with Emirates NBD, Mashreq, or ADIB when source-of-funds files are complete, and some banks allow non-resident accounts on a title deed before the visa stamp. Resident accounts usually need the stamped visa plus Emirates ID within about 2 to 4 weeks of biometrics for AED service-charge payments. Pre-signature checklist:

- Keep passport, title deed, and source-of-funds PDFs ready before the branch visit
- Confirm whether the bank allows non-resident opening on deed alone
- Activate the account before DEWA and service-charge Direct Debits start

### Which Banks Work Best for Russian Buyers

Emirates NBD, Mashreq, and ADIB have been most consistently accessible for Russian nationals with clean source-of-funds documentation. Neobanks like Wio Bank (Abu Dhabi-based digital bank) have also been used by newer arrivals. Practical tip: Open the bank account as early as possible, some banks allow non-resident account opening post-property purchase using the title deed. Others require a stamped visa. Do not leave banking to the last minute, as you need it for property service charge payments that can run AED 15,000 to 25,000 yearly on a 1,000 sq ft Marina or Downtown apartment, plus DEWA and day-to-day living costs.

## Step 5: How do you connect DEWA, Ejari, and home utilities?

DEWA activation typically needs 3 to 5 days plus a deposit often near AED 2,000 to 4,000, and Ejari registration is mandatory before most tenancy disputes under RERA. Owners who occupy may need active DEWA for address checks during the 60 to 90 day visa window. Checklist:

Allow 3 to 5 working days for DEWA activation and deposit refunds on handover. Internet and cooling providers (Empower or district cooling where applicable) are separate contracts. Keep paid DEWA receipts for service charge reconciliations with the owners association.

## Should you live in your Dubai property or rent it out?

Living in the unit typically suits buyers whose lifestyle matches the investment zone, while renting at 6-8% gross and leasing elsewhere is common when a AED 2,000,000 JVC holding funds a Marina lifestyle. Property managers usually charge 5-8% of annual rent for Ejari and maintenance cover. Our checklist:

**Living in it:** The property becomes your primary residence. You pay service charges and DEWA directly. No tenancy income but no management overhead. Works best when the property is your chosen area and suitable for your lifestyle.

Renting it out and renting elsewhere: Many Russian buyers find their investment property is in a different zone from their preferred lifestyle zone. A JVC apartment that qualified for Golden Visa (AED 2M with combined units) may not be where a family with Marina social preferences wants to live. In this scenario, renting out the investment property (at 6-8% gross yield) and renting a lifestyle-appropriate unit elsewhere can be financially neutral or positive.

Property management: If you travel frequently or are in Dubai part-time, a property management company handles tenancy, maintenance, and Ejari compliance for approximately 5-8% of annual rent. Service quality varies significantly, get referrals from the Russian-speaking community.

## What tax implications apply when Russian buyers relocate to Dubai?

UAE personal income tax and capital gains tax on Dubai property are typically 0%, while 5% VAT applies to selected goods and services, not property transfers. Russian tax residents spending 183+ days in Russia face 13-15% income tax and different capital gains treatment, so residency break planning matters. Pre-signature checklist:

**UAE:** Zero personal income tax, zero capital gains tax on property, zero inheritance tax on Dubai assets. Annual property tax does not exist. There is a 5% VAT on selected goods and services but not on property transactions.

**Russia:** Russian tax residents (183+ days in Russia annually) are taxed on worldwide income at 13-15% personal income tax and capital gains at 30% (for non-residents). Relocating to Dubai and breaking Russian tax residency requires careful planning, the [tax guide for Russian nationals in Dubai](/guides/tax-russian-nationals-dubai/) covers the specific Russian-UAE tax treaty context and residency break considerations.

The critical issue: if you own property in Russia and in Dubai, and you change tax residency, the Russian property may be subject to different tax treatment. Get qualified advice before the move.

## How does family sponsorship and school timing work after purchase?

Family sponsorship typically covers a spouse and children under GDRFA age rules, and KHDA school enrolment requires Emirates ID first. A September intake usually needs EID by August, so Golden Visa filing for an AED 2,000,000 qualifying deed often needs to start by April or May, with school deposits of AED 500 to 5,000 per child reserved. Our checklist:

- Confirm spouse and child sponsorship ages with GDRFA at filing time
- Align Emirates ID collection with KHDA enrolment deadlines
- Reserve school deposits of AED 500 to 5,000 per child in the cash buffer

**Spouse:** Standard family sponsorship with the Golden Visa holder as sponsor. Healthcare and Emirates ID required.

**Children:** Sponsored until age 21 for daughters (unmarried), age 18 for sons (unless enrolled in full-time education, then up to 25). Verify current GDRFA rules at time of application.

**School:** Children require Emirates ID for school enrolment in KHDA-regulated schools. This means the visa timeline determines the earliest possible school start. A September intake requires EID by approximately August, backwards from there, visa application should start in April-May at the latest. See the [Golden Visa family sponsorship guide](/guides/golden-visa-family-sponsorship/) for sponsorship rules.

The relocation experience for children varies significantly by age. Younger children adapt quickly to new school environments. Secondary-age teenagers, particularly those in crucial exam years (GCSE/A-Level equivalent in Russian system), may need more careful transition planning around curriculum continuity.

Professional networking for Russian entrepreneurs is genuinely international in Dubai. DIFC events, industry conferences, and chamber of commerce events mix Russian, European, South Asian, and Gulf professionals naturally.

Cultural touchstones include Russian restaurants and cafés in Marina and JLT, Russian supermarket sections in larger hypermarkets, and Russian New Year events in January. These connection points make the transition from Russia to Dubai feel less abrupt.

## What relocation mistakes do Russian property buyers make?

Setup costs beyond purchase typically add AED 50,000 to 120,000 for visa, DEWA, school deposits, furnishing, and overlap rent. Marina and Downtown service charges of AED 15 to 25 per sq ft can mean AED 15,000 to 25,000 yearly on 1,000 sq ft. Checklist:

Underestimating setup costs. Transaction costs (DLD, agent) are well-understood. Setup costs, Golden Visa fees, Emirates ID, school enrolment deposits, DEWA deposit, initial rental overlap, and furnishing, easily total AED 50,000-120,000 additional to the purchase price. Budget this explicitly.

Poor area choice relative to lifestyle needs. Buying in a lower-priced outer zone for yield reasons, then renting in Marina, is a valid strategy but should be planned. Buyers who assume they will live in JVC or DAMAC Hills and then find daily life inconvenient create unnecessary friction.

Not using Russian-speaking advisors. The Dubai service ecosystem for Russian speakers is sophisticated. Using generic English-only advisors is not necessary and can create miscommunication in legal and banking processes.

Ignoring maintenance obligations. Service charges are annual and compulsory. High-end buildings in Marina and Downtown charge AED 15-25 per sq ft annually. A 1,000 sq ft apartment carries AED 15,000 to 25,000 in annual service charges. Budget these before year one ends.

## What risks should Russian buyers plan for before relocating?

Visa minimums can change, so GDRFA rules must be verified at filing time rather than from forum posts years old. Banking KYC for Russian nationals typically needs audited source-of-funds and property payment receipts before large AED transfers of AED 100,000 or more clear without delay, and rental voids of 1 to 2 months can erase a quarter of planned net yield. Our checklist:

- Verify current Golden Visa and investor minimums with GDRFA before filing
- Keep audited source-of-funds and property payment receipts for bank KYC
- Confirm UAE transfer corridors before moving AED 100,000 or more
- Stress-test living costs against 1 to 2 months of rental void

Visa policy changes can affect investor minimums, so verify GDRFA rules at application time rather than relying on forum posts. Banking KYC for Russian nationals remains strict. Currency transfer routes can shift with sanctions compliance, so confirm corridors with your UAE bank before large transfers. Service charge hikes can also compress net yield if you rely on rental income to fund Dubai living costs.

## What relocation budget should Russian buyers plan beyond the purchase price?

Relocation budgets beyond purchase typically reserve AED 50,000 to 120,000 for visa, DEWA, school, furnishing, and first-year service charges on top of DLD at 4%. Furnishing a 2BR alone often runs AED 25,000 to 80,000 depending on finish level and community choice in Marina or JVC. Checklist:

| Cost line | Typical range (AED) | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Golden Visa government fees | 3,000 to 5,000 | Plus medical and Emirates ID |
| Agent-assisted visa processing | 3,000 to 8,000 | Optional but common for Russian speakers |
| DEWA deposit | 2,000 to 4,000 | Refundable on exit |
| School registration deposits | 500 to 5,000 per child | Non-refundable at many schools |
| Furnishing a 2BR apartment | 25,000 to 80,000 | Wide range by area and quality |
| First-year service charges | 12,000 to 30,000+ | Depends on tower and size |

Transaction costs on the property itself (DLD 4% transfer fee, trustee, and agency) sit outside this living setup budget but should be modelled together before you commit.

The 10-year Golden Visa horizon provides stability that matches family planning timelines, children through school, business establishment, and property appreciation over a 7-10 year hold period.
