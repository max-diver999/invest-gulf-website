Most Golden Visa coverage explains the AED 2 million rule and stops there. The harder question is which units actually sit at that line, and what you give up by choosing one of them. At AED 2 million a buyer in Dubai can hold a two-bedroom apartment in a metro-linked tower, a townhouse in an outer community, or two smaller yield units aggregated together, and those three choices produce materially different rent, different service charges and different exit timelines.

This page ranks the realistic options by what the threshold buys, not by what qualifies. The rules themselves are covered in the [UAE Golden Visa property guide](/guides/uae-golden-visa-property/).

Insider tip: buy at AED 2.05 million or higher rather than exactly AED 2 million, because the registered value governs eligibility and a Dubai Land Department valuation coming in two percent below your contract price is the single most common reason a Golden Visa file gets rejected after the deposit is already paid.

## What does the AED 2M Golden Visa threshold actually measure?

The threshold measures registered value, not the price you negotiated. The Dubai Land Department or, in Abu Dhabi, the Department of Municipalities and Transport records a figure against the title deed or the Oqood certificate, and the immigration authority reads that figure. Where the authority values a unit below the contract price, the lower number is what counts.

This distinction decides real applications. A buyer who agrees AED 2,000,000 and sees the unit registered at AED 1,960,000 has bought a property that does not qualify, and the remedy is either a second purchase to aggregate the shortfall or a resale at a loss once transaction costs are counted. Registered value can fall short of contract price in slower quarters, when a seller has priced against a peak comparable, when a unit carries an unusually large balcony area that the valuer discounts, or when the building has an open service charge dispute. The practical margin is not large: on AED 2 million a two percent gap is AED 40,000, which is less than the agency commission on the same deal. We treat AED 2.05 million as the working floor on every visa-motivated file we underwrite, because the cost of overshooting is a known AED 50,000 and the cost of undershooting is the whole application.

| What the rule counts | What it does not count |
|---|---|
| Registered value on title deed or Oqood | Contract or SPA price where higher |
| Combined value of units in one name | Units held in a spouse's separate name |
| Off-plan registered on Oqood | Reservation forms not yet on Oqood |
| Mortgaged units at full registered value | Equity you hold in the property |
| Dubai and Abu Dhabi units together | Furniture, fit-out or service charge credits |

## Which Dubai communities clear AED 2M with the most floor area?

Floor area per dirham varies by roughly a factor of two across communities that all reach the threshold. A two-bedroom apartment in Jumeirah Lake Towers trades between AED 1.6 million and AED 2.2 million, so the top of that band clears the line while remaining a mainstream, liquid unit type with metro access. Meydan Horizon two-bedroom stock at AED 1.8 million to AED 2.5 million reaches the same line with newer building fabric but a shallower resale market.

| Community | Unit type at the line | Typical price | Gross yield | Why it clears well |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| JLT | Two-bedroom apartment | AED 1.6M-2.2M | 6.5-7.2% | Metro-linked, deepest resale pool at the threshold |
| Meydan Horizon | Two-bedroom apartment | AED 1.8M-2.5M | 5.5-7.0% | Newer stock, premium address, thinner secondary market |
| Dubai Creek Harbour | Two-bedroom apartment | around AED 2.1M | 5.0-7.0% | Emaar build quality, long-cycle appreciation thesis |
| The Valley | Townhouse | AED 1.6M-2.8M | 6.5% at entry | Most built area per dirham, family tenant demand |
| DAMAC Hills | Three-bed townhouse | AED 2.5M-2.8M | 5.5% | Overshoots the line, buys space rather than yield |
| Tilal Al Ghaf | Townhouse | AED 2.2M-3.5M | 5-7% | Lifestyle premium, slowest to clear on resale |

Townhouses in outer communities give the most built area, and the tenant is a family on a three to five-year lease, which suppresses turnover cost. Apartments in metro-linked towers give the most liquidity, and liquidity is what matters if the visa is a means rather than an end. Detail on each of these sits on the [JLT area page](/uae/dubai/jlt/) and the [Meydan Horizon area page](/uae/dubai/meydan-horizon/).

## How much rent does an AED 2M qualifying unit produce?

An AED 2 million unit in Dubai typically produces gross rent of AED 110,000 to AED 145,000 a year, which is a gross yield in the 5.5 to 7.2 percent band depending on community and unit type. Net yield after service charges, management and vacancy usually lands 1.5 to 2.5 percentage points lower.

The gap between gross and net is where visa-motivated buyers most often misjudge the asset. Service charges in the communities that clear AED 2 million run from roughly AED 12 to AED 22 per square foot per year, so a 1,200 square foot two-bedroom carries AED 14,400 to AED 26,400 before management fees. Add a management fee near five percent of collected rent, a maintenance reserve, and one vacant month in a typical three-year cycle, and a headline 6.8 percent gross on JLT stock settles near 4.9 percent net. On a townhouse at the same price the arithmetic is friendlier on service charge per square foot but harsher on turnover, because a family tenant vacating leaves a larger unit to re-let in a narrower pool. We model both on a five-year hold rather than a single year, since the visa horizon and the ownership horizon are the same ten and five years respectively.

## Where does Abu Dhabi beat Dubai at the AED 2M line?

Abu Dhabi delivers more built area for the same registered value, and the threshold is identical because it is a federal rule rather than an emirate rule. Al Reem Island carries stock explicitly positioned in the AED 2 million and above band alongside entry units from AED 550,000, and Al Raha Beach offers waterfront layouts that reach the DMT-registered AED 2 million threshold with larger floor plates than Dubai equivalents.

The cost is exit depth. Dubai's secondary market clears a mainstream two-bedroom in weeks; Abu Dhabi's takes longer because the buyer pool is smaller and more of it is end-user rather than investor. Yas Island entry near AED 700,000 and Al Reem's ADGM-adjacent tenant base give a defensible rental thesis, but a buyer who expects to sell inside three years should weight liquidity above floor area. Community detail is on the [Al Reem Island page](/uae/abu-dhabi/al-reem-island/).

| Factor | Dubai at AED 2M | Abu Dhabi at AED 2M |
|---|---|---|
| Built area | Smaller for the price | Typically larger floor plates |
| Resale speed | Weeks in mainstream stock | Months, thinner buyer pool |
| Registering authority | Dubai Land Department | Department of Municipalities and Transport |
| Tenant profile | Mixed investor and expat | Government, energy, ADGM finance |
| Service charge band | AED 12-22 per sq ft | Comparable, newer stock at upper end |

## Is one AED 2M unit better than two smaller ones?

Aggregation raises yield and lowers convenience. Two units at roughly AED 1 million each in communities such as JVC, Motor City or Dubai Silicon Oasis print gross yields around 7 to 8.5 percent, against 5.5 to 7 percent on a single AED 2 million unit, so the income difference on the same capital is often AED 20,000 to AED 30,000 a year.

What aggregation costs is administrative load and exit flexibility. Two units mean two service charge accounts, two Ejari registrations, two sets of tenant risk and two sales to unwind if you ever need the capital. It also constrains disposal: selling one unit drops the combined registered value below AED 2 million and puts renewal at risk, so the portfolio has to be sold or replaced as a block rather than trimmed. The aggregation mechanics, including mixing Dubai and Abu Dhabi and mixing Oqood with title deeds, are set out in the [multiple properties guide](/guides/golden-visa-multiple-properties-uae/), and the disposal sequencing question is covered there in detail.

## What does an AED 2M purchase cost all-in?

A cash purchase at AED 2 million costs approximately AED 2,131,000 all-in before the visa itself. The Dubai Land Department transfer fee is four percent, agency commission is two percent plus five percent VAT on the commission, and trustee and title deed fees add roughly AED 5,000.

| Line item | Calculation | AED |
|---|---|---|
| Purchase price | Registered value | 2,050,000 |
| DLD transfer fee | 4% of registered value | 82,000 |
| Agency commission | 2% of price | 41,000 |
| VAT on commission | 5% of commission | 2,050 |
| Trustee and title deed | Fixed, DLD capped | 5,000 |
| Total cash to close | | 2,180,050 |
| Golden Visa application | Primary applicant, incl. medical and Emirates ID | 10,000-15,000 |

Buying off-plan direct from a developer usually removes the buyer-side commission, which cuts roughly AED 43,000 from that stack, and replaces the DLD transfer fee with a four percent Oqood registration charge at the same rate. The full fee breakdown for both routes is on the [cost of buying property in Dubai page](/guides/cost-of-buying-property-dubai/), and the off-plan qualification timing sits in the [off-plan Golden Visa guide](/guides/golden-visa-off-plan-property-uae/).

## Which AED 2M units are hardest to resell?

The hardest units to clear at this price are the ones bought for the threshold rather than for the market. Three-bedroom apartments in standalone towers are the clearest example: at AED 2 million the natural buyer for three bedrooms is a family, and families at that budget overwhelmingly prefer a townhouse with a garden in The Valley or DAMAC Hills.

A second trap is the stretched one-bedroom. Where a large one-bedroom is priced up to AED 2 million to serve visa buyers, it sits above what the one-bedroom market will pay and below what a two-bedroom buyer wants, so it clears only to another visa buyer, at a discount. A third is any unit in a building with an unresolved service charge escalation, because the next buyer's valuer will read the same Mollak record you did. Resale mechanics, including the NOC and the Form F sequence, are covered in the [selling property in Dubai guide](/guides/selling-property-dubai-guide/); the practical point at this threshold is that the exit pool for a mainstream two-bedroom is an order of magnitude deeper than for a purpose-bought qualifying unit.

## How should you sequence the purchase and the visa application?

Sequence the purchase to completion first and the visa application second, because the registered value is the input the immigration file needs and it does not exist until registration. For a ready resale that means the trustee appointment produces the title deed, and the application follows within days. For off-plan it means the Oqood certificate, which is issued after the developer registers the sale with the Land Department, typically within two to four weeks of the sale agreement.

Practical order of operations:

1. Confirm the community and unit type against net yield, not gross, and against resale depth.
2. Agree a price at or above AED 2.05 million and put the valuation risk in writing in the agreement.
3. Complete at the trustee office, or obtain the Oqood certificate for off-plan.
4. Read the registered value on the deed or Oqood and confirm it clears the line.
5. File the Golden Visa application, then add dependants once the primary visa is issued.

Where a mortgage is involved the bank's own valuation arrives before the immigration file, which gives an early warning if the registered figure is likely to disappoint. That mechanic is set out in the [mortgage Golden Visa guide](/guides/golden-visa-mortgage-property-uae/).

## What should you confirm before paying a deposit at the AED 2M line?

Confirm four things before any money moves, because each of them has ended an application after the deposit was non-refundable. The registered value question is first, and the other three concern whether the property is a good asset independently of the visa.

- Registered value headroom. Agree above AED 2.05 million and ask the agent for the last three registered comparables in the same building, not the last three asking prices.
- Service charge record. Pull the current rate per square foot and the two prior years. A tower moving from AED 14 to AED 20 per square foot has repricing risk that the rent will not absorb.
- Developer and escrow status for off-plan. The unit must be registered on Oqood and the project must hold funds in a regulated escrow account, as explained in the [escrow and Oqood guide](/guides/escrow-oqood-dubai-explained/).
- Exit pool. Ask who buys this unit type at this price when you sell. If the honest answer is another visa buyer, the discount on exit is already visible.
