## What are the 10 etiquette rules that matter most in Qatar?

The etiquette rules that matter most in Qatar typically mean modest dress in malls, public Ramadan fasting respect for about 30 days, licensed-only alcohol, permission before photos, and accepting Arabic coffee. Invest Gulf tells foreign buyers those five habits prevent most awkward moments within the first 30 days.

| Situation | The short rule |
|---|---|
| Malls, souqs, government offices | Shoulders and knees covered, no beachwear |
| Ramadan daylight hours | No eating, drinking, gum or smoking in public view |
| Alcohol | Licensed venues only, never in public, never before driving |
| Photography | Ask first, never Qatari women or children, never security sites |
| Greetings | Let a Qatari woman offer her hand first |
| Hospitality | Accept dates and Arabic coffee, a polite sip is enough |
| Public affection | Handholding at most, nothing more |
| Online posts | No mocking Islam, no naming and shaming, no defamation |
| Tipping | Welcome, not obligatory, round up or give a few riyals |
| When unsure | Watch what Qataris nearby are doing, then copy it |

Do not assume that because most residents are foreign, the rules do not apply. Enforcement can feel selective right up to the moment it is not, and the consequences range from a polite word from a security guard to fines, employer discipline or, in extreme cases, deportation. Getting this right is not about performing piety, it is about behaving like a considerate guest in a country where most of the people you meet are also guests.

## What can women wear in Qatar, and are shorts allowed?

Modest dress for women in Qatar typically means shoulders and knees covered in malls, souqs and government buildings, while shorts are fine at hotel pools and many Pearl or West Bay brunches. Invest Gulf tells foreign buyers to keep one conservative outfit ready for MOI visits within the first 30 days, and again during the roughly 30 day Ramadan window. Abayas are not mandatory for non-Muslim women in daily life.

| Setting | Men | Women |
|---|---|---|
| Malls and souqs | Shoulders covered, knees covered | Shoulders and knees covered, avoid deep necklines |
| Government offices and MOI | Long trousers, covered shoulders | Abaya not required for expats, conservative dress advised |
| Public family beaches | Swim shorts acceptable | One piece or modest bikini, never topless |
| Hotel pools and beach clubs | Standard swimwear | Standard swimwear within club rules |
| The Pearl and West Bay dining | Relaxed, but not beachwear at the table | Relaxed, carry a shawl for the mall afterwards |
| Mosque visits | Long trousers, remove shoes | Headscarf plus abaya or long loose clothing, remove shoes |

## What should men wear in Qatar?

Men's dress code in Qatar typically means long trousers and covered shoulders for malls, souqs and government buildings, while shorts belong at the marina, beach, gym and compounds. We tell foreign buyers that West Bay corporate meetings still read suits as respect within the first 30 days.

| Setting | Men | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Malls / souqs | Shoulders and knees covered | No beachwear |
| Government / MOI | Long trousers, covered shoulders | Dress up |
| Beach / marina / gym | Shorts fine | Switch before the mall |
| West Bay meetings | Suit or smart business casual | Friday often quiet |

## How do you behave during Ramadan in Qatar?

Ramadan behaviour in Qatar typically requires no eating, drinking, gum or smoking in public during daylight hours for about 29 to 30 days, and the rule applies to every resident regardless of religion. We flag visible water bottles in open meeting rooms as the top mistake in the first 12 months.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Eat and drink inside your home, a closed office kitchen, or designated hotel areas | Eat, drink, chew gum, or smoke in public view during daylight |
| Accept iftar invitations and break the fast at sunset with colleagues | Play loud music in residential areas during prayer times |
| Shorten meetings and expect lower afternoon energy | Push fasting colleagues into lunch meetings without an alternative |
| Dress more modestly than you usually would | Wear revealing clothing in malls or souqs |
| Use the greeting "Ramadan Kareem" if you are comfortable with it | Joke about fasting or about how tired fasting colleagues look |

Office culture shifts for the month. Many employers move to shorter hours for about 29 to 30 days, often something like 9am to 3pm, so plan work that needs decisions into the morning across a typical 6 hour day. Roads spike in the 30 to 60 minutes before maghrib as everyone races home for iftar, which is the one time of year when leaving the office early is the safer choice rather than the lazy one.

### Iftar and suhoor

Iftar, the meal that breaks the fast at sunset, is the social centre of the month. Employers, neighbours, schools and hotels host iftar tents, and accepting an invitation is a genuine investment in the relationship rather than a courtesy. Arrive on time, wait for the call to prayer before eating unless your host says otherwise, and try the dates and Arabic coffee that open most tables. Suhoor, the pre dawn meal, is quieter, with hotel buffets running late into the night. If you are staying in a hotel, remember that other guests are eating at 4am and sleeping afterwards.

### Eid al-Fitr

Eid is a multi day public holiday. Government offices close, schools break, and travel demand peaks in both directions. The greeting is "Eid Mubarak". Gift giving is common among Qatari families, and expats usually send messages to close colleagues rather than presents. If you are timing a move, avoid the first week of Ramadan and the Eid travel crush, and read the [Qatar relocation guide](/guides/qatar-relocation-guide/) before you fix dates with a shipper.

## What are the rules on alcohol in Qatar?

Alcohol in Qatar is restricted rather than banned: licensed venues serve it, residents can buy via a QDC permit tied to salary and sponsor approval, and public drinking or any drink-driving is illegal. We tell foreign buyers to sort permit paperwork in the first 30 days, because supermarket alcohol is not a fallback. Zero tolerance on blood alcohol means one glass before driving is already a career risk.

| Setting | Rule | Timing tip |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Licensed hotel / restaurant | Legal service | Keep it inside the venue |
| QDC resident permit | Salary + sponsor linked | Start in month 1 |
| Public spaces | No open alcohol | Applies 365 days |
| Driving | Zero measurable alcohol | Absolute |

## Do you tip in Qatar?

Tipping in Qatar is appreciated but never obligatory: when a service charge is already on the bill, rounding up is enough, and when it is not, about 10 percent is a normal thank-you for good table service. We tell foreign buyers that small cash notes still work better than card tips for porters, drivers and delivery staff. Salon and spa staff are also commonly tipped around 10 percent.

| Service | Typical gesture |
|---|---|
| Restaurant with service charge on the bill | Round up, nothing more expected |
| Restaurant without service charge | Around 10 percent if the service was good |
| Taxi and ride hailing | Round up to the nearest note |
| Hotel porter and valet | A few riyals in cash |
| Grocery and food delivery | A few riyals, more in summer heat |
| Salon, spa and barber | Around 10 percent |
| Building watchman and cleaner | Small cash at Eid, common among residents |

Cash still works better than card for tips, so keep small notes. Many of the people serving you are supporting families abroad, and a modest, consistent tip lands better than an occasional grand one.

## Can you take photos in Qatar?

Photography in Qatar requires consent for people, and photographing Qatari women or children without explicit permission is treated as a serious cultural and legal offence. We tell foreign buyers to default to asking first in souqs within the first 30 days, and to avoid government and military sites entirely.

| Subject | Risk |
|---|---|
| Qatari women and children | Serious offence, both cultural and legal sensitivity |
| Government, military and security buildings | Fines, equipment confiscation |
| Airport security zones | Prohibited |
| Strangers in souqs | Ask first, gesture at the camera, wait for the nod |
| Accidents and police scenes | Do not photograph, do not circulate |

Social media raises the stakes rather than lowering them. Posting identifiable images of other people without consent, especially Qatari nationals, creates both reputational and legal exposure, and blurring backgrounds is a cheap insurance policy. Drones require licensing and recreational flying is heavily regulated, so verify current official rules and never fly without authorisation.

## How does Qatari hospitality work if you are invited home?

Qatari home hospitality typically opens with dates and Arabic coffee accepted with the right hand; waving them away twice reads as rejecting the host, not as a diet preference. We tell foreign buyers that a polite sip plus a gentle cup-shake is enough, and that alcohol is the wrong gift unless you know the household. Expect shoe removal and possible majlis separation within your first 12 months of local invitations across a multi-year posting.

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| Accept coffee / dates once | Refuse twice as a default |
| Bring dates, chocolate, flowers | Bring alcohol to unknown homes |
| Follow host on seating / shoes | Point soles at people on cushions |

## How do men and women interact in public in Qatar?

Public affection in Qatar typically stays minimal: handholding between married couples usually passes, and anything beyond that is out of place in malls and family areas. We tell foreign buyers to let a Qatari woman offer her hand first, or use a chest-nod greeting. Mixed offices are normal; legal risk around same-sex relations and some housing rules still needs a fresh HR check rather than a 2 year old anecdote.

| Interaction | Practical rule | Timing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public affection | Handholding at most | Year-round |
| Handshake with Qatari women | Wait for her to offer | First meeting onward |
| Mixed workplaces | Normal in offices/hotels | Daily |
| Housing / cohabitation | Verify with HR | Before lease year 1 |

## Which Arabic phrases actually help in Qatar?

English covers hospitals, banks and school admissions in Qatar, so Arabic is a courtesy layer rather than a survival skill. We still recommend about 10 phrases, because greetings warm up the first 30 to 90 days of colleague and service interactions. Use them lightly; nobody expects fluency.

- "As-salamu alaykum" as a formal greeting, answered with "wa alaykum as-salam"
- "Marhaba" for a casual hello
- "Shukran" for thank you, "afwan" for you are welcome
- "Insha'Allah" for anything scheduled in the future
- "Khalas" for finished or that is enough
- "Ramadan Kareem" during the holy month
- "Eid Mubarak" during Eid
- "Yalla" for let us go
- "Min fadlak" or "min fadlik" for please
- "Ma'a as-salama" for goodbye

## How does religion shape daily life in Qatar?

Religion typically shapes daily life in Qatar through five daily prayers and Friday as the weekly holy day, when many businesses close Friday morning or the full day. We tell foreign buyers to pause meetings when a colleague steps out to pray during a normal 7 day office week.

| Rhythm | What changes | Buyer tip |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Five daily prayers | Short work pauses | Resume after, do not rush |
| Friday | Half day or closed | Avoid key meetings |
| Ramadan month | Shorter hours ~30 days | Plan around iftar traffic |

## Is Qatar safe for visitors and families?

Qatar is consistently among the safest Gulf countries for street-level crime, and residents routinely walk in Doha at night within the first 30 days without treating it as risky. We still flag traffic, summer heat for about 4 months from June to September, and strict defamation rules as the real planning risks.

| Risk type | Reality | Practical response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Street crime | Low vs most cities | Normal vigilance |
| Traffic | Main physical danger | Defensive driving |
| June to September heat | Health risk | Indoor daytime life |
| Online speech | Strict legal environment | Keep disputes private / formal |

## When is the best time to visit Qatar?

November to March is the best visit window for outdoor Qatar: Corniche walks, desert trips and terraces are pleasant, and hotel demand peaks for the same 5 months. We tell foreign buyers that April/October are shoulder-value months, while May to September is an indoor-life season with cheaper rooms. Ramadan shifts about 11 days earlier each year, so confirm dates before you lock flights.

| Period | What to expect |
|---|---|
| November to March | Coolest months, outdoor season, highest hotel demand |
| April and October | Warm shoulder months, good value, still workable outdoors |
| May to September | Very hot and humid, indoor life, cheapest hotel rates |
| Ramadan (moves each year) | Reduced daytime dining, striking evening atmosphere, verify dates |

Ramadan falls on the lunar calendar and shifts about eleven days earlier each year, so confirm the dates for your travel year. Visiting during Ramadan is rewarding if you want to see the country at its most social after sunset, and frustrating if you expect a normal daytime restaurant scene.

## How do souqs and bargaining work in Doha?

Bargaining is expected in Souq Waqif and the gold souq, and unwelcome in malls where prices are fixed. We tell foreign buyers to keep the tone friendly and to get substantial prices in writing, because warranty claims need receipts more than charm. Cash still helps in traditional souqs; cards dominate everywhere else within your first 30 days of errands.

| Venue | Bargaining | Tip |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Souq Waqif / gold souq | Expected | Walk away without drama |
| Malls / chains | Fixed prices | No haggling |
| Large purchases | Get price in writing | Keep receipts 12 months |

## What is driving like in Doha?

Driving in Doha means assertive merges, heavy camera enforcement, and zero tolerance for any alcohol behind the wheel. We tell foreign buyers that unpaid fines can block registration renewal, so treat cameras as a budget line from week 1 of year 1. Ramadan iftar spikes and school-run windows in West Bay, The Pearl and Lusail matter more than highway design when you choose a home for a 2 to 8 year stay.

| Pattern | When | Planning tip |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Camera fines | Year-round | Pay before renewal |
| Iftar traffic | Ramadan evenings | Leave 30 to 60 minutes early |
| School run | Weekday mornings / afternoons | Test the gate commute |

## What is business etiquette like with Qatari colleagues?

Business etiquette with Qatari colleagues puts relationship before process: a first meeting that ends in coffee and no signature is often success, not failure. We tell foreign buyers to schedule important decisions in the morning, avoid Fridays, and expect shorter Ramadan hours for about 30 days. Hierarchy matters, written follow-up within 1 to 2 days matters, and public criticism of a colleague does not.

| Rhythm point | Practical rule | Timing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First meeting | Relationship first | 30 to 60 minutes is normal |
| Decision meetings | Mornings | Avoid Friday entirely |
| Ramadan | Shorter hours | Plan around iftar traffic |
| Follow-up | Write it down | After verbal agreement |

## What is expat life in Qatar actually like?

Expat life in Qatar is comfortable and organised around compounds, schools and workplaces more than European-style neighbourhoods, and most people find a circle within the first 3 months. We see foreign buyers arrive on a 2 year plan and stay nearer 8 years, which is why a "temporary" lease still needs school-gate maths. Summer confines outdoor life for about 4 months; The Pearl, West Bay and Lusail concentrate the cosmopolitan version.

| Factor | Typical pattern | Planning note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Social circle | Forms in 3 months | Work + school + sport |
| Stated stay | 2 years | Often becomes 8 |
| Summer | About 4 indoor months | Club / compound pools matter |

## Which topics should you avoid discussing?

Avoid regional politics, ruling-family internals, religion-as-debate, and public labour arguments; visitors lose more than they gain in those rooms. We tell foreign buyers that online employer or landlord complaints can become defamation risk within the first 30 days, even when the underlying dispute is valid. Comparing Qatar unfavourably to Dubai in front of Qatari colleagues reads as rudeness, not analysis, and it travels badly for years afterward.

| Topic | Risk | Safer channel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regional politics | High social / legal | Private only |
| Employer / landlord disputes | Cybercrime / defamation risk | Formal complaint routes |
| Qatar vs Dubai rankings | Social rudeness | Keep comparative analysis offline |

## What do new arrivals in Qatar get wrong most often?

New arrivals usually prepare for big culture questions and miss small logistics within the first 30 days: a water bottle visible in a Ramadan meeting, a phone raised in a souq, or a compound outfit that fails at MOI. We see the same three gaps.

| Gap | When it bites | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dress register | MOI / mall week 1 | Keep one conservative outfit |
| Alcohol paperwork | First month social plans | Start QDC process early |
| School commute | 7:30am reality | Test before signing lease |

Insider tip: drive the school route at 7:30am before you fall in love with a Pearl or Lusail view, because a 40 minute brochure commute that becomes 70 minutes is a culture problem disguised as traffic.

Alcohol is legal only in licensed venues and via resident QDC permits, with zero tolerance for drink-driving, and tipping sits around 10 percent when no service charge appears. Our relocation briefs put photography consent, especially around Qatari women and children, in the first 30 days checklist beside MOI dress and school-gate timing. Sort QDC paperwork in month 1 if your household drinks, because supermarket alcohol is not a backup plan for visitors or new residents.
