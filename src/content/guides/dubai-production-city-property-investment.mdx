Dubai Production City — still called **IMPZ** on older title deeds — is where Dubai parks media warehouses and stacks residential towers beside them. Investors do not buy lifestyle. They buy **8–9.5% gross yields** on studios and one-bedrooms that rent to production staff, delivery fleet coordinators, and anyone who priced out of JVC but still works west Dubai.

**Quick answer:** Gross yield 8.0–9.5%. Entry AED 400K–750K. Highest-yield tier with trade-offs. Freehold.

## Practical decision filter for Dubai planning

Use exact building and household numbers, not generic city averages.

Use this final check before making the production city decision:

| Production City check | What to confirm |
|---|---|
| Real monthly cost | Rent plus utilities, chiller, school transport, parking and deposits |
| Commute proof | Morning route tested on a working day, not a midnight map estimate |
| Renewal risk | RERA index, landlord behaviour and comparable new leases |
| Exit or move-out friction | Notice period, agent fees, repainting, snagging and deposit recovery |
| Family fit | School seat, nursery access, car requirement and weekend lifestyle |

Production City note: Production City note: For investors, tenant affordability is the underwriting line. If the target tenant must spend more than roughly one third of household net income on rent, vacancy and renewal risk rise. For residents, the same rule applies in reverse: do not sign a lease that only works before utilities, school fees and transport are added.

Production City cross-check: cross-check the numbers against [Dubai Cost of Living Guide](/guides/dubai-cost-of-living-guide/), [Dubai Rent Prices by Area](/guides/dubai-rent-prices-by-area/), [Dubai Utility Bills DEWA](/guides/dubai-utility-bills-deewa/), [Dubai School Fees by Curriculum](/guides/dubai-school-fees-by-curriculum/) and [Dubai Property Investment Guide](/guides/dubai-property-investment-guide/).

## IMPZ building due diligence checklist

1. Occupancy rate ask building management
2. Service charge payment certificate 3 years
3. Elevator maintenance contract current?
4. Tenant nationality mix — stability indicator
5. Parking allocation per unit — affects rent
6. Proximity to bus stop for JVC workers
7. Compare 3 similar units — do not overpay for floor alone
8. Holiday home restriction in bylaws
9. Short-term sublet clause in owner association
10. DEWA average for floor type

IMPZ rewards **patient landlords** who maintain units and price rent competitively — turnover is the enemy of net yield.

## Production City tenant-demand filter

Production City is a yield-first market. It works when the unit serves JLT, Media City and mid-market commuter demand without losing too much rent to service charges and cooling.

| Check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| parking allocation | This is the first practical constraint to verify before relying on the headline numbers |
| district cooling cost | Small daily frictions become large costs over a full year |
| service-charge trend | This changes the real budget, not just the lifestyle preference |
| active listings in same tower | It separates a clean decision from a brochure-led decision |
| commute to JLT and Marina | It protects against forced moves, weak tenants or poor renewal outcomes |

A slightly more expensive tower with lower cooling bills can produce better net yield. Investor-heavy towers need a vacancy allowance even if gross yield looks strong.

Production City resident test: the practical test is whether the choice still works in an ordinary weekday, not just during a viewing. For investors, the test is whether a normal tenant can afford the all-in cost and wants to renew after the first year. If either answer is weak, the cheaper or better-looking option is probably not the stronger decision.



## Local scenario test

Production City scenario: use a real scenario before treating the guide as complete. Define the household or tenant, write down the monthly budget, then test the exact property or school route against that profile. The answer changes quickly when one assumption moves.

| Production City scenario item | Conservative input |
|---|---|
| Income or tenant budget | Use net monthly income, not gross salary |
| Housing or rent line | Include deposits, fees and renewal risk |
| Transport | Include second car, parking, Salik or bus fees |
| Time cost | Count weekday commute, not only weekend convenience |
| Exit plan | Know what happens if the lease, school, or tenant profile changes |

Production City stress test: the best decision is the one that still works after the optimistic assumptions are removed. If the deal or relocation only makes sense with perfect traffic, full occupancy, no utility surprise and no fee increase, it is too fragile.


**Production City final rule:** write the numbers in one place before signing. Rent, utilities, school or tenant costs, transport, deposits, and exit friction should be visible on the same page. That single sheet usually reveals whether the decision is solid or just emotionally attractive.
