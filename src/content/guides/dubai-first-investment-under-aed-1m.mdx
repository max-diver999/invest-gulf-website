Under AED 1 million is where most first Dubai purchases actually happen, and it is the price band with the widest gap between a good decision and a bad one. The same money buys a studio in a tower with an AED 18 per square foot service charge or a one-bedroom in a community charging AED 10, and after five years those two choices differ by more than the deposit.

This page maps what the budget buys by community, using entry prices and service charge rates published on our area pages, and works the numbers through to net yield rather than stopping at the headline gross figure.

Insider tip: ask for the service charge per square foot for the last three years before you ask for the rent, because a tower moving from AED 12 to AED 18 per square foot has quietly repriced your net yield by more than a full percentage point and no listing will mention it.

## What does AED 1 million actually buy in Dubai in 2026?

At this budget the choice is between a studio in a well-run community, a one-bedroom in a mid-market one, or a two-bedroom in the cheapest tier. Studios start around AED 380,000 in Discovery Gardens, AED 400,000 in Dubai Production City and Dubai South, and AED 420,000 to AED 430,000 in Town Square, Jebel Ali Village and Jumeirah Village Circle.

## Which communities give the best net yield under AED 1M?

Dubai Production City and Dubai Sports City lead the gross yield table at this budget, with stock between AED 400,000 and AED 750,000 printing roughly 8 to 9.5 percent gross. Jumeirah Village Circle reaches 9.2 percent on well-bought studios and Town Square runs 6.8 to 8.4 percent.

Gross is not what you keep. A Dubai Production City unit at AED 450,000 generating AED 34,000 a year prints 7.6 percent gross, but a 500 square foot layout at AED 13 per square foot carries AED 6,500 of service charge, a five percent management fee takes AED 1,700, and one vacant month in a three-year cycle costs about AED 940 a year averaged out. Net settles near 5.5 percent. Run the same arithmetic on a Dubai Silicon Oasis one-bedroom at AED 650,000 generating AED 42,000 and the gross is 6.5 percent, but the larger floor plate at AED 14 per square foot means service charge alone takes AED 10,500 and net lands closer to 4.3 percent. The highest gross figure in the table is rarely the highest net figure, and the ranking changes once the service charge rate is in the model.

## How much cash do you need beyond the purchase price?

Closing costs at this budget run about 6.5 percent of the price for a cash buyer. On an AED 800,000 purchase that is roughly AED 52,000, so the all-in figure is near AED 852,000 before any furnishing or first-year running cost.

| Line item | Calculation | AED |
|---|---|---|
| Purchase price | Agreed price | 800,000 |
| DLD transfer fee | 4% of price | 32,000 |
| Agency commission | 2% of price | 16,000 |
| VAT on commission | 5% of commission | 800 |
| Trustee and title deed | Fixed, DLD capped | 4,200 |
| Total cash to close | | 853,000 |
| Effective premium over price | 53,000 / 800,000 | 6.6% |

A mortgaged buyer at 75 percent loan to value on the same price needs the 25 percent deposit of AED 200,000 plus the same fee stack plus mortgage registration at 0.25 percent of the loan, a bank arrangement fee near one percent, and a valuation near AED 3,000, which totals approximately AED 265,000 at closing. The full breakdown for both routes is on the [cost of buying property in Dubai page](/guides/cost-of-buying-property-dubai/).

## What do service charges do to a sub-AED 1M return?

Service charges are the single largest controllable cost at this budget, and the spread across entry-level communities is wide enough to reverse a ranking. Rates run from roughly AED 6 per square foot in Jebel Ali Village to AED 18 per square foot in parts of Dubai Sports City.

On a 550 square foot studio the difference between AED 10 and AED 16 per square foot is AED 3,300 a year. Against an AED 480,000 purchase that is 0.7 percentage points of net yield, every year, for as long as you hold. Over a five-year hold it is AED 16,500, roughly a third of the closing costs on the same purchase. Service charges also move: a building that defers maintenance for three years and then funds a chiller replacement can reprice from AED 12 to AED 18 in a single budget cycle, and the owners have no vote on whether the work is needed. Reading the last three years of the rate rather than the current year is the cheapest due diligence available at this budget, and it is the check first-time buyers most often skip.

## Should a first investment be a studio or a one-bedroom?

Studios produce the higher gross yield and the lower ticket, so they suit an income objective and a long hold. One-bedroom units have a wider tenant pool and a deeper resale market, so they suit a buyer who may sell within five years.

The trade is roughly half a percentage point of yield against exit speed. A studio in JVC at AED 480,000 may print close to 9 percent gross where a one-bedroom at AED 780,000 prints 7 percent, but the one-bedroom clears faster on resale because it serves couples and sharers as well as single tenants. Turnover cost also differs: studios re-let more often, and each vacancy plus agency re-let fee costs about a month of rent. On a first purchase, where the plan is more likely to change than on a fifth, the deeper exit pool usually earns its cost. The broader hold-versus-sell arithmetic is set out in the [rent versus buy guide](/guides/rent-vs-buy-dubai-expat/).

## Can you finance a sub-AED 1M purchase as a non-resident?

Financing is available but the terms tighten as the ticket falls. Non-resident buyers typically need a minimum 20 to 25 percent deposit on properties up to AED 5 million, face a shorter maximum term than residents, and meet bank minimum loan sizes that a small studio purchase can fall below.

The practical threshold is around AED 500,000 of loan. A buyer taking 75 percent on a AED 450,000 studio is asking for AED 337,500, which several lenders will not write, so entry-level purchases below roughly AED 600,000 are frequently cash deals by necessity rather than choice. Where financing is available, the arrangement fee near one percent and the valuation fee are fixed costs that weigh more heavily on a small ticket: on AED 600,000 those two items alone are roughly 1.3 percent of the price. Non-resident terms, deposit tiers and documentation are covered on the [non-resident mortgage page](/guides/non-resident-mortgage-dubai/) and the underlying Central Bank ratios on the [mortgage rules page](/guides/uae-central-bank-mortgage-rules/).

## Which sub-AED 1M mistakes cost first-time investors most?

The expensive mistakes at this budget are not about picking the wrong community. They are about buying a headline gross yield without checking what sits underneath it.

- Buying on gross yield alone. A 9 percent gross with an AED 18 per square foot service charge nets less than a 7 percent gross at AED 10.
- Ignoring the service charge history. The current year rate tells you nothing about whether a repricing is due.
- Underestimating vacancy. One vacant month a year is roughly 8 percent off the annual rent, which is more than most yield differences between communities.
- Buying the cheapest unit in the building. The lowest floor facing the car park re-lets slowest and discounts hardest on exit.
- Treating the purchase as the first half of a Golden Visa plan without confirming the aggregation mechanics first.

## How does a first unit fit a longer Golden Visa plan?

A single sub-AED 1 million unit does not qualify for the property route to the UAE Golden Visa, which requires a registered value of at least AED 2 million. Two or more units registered in the same name can be aggregated to that threshold, which is why some investors treat a first entry-level purchase as the opening half of a visa plan.

The aggregation route has a real advantage and a real constraint. The advantage is yield: two units around AED 1 million each in the communities above print gross yields near 7 to 8.5 percent where a single AED 2 million unit prints 5.5 to 7 percent. The constraint is disposal, because selling one unit drops the combined registered value below the line and puts renewal at risk. If a visa is part of the plan, decide before the first purchase whether both units will be bought in the same name, since a unit registered in a spouse's separate name does not count toward the same applicant's total. The mechanics are set out in the [Golden Visa property guide](/guides/uae-golden-visa-property/).

## What should you check before you commit under AED 1M?

Five checks, in this order, before any deposit is paid. Together they cost nothing but an afternoon, and each one guards against a loss larger than the roughly AED 53,000 of closing costs on an AED 800,000 purchase. The service charge check alone is worth 0.7 percentage points of net yield a year on a typical 550 square foot studio.

1. Service charge per square foot for the current year and the two prior years, from the Mollak record rather than the agent.
2. Actual Ejari-registered rents in the same building, not the asking rents on portals. Rent benchmarking is covered on the [RERA rent calculator page](/guides/dubai-rent-increase-calculator-rera/).
3. The building's vacancy pattern and the typical re-let time for your unit type and floor.
4. Whether the loan size clears the lender's minimum, if financing is intended.
5. Who buys this unit when you sell, and at what discount to the floor above.

The transaction sequence itself, from offer through trustee, is on the [step-by-step buying guide](/guides/how-to-buy-property-dubai-step-by-step/).
