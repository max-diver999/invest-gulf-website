## What fee tiers and hidden costs should families budget?

Fee tiers in Doha typically mean mid British programmes around 45K to 65K per year, premium IB or British near 70K to 95K+, and hidden add-ons of 2K to 14K for registration and transport that Invest Gulf adds before employer allowance maths across a 12 month cash plan in this market.

### Mid tier (about 45K to 65K/year)
- Established British schools
- Select American programmes

### Premium tier (about 70K to 95K+/year)
- Top IB and British near West Bay / Pearl catchments
- Smaller classes, heavy extracurricular load

Hidden costs checklist:

- Registration: 2K to 10K (often 5% to 15% of one term)
- Transport: 8K to 14K/year
- Exam fees: 3K to 8K in IGCSE-heavy years
- Uniforms, trips, device schemes across 9 months

## How do British, IB, and Indian pathways differ in practice?

British pathways typically feed UK universities through IGCSE and A-Level, IB Diploma years often price at 80K to 95K+, and Indian CBSE keeps many families near 25K to 40K while accepting longer academic days that Invest Gulf flags before lifestyle assumptions for foreign buyers in Doha for 5 years.

- British: widest campus count; subject drop narrows at A-Level
- IB: strongest for families relocating every 3 to 5 years; DP intensity is real
- Indian: Al Wakrah and Abu Hamour catchments common; bus or drive required
- American: smaller cohort than British; fees similar to mid-high British

Switching after Year 8 is hard; CBSE to British is more manageable until Year 7.

## What Arabic and age-band issues change the shortlist?

Arabic requirements typically mean 3 to 5 periods weekly for most international pupils, while age-band risk peaks in IGCSE and IB Diploma years when fees hit 80K to 95K and subject blocks lock for 2 years in this market on Invest Gulf age-band notes for the area.

| Age band | Main lock-in risk |
| --- | --- |
| Primary | Homework and Arabic load |
| Years 12 to 14 | IGCSE subject choices |
| Ages 15 to 18 | Exam fees and predicted grades |

### Primary (ages 6 to 11)
Priority: literacy foundations, homework policy, class size facts, pastoral named contacts.

### Lower secondary (ages 12 to 14)
Priority: subject breadth before IGCSE options meetings, usually Year 8.

### IGCSE and sixth form (ages 15 to 18)
Priority: exam board, predicted-grade culture, counsellor ratio; premium fees peak here.

Heritage Arabic families should ask whether a native track exists; many British campuses run one mixed stream only.
