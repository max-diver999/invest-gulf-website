## How do international transfers work from the UAE?

International transfers from the UAE typically mean sending through a CBUAE-licensed bank, exchange house, or fintech under Money Service Business rules. AED is pegged to USD at 3.6725, so dollar-denominated sends carry zero currency risk, while GBP, EUR, and INR require spread analysis. Transfers above AED 55,000 require source-of-funds documentation.

CBUAE licenses all operators. Exchange houses, banks, and fintech remittance providers fall under the same AML framework once amounts cross reporting thresholds.

Our analysis treats MSB and bank rails as interchangeable only below property-scale documentation thresholds.

- Banks: MT103 and trustee acceptance
- Exchange houses: corridor pricing on INR and PHP
- Wise: mid-market FX under AED 500,000

Property-scale remittances from Dubai require planning around documentation and timing more than the advertised fee line. For transfers above AED 55,000, CBUAE-licensed banks and exchange houses request source-of-funds letters, and developers typically insist on UAE bank SWIFT with an MT103 trail before trustee day. Wise remains competitive for EUR, GBP, and USD under roughly AED 500,000, while Al Ansari and Lulu Exchange often beat fintech pricing on INR and PHP by 0.2 to 0.4 percentage points. Align outbound wires 5 to 7 business days before DLD appointments because correspondent routing can add 2 to 3 days and first-inbound AML holds can add 1 to 2 more. Retain SPA references in payment fields and keep printed confirmations for both UAE trustees and home-country tax files.

## When do you need a UAE bank SWIFT transfer?

UAE bank SWIFT is required when DLD trustees, the developer escrow desk, or overseas correspondent banks need a formal MT103 trail. Property purchases and audit-heavy proceeds often reject MSB-only rails. Expect 1 to 5 business days, AED 50 to 120 bank fees, and 1.2 to 2.5% FX; we treat SWIFT as default for trustee day.

Bank SWIFT is essential in three situations:

1. Property purchase transfers
DLD trustees and developer escrow agents typically require funds to arrive via UAE bank account, not a Wise wallet or exchange house. The MT103 SWIFT confirmation creates the documented trail needed for:
- AML source-of-funds compliance at the developer
- DLD trustee verification
- Home-country capital movement records (India RBI, UK HMRC, etc.)

2. Large one-off transfers requiring audit trail
Business proceeds, inheritance, sale of overseas property, any source requiring a documented chain prefers SWIFT, which produces an MT103 with reference fields.

3. Correspondent bank requirements
Receiving banks in some jurisdictions (US, Germany, Switzerland) only accept SWIFT from licensed banks, not from MSB intermediaries.

Major UAE banks for international SWIFT transfers:

- Emirates NBD, broad correspondent network; competitive FX desk for large transfers
- First Abu Dhabi Bank (FAB), strong on major currencies; private banking desk available
- Mashreq, historically competitive rates for individual customers; strong USD and GBP
- ADCB, good for salary-linked accounts; competitive when relationship established
- HSBC UAE, direct connection to HSBC network reduces correspondent fees in 40+ countries
- Standard Chartered UAE, efficient for UK, India, Singapore corridors via group accounts

Opening a UAE bank account is the prerequisite step; see the [Dubai bank account opening guide](/guides/open-bank-account-dubai/).

## How should you wire funds for a Dubai property purchase?

Wiring funds for a Dubai property purchase typically means clearing AED into a UAE bank before DLD trustee day, with SWIFT MT103 as the trail. Lock non-USD FX at SPA signing, allow 5 to 7 business days, and plan for 2 to 3 days of correspondent routing plus 1 to 2 days of AML hold risk.

- Confirm UAE account is open and can receive SWIFT
- Place SPA reference in the payment narrative
- Retain the MT103 printout for the trustee pack

Step 3: Book FX before wiring
If transferring non-USD: lock in your rate when SPA is signed, not on trustee day. USD buyers: peg removes rate risk; wire any time before trustee.

Step 4: SWIFT transfer with SPA reference
Use SWIFT from your home bank. In the payment reference field: include your name, SPA reference number, and property address. This threads through the MT103 and satisfies DLD trustee AML inquiry.

Step 5: Receive MT103 confirmation
The sending bank issues an MT103 slip (SWIFT confirmation). Print and retain. DLD trustees and developer finance departments regularly request this as proof of international transfer. Wise and exchange house transactions do not generate MT103, another reason bank SWIFT is standard for property transfers.

Step 6: Allow 5 to 7 business days before trustee appointment
Correspondent bank routing can add 2 to 3 days. CBUAE AML hold on large first inbound can add 1 to 2 more. Book trustee appointment after confirming cleared funds in UAE account.

**Step 7: Manager's cheque or local transfer for DLD**
Once funds clear in UAE, convert to manager's cheque (cashier's cheque) for developer payment and DLD. Personal transfers work for some developers; others require manager's cheque as final instrument.

## What AML and source-of-funds rules apply to large transfers?

AML rules for large Dubai transfers typically require source-of-funds letters above AED 55,000 at licensed banks and exchange houses, plus SPA and passport proof on property wires. CRS and FATCA cover account balances for non-UAE nationals, not every remittance line. Invest Gulf buyers treat clean documentation as the default red-flag filter.

**CRS/FATCA reporting:**
UAE participates in the Common Reporting Standard (CRS). Banks report account information on non-UAE-national residents to their home tax authorities annually. FATCA reporting applies to US person accounts specifically.

## How can you reduce FX and transfer fees from Dubai?

Reducing FX fees from Dubai typically means comparing total landed cost, not the advertised fee alone. On AED 100,000 to GBP, a 0.3% spread gap is AED 300 per send. Our analysis favours batching, avoiding double conversion, and splitting a UAE salary account for SWIFT from a remittance wallet.

- Batch less urgent payments to hit better FX tiers at exchange houses.
- Avoid double conversion (AED to USD to INR) unless the receiver requires it.
- Use limit orders or rate alerts during SPA negotiations for property.
- Keep one UAE salary account for SWIFT property wires and a separate wallet for daily remittance.
