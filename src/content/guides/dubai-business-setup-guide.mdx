# Dubai Business Setup Guide 2026: Costs, Mainland vs Freezone & Step-by-Step Process

Quick answer: Setting up a business in Dubai in 2026 is genuinely accessible for foreigners, 100% ownership is permitted on mainland for most activities, freezone packages start under AED 10,000 per year, and the process runs 1–4 weeks for standard structures. The core decision is mainland (full UAE market access) versus freezone (simpler, cheaper, but with local sales restrictions).

TL;DR: Dubai offers two parallel business licensing tracks. Mainland (DED-licensed) gives unrestricted UAE market access including government contracts, costs AED 15,000–45,000 year one with Ejari office requirement. Freezone gives fast setup, fixed packages, and international trade flexibility, costs AED 6,500–25,000 depending on zone and visa needs, with restrictions on direct local retail. Since 2021, foreigners own 100% mainland without a local sponsor in most activities. Corporate tax at 9% applies to profits above AED 375,000 from June 2023; see [UAE corporate tax guide](/guides/uae-corporate-tax-expats/). Freelance permit is a third, lighter-touch option; see [UAE freelance permit](/guides/uae-freelance-permit-dubai/).

> Disclaimer: Business setup regulations, activity codes, and freezone pricing change regularly. This guide provides planning context, consult a UAE-licensed business setup consultant or law firm for your specific structure and activity before committing funds.

## The Dubai Business Landscape: Context Before Choosing

Dubai processed over 50,000 new business registrations in 2025 across mainland and freezone jurisdictions. The city's business infrastructure, banking, logistics, professional services, is mature and functions efficiently for international operators.

Three realities shape the decision for new business owners:

UAE corporate tax is now active. From June 2023, a 9% corporate tax applies to taxable income above AED 375,000. Qualifying freezone income may still attract 0% under specific conditions, but this requires careful structuring, the blanket "tax-free freezones" era has nuance. Factor this into your financial model before assuming freezone equals zero tax.

100% foreign ownership is the norm now, not the exception. The 2021 Commercial Companies Law amendment removed the mandatory 51% UAE national shareholding requirement for most mainland activities. For most commercial, professional, and technology businesses, you own 100% outright.

The market you serve determines your structure. Selling to UAE consumers and businesses directly? Mainland. Providing international services, exporting, or operating as a holding company? Freezone is structurally simpler. Mixing both? Either works with appropriate arrangement for local distribution.

## Mainland vs Freezone: Full Comparison

| Feature | Mainland (DED) | Freezone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regulator | DED (Dept of Economic Development) | Individual freezone authority |
| UAE market access | Unrestricted, sell direct to any UAE customer | Restricted, need mainland agent for local retail |
| Government contracts | Yes | Generally no (varies by freezone) |
| Foreign ownership | 100% for most activities | 100% always |
| Office requirement | Ejari-registered office (physical or flexi in some zones) | Freezone workspace (flexi to dedicated) |
| Visa allocation | Based on office sq m (~1 per 10 sq m) | Based on package (2–15+ depending on workspace) |
| Corporate tax | 9% on profits above AED 375K | 9% standard; qualifying income may attract 0% |
| Setup timeline | 1–4 weeks for standard activities | 3–10 business days for most freezones |
| First-year cost range | AED 15,000–45,000 all-in | AED 6,500–80,000 depending on zone |
| Renewal complexity | DED + office lease renewal annually | Fixed package renewal; simpler |
| Banking | All UAE banks | Some banks restrict freezone accounts; DIFC/ADGM easiest |

The table simplifies nuance, specific activity codes, number of partners, and scale all shift these figures. Use it as a framework, not a final cost.

## Mainland Company Setup: Full Process

### Activity Code and Legal Structure

Step 1, Define your activity: DED classifies business activities into commercial, professional, and industrial categories. The activity code determines licence type, minimum capital requirements, and whether additional approvals are needed (e.g., financial services need CBUAE approval; food businesses need municipality sign-off).

Step 2, Choose legal structure:

| Structure | Best for | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LLC (Limited Liability Company) | Most SMEs and trading | 2–50 shareholders; most flexible |
| Sole Establishment | Individual professionals | 1 owner; personal liability |
| Branch of foreign company | Existing foreign entity | Requires NOC from parent; UAE national agent |
| Civil company | Professional partnerships | Doctors, lawyers, engineers |
| Public/Private Joint Stock | Large corporates | Complex; regulatory approval required |

Most expat business setups in Dubai use LLC, it is the workhorse structure combining liability protection with operational flexibility.

### Mainland Cost Breakdown

| Cost item | Indicative AED | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DED trade licence fee | 3,000–12,000 | Varies by activity and category |
| Initial approval / registration | 300–600 | One-time filing |
| Memorandum of Association notarisation | 1,500–3,000 | Legal drafting + notary |
| Ejari office lease (minimum) | 8,000–20,000/year | Depends on location and size |
| Municipality fee | 5% of rent | Automatically added |
| Chamber of Commerce membership | 500–1,200 | Required for most licences |
| Immigration card / establishment card | 3,500–5,000 | Enables visa sponsorship |
| Each employee/owner visa | 3,500–6,000 per person | Visa + Emirates ID + medical |
| PRO service fees | 3,000–8,000 | Recommended; handles government liaison |
| Year-one total (1 owner, 1 employee, basic office) | AED 25,000–50,000 | Before banking deposits |

Location of office: Dubai mainland office can be in any community, Business Bay, JLT, DWTC, JBR, Al Quoz industrial. Rent per sq ft varies enormously. For a minimum DED-compliant tenancy with basic Ejari, budget AED 8,000–20,000 per year at minimum.

## Freezone Selection: Major Dubai Freezones

Dubai has over 30 free zones across different sectors and geographies. Choosing the right one matters for banking accessibility, visa allocation, and activity fit.

| Freezone | Sector focus | Starting licence cost (approx) | Banking accessibility |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [DMCC](/guides/dmcc-company-setup/) | Commodities, gold, trading, crypto | AED 18,000–35,000 | Good, Mashreq, Emirates NBD |
| DIFC | Financial services, funds, fintech | AED 25,000–80,000+ | Excellent, all banks |
| Dubai South | Aviation, logistics, e-commerce | AED 12,000–25,000 | Moderate |
| IFZA | General trading, services, tech | AED 6,500–15,000 | Moderate |
| SHAMS (Sharjah) | Media, freelance, services | AED 5,500–10,000 | Lower, some banks reluctant |
| JAFZA | Logistics, heavy industry, import/export | AED 18,000–35,000 | Good |
| DAFZA | Aviation and airport proximity | AED 20,000–40,000 | Good |
| Meydan Free Zone | General trading, services | AED 9,000–20,000 | Moderate |

DIFC and ADGM (Abu Dhabi) are distinct, they operate under English common law in their own jurisdiction, which matters for financial services, funds, and disputes. Banking is easiest from DIFC for international businesses.

IFZA and SHAMS are the budget entry points popular with solopreneurs and consultants. Banking can be challenging from these zones, Emirates NBD, Mashreq and some other banks are selective about account opening for lower-tier freezone entities.

## Freezone Setup Process: Step by Step

1. Select freezone based on activity, visa needs, and banking compatibility. For JLT-based trading and services, see the [DMCC company setup guide](/guides/dmcc-company-setup/).
2. Choose package: flexi-desk (1–2 visas), shared office (3–5 visas), dedicated unit (5–15+ visas).
3. Submit application online or via approved agent: passport copies, business plan (some zones), proposed company name.
4. Approval and payment: most standard setups approved within 3–7 business days. Pay licence and workspace fees.
5. Licence issuance: digital licence issued; physical certificate follows.
6. Establishment card from freezone authority: enables visa applications.
7. Visa application: owner visa + any employee visas through freezone immigration services.
8. Bank account opening: begin immediately; some banks take 4–8 weeks and require operational documentation.

Common delays: name rejection (similar to existing entity), additional NOC required for regulated activities, bank account opening timeline.

## Visa Allocation and Employee Sponsorship

Once your company is established, mainland or freezone, it becomes a legal sponsor for employment visas. The number of visas you can issue depends on your office arrangement.

| Office type | Typical visa allocation |
| --- | --- |
| Virtual office / flexi-desk | 1–3 visas (freezone only) |
| Shared office 200–300 sq ft | 3–5 visas |
| Dedicated office 500 sq ft | 5–10 visas |
| Full floor / larger | Proportional to space |

**Mainland DED rule:** approximately 1 visa per 10 sq m of registered office space. A 100 sq m office in Business Bay theoretically supports 10 visa allocations, in practice, subject to DED inspection and activity type.

**Owner visa:** the company sponsor structure allows you as owner to obtain a UAE residence visa through your own company. This ties your Emirates ID to the entity. For investors considering property purchase, note that business visa and property investor visa have separate pathways; see [UAE golden visa property guide](/guides/uae-golden-visa-property/).

**Employee visa process:** for each employee hired, the full visa process runs through your establishment card, approximately 3–6 weeks per hire from job offer to Emirates ID. See [UAE employment visa process](/guides/uae-employment-visa-process/) for detailed steps and costs.

## Corporate Banking: Opening Your Business Account

Business bank account opening in the UAE is the step most business owners underestimate. Banks are cautious post-2022 global AML tightening and have elevated their documentation requirements significantly.

### What Banks Typically Require

| Document category | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Company documents | Licence, MOA, establishment card, share certificate |
| Owner KYC | Passport, Emirates ID, proof of address |
| Business evidence | Business plan, invoices or contracts, client list |
| Source of funds | Personal bank statement or existing business accounts |
| Website / social presence | Some banks request operational evidence |

**Timeline:** straightforward applications at Emirates NBD or Mashreq take 3–6 weeks. Complex ownership structures, certain nationalities, or high-risk activity codes may take 8–12 weeks or face rejection.

**Banks more accessible for new businesses:**
- Emirates NBD Business Banking, popular starting point
- Mashreq Business Edge, digital onboarding flow
- RAK Bank Business, historically less restrictive for freezone entities
- ADCB, strong for mainland companies

**Banks more difficult for new freezone entities:**
- Some major international banks require 12+ months of operation and evidence of revenue before accepting small freezone accounts

**Alternative approach:** start with a UAE neo-bank or payment platform (Wio, Pyypl, Telr) for transactions while the full bank account clears, this gives you an operational payment capability without waiting 8 weeks.

## PRO Services: What They Are and When You Need Them

PRO (Public Relations Officer) services are a class of business support specific to UAE company administration. A PRO handles government portal submissions, document collection and authentication, and the queue-management that characterises UAE bureaucracy.

**What PROs typically handle:**
- Licence applications and renewals with DED/freezone authority
- Ministry of Human Resources submissions for mainland employment
- Medical test scheduling and results collection for visa applicants
- Emirates ID biometrics appointments
- Document attestation and translation

**When PRO matters most:** month one, and every renewal cycle. For a single-owner small business, a PRO service costs AED 3,000–8,000 per year and eliminates days of queue management. For growing teams with 5+ staff, an in-house PRO or dedicated outsourced PRO firm is standard.

## Corporate Tax: What Changes for Your Structure in 2026

Corporate tax landed in the UAE at 9% on taxable income above AED 375,000, effective from financial years starting June 2023. Here is how that changes the setup calculation:

**Small Business Relief:** businesses with revenue under AED 3 million may elect for Small Business Relief which effectively treats taxable income as zero, intended to protect startups and micro-businesses. Verify eligibility and election procedure with a UAE tax adviser.

**Freezone qualifying income:** certain freezone businesses with qualifying income (from international customers, within the freezone, or specific permitted activities) retain 0% corporate tax on that income. However, if the same freezone entity earns non-qualifying income (e.g., significant UAE local sales), the entire income may become taxable at 9%. The rules are detailed, do not assume 0% without a tax opinion.

**Holding structure:** investors using a UAE company to hold property should note that income from commercial property is taxable; residential property rental is generally outside scope. See [buying property through UAE company](/guides/buy-property-through-uae-company/) for how corporate ownership interacts with property tax position.

**Practical planning:** most solopreneurs and small professional services businesses operating through a freezone or mainland entity earning under AED 375,000 profit per year are effectively zero-taxed under current rules. Above that threshold, the 9% rate applies to the excess.

## Selecting Business Activity Codes: Common Mistakes

**Activity code specificity matters:** if your licence says "general trading" but you are operating a specific regulated activity (food import, pharmaceutical wholesale, financial advisory), you will face compliance problems during inspections or banking. Invest time in the correct activity code at registration.

**Multi-activity licences:** DED and most freezones allow multiple activity codes on a single licence, often at incremental cost of AED 500–2,000 per additional activity. This is worth doing at setup rather than amending later.

**Regulated activities requiring additional approval:** certain activities need sign-off beyond DED or the freezone authority:

| Activity type | Additional approval |
| --- | --- |
| Financial services / investment advisory | Securities and Commodities Authority (SCA) or CBUAE |
| Insurance | CBUAE |
| Healthcare / clinic | DHA (Dubai Health Authority) |
| Education / training | KHDA or MoE |
| Food and beverage | Dubai Municipality |
| Real estate brokerage | RERA/DLD |

Build the additional approval timeline into your setup plan, regulatory approvals add 4–12 weeks in most cases.

## Real Estate Business in Dubai: Specific Requirements

If you are setting up a real estate business, brokerage, property management, investment advisory, specific layers apply beyond the standard company structure:

**RERA registration:** real estate brokers in Dubai must register with RERA (Real Estate Regulatory Agency) under DLD. Individual brokers need a RERA card (AED 3,000–5,000 including mandatory training). The company must be mainland or Dubai-licensed.

**DLD account:** property transactions require DLD registration and escrow account compliance for developers; brokerage fees are controlled by DLD fee schedules.

**Off-plan sales:** marketing off-plan properties requires developer NOC and broker registration. Unauthorised off-plan marketing is a violation.

**International investors:** many foreign investors set up an entity specifically to hold Dubai property for tax efficiency or inheritance planning. See [best areas to buy property in Dubai](/guides/best-areas-buy-property-dubai/) for investment context and [UAE golden visa property](/guides/uae-golden-visa-property/) for how property ownership connects to residency.

## FAQ

**Can I set up a Dubai company remotely from outside the UAE?**
Yes, the initial incorporation and document submission can often be handled remotely or through a power of attorney. However, your UAE visa and Emirates ID require physical presence in the UAE for biometrics. Most owners visit for 3–5 days to complete the visa process.

**What is the minimum share capital for a Dubai LLC?**
There is no minimum share capital requirement for most LLC activities since the 2021 law amendment. Previously, AED 300,000 was nominal, this no longer applies for most commercial activities. Regulated activities (banking, insurance) still have minimum capital requirements.

**Do I need a local Emirati partner for a mainland company?**
No, for the vast majority of commercial and professional activities since 2021. Certain strategic sectors retain restrictions, check your specific activity code against the current restricted list.

**What is an Ejari and why does my business need it?**
Ejari is Dubai's tenancy registration system. All commercial tenancies in Dubai must be registered through Ejari. DED requires an Ejari-registered office address to issue a mainland licence. The landlord typically provides this through their Ejari account.

**How long does business bank account opening take in Dubai?**
Typically 4–8 weeks for a clean application at UAE banks. Complex ownership, high-risk activity, or certain nationalities may extend to 10–12 weeks. Begin the process immediately after licence issuance, do not wait until your visa is finalised.

**Can my freezone company hire staff who work in Dubai mainland offices?**
Freezone companies can have employees on freezone visas who work within the freezone zone. Working regularly at a Dubai mainland client office raises immigration compliance questions, technically, freezone visa holders need to work within the freezone or remotely. For substantial mainland operations, a mainland entity or dual structure is the cleaner approach. Get legal advice for your specific situation.
