## What Are Realistic Rental Yields in Saudi Arabia in 2026?

Realistic Saudi rental yields typically range from 4% to 6% gross in Riyadh and Jeddah designated zones, or about 3.5% to 4.5% net after management and vacancy. Our underwriting treats giga-project brochure yields as projections, not evidence. Review REGA rules in the [Saudi foreign-buyer property guide](/guides/saudi-arabia-property-foreigners-guide/) before purchase.

Saudi rental yields trail regional leaders Dubai (7 to 9%) and Abu Dhabi (6 to 8%) but reflect different market fundamentals. Saudi offers earlier-stage exposure to Vision 2030 infrastructure spending, while the UAE delivers mature rental markets with deep liquidity and building-level rent data.

| Market comparison | Saudi (designated zones) | UAE (Dubai) | UAE (Abu Dhabi) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gross rental yield range | 4 to 6% | 7 to 9% | 6 to 8% |
| Market maturity | New (Law M/14, Jan 2026) | 20 plus years foreign ownership | 15 plus years |
| Rental data depth | Limited, zone-dependent | Deep (Ejari, RERA data) | Moderate |
| Tenant base | Corporate, Premium Residency | Diverse: residents, tourists | Corporate, long-term residents |
| Primary investment thesis | Capital growth (Vision 2030) | Yield plus moderate growth | Stable yield, gradual growth |
| Liquidity for exit | Developing | High (205K plus transactions per year) | Moderate |

The trade-off: Saudi zones offer lower current yields but potential higher capital appreciation as Vision 2030 projects mature. The UAE offers proven yields with established tenant bases but limited first-mover advantage. Buyers who want both sides of that trade usually split capital, and can compare the two markets line by line in our [Saudi versus UAE property investment analysis](/compare/saudi-vs-uae-property-investment/) or ask the desk for a [side-by-side Gulf yield comparison](/gulf-property-investment-consultation/) using their own budget.

## Rental Yields by Designated Zone: Riyadh, Jeddah and the Giga-Projects

Riyadh designated zones typically lead at 5% to 6% gross, Jeddah coastal stock sits at 4.5% to 5.5%, and giga-projects still have no rental track record. The spread reflects tenant depth: corporate and diplomatic demand in Riyadh versus more seasonal Jeddah tenants. Our analysis treats pre-handover yield quotes as developer projections, not evidence, for foreign buyers.

| Designated zone | Indicative gross yield | Indicative net yield | Asking rent benchmark |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Riyadh residential districts | 5 to 6% | 4 to 5% | SAR 100 to 150 per sqm per year |
| ROSHN communities, Riyadh | 5.5 to 6% | 4.5 to 5% | SAR 100 to 150 per sqm per year |
| Jeddah waterfront and coastal | 4.5 to 5.5% | 3.5 to 4.5% | SAR 70 to 110 per sqm per year |
| NEOM, Red Sea, Qiddiya | No rental history | Not modellable yet | No lease comparables |

### Riyadh Designated Zones: 5 to 6% Gross

Select Riyadh residential districts remain the most underwritable part of the Saudi market for foreign landlords.

- Gross yield range: 5 to 6%
- Tenant profile: diplomatic, corporate assignees, Premium Residency holders
- Rental rates: SAR 100 to 150 per sqm annually for foreign-accessible units
- Market depth: limited supply drives premium pricing

Riyadh is also where district-level evidence is easiest to assemble, because corporate housing budgets are documented and renewal behaviour is visible. Our [Riyadh property investment breakdown](/areas/riyadh-property-investment/) covers which districts sit inside designated zones and how supply is scheduled.

### Jeddah Designated Zones: 4.5 to 5.5% Gross

Jeddah waterfront projects trade lower gross yields for a broader individual tenant base.

- Gross yield range: 4.5 to 5.5%
- Tenant profile: tourism industry, expat professionals, seasonal residents
- Lease terms: 6 to 12 months typical, seasonal variation
- Rental rates: SAR 70 to 110 per sqm annually
- Seasonality: 20 to 30% premium during Hajj and Umrah seasons

That seasonal premium is real but it does not lift the annual number as much as buyers expect, because the peak weeks are short and furnishing plus turnover costs rise with them. The [Jeddah rental yield guide](/guides/jeddah-rental-yield-guide/) works through the annualised math.

### Giga-Projects: No Established Yields

NEOM, the Red Sea Project and Qiddiya sit in a separate category because there is nothing to measure yet.

- Rental history: none (construction phase)
- Projected timelines: 3 to 10 years to occupancy
- Rental assumption risk: high
- Model approach: assume zero rental income during construction, then a conservative 3 to 4% gross after completion

## Does Premium Residency Actually Lift Rental Demand?

Premium Residency typically adds tenant stability rather than a step-change in Saudi yields. Holders on the SAR 4M investment track often want furnished, managed units and can support a 0.5% to 1% rent premium, but the 2026 population remains small. Our research treats the programme as a supporting factor, not the demand engine, for foreign buyers.

Most rental demand still comes from traditional iqama holders and corporate housing programmes. Premium Residency adds stability but does not fundamentally change yield calculations, and no lender or valuer in the market is pricing it as a guaranteed uplift. If residency is your actual objective rather than income, read the eligibility mechanics in the [Saudi Premium Residency property guide](/guides/saudi-premium-residency-property/) first and treat the rent as a partial offset to carry costs.

## Who Rents in Saudi Designated Zones, and on What Lease Terms?

Saudi designated-zone tenants typically split into corporate housing programmes and senior individual expatriates. Corporate leases run 1 to 2 years with company guarantees at SAR 100 to 150 per sqm, while individual leases run 6 to 18 months at SAR 80 to 120 per sqm. Our underwriting prices corporate stock as lower void risk for foreign buyers.

### Corporate Housing Demand

Tenant profile:
- International companies with Saudi operations
- Diplomatic missions
- Consultancy and professional services firms
- Oil and gas sector expatriates

Lease structure:
- Duration: 1 to 2 years
- Payment: SAR, company guarantee
- Furnishing: fully furnished expected
- Deposit: 1 to 2 months rent
- Utilities: often company-covered

Rental rates: SAR 100 to 150 per sqm annually for quality units in designated zones.

### Individual Expatriates

Tenant profile:
- Senior expatriate professionals
- Premium Residency holders
- Diplomats and embassy staff

Lease structure:
- Duration: 6 to 18 months
- Payment: SAR or USD mix
- Furnishing: semi-furnished to furnished
- Deposit: 2 to 3 months rent
- Utilities: tenant responsibility

Rental rates: SAR 80 to 120 per sqm annually, varies significantly by zone and quality.

The practical implication for a foreign landlord is furnishing policy. A unit specified for corporate housing costs more to fit out but leases faster and renews more often; a unit specified for the individual market saves capital expenditure and gives up rate and tenure.

## Management Costs and Net Yields: What to Budget

Saudi management costs typically consume 15% to 20% of gross rent, turning a 5% to 6% gross yield into about 4% to 5% net. Property management runs 8% to 12% and vacancy 2% to 3% in established districts or 10% to 15% in thin zones. We budget the wider vacancy band unless comps prove otherwise.

| Cost line | Indicative range | Why it moves net yield |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Property management | 8 to 12% of gross rent | Fewer licensed managers means less fee competition |
| Maintenance | 2 to 3% of gross rent | Higher on furnished corporate stock |
| Vacancy allowance | 2 to 3% established, 10 to 15% thin zones | Longer marketing periods in new districts |
| Service charges, ROSHN communities | SAR 15 to 25 per sqm per year | Charged on built area, not on rent |
| Service charges, premium giga-project stock | SAR 30 plus per sqm per year | Amenity-heavy schemes carry the most |
| Registration and compliance | Verify current REGA schedule | Zone-dependent, confirm before offer |

Service charges vary significantly by project. ROSHN communities typically charge SAR 15 to 25 per sqm annually, while luxury giga-projects may charge SAR 30 plus per sqm. Always verify the service charge schedule with the developer in writing, because it lands directly on net return and is the single most common gap in Saudi underwriting packs we review. Note that Saudi transaction and registration costs are set by REGA and the relevant Saudi authorities: do not copy Dubai transfer-fee assumptions into a Saudi model, and do not accept a broker's verbal figure without the current official schedule.

## How Deep and Liquid Is the Saudi Rental Market?

Saudi designated zones typically remain thin in 2026, with roughly 5,000 rental units and 45 to 90 days to lease versus Dubai's deeper freehold pool. Fewer units means less rate competition inbound, but longer voids and weaker comps on exit. Our research treats liquidity as a yield risk for foreign buyers, not a title risk.

| Metric | Saudi designated zones | Dubai freehold |
|---|---|---|
| Available rental units | Roughly 5,000 units (estimate) | 150,000 plus units |
| Annual lease transactions | Roughly 2,000 to 3,000 | 80,000 plus |
| Average days to lease | 45 to 90 days | 15 to 30 days |
| Rental management companies | 5 to 10 active | 50 plus established |
| Tenant pool diversity | Limited, corporate-heavy | High diversity |

Practical impact: landlords in Saudi designated zones face longer void periods, higher tenant acquisition costs, and limited management service options. That affects net yields and requires more hands-on investment management than an equivalent Dubai holding, where the numbers in our [Dubai rental yield guide](/guides/dubai-rental-yield-guide/) can be checked building by building.

## Yield Targets by Ticket Size: SAR 2M to 4M and Above

Saudi yield targets typically favour smaller Riyadh tickets: SAR 2M to 4M apartments aim for 5% to 6% gross and 4% to 5% net, while SAR 4M to 8M stock aims for 4.5% to 5.5% gross. We consistently model longer voids at the top end for foreign buyers.

| Ticket size | Target stock | Annual rent | Gross yield | Net yield after costs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SAR 2M to 4M | 2 to 3 bed apartments, ROSHN or select Riyadh zones | SAR 100K to 240K | 5 to 6% | 4 to 5% |
| SAR 4M to 8M plus | Larger apartments, Jeddah coastal or premium Riyadh villas | SAR 200K to 400K | 4.5 to 5.5% | 3.5 to 4.5% |

### SAR 2 to 4 Million Properties (Core Range)

Tenant profile: corporate housing, Premium Residency families. Management complexity: moderate. This is the band where corporate demand and foreign-accessible supply overlap most cleanly, and where a landlord can realistically hold three comparable lease quotes before signing.

### SAR 4 Million Plus Properties (Premium Range)

Tenant profile: senior executives, diplomats, Premium Residency holders. Management complexity: higher, because quality expectations rise and void risk lengthens. Ask for the service charge schedule and the current REGA registration requirements in writing before any deposit, and treat a refusal to provide either as a reason to walk.

## How Construction Timelines Change the Yield Math

Off-plan Saudi yields typically hinge on timing: NEOM and giga-project handovers can span 5 to 10 years, so models should assume zero rent until completion and then a conservative 3% to 4% initial gross. Every year of delay is a year of carry with no offsetting income. Our underwriting separates handover dates from first-lease dates for foreign buyers.

## Which Rental Risks Matter Most in Saudi Arabia?

Saudi rental risks typically centre on regulatory evolution, a thin tenant pool, corporate concentration, construction delay, and SAR currency translation. None are automatic walk-aways, but each requires wider underwriting bands than Dubai. Invest Gulf red-flag reviews stress-test those five lines before foreign buyers commit capital.

1. Regulatory evolution: Law M/14 implementing regulations may affect rental permissions
2. Thin tenant pool: limited expatriate population in designated zones
3. Corporate concentration: heavy reliance on corporate housing creates concentration risk
4. Construction delays: giga-projects may not deliver rental income on projected timelines
5. Currency exposure: SAR exchange rate affects USD-denominated returns

### Risk Mitigation Strategies

Diversification:
- Target established zones such as ROSHN communities before giga-projects
- Mix tenant types: corporate, individual and Premium Residency
- Consider multiple smaller units instead of one large unit

Conservative modelling:
- Use 4% gross yield assumptions for financial planning
- Budget 20% of gross rent for management and void costs
- Model 2 to 3 year rent-up periods for new communities

Professional management:
- Engage Saudi-licensed property management early
- Verify rental permissions with REGA before purchase
- Structure lease agreements under Saudi tenancy law

## Registration, Leases and Paperwork Foreign Landlords Need

Foreign landlords typically need a REGA ownership certificate, zone-dependent municipal rental licences, formal lease registration, and a documented Zakat answer before letting, and the checklist is zone-specific and should be confirmed against current official rules rather than a broker summary. We treat missing paperwork as a deal stop even when gross yields quote 5% to 6%, because a 30 to 90 day registration stall can erase a full quarter of the first-year net income model.

1. Property registration: valid REGA ownership certificate
2. Rental licence: municipal licence for rental activity (zone-dependent)
3. Lease registration: formal lease registration with the relevant authority
4. Tax obligations: verify Zakat applicability for rental income against current official rules

The tax question is the one most often waved away at the offer stage. The [Saudi tax and Zakat guide for expats](/guides/saudi-tax-zakat-expats/) sets out what to ask, and any figure you are given should be confirmed with a licensed Saudi adviser before you rely on it in a net yield model.

### Lease Documentation Standards

Required lease elements:
- Tenant and landlord identification (iqama or passport)
- Property description and REGA registration number
- Rental amount, payment schedule, deposit terms
- Maintenance responsibilities and service charge allocation
- Termination clauses and dispute resolution

**Language requirements:** an Arabic lease is required; an English translation is permissible as reference, but the Arabic text governs legal interpretation.

## When Saudi Makes Sense, and When to Choose the UAE Instead

Saudi typically makes sense when the thesis is capital growth, Premium Residency, or business presence, and you can fund a 2 to 3 year rent-up period. Choose the UAE when the deal only works on current 7% to 9% income. We see most disappointed landlords buy Saudi on a Dubai-style income assumption.

### Consider the UAE Instead If:

- You need net yields at the top of the Gulf range for the investment to be viable
- You want immediate rental income from day one
- You prefer established tenant pools and management infrastructure
- Liquidity and exit flexibility are important
- You want proven rental market data for planning

### Consider Saudi If:

- Vision 2030 capital growth, not current income, is the reason you are buying
- Premium Residency or a corporate footprint in the Kingdom has independent value to you
- You can hold through a slower letting cycle without needing the rent to service debt
- You accept that comparable evidence will be thin for the next few years

## What Could Move Saudi Yields Through 2027?

Saudi gross yields typically stay in the 4% to 6% band through 2027, with mild compression as supply arrives and some net-yield improvement as management matures, while tenant growth, infrastructure completion, and REGA clarity remain the three drivers we track for foreign buyers. Our outlook keeps modelled asks in that band rather than extrapolating brochure peaks above 6%, because new designated-zone supply and 8% to 12% management costs still set the net floor near 3.5% to 4.5% for most Riyadh and Jeddah tickets.

**Tenant base growth:** Premium Residency programme expansion and Vision 2030 employment creation should grow expatriate tenant populations, supporting rental demand.

**Infrastructure completion:** airport expansions, entertainment districts, and giga-project phase openings may improve rental appeal and occupancy rates.

**Regulatory clarity:** REGA implementing regulations will provide clearer rental frameworks, potentially reducing the regulatory risk premium built into current yield expectations.

**Model expectation:** 4 to 6% gross yields in 2026 may moderate to 4.5 to 5.5% as supply increases, but net yields may improve as management costs fall with market maturation.

## Pre-Purchase Rental Yield Checklist

A pre-purchase Saudi rental checklist typically requires REGA letting confirmation, three lease comps, written service charges, and a 10% to 15% vacancy assumption before any deposit. Treat undocumented items as open risks, not formalities. We use this checklist as the minimum gate.

- Confirm the zone designation allows foreign rental, verified with REGA
- Verify that completed projects in the zone have active rental markets
- Obtain comparable rental rates from at least three similar units
- Check the service charge schedule and calculate the impact on net yield
- Identify two or three potential property management companies
- Model conservative vacancy (10 to 15%) and management costs (15 to 20%)
- Confirm lease registration requirements for the specific zone
- Assess corporate tenant demand in the immediate area
- Verify Premium Residency holder concentration as a potential tenant base
- Plan for a 2 to 3 year market development period in newer zones

## Yield Targets by Buyer Scenario

Saudi buyer scenarios typically justify different yield targets: Premium Residency-led purchases near 4.5% to 5.5% gross, Riyadh corporate units near 5% to 6%, Jeddah coastal near 4.5% to 5.5%, and giga-projects as unproven until lease history exists. Mixing those frames is how otherwise sound assets disappoint when a NEOM timeline is underwritten like a ROSHN corporate let. We use the table below as the buyer-scenario checklist for foreign buyers before any deposit leaves the account.

| Buyer scenario | Sensible yield target | What to verify before transfer |
|---|---|---|
| Premium Residency-led purchase | 4.5 to 5.5% gross | Eligibility, family use, resale rules |
| Riyadh corporate-rental unit | 5.0 to 6.0% gross | Employer anchors, lease comparables, management fees |
| Jeddah coastal apartment | 4.5 to 5.5% gross | Seasonality, furnishing costs, tenant mix |

This is why a Saudi shortlist should not copy a Dubai yield filter. Dubai can be screened quickly by building-level rent data. Saudi requires a slower check: zone designation, project delivery status, lease demand, and whether the buyer's residency or capital-growth objective justifies lower current income. ## What Invest Gulf Sees in Saudi Rental Underwriting

Invest Gulf Saudi enquiries in 2026 typically show a cost-disclosure gap, not a rent-level gap: a unit marketed at 6% gross often rebuilds at 4.5% to 5% after management of 8% to 12% and realistic vacancy. Districts with 45 to 90 days to lease compress further. Foreign buyers should treat brochure peaks as starting points only.

The documents that decide Saudi rental underwriting are unglamorous but measurable. A written service charge schedule, three building-level lease comparables rather than a district average, REGA confirmation that the zone permits foreign letting, and a clear Zakat treatment answer typically separate bankable files from stalled ones. Files that arrive with those four items settle quickly; files that start after an offer is signed are where deals stall for 30 to 90 days or longer. Where a seller will not put the service charge schedule in writing, our underwriting treats that as a walk-away signal rather than a negotiation point.

The second recurring issue is timeline optimism on off-plan stock. Buyers model a handover date, not a letting date, and the two can be a year apart even on a project that delivers on time. Furnishing, REGA registration, marketing and the first tenant search all sit after handover, so a first-year yield built on twelve months of rent overstates income before anything goes wrong.
