## What are UAE fixed deposit rates in 2026?

Indicative UAE fixed deposit rates sit between 3.5% and 5.25% per annum for standard AED tenures in mid-2026, with promotional 3-month products and large-balance placements reaching around 5.50%. Rates follow the CBUAE Base Rate, which stood at approximately 4.40% in mid-2026 after the Federal Reserve's measured easing cycle, because the dirham's peg to the dollar means UAE deposit pricing tracks US policy rather than local conditions.

That link is the whole story of why UAE deposits became interesting. Before 2022 the base rate sat near zero and cash earned nothing. When it rose above 5% in lockstep with Fed hikes, deposit rates followed, and expats holding AED or USD could suddenly earn a meaningful, near-risk-free return on money that was simply waiting: ahead of a property purchase, during a relocation, or as the cash allocation in a broader portfolio.

Three structural features make UAE deposits work for expat savers regardless of the exact rate on offer. The dirham's peg to the dollar at 3.6725 removes currency risk for anyone earning or thinking in dollars. Interest income is not taxed locally. And CBUAE supervision of licensed banks provides a regulated floor under the institutions you are lending to.

## UAE fixed deposit rates by bank and tenure

RAKBANK and Mashreq consistently publish the highest retail AED rates, while Emirates NBD, FAB and ADCB advertise lower headline numbers and compensate elsewhere. The figures below are indicative for the standard retail tier as of mid-2026.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Emirates NBD | 3.50% | 3.75% | 4.00% | 4.25% | 10,000 |
| FAB | 3.40% | 3.70% | 4.10% | 4.35% | 25,000 |
| ADCB | 3.25% | 3.60% | 3.90% | 4.20% | 5,000 |
| RAKBANK | 3.75% | 4.10% | 4.50% | 4.85% | 5,000 |

| CBD (Commercial Bank of Dubai) | 3.50% | 3.80% | 4.20% | 4.60% | 10,000 |

Rates are indicative for the standard retail tier. Verify with each bank at placement.

The spread between the top and bottom of that 12-month column is around 65 basis points, which on AED 500,000 is a difference of roughly AED 3,250 over a year. That is the size of the prize for shopping around, and it is why the ranking matters more than brand familiarity.

Why RAKBANK and Mashreq lead is not accidental. Both have more concentrated loan books than the largest banks and actively compete for retail deposit funding, so paying up for liabilities is a deliberate strategy rather than a promotion. Emirates NBD and FAB advertise lower rates but compensate with deeper product ecosystems, private banking rate sweeteners on high balances, and bundled benefits such as waived fees and priority service for customers who hold a broader relationship. If a single FD is all you want from the bank, the rate is the only thing that matters. If you also need a mortgage, a corporate account or wealth services, the relationship banks may be worth the basis points. ## Islamic deposit profit rates: Emirates Islamic, DIB and ADIB

Islamic term deposits in the UAE are usually structured as Wakala, where you appoint the bank as your investment agent to deploy capital on your behalf against a declared expected profit rate. The rate is expected rather than guaranteed, though major UAE Islamic banks have consistently met or exceeded declared Wakala rates on retail products. Murabaha, a cost-plus commodity sale where the mark-up represents your return, appears more often in wholesale and private banking tiers.

| Islamic bank | Product | Expected profit rate (12m AED) | Min. amount |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Emirates Islamic | Wakala FD | 4.20% | AED 10,000 |
| FAB Islamic | Wakala Deposit | 4.35% | AED 25,000 |
| ADCB Islamic | Wakala | 4.15% | AED 5,000 |
| Dubai Islamic Bank (DIB) | Wakala | 4.50% | AED 5,000 |
| Abu Dhabi Islamic Bank (ADIB) | Term Deposit (Wakala) | 4.60% | AED 5,000 |

Expected profit rates are indicative. Wakala profits are not guaranteed but have historically been delivered as declared by major UAE Islamic banks.

Dubai Islamic Bank and ADIB are pure Islamic banks with no conventional window, and they tend to publish slightly higher profit rates than the Islamic windows of conventional banks, partly to attract depositors who might otherwise take a conventional product. In practice the top Islamic rates land close to the mid-range of conventional AED deposits, so choosing Sharia-compliant structure does not usually mean accepting a materially lower return.

## Which tenure gives the best UAE deposit rate?

Twelve months offers the best rate efficiency across every bank in the tables above, and the increment from 6 to 12 months is larger than the increment from 1 to 3. The catch is confidence: you need to be reasonably sure you will not need the capital, because breaking a deposit early is where the arithmetic turns against you.

Beyond 12 months, UAE banks will quote 2-year and 3-year deposits on request, but published rates do not always rise proportionally with term. That said, in a declining-rate environment after Fed easing, locking a longer term can be worthwhile if you can secure a rate comparable to the 12-month level, because you are protecting against reinvestment at a lower rate later.

## What happens if you break a UAE fixed deposit early?

Terms vary by bank and product, and there is no single market standard. On some UAE deposits early withdrawal means forfeiting accrued interest entirely; others apply a redemption penalty or pay a reduced rate for the period actually held. Confirm the break clause in writing before you place funds, especially if the money is earmarked for a property deposit or relocation costs where timing can move.

This is the reason laddering exists rather than a theoretical nicety. If your capital has a possible call on it inside the term, splitting across tenures costs a little yield and removes the risk of surrendering a year of interest at the worst possible moment.

## Rate-lock strategy for property buyers

If you are accumulating a down payment and watching [Dubai mortgage rates](/guides/dubai-mortgage-rates-2026/), a laddered structure keeps most of the yield without locking everything:

1. Place 50% of saved capital in a 12-month deposit at the best available rate.
2. Place 30% in a 6-month deposit for mid-cycle liquidity.
3. Keep 20% in a high-yield savings account for immediate access.

This preserves earning power if your property timeline shifts, which it usually does. Transaction costs on a Dubai purchase, the DLD fee, agency commission and mortgage registration, run around 4% to 7% of the purchase price, and having that liquid means you never break a deposit to cover a fee that was always going to arrive. ## Can expats open a fixed deposit in the UAE?

Yes, and for UAE residents with a current account and Emirates ID the full rate menu is available at every bank in this guide. Most banks require the deposit to be funded from a linked current account, since cash paid directly into a deposit is not standard practice, so [opening a current account](/guides/open-bank-account-dubai/) is the prerequisite step rather than an optional one.

### Pre-residency and visit visa holders

Some banks accommodate customers still inside the residency application window:

- RAKBANK and Mashreq have the most flexible pre-Emirates-ID policies for account opening, which can include savings accounts usable for deposit placement.
- Mashreq Neo, the digital account, can be opened with a passport and entry permit, with deposit access typically unlocking within 1 to 3 days of Emirates ID confirmation.
- Emirates NBD, FAB and ADCB require Emirates ID for standard retail deposits. Visit visa holders are directed to non-resident or investment accounts on different terms.

### Non-residents living outside the UAE

Several UAE banks offer non-resident deposits to people who own UAE property or hold business interests here, generally at higher minimums:

- FAB Non-Resident Account: property owners and business owners can open with a passport, proof of income and source-of-funds documentation. Deposit access is available with minimums from around AED 50,000.
- Emirates NBD Non-Resident: available for selected nationalities on a relationship-managed basis, with a minimum around AED 250,000.
- Mashreq International: overseas nationals can apply online with enhanced KYC, with deposit access on account activation.

Expect enhanced due diligence on any non-resident application: 3 to 6 months of home-country bank statements, proof of employment or business ownership, and source of funds documentation for transfers above around AED 100,000. The full non-resident process, including what banks decline and why, is set out in the [non-resident UAE bank account guide](/guides/open-bank-account-non-resident-uae/).

## How to open a UAE fixed deposit step by step

The process is short once the account exists. Where people lose time is documentation and the funding transfer, not the deposit itself.

1. Open or confirm a current account at the bank offering the rate you want. Rates are not portable between banks, so the account has to be where the rate is.
2. Confirm the current published rate for your exact amount, tenure and currency. Advertised rates are tiered, and the number you saw may apply to a higher balance band than yours.
3. Fund the current account and let the transfer clear. Large inbound transfers may trigger source-of-funds questions, which is normal and faster to answer with documents ready.
4. Place the deposit through the app, online banking or the branch, specifying tenure, currency and maturity instruction.
5. Choose the maturity instruction deliberately: automatic renewal at the then-prevailing rate, renewal of principal only with interest paid out, or full payout to the current account. Automatic renewal at a lower future rate is the most common way savers quietly lose yield.
6. Keep the deposit confirmation and note the maturity date and the break clause somewhere you will actually see it.

## Is fixed deposit interest taxed for UAE expats?

The UAE levies no personal income tax on deposit interest earned locally, so nothing is withheld at source. Your home country is a separate question, and for many expats it is the question that decides the net return.

UK residents are taxed on worldwide income. US citizens must report under FATCA regardless of where they live. German residents owe Abgeltungssteuer on investment income. And UAE banks report account holder data under the Common Reporting Standard, assuming a tax information exchange agreement is in place with your country, so treat UAE deposit interest as visible to your home tax authority rather than invisible. Whether you remain tax-resident at home while living in the UAE is the pivotal question, and it usually turns on days present and ties rather than on where the bank account sits. The [double tax treaty guide for the UAE](/guides/double-tax-treaty-uae/) covers the framework, and a tax adviser in your home country should confirm your position.

## Fixed deposit or Dubai property: which fits your horizon?

Deposits and property answer different questions, and the honest comparison is about horizon rather than headline yield. A 12-month deposit at 4% to 5% gives you liquidity, capital certainty and a dirham-pegged return with no management effort. Dubai investment property targets 6% to 9% gross rental yield with potential capital appreciation of 5% to 15% per year in growth cycles, but carries illiquidity, transaction costs of 4% to 7%, ongoing management and genuine market risk.

| Factor | UAE fixed deposit | Dubai investment property |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Indicative return | 3.5% to 5.25% p.a. | 6% to 9% gross rental yield |
| Capital movement | Principal fixed | Can rise or fall |
| Entry costs | None material | Around 4% to 7% of price |
| Liquidity | Days, subject to break terms | Months to sell |
| Effort | None after placement | Tenants, service charges, management |
| Sensible horizon | Under 2 years | Five years and longer |

For expats accumulating a down payment, a deposit held for 6 to 18 months while completing research and mortgage pre-approval is a sensible capital preservation strategy rather than a missed opportunity. For long-term wealth building, property's combination of yield and appreciation has typically outperformed a deposit over a five-year horizon, particularly in supply-constrained prime districts, once leverage and rental income are both accounted for. The trade-off is that the property return is not guaranteed and the deposit return effectively is. If you are weighing yield against capital growth specifically, [Dubai capital appreciation versus rental yield](/guides/dubai-capital-appreciation-vs-yield/) sets out how the two behave differently across cycles, and the [Dubai property investment guide](/guides/dubai-property-investment-guide/) covers the full fee stack.

## Five ways to improve your UAE deposit return

1. Compare beyond your existing bank. The spread across the banks in this guide is around 65 basis points on a 12-month AED deposit, and loyalty is not rewarded with rate.

2. Time placements around CBUAE rate announcements. The CBUAE meets eight times a year, mirroring the FOMC. If a cut is expected, locking a 12-month deposit before the meeting protects your rate. If a hike is expected, staying short and rolling at the new level is better.

3. Look across the Gulf if you have the banking relationships. Deposit rates and minimums differ in Qatar, Bahrain and Oman, and occasionally another GCC market offers a structurally better short-term rate for a similar dollar-pegged risk profile.

4. Watch promotional booster rates. Banks run 3-month promotional deposits at 50 to 100 basis points above the standard menu, usually announced to existing customers through app notifications. These are genuine improvements rather than teaser-then-reset products, because a UAE deposit rate is fixed for the stated term.

5. Consider splitting across two banks. The UAE has no formal deposit guarantee scheme equivalent to the UK FSCS or US FDIC; protection comes through CBUAE systemic supervision rather than an explicit per-depositor limit. Splitting AED 500,000 across two Tier-1 banks is prudent concentration management, even though the practical risk of a major UAE bank failure is considered very low by international standards.

## What Invest Gulf hears from savers comparing deposits and property

Most people who reach us from a deposit rate search are not choosing between saving and investing. They are parking money with a purchase already in mind, and the question they actually want answered is how long the cash needs to sit there. In our files the recurring mismatch is a 12-month deposit placed against a property timeline that turns out to be four or five months, which means either breaking the deposit or delaying the purchase.

The second pattern is a yield comparison made on the wrong basis. A deposit rate is net: 4.85% is what you receive. A quoted gross rental yield is not, and once service charges, vacancy allowance and management fees are deducted, the net figure on a Dubai apartment can land two to three percentage points below the marketing number. That does not make property the weaker option over a five-year horizon, but it does mean the correct comparison is a net rental figure against the deposit rate, not a gross one. Ask for the service charge schedule for the specific building before you accept any yield claim.

The third pattern is savers who never move at all, rolling a deposit automatically for years at whatever rate applies on each renewal date. Automatic renewal is convenient and it is also where yield quietly leaks. Put the maturity date in your calendar, compare the market that week, and decide again. ## Bottom line on UAE fixed deposits in 2026

UAE fixed deposits do a specific job well: preserving capital at 3.5% to 5.25% with no local tax and no currency risk for dollar earners. The administrative barrier is low if you already hold a UAE account, the tax position is clean for UAE-resident expats with home-country rules still applying, and liquidity is manageable through laddered placements.

RAKBANK and Mashreq are the rate leaders for standard retail AED deposits, ADIB and DIB lead the Islamic table, and the relationship banks are worth their lower headline rates only if you need more from them than a deposit. Twelve months is the efficient tenure. Confirm the break clause, set the maturity instruction deliberately, and check the rate again at renewal.

Property is the complementary tool rather than the competing one. It targets higher total returns but demands a multi-year horizon, transaction capital and active management. Most Gulf expat plans use both: deposits for the money that has a job in the next 18 months, property for the money that does not.
