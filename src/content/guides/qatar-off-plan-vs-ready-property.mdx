faq:
  - question: "Is Qatar off-plan property safe for foreign investors?"
    answer: "Qatar has an escrow law (Law No. 6 of 2014) requiring developers to hold buyer payments in project-specific escrow accounts, only released on construction milestones. This provides basic buyer protection similar to Dubai's RERA escrow system. However, Qatar's developer regulatory oversight is less developed than Dubai's, with fewer secondary enforcement mechanisms. Verify that your specific project is registered under the escrow law and that the developer has completed at least one prior project before committing."
  - question: "Do Qatar off-plan properties offer capital appreciation like Dubai?"
    answer: "Qatar's off-plan capital appreciation track record is more limited than Dubai's, due to smaller market size and fewer price comparable transactions. Lusail City off-plan purchases from 2018–2021 have delivered modest appreciation of 10–20% by 2026 handover, not the 30–50% seen in some Dubai off-plan launches. Qatar off-plan is primarily a construction discount play (5–15% below ready pricing) rather than speculative capital gains. Confirm your exit before buying — the Qatar secondary market is thinner than Dubai's."
  - question: "How does Qatar's escrow system compare to Dubai's RERA?"
    answer: "Qatar's escrow law (2014) requires developer escrow accounts per project, milestone-based fund release, and registration of off-plan contracts. Dubai's RERA system adds: mandatory DLD registration of sale and purchase agreements (No-Objection Certificate framework), online OQOOD system for off-plan tracking, and stronger secondary market enforcement. Qatar's system is functional but less digitised and less transparent — buyers cannot easily track escrow status online. Require your lawyer to verify escrow account registration directly with the developer's bank."
  - question: "Can I get a mortgage for Qatar off-plan property?"
    answer: "Qatar mortgage market for foreign buyers is limited. Local banks (QNB, Commercial Bank of Qatar, Doha Bank) offer mortgages to Qatar residents but rarely to non-residents on off-plan purchases. LTV ratios for foreign buyers are typically 50–60% even on ready properties. Most foreign off-plan purchases in Qatar are cash transactions. If financing is essential to your investment model, Qatar off-plan is structurally difficult — consider ready property with better mortgage prospects or look at Dubai's more developed foreign mortgage market."
  - question: "Which Qatar developers have the strongest track record for off-plan delivery?"
    answer: "Qatar's largest and most reliable developers by off-plan track record: Barwa Real Estate (government-linked, consistent delivery), United Development Company (UDC, developer of The Pearl — 20+ years of freehold delivery), Qatari Diar (sovereign developer, landmark projects), Aamal Company (listed, diversified developer). Lusail-focused developers with partial track records: Msheireb Properties (Mushayrib district, delivered phase 1), Al Rayyan Tourism and Investment Company. Avoid small unlisted Lusail developers without a delivered project — verify registration and escrow before payment."
  - question: "What are the typical payment plans for Qatar off-plan property?"
    answer: "Qatar off-plan payment plans: typically 30–40% on booking and during construction milestones, 60–70% on handover. Some developers offer post-handover payment plans (10–20% over 2–3 years after completion), but these are less common than in Dubai. Construction milestone payments in Qatar are usually tied to % of structure completed (foundation, floors, topping out, fit-out, handover). Always verify milestone payments align with actual escrow release conditions and not just developer invoicing dates."
  - question: "What happens if a Qatar developer fails to deliver off-plan property?"
    answer: "Under Qatar's escrow law, funds in the project escrow account should be protected and refundable if the project is cancelled. In practice, enforcement has been tested only in limited cases. Contractual dispute resolution is through Qatari courts (Arabic language proceedings) or arbitration if specified in the SPA. Keep all original payment receipts, escrow account confirmation, and SPA in a safe location. Qatar does not have an equivalent to Dubai's RERA dispute resolution centre — consider mandatory arbitration clauses when negotiating your SPA."

### Required Documents Before Off-Plan Payment

1. Escrow account confirmation: Written letter from the project-specific bank account (not developer's general account) confirming your payments will be held in escrow.
2. Ministry of Municipality approval: The off-plan project must be registered and have development approval.
3. Freehold zone eligibility: Confirm the plot is in a designated foreign freehold zone: not all Lusail parcels are.
4. Developer track record documents: Request copies of handover certificates from their most recent delivered project.
5. SPA with milestone payment schedule: Sale and Purchase Agreement signed before any funds transfer, specifying escrow bank, milestone percentages, and delay penalties.
6. Insurance on off-plan contract: Some developers offer purchaser protection insurance: verify coverage for developer insolvency.

### Transaction cost snapshot

Purchasing property in Qatar incurs transfer fees of 2–4 % of the sale price, registration charges of 500–5,000 AED, agency commission of 1–2 % (buyer side, where applicable), and conveyancing/NOC fees of 500–5,000 AED. Mortgage arrangement fees add 0.25–1 % of the loan amount. Total acquisition costs typically sit at 7–9 % above the headline price when financing is involved, or 4–6 % for cash purchases.
