# Dubai Weekend Guide for Expats 2026: Friday Brunch, Beaches & Beyond


<TldrBlock text={"Dubai's weekend is Saturday-Sunday. Friday is the Islamic holy day, quieter mornings, full activity from noon. The Friday brunch is the city's signature social event. Weekends offer everything from luxury beach clubs to free desert hikes."} />

## How the UAE weekend works: the 2022 shift

Until January 2022, the UAE weekend was Friday–Saturday. The government switched to Saturday–Sunday to align with global financial markets and improve international business connectivity.

| Sector | Current weekend |
| --- | --- |
| Federal government | Saturday–Sunday |
| Most private sector | Saturday–Sunday |
| Schools (KHDA-regulated) | Saturday–Sunday |
| Retail / hospitality | Open 7 days |
| Religious observance | Friday (Islamic holy day) |
| Some traditional businesses | May still observe partial Friday closure |

**Friday prayers:** The most visible Friday impact. From approximately 11:30 to 13:30, Friday prayers occur at mosques across Dubai. Some small businesses, banks, and government offices close during this window. Malls, hotels, and restaurants stay open.

**Public holidays:** The UAE observes Islamic calendar holidays (Eid Al Fitr, Eid Al Adha, Islamic New Year, Prophet's Birthday) plus national secular ones (UAE National Day on 2–3 December, Commemoration Day on 30 November). Dates shift annually with the Islamic calendar, check UAE government channels for confirmed dates each year.

## Friday morning: the quiet window

Friday mornings (before noon) in Dubai have a particular atmosphere: quieter roads, fewer people in public spaces, a Sunday-morning feel in a Muslim context.

| Time window | Atmosphere |
| --- | --- |
| 07:00–10:00 | Peaceful, beaches and parks quiet |
| 10:00–11:30 | Pre-prayer; still relaxed |
| 11:30–13:30 | Prayer window; some areas quieter |
| 14:00 onwards | Full social activity resumes |

**What to do Friday morning:**

- Walk or run along Jumeirah Beach Walk, fewer people than Saturday
- Kite Beach or JBR early swim before crowds
- Farmers markets (Ripe Market at various locations)
- Friday morning yoga and outdoor fitness events (community organised)
- Shopping at mall before brunch crowd arrives

## Friday brunch: the Dubai institution

Friday brunch is unique to Dubai's expat and hospitality culture. It runs from approximately 12:30 to 16:00, combining social dining with a leisure afternoon.

### How it works

Most hotel restaurants offer a dedicated brunch format:

- Set menu or buffet with themed food stations
- Drinks packages from soft to fully open bar
- 2.5–4 hour experience; entertainment sometimes included (DJ, live music)
- Smart casual to smart dress code at most venues

### Cost tiers (2026 estimates)

| Package type | Cost per person AED | Includes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Soft (non-alcoholic) | 200–350 | Food, soft drinks, juices, mocktails |
| House package | 350–550 | Food, house wine, beer, spirits |
| Premium package | 500–750 | Food, premium alcohol labels |
| Luxury brunch | 700–1,500 | Fine dining, Champagne, premium spirits |

**Popular brunch venues:**

- Atlantis The Palm, multiple restaurant brunches
- JW Marriott Marquis, Graze brunch, highly regarded
- Palazzo Versace, opulent; book far ahead
- The Ivy, DIFC; fashion and media crowd
- Jumeirah Beach Hotel, family-friendly options
- Address Boulevard, Downtown views

**Booking:** Essential 1–3 weeks ahead for popular venues. Same-day walk-ins rare on Fridays. Use the venue's website, OpenTable, or The Fork.

**Family brunches:** Many venues offer reduced children's pricing (AED 0 free under 5, AED 75–200 for children 5–12). Look for keywords "family brunch" or "kids eat free."

## Evening and nightlife culture

Dubai's nightlife is hotel-centric, with a lively but contained club scene.

| Venue type | Opening hours | Character |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hotel rooftop bars | 18:00–02:00 | After-work; views; cocktails |
| Licensed restaurants | 12:00–01:00 | Dinner-focused |
| Nightclubs | 22:00–04:00 | Hotel-based; smart dress |
| Live music venues | 20:00–02:00 | DIFC and JBR cluster |
| Shisha cafes (non-alcoholic) | 18:00–02:00 | Cultural; widely popular |

**DIFC restaurant strip:** The Gate Village and surrounding area in DIFC has the highest density of licensed fine dining in Dubai. Popular with finance and corporate professionals; busiest Thursday and Friday evenings.

**JBR / Marina Walk:** More casual; outdoor dining, beach club bars, waterfront atmosphere. Busy Friday and Saturday nights.

**Clubs:** Dubai's club scene is smaller than London or New York. Major venues include White Dubai, Soho Garden, Drai's at Meydan. Smart dress code enforced; table reservations needed for premium access.

## Making the most of the first weekend in Dubai

Week one is disorienting, unfamiliar area, no established routines, no community yet. A structured first weekend builds orientation quickly:

**Day 1 (first Saturday):**
- Morning walk through your neighbourhood
- Visit the nearest supermarket and mall to understand layout
- Lunch at a local restaurant (no need for reservations)
- Late afternoon: explore by metro, one stop each direction from the nearest station

**Day 2 (first Sunday):**
- Visit a public beach early (before 10:00)
- Dubai Creek and Al Fahidi area, free; historical; orienting
- Dinner at a restaurant in a different area to test taxi and metro logistics

**First Friday:**
- Sleep in; quieter morning is the gift
- Visit the Ripe Market or any local farmers market if operating near you
- Book and attend a brunch with colleagues if available
- Evening: JBR Walk or DIFC evening stroll

## FAQ

**What time do brunch venues open on Fridays?**
Typically 12:30–13:00. Some venues open as early as 12:00. Bookings are time-slotted, arrive within 15 minutes of your reservation time.

**Can children attend Friday brunches in Dubai?**
Many venues offer family-friendly brunches with children's menus and pricing. Specify at booking if you have children. Avoid adult-only venues which typically enforce age minimums (21 or 25).

**Is it appropriate to visit mosques on weekends?**
The Dubai Museum of Islamic Civilisation and Jumeirah Mosque (Grand Mosque visits via Sheikh Mohammed Centre for Cultural Understanding) welcome non-Muslim visitors. Dress modestly. Visits must be timed around prayer times.

**What is the dress code for Dubai restaurants and beach clubs?**
Smart casual for most restaurants; smart for DIFC venues. Beach clubs: swimwear at the beach, cover up in restaurant areas. Nightclubs: no shorts, no sandals at most venues for men. Women's dress code generally more flexible.

**Are there free events in Dubai on weekends?**
Yes, festivals at Global Village (seasonal entry fee), Dubai Creek Park events, Al Fahidi Cultural Festival, cultural programming at Alserkal Avenue in Al Quoz, and community fitness events at major parks. Check Dubai Calendar app.

**How do I meet people and build a social network in Dubai?**
Community sports leagues (football, cricket, netball), fitness classes, Meetup.com groups, LinkedIn expat community events, building WhatsApp groups, and company social events. Friday brunches are a core social bonding experience for established expats.

**What happens on UAE National Day (2–3 December) weekend?**
Large public celebrations across the city, fireworks, light shows, Zabeel Park events, free entertainment. Roads become congested. most expats travel for the long weekend. Hotels book out months ahead. Restaurants operate but expect extended waits.

**Is Global Village worth visiting for expat families?**
Yes, highly recommended as a family weekend venue between October and April. Budget AED 18 entry plus AED 200–400 for food and entertainment per person. Visit Thursday or Friday evening for atmosphere; Saturday afternoon for quieter entry. See [Dubai relocation guide](/guides/dubai-relocation-guide/) for the fuller family lifestyle picture.
