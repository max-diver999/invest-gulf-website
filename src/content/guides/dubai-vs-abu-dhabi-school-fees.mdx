## How do KHDA and ADEK regulate school fees differently?

Both regulators typically tie the fee increase a school may request to its inspection rating, which is why the Dubai and Abu Dhabi gap widens over a school career. Dubai private schools sit under KHDA; Abu Dhabi sits under ADEK with indicative caps near 0 to 5.8%. Invest Gulf reads compounding across years, not entry fees alone.

Abu Dhabi school fees typically run 10 to 20% lower than Dubai at comparable British and IB tiers, worth roughly AED 8,000 to 20,000 per child per year for many mid-track families. A British-curriculum household with two children at ADEK Very Good schools might pay AED 130,000 to 160,000 combined, against AED 150,000 to 190,000 at KHDA Very Good equivalents in Dubai. Across a ten-year school career that gap can compound to AED 200,000 to 300,000 before extras. Invest Gulf still weighs work location first, because a 90 to 120 minute daily inter-emirate commute erodes the tuition saving in fuel, vehicle wear, and time. Premium IB diploma years above AED 95,000 converge in both cities, so city choice there is school-specific rather than emirate-wide.

Regulator checklist we use before any lease:

- Confirm current KHDA or ADEK rating and the permitted increase band
- Verify the year's circular rather than last year's PDF
- Model Foundation Stage through Year 13, not only the entry quote
- Re-check ADEK vs KHDA compounding every inspection cycle

More detail sits in [KHDA school ratings explained](/guides/khda-school-ratings-explained/) and [ADEK school ratings in Abu Dhabi](/guides/adek-school-ratings-abu-dhabi/).

## What do British curriculum schools cost by year group?

British curriculum schools typically form the largest sector in both cities, and year group matters as much as rating for the AED bill. Foundation Stage entry is 20 to 30% cheaper than secondary years in both fee tables. Invest Gulf warns that the FS1 quote is not the Year 9 fee.

### Dubai British fees (indicative, AED per year)

| Stage | KHDA Good | KHDA Very Good | KHDA Outstanding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FS1-FS2 (Nursery/KG) | 30,000-45,000 | 40,000-60,000 | 55,000-75,000 |
| Year 1-6 (Primary) | 40,000-60,000 | 55,000-75,000 | 65,000-90,000 |
| Year 7-11 (Secondary) | 50,000-70,000 | 65,000-85,000 | 75,000-110,000 |
| Year 12-13 (Sixth Form) | 55,000-75,000 | 70,000-90,000 | 80,000-115,000 |

### Abu Dhabi British fees (indicative, AED per year)

| Stage | ADEK Good | ADEK Very Good | ADEK Outstanding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FS1-FS2 (Nursery/KG) | 22,000-35,000 | 32,000-50,000 | 45,000-65,000 |
| Year 1-6 (Primary) | 30,000-50,000 | 45,000-65,000 | 55,000-80,000 |
| Year 7-11 (Secondary) | 40,000-60,000 | 55,000-75,000 | 65,000-90,000 |
| Year 12-13 (Sixth Form) | 45,000-65,000 | 60,000-80,000 | 70,000-95,000 |

The key reading from these tables is that the spread within each city is larger than the gap between cities. A family choosing a KHDA Good school in Dubai pays less than a family choosing an ADEK Outstanding school in Abu Dhabi. Rating tier matters more than city selection at the extremes, which is worth knowing before anyone relocates emirate purely to save on fees.

In practice, Year 7 through Year 13 across seven years, a KHDA Outstanding track in Dubai comes to roughly AED 560,000 to 770,000 in aggregate before extras. An ADEK Outstanding track in Abu Dhabi runs approximately AED 455,000 to 665,000. A gap of AED 100,000 or more across the school cycle is material for families building a property position at the same time. School-by-school detail for Dubai sits in [British schools in Dubai](/guides/british-schools-dubai/) and the wider market in the [Dubai international schools guide](/guides/dubai-international-schools-guide/).

## What do IB programmes cost, and what does the diploma add?

IB programmes typically sit at the premium end in both cities, and diploma years are where Dubai and Abu Dhabi fee bands converge. Dubai DP years often cost AED 75,000 to 125,000; Abu Dhabi tracks 10 to 20% lower until the top tier. We compare diploma pass rates before headline FS1 fees.

### Dubai IB fees (indicative, AED per year)

| Programme | Entry range | Mid range | Premium range |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PYP Primary | 50,000-65,000 | 65,000-80,000 | 80,000-95,000 |
| MYP Secondary | 60,000-80,000 | 80,000-95,000 | 90,000-110,000 |
| DP Years 12-13 | 75,000-95,000 | 90,000-110,000 | 100,000-125,000 |

Abu Dhabi IB schools generally track 10 to 20% below Dubai equivalents at comparable ADEK and KHDA tiers, in line with the wider pattern, but the premium diploma years narrow that gap considerably: top schools in both cities reach AED 95,000 to 120,000. Confirm actual numbers against each school's current published fee schedule rather than a city-level average.

One line families miss: IB Diploma examination fees are charged separately by the International Baccalaureate Organisation and are not subject to KHDA or ADEK caps. Those run roughly AED 5,000 to 9,000 per candidate on top of tuition in both cities, which means an all-in diploma year at a premium Abu Dhabi school can match or exceed a Dubai competitor once examinations are counted.

For families targeting global university entry across the US, UK, continental Europe and beyond, the diploma remains the most universally recognised pre-university qualification available from the UAE. Schools with strong diploma mean scores, above 34 points, use that as justification for the top fee tier, and in admissions terms they are usually right to.

## What do American curriculum schools cost, and where is the choice?

American curriculum schools typically run a K-12 structure with Cognia or NEASC accreditation pathways in both emirates. Dubai mid-tier high-school bands often cost AED 60,000 to 82,000 per year. We map Dubai first when families need wider American school choice near freehold housing areas.

| KG (K to Grade 2) | 38,000-55,000 | 58,000-78,000 |

- Dubai dedicated American campuses: roughly 20 to 25
- Abu Dhabi dedicated American campuses: roughly 10 to 15
- Premium high school Dubai: AED 82,000 to 100,000
- Choice constraint in Abu Dhabi usually beats a small tuition delta

## Which curricula offer genuine value?

Indian CBSE is typically the value tier in both cities, at roughly one third the cost of premium British or IB options. Dubai CBSE bands often run AED 15,000 to 40,000 per year; Abu Dhabi AED 14,000 to 28,000. We treat CBSE as the curriculum that frees the largest housing budget.

### CBSE fee comparison (indicative, AED per year)

| Stage | Dubai KHDA-rated | Abu Dhabi ADEK-rated |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary (Grades 1-5) | 15,000-28,000 | 14,000-22,000 |
| Middle (Grades 6-8) | 22,000-33,000 | 18,000-27,000 |
| Secondary (Grades 9-10) | 26,000-38,000 | 22,000-32,000 |
| Senior Secondary (Grades 11-12) | 28,000-40,000 | 24,000-35,000 |

Indian ICSE and state board schools operate in both cities at similar price points. The French curriculum, AEFE affiliated, is available at a handful of schools, combining AEFE-regulated tuition with local market adjustments at roughly AED 35,000 to 65,000 per year in Dubai and AED 30,000 to 55,000 in Abu Dhabi. German curriculum schools are concentrated in Dubai, so families committed to the Abitur path will find Dubai more practical.

Pakistani, Bangladeshi, Philippine and Sri Lankan curriculum schools cluster in the areas with the largest communities of each nationality: Bur Dubai and Deira in Dubai, Mussafah and Mohammed Bin Zayed City in Abu Dhabi. These typically run AED 8,000 to 20,000 per year. They are not competing for Outstanding ratings, and they serve an important community function at a price point that makes the UAE viable for families the premium sector prices out. Curriculum-by-curriculum Dubai pricing is broken out further in the [Dubai school fees by curriculum guide](/guides/dubai-school-fees-by-curriculum/).

## What are the hidden costs on top of tuition?

Hidden school costs typically add AED 10,000 to 20,000 per child per year above tuition, and closer to AED 25,000 at premium schools with full activities. We consistently model treat the advertised tuition line as incomplete until buses, exams, and uniforms are stacked in the same budget.

Hidden school costs in Dubai and Abu Dhabi typically add AED 10,000 to 20,000 per child per year above tuition, and closer to AED 25,000 at premium schools with full activities. Registration often costs AED 2,000 to 8,000 once, buses AED 7,000 to 14,000, uniforms AED 1,500 to 4,000, and secondary exam fees AED 3,000 to 9,000. Our analysts model treat transport as the largest variable once the curriculum track is fixed, because neither city is meaningfully cheaper on uniforms or exam boards. CBSE families with light extracurricular spend can keep add-ons under AED 8,000, which is why value curricula change the housing budget conversation more than a small tuition tweak between the two emirates for most households.

| Cost item | Typical AED/year |
| --- | --- |
| Registration (one-time) | 2,000-8,000 |
| School bus | 7,000-14,000 |
| Uniforms and PE | 1,500-4,000 |
| Textbooks | 1,000-3,000 |
| Extracurriculars | 1,500-5,000 |
| Exam fees (secondary) | 3,000-9,000 |
| Technology levy | 2,000-5,000 / 2-3 years |

Transport is the largest variable; see [Dubai school bus and transport fees](/guides/dubai-school-bus-transport-fees/).

## So which city is actually cheaper?

Abu Dhabi typically delivers a 10 to 20% school-fee saving across most curriculum tracks, worth roughly AED 8,000 to 20,000 per child per year. We still rank work location above that gap, because a 90 to 120 minute inter-emirate commute erodes the cash advantage fast.

City-cost checklist:

- Two children ADEK Very Good: about AED 130,000 to 160,000 combined
- Two children KHDA Very Good: about AED 150,000 to 190,000 combined
- Ten-year compounding gap: roughly AED 200,000 to 300,000 before extras
- Peak Dubai to Abu Dhabi school run: 90 to 120 minutes each way

Abu Dhabi rent can run 10 to 15% lower in comparable categories, which widens the total advantage when the job is also in Abu Dhabi. Full monthly picture: [Dubai vs Abu Dhabi cost of living](/guides/abu-dhabi-cost-of-living/) and [Abu Dhabi cost of living](/guides/abu-dhabi-cost-of-living/).

## How do school fees map onto a property budget?

School fees and housing typically share one monthly budget, so two children at KHDA Very Good schools can remove AED 150,000 to 190,000 per year before rent or a mortgage. We sequence fees first, then commute, then the lease or purchase decision in either emirate.

Property-budget checklist:

1. Fix the school shortlist and current fee PDFs
2. Map peak-hour home to school to office minutes
3. Only then sign a lease or commit to a purchase

Families who reverse the order often spend 6 months on a waiting list; see [Dubai school waiting lists](/guides/dubai-school-waiting-lists/).

Where a purchase is part of the plan, the arithmetic changes again, because a mortgage payment behaves differently from rent in a fee-heavy household budget. ## Which scenario matches your family?

Family scenarios typically hinge on work location first, then curriculum continuity, then the AED fee gap between emirates. We keep mid-track British or IB moves inside the same city unless a natural transition year arrives, because a 10 to 20% fee saving rarely offsets disrupted exam years.

Scenario checklist we use:

- Dubai role, two mid-track British children: stay in Dubai; commute cost beats fee saving
- Abu Dhabi role, FS1 entry: ADEK Very Good primary often AED 55,000 to 75,000, about AED 12,000 to 18,000 under Dubai peers
- IB diploma teenager: compare schools above AED 95,000 on pass rates, not FS1 fees
- Value-focused family: CBSE under AED 40,000 frees the largest housing budget

## Red flags before you enrol

Red flags before enrolment typically include unwritten waitlist positions, fee PDFs older than 12 months, and bus routes untested against your actual lease address in either emirate. We also check whether the last KHDA or ADEK rating was downgraded, because that changes quality and the permitted annual fee increase.

Insider tip: Ask for the current fee schedule PDF and the last inspection report in the same email thread; schools that stall on either document usually stall again when the annual increase letter arrives mid-contract.

Red flag checklist:

- Waitlist position not confirmed in writing.
- Fee schedule PDF older than twelve months, which usually means the current increase has not been disclosed to you.
- Bus route not mapped against your actual lease address.
- KHDA or ADEK rating downgraded at the last inspection cycle, which affects both quality and the permitted increase.
- Registration fee described as refundable without that appearing in the written terms.
- A curriculum that cannot continue to the exit qualification you want at the same school.

## How to choose between the two cities

City choice typically starts with work location, then curriculum continuity, then the housing budget that survives committed school fees of AED 40,000 to 190,000 per year for many families. We test the home-school-office triangle in peak traffic for 45 to 90 minutes before any lease is signed.

Decision checklist:

- Work city overrides a 10 to 20% fee saving
- Mid-track IB or British moves stay local unless Year 6 to 7 or FS transition
- Confirm ratings and fee PDFs in both cities before housing
- Finalise lease or purchase only after school and commute are locked
