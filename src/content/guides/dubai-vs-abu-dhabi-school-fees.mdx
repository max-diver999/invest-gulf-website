## How do KHDA and ADEK regulate school fees differently?

Both regulators tie the fee increase a school may request to its inspection rating, which is why the gap between the two cities widens over a school career rather than at the point of entry. Dubai private schools sit under KHDA, the Knowledge and Human Development Authority, which inspects annually and rates schools from Outstanding down to Unsatisfactory. That rating sets the maximum annual increase the school can apply. Parents choosing an Outstanding school get the inspection scores, and they also accept faster compounding.

Abu Dhabi runs the same logic through ADEK, the Abu Dhabi Department of Education and Knowledge, which replaced the older ADEC structure and now covers all private schools in the emirate including Al Ain and Al Dhafra. Ratings run Outstanding, Very Good, Good, Acceptable, Weak and Very Weak, and fee increases need ADEK pre-approval against a band table linked to the inspection outcome. Indicative published caps sit in a band of roughly 0 to 5.8% depending on rating, so verify the current year's circular with KHDA and ADEK rather than assuming last year's number.

The practical result: a family enrolling in Foundation Stage at a Good-rated school and staying through Year 13 typically finishes 15 to 25% lower in Abu Dhabi than on an equivalent KHDA Good track in Dubai, largely because of slower compounding under the ADEK bands. At the premium end, an Outstanding British or IB full continuum, the gap narrows, but Abu Dhabi still typically trails Dubai by 8 to 12% in cumulative spend by secondary. More detail on the rating systems sits in [KHDA school ratings explained](/guides/khda-school-ratings-explained/) and [ADEK school ratings in Abu Dhabi](/guides/adek-school-ratings-abu-dhabi/).

## What do British curriculum schools cost by year group?

British is the largest sector in both cities, and the year group matters as much as the rating: Foundation Stage entry is typically 20 to 30% cheaper than secondary years, so the fee you are quoted at admission is not the fee you will be paying in Year 9.

### Dubai British fees (indicative, AED per year)

| Stage | KHDA Good | KHDA Very Good | KHDA Outstanding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FS1-FS2 (Nursery/KG) | 30,000-45,000 | 40,000-60,000 | 55,000-75,000 |
| Year 1-6 (Primary) | 40,000-60,000 | 55,000-75,000 | 65,000-90,000 |
| Year 7-11 (Secondary) | 50,000-70,000 | 65,000-85,000 | 75,000-110,000 |
| Year 12-13 (Sixth Form) | 55,000-75,000 | 70,000-90,000 | 80,000-115,000 |

### Abu Dhabi British fees (indicative, AED per year)

| Stage | ADEK Good | ADEK Very Good | ADEK Outstanding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FS1-FS2 (Nursery/KG) | 22,000-35,000 | 32,000-50,000 | 45,000-65,000 |
| Year 1-6 (Primary) | 30,000-50,000 | 45,000-65,000 | 55,000-80,000 |
| Year 7-11 (Secondary) | 40,000-60,000 | 55,000-75,000 | 65,000-90,000 |
| Year 12-13 (Sixth Form) | 45,000-65,000 | 60,000-80,000 | 70,000-95,000 |

The key reading from these tables is that the spread within each city is larger than the gap between cities. A family choosing a KHDA Good school in Dubai pays less than a family choosing an ADEK Outstanding school in Abu Dhabi. Rating tier matters more than city selection at the extremes, which is worth knowing before anyone relocates emirate purely to save on fees.

On total secondary spend, Year 7 through Year 13 across seven years, a KHDA Outstanding track in Dubai comes to roughly AED 560,000 to 770,000 in aggregate before extras. An ADEK Outstanding track in Abu Dhabi runs approximately AED 455,000 to 665,000. A gap of AED 100,000 or more across the school cycle is material for families building a property position at the same time. School-by-school detail for Dubai sits in [British schools in Dubai](/guides/british-schools-dubai/) and the wider market in the [Dubai international schools guide](/guides/dubai-international-schools-guide/).

## What do IB programmes cost, and what does the diploma add?

IB sits at the premium end in both cities, and the diploma years are where the two markets converge rather than diverge.

### Dubai IB fees (indicative, AED per year)

| Programme | Entry range | Mid range | Premium range |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PYP Primary | 50,000-65,000 | 65,000-80,000 | 80,000-95,000 |
| MYP Secondary | 60,000-80,000 | 80,000-95,000 | 90,000-110,000 |
| DP Years 12-13 | 75,000-95,000 | 90,000-110,000 | 100,000-125,000 |

Abu Dhabi IB schools generally track 10 to 20% below Dubai equivalents at comparable ADEK and KHDA tiers, in line with the wider pattern, but the premium diploma years narrow that gap considerably: top schools in both cities reach AED 95,000 to 120,000. Confirm actual numbers against each school's current published fee schedule rather than a city-level average.

One line families miss: IB Diploma examination fees are charged separately by the International Baccalaureate Organisation and are not subject to KHDA or ADEK caps. Those run roughly AED 5,000 to 9,000 per candidate on top of tuition in both cities, which means an all-in diploma year at a premium Abu Dhabi school can match or exceed a Dubai competitor once examinations are counted.

For families targeting global university entry across the US, UK, continental Europe and beyond, the diploma remains the most universally recognised pre-university qualification available from the UAE. Schools with strong diploma mean scores, above 34 points, use that as justification for the top fee tier, and in admissions terms they are usually right to.

## What do American curriculum schools cost, and where is the choice?

American schools run a K-12 structure aligned to US grade levels, following either direct US accreditation through bodies such as Cognia or NEASC, or a locally approved American track. Advanced Placement courses in Grades 11 and 12 do the job A-Levels do for UK-bound students.

### Dubai American fees (indicative, AED per year)

| Grade band | Mid-tier | Premium tier |
| --- | --- | --- |
| KG (K to Grade 2) | 38,000-55,000 | 58,000-78,000 |
| Elementary (Grades 3-5) | 45,000-65,000 | 65,000-85,000 |
| Middle School (Grades 6-8) | 55,000-75,000 | 75,000-92,000 |
| High School (Grades 9-12) | 60,000-82,000 | 82,000-100,000 |

Abu Dhabi has fewer dedicated American schools than Dubai, roughly 10 to 15 against 20 to 25, so the constraint there is choice rather than price. Families committed to the American track and weighing the two cities often find Dubai's larger market gives a better school-to-neighbourhood fit for the specific area they want to live in, which matters more day to day than a few thousand dirhams of tuition. The Dubai market is covered school by school in [American schools in Dubai](/guides/american-schools-dubai/), and the Abu Dhabi picture in the [Abu Dhabi international schools guide](/guides/abu-dhabi-international-schools-guide/).

## Which curricula offer genuine value?

Indian CBSE is the value tier in both cities, at roughly one third the cost of premium British or IB options and with a strong academic record in mathematics and the sciences. Central Board of Secondary Education schools follow the Indian national curriculum and serve the large South Asian professional community in both emirates.

### CBSE fee comparison (indicative, AED per year)

| Stage | Dubai KHDA-rated | Abu Dhabi ADEK-rated |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary (Grades 1-5) | 15,000-28,000 | 14,000-22,000 |
| Middle (Grades 6-8) | 22,000-33,000 | 18,000-27,000 |
| Secondary (Grades 9-10) | 26,000-38,000 | 22,000-32,000 |
| Senior Secondary (Grades 11-12) | 28,000-40,000 | 24,000-35,000 |

Indian ICSE and state board schools operate in both cities at similar price points. The French curriculum, AEFE affiliated, is available at a handful of schools, combining AEFE-regulated tuition with local market adjustments at roughly AED 35,000 to 65,000 per year in Dubai and AED 30,000 to 55,000 in Abu Dhabi. German curriculum schools are concentrated in Dubai, so families committed to the Abitur path will find Dubai more practical.

Pakistani, Bangladeshi, Philippine and Sri Lankan curriculum schools cluster in the areas with the largest communities of each nationality: Bur Dubai and Deira in Dubai, Mussafah and Mohammed Bin Zayed City in Abu Dhabi. These typically run AED 8,000 to 20,000 per year. They are not competing for Outstanding ratings, and they serve an important community function at a price point that makes the UAE viable for families the premium sector prices out. Curriculum-by-curriculum Dubai pricing is broken out further in the [Dubai school fees by curriculum guide](/guides/dubai-school-fees-by-curriculum/).

## What are the hidden costs on top of tuition?

Budget AED 10,000 to 20,000 per child per year above tuition, and closer to AED 25,000 at a premium school with full extracurricular participation. Tuition is the number schools advertise; the total is what families actually pay.

| Cost item | Typical range (AED/year) | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Registration / admission fee | 2,000-8,000 (one-time per enrolment) | Some schools waive for siblings |
| School bus (one way or return) | 7,000-14,000 | Return service adds roughly 40% over one-way |
| Uniforms and PE kit | 1,500-4,000 | Higher at premium schools with branded suppliers |
| Textbooks and stationery | 1,000-3,000 | Lower at schools using digital resources |
| Extracurricular activities | 1,500-5,000 | Sports clubs, arts, after-school programmes |
| School trips (domestic and abroad) | 1,000-4,000 | International trips at secondary can exceed AED 5,000 |
| IGCSE / A-Level / IB DP exam fees | 3,000-9,000 | Secondary only, capped by exam boards rather than KHDA or ADEK |
| Technology levy | 2,000-5,000 every 2 to 3 years | Device and platform charges, varies widely |
| After-school care | 5,000-9,000 | Relevant where pick-up clashes with working hours |

At a value-tier CBSE school with minimal activity spend the add-on can stay under AED 8,000. Neither city is meaningfully cheaper than the other on these line items, which is the point: the difference between Dubai and Abu Dhabi shows up in base tuition, not in the supplementary charges. Transport is the single biggest variable, and the [Dubai school bus and transport fees guide](/guides/dubai-school-bus-transport-fees/) explains how route distance changes the number.

## So which city is actually cheaper?

Abu Dhabi delivers a real saving of 10 to 20% on school fees across most curriculum tracks, worth roughly AED 8,000 to 20,000 per child per year, but that saving narrows at the premium tier and has to be weighed against a different property market and a different job market.

Put concretely: a British-curriculum family with two school-age children at ADEK Very Good schools in Abu Dhabi might pay AED 130,000 to 160,000 per year in combined tuition, against AED 150,000 to 190,000 at KHDA Very Good equivalents in Dubai. An annual saving of AED 20,000 to 30,000 compounds to AED 200,000 to 300,000 across a ten-year school career.

That is a meaningful figure, and it still needs context. Abu Dhabi typically offers lower rent for comparable housing, on the order of 10 to 15% in many categories, which pushes the total further in its favour. But it has fewer employment centres and a different lifestyle profile, and a family working primarily in Dubai while schooling in Abu Dhabi faces a 90 to 120 minute daily commute that consumes the saving in fuel, vehicle depreciation and time. The full monthly picture across rent, transport, utilities and groceries sits in the [Dubai vs Abu Dhabi cost of living comparison](/guides/dubai-vs-abu-dhabi-cost-living/) and the [Abu Dhabi cost of living guide](/guides/abu-dhabi-cost-of-living/).

## How do school fees map onto a property budget?

Families almost never make this decision on fees alone, because school fees and housing costs are drawn from the same monthly figure. Committing to two children at KHDA Very Good schools takes AED 150,000 to 190,000 per year off the table before rent or a mortgage is considered, and that constraint decides districts more often than district preference decides schools.

The sequence that works is fees first, then commute, then housing. Fix the school shortlist, map the real peak-hour triangle from home to school to office, and only then sign a lease or commit to a purchase. Families who reverse it sign in an area where their preferred school has no availability and spend six months on a waiting list or in a compromise school, a pattern documented in the [Dubai school waiting lists guide](/guides/dubai-school-waiting-lists/).

Where a purchase is part of the plan, the arithmetic changes again, because a mortgage payment behaves differently from rent in a fee-heavy household budget. The [school fees vs property budget guide](/guides/school-fees-vs-property-budget-dubai/) works through the trade-off, and our advisers can run the same exercise against live stock in either emirate through a [Gulf property consultation](/gulf-property-investment-consultation/), whether the answer ends up being [Dubai](/invest-dubai-property/) or [Abu Dhabi](/abu-dhabi-property-investment/).

## Which scenario matches your family?

**Dubai-based role, two children mid-track in British curriculum.** Stay in Dubai. The fee saving from moving emirate is real but smaller than the cost of a daily inter-emirate commute, and mid-track curriculum disruption carries its own price.

**Abu Dhabi role, one child entering FS1.** Abu Dhabi British schools at the ADEK Very Good tier run AED 55,000 to 75,000 per year in primary, roughly AED 12,000 to 18,000 less than comparable Dubai schools. Pair that with Khalifa City or Saadiyat housing and the fee advantage survives intact, with no commute friction to erode it.

**IB diploma track, university-bound teenager.** Premium diploma years converge above AED 95,000 in both cities. Compare specific schools, for example GEMS World Academy in Dubai against Cranleigh Abu Dhabi, on diploma pass rates and university placement rather than on headline FS1 fees. A cheaper primary track that cannot carry through to the diploma is a false economy.

**Value-focused family, flexible on curriculum.** CBSE in either city keeps the education line under AED 40,000 per child even at senior secondary, which frees a substantially larger housing budget. This is the choice that changes the property conversation most.

## Red flags before you enrol

- Waitlist position not confirmed in writing.
- Fee schedule PDF older than twelve months, which usually means the current increase has not been disclosed to you.
- Bus route not mapped against your actual lease address.
- KHDA or ADEK rating downgraded at the last inspection cycle, which affects both quality and the permitted increase.
- Registration fee described as refundable without that appearing in the written terms.
- A curriculum that cannot continue to the exit qualification you want at the same school.

## How to choose between the two cities

Work location overrides almost everything else. After that, in order: the curriculum you are on or want, the school shortlist with current ratings in both cities, the housing budget that survives once fees are committed, and the commute triangle tested in real peak-hour conditions rather than on a map.

Continuity matters more than city for children already mid-track, particularly on IB or British programmes. Moving a child from IGCSE Year 9 in one city to IB MYP Year 4 in another creates academic disruption that no fee saving offsets. If your child is mid-track, the cheaper city only makes sense at a natural transition point, Foundation Stage to Primary or Year 6 to Year 7.

The families who get this right fix the school shortlist first, map the commute second, and finalise the lease or purchase only after both are confirmed.
