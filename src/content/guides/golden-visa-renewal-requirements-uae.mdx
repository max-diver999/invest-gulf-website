A property Golden Visa is granted on a photograph of the land register taken on the day you applied. Renewal takes a second photograph. Almost everything written about the ten-year visa describes the first one, which is why holders arrive at year ten assuming the question has already been answered.

It has not. The AED 2 million test is re-run against what the register shows on the filing date, and three ordinary events between grant and renewal change that picture: selling and replacing a unit, taking a handover, and holding property through a market that repriced downwards.

## What is actually re-tested at renewal?

Two things: the value the register records against your name today, and the continuity of the holding behind it. The grant-day figure has no standing at renewal, and neither does the price you paid.

That distinction is why holders with unchanged circumstances renew without noticing there was a test at all. Someone who bought one apartment for AED 2.3 million in 2016, kept it, and never refinanced presents a register entry that answers both questions on sight. The files that fail belong to people who were more active, not less careful: they traded up, took handover of an off-plan unit, or restructured a portfolio across family members. Each of those is a normal thing for an owner to do over a decade, and each one leaves a mark on the register that the second photograph can catch.

The grant-side rules, thresholds and eligible zones sit on the [Golden Visa property guide](/guides/uae-golden-visa-property/). What follows is only what changes when the same test is applied to a portfolio that has had ten years to move.

## How does a sell-and-replace break a file that looks fine?

Because a sale and a purchase register on different days, and the gap between them is a period in which the register shows nothing qualifying in your name.

Owners think of an upgrade as one event. The register treats it as two, in an order set by the counterparties rather than by you. Transfer out completes on the day the buyer's funds clear at the trustee; registration in completes when your own purchase reaches the same stage, which depends on a seller, a developer or a lender you do not control.

| Sequence | What the register shows on filing day | Renewal position |
|---|---|---|
| Buy first, then sell | Both units, then the new one alone | Qualifies throughout |
| Same-day back to back | One qualifying holding, uninterrupted | Qualifies, if both complete |
| Sell, then buy 6 weeks later | Nothing for 6 weeks | Fails if filed in the gap |
| Sell, buy delayed by a chain | Nothing, for an unknown period | Fails, and the end date is not yours |

Buying before selling requires carrying two positions at once, which is a financing question rather than a visa one. Where that is not available, the workable answer is to keep the renewal filing date away from the transition entirely. The mechanics of the disposal side, including the NOC and the trustee sequence, are on the [selling guide](/guides/selling-property-dubai-guide/).

## Why is handover a risk rather than a milestone?

An off-plan unit qualifies on its Oqood registration, and it keeps qualifying right up to the moment that entry is retired. At handover the Oqood record is replaced by a title deed, and the replacement is a process with a beginning and an end rather than an instant.

The exposure is narrow and entirely self-inflicted when it happens. A holder who takes handover in the same month they file for renewal has arranged for the register to be mid-sentence about their only qualifying asset on the day somebody official reads it. Nothing has gone wrong with the property, the developer or the visa; two of the owner's own dates were allowed to land on top of each other. Move either one. The Oqood mechanics and what the certificate does at grant are covered on the [off-plan Golden Visa page](/guides/golden-visa-off-plan-property-uae/).

## What does a falling market do to a marginal portfolio?

It moves the registered value, and the registered value is the number under test. A holding bought at AED 2.05 million in a strong quarter and revalued downward sits below the line without the owner selling anything or doing anything wrong.

This is the one failure mode with no timing fix, and the arithmetic is worth stating plainly. A portfolio bought at exactly AED 2.0 million has zero headroom: any downward revision fails it. One bought at AED 2.3 million absorbs a 13% fall before the threshold is in question. The cost of that headroom at purchase was AED 300,000 of capital, which is roughly what a decade of renewal anxiety is worth to most holders, and it is why the [best-value AED 2M units page](/guides/aed-2m-golden-visa-best-value-units/) argues for buying above the line rather than at it. For an owner already at the line, the options are to add a second qualifying unit or to establish the current registered figure early enough that adding one is still possible.

## Does a mortgage change the number or only the paperwork?

Only the paperwork, and this is the point holders most often get backwards. The test reads registered value, not equity, so a unit registered at AED 2.4 million with AED 1.6 million outstanding is a AED 2.4 million holding for renewal purposes.

What the mortgage adds is a dependency on a third party inside your timeline. Lenders are asked for a no-objection letter, and the letter arrives when the bank's process delivers it. A file that is complete in every other respect waits on that document, which is an argument for requesting it first rather than last. The full financing position, including what changed for mortgaged applicants in 2024, is on the [mortgage Golden Visa guide](/guides/golden-visa-mortgage-property-uae/) and dated in the [rule changes log](/guides/uae-golden-visa-property-rule-changes-log/).

## What does the preparation sequence look like?

Six months, and the order matters more than the length, because only the first step has a remedy that takes time to execute.

1. Establish the registered value the authority will read. Not the purchase price, not a portal estimate, not a bank valuation.
2. If it sits within AED 200,000 of the threshold, decide now whether to add a qualifying unit. This is the only step that cannot be done quickly.
3. Map your own transactions against the filing window: any sale, purchase or handover mid-registration moves the filing date, not the transaction.
4. Request the bank no-objection letter where a mortgage exists.
5. Assemble documents. This is the part everyone starts with and it is the part that never fails.
6. File on a date when nothing of yours is in motion at the register.

Aggregation across several units, including the rules on mixing emirates and instruments, is covered on the [multiple properties guide](/guides/golden-visa-multiple-properties-uae/).

## What does a change of name on the register do?

It moves the holding out of the applicant's file, whatever the family arrangement behind it. The register records a person, and the test reads that person's holdings rather than a household's.

Three ordinary events do this without anyone intending a visa consequence. A unit transferred to a spouse for estate-planning reasons leaves the sponsor's register entry and joins theirs, so the sponsor files on what remains. A joint purchase where each party is recorded with a share splits one AED 2.4 million entry into two of AED 1.2 million, neither of which reaches the line alone. And an inheritance mid-decade can leave title unregistered for months while an estate is administered, which is a break in continuity rather than a change of value.

None of these is irreversible, and all three are cheap to fix while the register can still be corrected. What none of them survives is being discovered on the filing date, because the remedy in every case is another registration, and another registration takes weeks that a pending application does not have.

## Who carries the consequence when a renewal fails?

The household, all at once. Dependant visas exist under the sponsor's status rather than beside it, so a lapse does not affect one person and leave the others in place.

That asymmetry is the argument against treating a marginal file as something to test. An applicant with AED 2.05 million of registered value and a sale pending is not making a decision about their own paperwork; they are deciding on behalf of a spouse, children in a school year, and often a mortgage that was underwritten on residency. The costs of fixing a thin position in advance are known and modest. The cost of discovering the position inside a rejected application is a household schedule that nobody planned, at a moment when the fixes that were available six months earlier have all expired.
