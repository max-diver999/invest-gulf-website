### Citable factual block 1

An Oman salary account normally depends on an active residence card, passport, employer salary certificate, and the receiving bank's current onboarding rules. The Central Bank of Oman regulates licensed banks, but each bank and employer can set its own payroll process, document format, and product eligibility. Bank Muscat, NBO, and HSBC Oman should therefore be compared against the same employer letter and the same remittance corridor, not against an old forum recommendation. The Omani rial has long been pegged at about OMR 0.385 per US dollar, yet that currency stability does not fix the spread on transfers to INR, PHP, or other currencies. Source check: confirm requirements directly with the selected bank and retain its dated written response before moving salary or savings. Review the evidence every 12 months, and record the date, source, unit, applicant, and assumptions in the decision file.

### Citable factual block 2

The cost of remitting from Oman is the exchange rate delivered to the beneficiary plus any transfer fee, not the fee line alone. A practical comparison sends the same OMR 500 amount through two licensed bank or exchange-house channels on the same day, then records the recipient amount, stated fee, and transfer time. That method avoids treating a promotional rate as a permanent saving. International transfers can also require correct beneficiary IBAN details and may involve intermediary charges, so the sending receipt is part of the audit trail. **Insider tip:** test a small transfer before making a school-fee or property-payment transfer. Source check: use the Central Bank of Oman licensed-institution list and each provider's live rate confirmation, because product pricing and eligibility can change without notice. Review the evidence every 12 months, and record the date, source, unit, applicant, and assumptions in the decision file.
