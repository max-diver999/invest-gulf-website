Dubai runs on international business hours, but five daily prayers, Friday Jumu'ah, and Ramadan still shape meeting culture, commute timing, and office etiquette. Non-Muslim expats need awareness and respect, not participation.

<TldrBlock text={"Five daily prayers shape the rhythm of Dubai's day. Friday midday prayer has the most visible business impact. Ramadan reduces working hours by law. Non-Muslim expats need awareness, not participation; respectful acknowledgement is enough."} />

## What are the five daily prayer times in Dubai?

Prayer times shift with sunrise and sunset every day. Use Muslim Pro, Athan, or the UAE Islamic Affairs app for precise schedules.

| Prayer | Arabic name | Approximate time | Significance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dawn prayer | Fajr | 04:30-05:30 | Before sunrise; earliest |
| Midday prayer | Dhuhr | 12:00-13:00 | Solar noon |
| Afternoon prayer | Asr | 15:30-16:30 | Mid-afternoon |
| Sunset prayer | Maghrib | 18:00-19:30 | Varies by season |
| Night prayer | Isha | 20:00-21:00 | After full dark |

In June, sunrise is before 06:00 and sunset after 19:00; in December, sunrise is after 07:00 and sunset near 17:30. The adhan (call to prayer) from mosques is part of Dubai's soundscape; it is not directed at non-Muslims and requires no response.

## How do prayer times affect daily business in Dubai?

Most international private firms do not stop the workday for each prayer; Muslim colleagues use prayer rooms or natural breaks. Avoid mandatory all-hands at Dhuhr (12:00-13:00) or during Friday 11:30-13:30 when Muslim teammates participate in Jumu'ah. Commercial buildings above certain size must provide prayer rooms; know where yours is.

## How does Friday Jumu'ah affect meetings and traffic?

Jumu'ah is the weekly midday congregational prayer. Mosques fill roughly 11:30-13:30; roads near large mosques congest. Schedule client meetings before 11:00 or after 14:30 on Fridays when Muslim participation is required.

## What changes in Dubai during Ramadan?

Business tempo slows; many teams prefer morning collaboration or post-iftar evening meetings. Lunch meetings with fasting colleagues are sensitive. Client gifts of dates or neutral hampers are appreciated; avoid alcohol gifts during Ramadan.

## What are Ramadan working hours in Dubai?

UAE Labour Law cuts private-sector working hours by two hours daily during Ramadan for all employees, Muslim and non-Muslim. Many offices run about 09:00-15:00; government hours may shorten further. Confirm HR policy each year; some international firms blend remote flexibility with the legal reduction.

| Sector | Typical Ramadan schedule |
| --- | --- |
| Private sector | 6 hours/day instead of 8; commonly 09:00-15:00 |
| Federal government | As announced; typically 09:00-14:30 |
| Dubai government | Often similar to federal |
| Banks | Reduced branch hours; online continues |
| Retail and hospitality | Late-night shifts increase |

Energy often dips late afternoon during fasting; schedule demanding work in the morning during Ramadan.

## What Ramadan etiquette should non-Muslims follow at work?

Eat and drink discreetly away from open offices. Iftar invitations are important relationship signals; company iftars and hotel buffets (often AED 150-400 per person) are normal cultural experiences. Many firms defer major decisions until after Eid Al Fitr; plan deal timelines accordingly.

## How do Islamic holidays shift on the Gregorian calendar?

Hijri holidays move about 11 days earlier each Gregorian year. Plan annual leave with both calendars.

| Holiday | Hijri basis | Gregorian shift |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ramadan and Eid Al Fitr | 9th month | ~11 days earlier annually |
| Eid Al Adha | 10th of Dhul Hijjah | ~11 days earlier annually |
| Islamic New Year | 1 Muharram | Shifts annually |
| Prophet's Birthday | 12 Rabi Al Awwal | Shifts annually |
| National Day | 2-3 December | Fixed Gregorian date |

## How should expats handle Eid Al Fitr and Eid Al Adha?

Book Eid travel early; flights and hotels sell out quickly. Combined with National Day, expect roughly 10-12 public holidays per year from Islamic observances plus fixed December holidays.

## What mosque etiquette should visitors follow?

Non-Muslims may visit designated mosques when modest dress rules are respected: abaya and headscarf for women, long trousers for men, shoes removed, voices low, no photography during prayer.

## How does Dubai compare with other Gulf cities on work culture?

Dubai is the most internationally calibrated GCC business hub; prayer rhythm remains, but multinational office norms are well established. See [Dubai culture etiquette for expats](/guides/dubai-culture-etiquette-expats/) and [Dubai weekend culture](/guides/dubai-weekend-culture/) for adjacent context.

## What should you confirm before relocating for work?

Ask HR about Ramadan hours, prayer room location, and Friday meeting norms. Model school runs and commute around Friday midday traffic if you drive past major mosque districts.

## Do expats need to pray or fast?

No. Non-Muslims are not required to participate in Islamic worship or fasting. Respectful behaviour in shared spaces is expected, especially during Ramadan and around prayer rooms.

## How should you schedule government visits around prayer and Ramadan?

Visit RERA, DLD, or courts early on Fridays; service quality drops after midday prayer congestion. During Ramadan, arrive at opening time; afternoon queues lengthen when hours shorten.

| Visit type | Best window | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DLD / trustee | Weekday mornings | Friday 11:30-13:30 |
| Friday government | Before 11:00 | Midday prayer peak |
| Ramadan services | Opening hour | Late afternoon |

## Can you eat at your desk during Ramadan?

You may eat in private areas, but avoid visible food and drink in open offices where colleagues fast. Use pantries or closed meeting rooms; see [Dubai culture etiquette for expats](/guides/dubai-culture-etiquette-expats/) for broader Ramadan guidance.

## What gifts work for iftar or Eid clients?

Dates, chocolates, and neutral sweet boxes work well. Avoid alcohol during Ramadan even if the host is non-Muslim. Corporate hampers without alcohol are safe defaults.

## Does the Saturday-Sunday weekend change Friday observance?

Friday remains the Islamic holy day even though many sectors treat Friday as a working day. Jumu'ah prayer timing is unchanged; plan meetings accordingly alongside [Dubai weekend culture](/guides/dubai-weekend-culture/).

## How do prayer apps help new arrivals?

Muslim Pro, Athan, and the UAE Islamic Affairs app publish daily times adjusted for Dubai coordinates. They are useful for scheduling even if you do not pray, because they explain when colleagues may step away.

## How do workplace prayer rooms work in Dubai offices?

Commercial buildings above certain size must provide prayer facilities. Muslim colleagues may leave briefly for Dhuhr or Asr; treat it like a short break. Non-Muslims should not use prayer rooms for calls or meals; keep them clean and quiet out of respect.

## How does Ramadan affect client entertainment and sound?

Night markets, iftar tents, and late retail hours increase evening activity while daytime meetings slow. Outdoor venues may adjust music and lighting during Ramadan; check venue policies before booking client entertainment. Noise curfews in residential towers can tighten; read building notices each year.

## What should recruiters tell candidates about Islamic work culture?

Set expectations on Friday prayer windows, Ramadan hours, and dress norms in client-facing roles. Candidates from non-Muslim majority countries often appreciate a one-page HR summary before relocation, it reduces first-month friction more than generic cultural sensitivity slides.

## How should property investors respect prayer culture during site visits?

Schedule snagging and handover walks outside Friday 11:30-13:30 when Muslim contractors and inspectors may be unavailable. During Ramadan, morning site visits run smoother than late-afternoon walks in summer heat while teams fast. This is operational courtesy, not a legal requirement for non-Muslim buyers, but it keeps handover timelines realistic.

## How do adhan and tower living interact?

Older low-rise neighbourhoods hear the adhan clearly five times daily. Many high-rise units with double glazing hear a faint call or none at all. Neither experience is "wrong"; it affects light sleepers choosing between suburban villas and downtown towers. If you view property near a mosque, visit at Maghrib once before you sign, you learn more about evening noise than a midday open house shows.

## What mistakes do new managers make during Ramadan?

Scheduling lunch-and-learn sessions, celebrating with champagne in open plan areas, or pressing for evening deadlines without acknowledging iftar timing are common first-month errors. Fix is simple: ask HR for the published Ramadan calendar, shift hard deadlines to mornings, and treat iftar invites as team-building when offered.

International schools and nurseries often publish Ramadan hours separately from corporate HR policies. Parents should confirm pick-up windows and after-school activities each year, because shortened workdays do not automatically shorten academic schedules. If your spouse also works in Dubai, align both employers' Ramadan policies before you assume shared school run availability at 15:00. Keep a printed HR policy link in your onboarding folder for nanny and driver schedules too.

Most international employers treat Dhuhr and Asr as informal prayer breaks rather than company-wide shutdowns: Muslim staff step out for five to fifteen minutes while others continue calls. During Ramadan, plan core deliverables before 14:00 when energy dips, and avoid Friday client workshops between 11:30 and 13:30 unless you confirm attendance in advance. HR teams often publish a one-page calendar covering shortened Ramadan hours, Friday prayer windows, and Eid bridge days so remote colleagues in other time zones do not book over local observance.
