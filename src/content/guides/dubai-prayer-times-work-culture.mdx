# Dubai Prayer Times & Islamic Work Culture 2026 Guide


<TldrBlock text={"Five daily prayers shape the rhythm of Dubai's day. Friday midday prayer has the most visible business impact. Ramadan reduces working hours by law. Non-Muslim expats need awareness, not participation, respectful acknowledgement is all that is needed."} />

## The five daily prayers: schedule and significance

Islamic practice requires five prayers daily. The times are calculated from the position of the sun and shift slightly each day. In Dubai, approximate times are:

| Prayer | Arabic name | Approximate time | Significance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dawn prayer | Fajr | 04:30–05:30 | Before sunrise; earliest |
| Midday prayer | Dhuhr | 12:00–13:00 | Solar noon |
| Afternoon prayer | Asr | 15:30–16:30 | Mid-afternoon |
| Sunset prayer | Maghrib | 18:00–19:30 | Varies significantly by season |
| Night prayer | Isha | 20:00–21:00 | After full dark |

**Seasonal variation:** Prayer times shift substantially with Dubai's daylight hours. In June, sunrise is before 06:00 and sunset after 19:00. In December, sunrise is after 07:00 and sunset around 17:30. The Dhuhr and Asr prayers shift correspondingly.

**The Adhan:** The call to prayer (adhan) is broadcast from mosques throughout Dubai at each prayer time. Non-Muslims will hear this as a natural part of the city's soundscape. It is not directed at non-Muslims and requires no response. In many parts of the city it is clearly audible; in newer high-rise areas it may be muffled.

## How prayer times affect daily business in Dubai

The impact on international private sector companies is less than most expats expect and more than some assume.

| Company type | Prayer impact |
| --- | --- |
| International corporation (DIFC, Business Bay) | Minimal; prayer rooms provided; Muslim staff self-manage |
| Local UAE company with majority Emirati staff | Dhuhr and Asr may see brief pauses; prayer room standard |
| Government entities (DLD, RERA, courts) | More structured; may be briefly unavailable at prayer times |
| Traditional local businesses | May close briefly at Dhuhr and Asr |
| Retail and hospitality | Most international brands operate continuously |

**Practical guidance:**

- Do not schedule mandatory all-hands meetings at Dhuhr (12:00–13:00) or Friday prayer window (11:30–13:30) if your team includes Muslim colleagues
- Know where your office prayer room is, required by law in commercial buildings above certain size
- If a Muslim colleague steps away briefly mid-afternoon, prayer is the likely reason, treat as naturally as a coffee break

## Friday Jumu'ah prayer: the most impactful weekly event

Friday is the Islamic holy day. Jumu'ah prayer is a communal obligation for Muslim men and is observed at mosques across Dubai from approximately 11:30 to 13:30, with the sermon (khutbah) followed by the prayer.

**Impact on business:**

| Activity | Friday morning (before noon) | Friday prayer window (11:30–13:30) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meetings with Muslim colleagues | Productive, clear heads | Schedule outside this window |
| Government office visits | Shorter hours, go early | May be closed |
| Bank branches | Check hours; some close midday | Unavailable |
| Courts and legal processes | Limited Friday activity | Unavailable |
| Retail and food | Fully open | Fully open |
| Hotels and hospitality | Fully open | Fully open |

**Traffic near mosques:** Roads near major mosques experience significant congestion during and immediately after Jumu'ah prayer. If driving on Friday midday, route planning around major mosque clusters (Al Farooq Omar bin Al Khattab Mosque, Jumeirah Grand Mosque area, Al Ittihad Mosque) saves significant time.

**For expats scheduling client meetings:** Avoid scheduling Friday 11:30–13:30 for any meeting that requires Muslim client or colleague participation. Morning (09:00–11:00) or afternoon (14:30 onwards) are reliably available.

## Ramadan: the significant annual period

Ramadan is the ninth month of the Islamic lunar calendar, during which Muslims fast from before Fajr (pre-dawn) to Maghrib (sunset). The dates shift approximately 11 days earlier each year.

**2026 Ramadan dates (approximate):** February 17 to March 18, 2026 (verify official announcement, dates confirmed by moon sighting).

| Ramadan element | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| Suhoor | Pre-dawn meal before fast begins |
| Fasting hours | No eating, drinking, or smoking from Fajr to Maghrib |
| Iftar | Breaking of the fast at Maghrib, social event |
| Tarawih | Optional extended night prayers |
| Laylat Al Qadr | The Night of Power (last 10 days); heightened observance |
| Eid Al Fitr | End of Ramadan; public holiday typically 3–4 days |

## Ramadan working hours: the legal framework

UAE Labour Law Article 65 mandates a **2-hour reduction** in daily working hours during Ramadan for all employees, regardless of religion.

| Sector | Typical Ramadan schedule |
| --- | --- |
| Private sector | 6 hours/day instead of 8; commonly 09:00–15:00 |
| Federal government | As announced; typically 09:00–14:30 |
| Dubai government | As announced; often similar to federal |
| Banks and financial | Branch hours reduced; online services continue |
| Retail and hospitality | Shift hours; late-night operations increase |

**For expats:** Your working hours also reduce by 2 hours during Ramadan, even as a non-Muslim. Confirm with HR, some international companies apply the letter of the law exactly; others maintain normal hours but allow flexible remote working.

**Productivity reality:** The end of fasting day (16:00–18:00) sees reduced energy levels among fasting colleagues. Morning hours are typically more productive for cross-team collaboration. Schedule demanding work for mornings during Ramadan.

## Ramadan etiquette in the workplace

Non-Muslim expats who respect Ramadan culture build stronger local and regional relationships.

**At the office:**

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| Eat and drink in designated areas away from fasting colleagues | Eat or drink conspicuously at your desk or in open areas |
| Acknowledge and celebrate Ramadan Kareem (Happy Ramadan) | Assume fasting colleagues can join lunch meetings |
| Schedule important meetings in the morning | Schedule complex work sessions late afternoon |
| Participate in iftar invitations, significant relationship builder | Decline iftar without good reason |
| Dress more conservatively during the month | Be unaware of the general heightened religious atmosphere |

**Iftars at work:** Many companies organise communal iftar dinners during Ramadan. These are important bonding events, attend if possible. Hotel and restaurant iftars are a cultural experience worth participating in at least once. Buffets at major hotels run AED 150–400 per person during Ramadan.

## Sound and light: the Ramadan sensory experience

Ramadan Dubai has a distinct atmosphere that newcomers often find striking:

| Element | Ramadan experience |
| --- | --- |
| Mosques | Louder, more frequent adhan; Tarawih prayers audible after Isha |
| Restaurants | Many operate in "Ramadan tent" format; iftar buffets prominent |
| Streets | Very quiet during day; very busy post-iftar |
| Noise levels | Respectful reduction in music and entertainment during day |
| Decorations | Crescent and lantern lighting across the city |
| Cannon fire | Traditional iftar cannon fired in some locations, marks sunset |

The post-iftar hours (approximately 19:30–01:00) become the most active social time of the day during Ramadan. Restaurants fill, malls extend hours, and families celebrate together. For non-Muslims, this creates an unexpected late-night city with a festive atmosphere.

## Workplace prayer rooms: what to expect

UAE building regulations require prayer facilities in commercial buildings above a certain scale. In practice, virtually all significant office buildings and malls in Dubai have prayer rooms.

| Location type | Prayer room availability |
| --- | --- |
| Major office towers | Dedicated rooms per floor or shared by floors |
| Shopping malls | Clear signage; separated male/female areas |
| Airport | Multiple dedicated areas |
| Hotels | Standard provision |
| Construction sites | Mobile prayer facilities provided |

**For non-Muslim expats:** You are welcome to use the quiet space for personal reflection, though be aware of the primary purpose. Do not use prayer rooms as a quiet working space during prayer times. Follow any posted protocols for footwear removal or direction of seating.

## Understanding Islamic timekeeping: Hijri calendar

Business in Dubai runs primarily on the Gregorian calendar. But public holidays and religious observances follow the Hijri (Islamic lunar) calendar. This is why Islamic holidays shift approximately 11 days earlier each year in Gregorian terms.

| Holiday | Hijri basis | Gregorian shift |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ramadan and Eid Al Fitr | 9th month of Hijri calendar | Shifts ~11 days earlier annually |
| Eid Al Adha | 10th of Dhul Hijjah | Shifts ~11 days earlier annually |
| Islamic New Year | 1 Muharram | Shifts annually |
| Prophet's Birthday | 12 Rabi Al Awwal | Shifts annually |
| National Day | 2–3 December | Fixed Gregorian date |

**Practical impact:** Forward planning of 6–12 months requires checking both calendars. Eid Al Adha in summer 2026 will be different from summer 2027. Book annual leave accounting for Eid timing.

## Ramadan and business development: the relationship angle

Ramadan is one of the most valuable relationship-building periods in UAE business culture, particularly for anyone working with Emirati or Arab clients:

| Opportunity | Approach |
| --- | --- |
| Ramadan gift-giving | Send dates, specialty sweets, or neutral hampers to clients, no alcohol |
| Iftar invitation from clients | Accept without hesitation, significant relationship signal |
| Iftar hosting for clients | Budget AED 200–600 per person; hire a good venue |
| Ramadan charity contribution | CSR moment; many companies contribute to food drives |
| Slowing deal-making | Major decisions often deferred post-Ramadan; plan accordingly |

**The post-Ramadan window:** The two weeks after Eid Al Fitr often see a burst of deal-making and decision-taking. Decisions that were deferred during Ramadan come to a close. Plan major business conversations for this window.

## Eid Al Fitr: end of Ramadan

Eid Al Fitr marks the end of Ramadan with a public holiday typically lasting 3–4 days in the UAE.

| Element | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Duration | 3–4 days announced by UAE government annually |
| Character | Family gatherings, gift exchange, new clothes |
| Business | Most companies close or operate skeleton staff |
| Greeting | "Eid Mubarak" (Blessed Eid) or "Eid Saeed" (Happy Eid) |
| Fireworks | Major displays across Dubai |
| Travel | most residents travel; Dubai airport at peak capacity |

**For expats:** Book Eid travel well in advance, flights, hotels, and popular destinations sell out months ahead. most expats use this as a regional travel opportunity (Jordan, Turkey, Egypt are popular).

## Eid Al Adha: the second Eid

Eid Al Adha occurs 70 days after Eid Al Fitr and commemorates Ibrahim's willingness to sacrifice. Another 3–4 day public holiday.

| Element | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Religious basis | Sacrifice of an animal (typically camel, cow, or sheep) |
| Urban Dubai experience | Sacrifice done away from urban areas; residential neighbourhoods unaffected |
| Business impact | Same as Eid Al Fitr, closure 3–4 days |
| Greeting | "Eid Mubarak", same greeting applies |

**For planning purposes:** Two Eid periods per year mean approximately 6–8 public holiday days from Islamic observances alone. Add UAE National Day (2–3 December) and you have roughly 10–12 public holidays annually. Coordinate annual leave planning with these windows.

| --- | --- | --- |
| As-salamu alaykum | Peace be upon you | Standard greeting (appropriate for all) |
| Wa alaykum as-salam | And upon you peace | Response to above |
| Ramadan Kareem | Generous Ramadan | During Ramadan |
| Eid Mubarak | Blessed Eid | During both Eids |
| Inshallah | If God wills it | Heard constantly, acceptance and intention |

**Understanding Inshallah in business:** Western expats sometimes interpret "Inshallah" as evasion. It is more accurately an acknowledgement that outcomes are not fully within human control. In practice, follow up respectfully, the phrase does not mean "no." Clear deadlines and written confirmation help bridge the cultural gap.

## FAQ

**Do I need to pray or observe religious practices as a non-Muslim expat in Dubai?**
No, non-Muslims are not required to participate in any Islamic religious practice. Respect and awareness are required; participation is not.

**Will I hear the adhan (call to prayer) from my apartment?**
In most areas of Dubai, yes, it is audible from nearby mosques, especially in older areas. In newer high-rise towers with double glazing, it may be faint. It occurs five times daily, starting before dawn. most expats find it a characteristic and valued part of the city's atmosphere.

**How do I handle a client meeting that runs through prayer time?**
For Dhuhr and Asr with international clients, meeting continuation is typically fine. For Friday Jumu'ah, pause the meeting or schedule around it. If a Muslim client excuses themselves briefly, continue the meeting and welcome them back without comment.

**Is working from home during Ramadan common?**
Yes, particularly common in international companies. The 2-hour reduction makes for shorter office days and many employers allow remote working flexibility. Confirm your employer's specific Ramadan policy.

**What should I bring to an iftar dinner as a guest?**
Dates and chocolates are always appropriate. A nice sweets box. Avoid alcohol as a gift, even to non-Muslim hosts during Ramadan. See [Dubai alcohol rules guide](/guides/dubai-alcohol-rules-expats/) for alcohol gift-giving context.

**How does prayer time affect government service visits (RERA, DLD, courts)?**
Visit government offices before 11:00 on Fridays for the most reliable service. During Ramadan, visit as early as possible, hours are reduced and queues can be long early. Afternoons during Ramadan at government offices are often very slow or fully closed.

**Can I eat lunch at my desk during Ramadan?**
Technically you can in private, avoid eating visibly in shared open offices or meeting rooms during Ramadan out of respect for fasting colleagues. Use the pantry or a private area. See [Dubai culture guide](/guides/dubai-culture-etiquette-expats/) for the full Ramadan etiquette framework.

**Does the weekend shift to Saturday–Sunday affect prayer culture?**
Friday remains the Islamic holy day regardless of the administrative weekend. The 2022 weekend change means Friday is now a working day for many sectors, but Jumu'ah prayer remains observed mid-Friday as before. See [Dubai weekend guide](/guides/dubai-weekend-culture/) for the full weekend structure.
