faq:
  - question: "Do I need private health insurance in Qatar as an expat?"
    answer: "Most employed expats get employer-provided medical insurance on a tiered network. However, coverage varies widely - some employers provide basic Hamad public system access only, while others offer premium private hospital networks. Check your employment package details and consider family top-up insurance if needed."
  - question: "Can I use Hamad public hospitals without insurance?"
    answer: "Yes, with a valid Health Card linked to your QID, you can access Hamad Medical Corporation (HMC) facilities at subsidised rates. Emergency care is provided regardless of insurance status, though non-emergency treatments may have waiting periods and co-payments for expats."
  - question: "How much does private healthcare cost in Qatar?"
    answer: "Consultation fees range from QAR 150-400 at private hospitals like Al Ahli and Doha Clinic. Specialist appointments cost QAR 300-600. Without insurance, budget QAR 500-2,000 monthly for family private healthcare, depending on usage and hospital tier chosen."
  - question: "Is dental care included in Qatar health insurance?"
    answer: "Dental is often excluded or heavily capped in standard employer insurance plans. Many policies limit dental to QAR 1,000-3,000 annually, which covers basic cleanings but not major procedures. Check your schedule of benefits, not marketing materials, for actual dental coverage."
  - question: "Which hospitals should I use for maternity in Qatar?"
    answer: "Sidra Medicine offers excellent obstetrics and pediatrics. Private options include Women's Hospital, Al Ahli, and Doha Clinic. Book early - good private obstetrics fills months ahead. Hamad also provides maternity services at subsidised rates with Health Card coverage."
  - question: "What should I do in a medical emergency in Qatar?"
    answer: "Call 999 for ambulance services - English operators available. Major trauma automatically routes to Hamad Medical Corporation. For non-emergency urgent care, private hospital ERs are faster but more expensive. Keep insurance cards and QID with you always."
