Saudi Arabia opened two doors for foreign capital in early 2026: Law M/14 zone property ownership under REGA supervision, and the existing Premium Residency programme commonly cited at SAR 4 million on an investment track (confirm current official rules). Developers market them as one product. They are parallel legal pathways that require separate verification, separate applications, and separate due diligence.

This guide explains how Premium Residency and designated-zone property interact, what foreigners can actually buy, REGA verification steps, cost stacks, yield reality, and the mistakes that early-market buyers cannot afford.

## Premium Residency and zone ownership are separate pathways?
Saudi Premium Residency and foreign ownership in a designated zone are separate approvals, even where a buyer is considering the commonly cited SAR 4 million investment track. Law M/14 governs property access, while Premium Residency has its own category rules. **Insider tip:** obtain written confirmation from both authorities before treating one approval as evidence of the other.

Law M/14 (effective 22 January 2026) creates the legal basis for non-Saudi ownership in REGA-approved designated zones. Premium Residency predates Law M/14 and operates under separate programme rules.

A buyer might:
- Qualify for Premium Residency through approved investment instruments without buying zone property (confirm current official rules)
- Buy zone property without receiving Premium Residency
- Potentially link both only if programme rules explicitly connect property value to Premium Residency category (confirm current official rules)

Never assume linkage from a sales deck.

The Saudi Premium Residency Center is the recognised starting point for residency category requirements, while REGA is the recognised authority for property regulation and developer registration. A foreign buyer considering a SAR 4 million investment route should treat those as two separate official checks. The residency application may require evidence of the qualifying category, whereas a property transaction requires confirmation that the specific unit, title type, and development are available to a non-Saudi buyer. A developer brochure cannot substitute for either confirmation. Save dated copies of the official programme guidance, REGA search results, the sale and purchase agreement, and any written response that addresses the proposed unit. If an adviser says property value automatically meets a residency threshold, ask which current rule creates that link and request the official reference before paying a reservation fee.

The Saudi Premium Residency Center is the official programme source for the current residency categories, required evidence, and applicant process. REGA is the official property-regulation source for the current foreign-buyer framework, designated-zone eligibility, and developer registration. A buyer planning around SAR 4 million should keep these checks separate: a residency category is not proof that a particular unit can be sold to a non-Saudi, and a permitted property purchase is not proof that it qualifies for residency. Before any non-refundable payment, save the dated official programme guidance, REGA search result, developer registration evidence, proposed contract, and written answer addressing the specific unit. Ask Saudi-qualified counsel to confirm the title path, payment conditions, and any claimed relationship between the property and the residency route. If the official position changes before completion, revise the transaction plan rather than relying on an earlier sales explanation.

A Saudi foreign-buyer file should be built around two recognised authorities and one transaction record. REGA should be used to verify the unit, title form, designated-zone status, and developer position under the current rules. The Saudi Premium Residency Center should be used to verify the applicable category, financial evidence, family requirements, and any current investment test. For a plan involving SAR 4 million, retain every answer with the date, page reference, and exact property description. Then compare those answers with the sale and purchase agreement, payment schedule, and bank source-of-funds requirements. This record helps identify a mismatch before money moves, especially where a developer refers broadly to a project while the official rule applies to a particular zone or unit. A brochure, an agent message, or an undated presentation is not an official confirmation.

Comparison anchor: UAE Golden Visa at AED 2M (~USD 545K) is lower capital, more documented, and tied to mature property markets. Saudi Premium Residency is **higher capital, earlier-stage regulation, Vision 2030 growth optionality**.

## REGA zone approval determines what foreign buyers can purchase?
REGA approval determines whether a foreign buyer can acquire a specific Saudi property, because foreign ownership is not a blanket right across the country. Law M/14 took effect on 22 January 2026, but the relevant question remains the registered zone, unit type, and current implementing rule. **Insider tip:** verify the exact unit, not just the project name.

## How do foreign buyers complete this purchase legally?
Foreign buyers should run the residency and property processes in parallel, starting with identity, source-of-funds, zone eligibility, developer registration, and contract review. In off-plan projects, a 24 to 48 month handover gap can separate a deposit from usable property. **Insider tip:** make every payment conditional on a documented legal and project-status check.

Off-plan in giga-projects (NEOM, Red Sea) carries construction timeline risk measured in years. Premium Residency planning and property handover may diverge by 24 to 48 months.

## How does Premium Residency differ from employer iqama for lifestyle planning?
Premium Residency is intended for eligible independent applicants, while an employer iqama is tied to employment sponsorship and its conditions. The difference matters for households planning work, dependants, and housing over a 5 to 10 year horizon. **Insider tip:** map each family member's status separately instead of assuming one property purchase solves every visa question.

Most expats still live on employer iqama. Premium Residency suits wealthy independents, retirees-with-means, and Vision 2030 long-term believers who can absorb capital lock-up and regulatory evolution.

## What documents should Premium Residency applicants prepare alongside zone property?
Applicants should prepare identity, qualifying-investment evidence, compliance documents, and property records as two related but separate files. For first-time cross-border buyers, an 8 to 16 week planning allowance can be sensible where banks request enhanced source-of-funds review. **Insider tip:** keep dated portal screenshots and certified translations in the same transaction folder.

Premium Residency Centre files run in parallel with REGA property checks. Treat them as two approvals, not one bundle.

| Document pack | Premium Residency track | Zone property purchase |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity and passport | Required | Required |
| Proof of qualifying investment | SAR 4M track evidence (confirm) | SPA and payment receipts |
| Criminal / compliance checks | Programme specific (confirm) | Bank KYC for transfers |
| Property eligibility | Optional unless linked | REGA zone letter for unit |

Allow 8 to 16 weeks for first-time foreign buyers when cross-border sourcing of funds triggers enhanced bank review. Keep dated REGA portal screenshots whenever zone lists update mid-process.

REGA and the Saudi Premium Residency Center answer different questions in a foreign-buyer file. REGA should be used to verify the current property framework, developer status, designated-zone eligibility, and the legal form of the unit. The Premium Residency Center should be used to verify the current category, financial evidence, applicant requirements, and whether any property investment is recognised for that route. This distinction matters when an applicant is planning around SAR 4 million: the property price, the purchase cost, and the qualifying-investment evidence may not be interchangeable. Keep the official answer with the date and the exact unit reference. Then have Saudi-qualified counsel review the SPA, title path, payment conditions, and any statement about residency before funds move. If the official position changes between reservation and completion, update the underwriting and do not rely on an earlier verbal explanation.
