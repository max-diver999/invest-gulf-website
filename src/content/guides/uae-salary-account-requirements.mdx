How WPS works in practice:

1. The employer registers with a MOHRE-approved **WPS agent** (a bank or exchange house licensed to process WPS payroll, such as Emirates NBD, FAB, ADCB, Al Ansari Exchange, or UAE Exchange).
2. Each month, the employer uploads a payroll file through the WPS portal, specifying employee IDs, salary amounts, and destination IBAN numbers.
3. The WPS agent processes the bulk transfer and reports the transactions back to MOHRE's Wage Indicator system.
4. MOHRE monitors compliance. If a company pays late or short by more than 10%, the system flags a violation.

**Who must use WPS:** All mainland private-sector employers registered with MOHRE. Free zone companies fall under their respective free zone authority rules (DIFC, JAFZA, and most other major free zones have equivalent salary protection systems). Government entities use separate payroll channels but follow similar principles.

**WPS deadlines:** Employers must pay salaries no later than the **14th of the following month** for most categories. Companies with more than 100 employees face stricter rolling monitoring. Violations result in fines starting at AED 1,000 per affected employee per month, plus a ban on new work permit applications until arrears are cleared, a severe operational penalty that most responsible employers work hard to avoid.

**What this means for you as an employee:** If your salary arrives late without explanation, you have formal grounds to file a complaint through the MOHRE app or the Tawafuq portal. Keep your monthly payslips and WPS payment confirmations as evidence.

**What a valid salary transfer letter must contain:**

- Full legal name of the employee exactly as on the passport
- Passport number and expiry date
- Emirates ID number (or application reference if ID is pending)
- Job title and employment start date
- Gross monthly salary, often broken into basic, housing allowance, and transport allowance components
- Company name, trade licence number, and registered address
- Name and signature of an authorised HR signatory
- Official company stamp
- Date of issue (most banks require a letter dated within 30–60 days)
- Bank name and branch (some banks require the specific branch address)
- The statement that salary will be transferred exclusively to the named account

**Banks that provide their own templates:** FAB, Emirates NBD, and ADCB each offer downloadable PDF salary letter templates for HR departments to complete. Using the bank's own template speeds up processing because the compliance team does not need to reformat data. Your employer's HR team should be familiar with this request, it is routine.

**Common rejection reasons:**
- Salary on the letter does not match the WPS file (even by a few hundred AED)
- Letter is generic and does not name the bank or account
- Stamp is missing or appears printed rather than embossed/wet-ink
- Signatory is not on the bank's registered authorised signatories list for the company account

If HR refuses or delays issuing the letter, escalate to your manager or request the HR manager directly, this is a standard onboarding task, not a special request, and delay can hold up your access to salary-linked products.

**Personal loans:** UAE banks offer salary-linked personal loans at substantially lower rates than unsecured loans. Most banks cap the loan at 20–25 times the monthly gross salary (capped at AED 2 million by Central Bank regulation). Debt burden ratio limits apply, typically no more than 50% of gross salary committed to total monthly debt repayments. Without a salary account at the lending bank, you will either be declined or offered terms 2–4 percentage points higher.

**Credit cards:** Salary transfer customers qualify for credit cards with limits typically set at 2–3 times monthly salary. Some banks (FAB, Emirates NBD) automatically issue a complimentary card when salary transfer is confirmed. Non-salary applicants need a minimum balance deposit as security for the same card.

**Overdrafts:** Many banks extend a pre-approved overdraft facility of one month's salary to salary account holders, a useful buffer in the first few months before your financial life in the UAE is fully stabilised.

**Fee waivers:** Standard current accounts in the UAE charge a monthly fee of AED 25–75 if the average balance falls below a threshold (often AED 3,000–10,000). Salary transfer customers typically have this fee waived entirely as long as the salary arrives each month.

**Mortgage eligibility:** If you plan to buy property in Dubai, the mortgage lender will almost always require your salary to be transferred to an account at their bank. **Use your bank's salary certificate template:** HR departments receive dozens of these requests. Handing them a pre-formatted template from the bank shortens turnaround from 5–7 days to 1–2 days.

**Request a salary certificate in English and Arabic:** Some banks require the Arabic version for compliance filing. If your company HR can produce both simultaneously, it prevents a return trip.

**Keep payslips:** WPS generates a record, but your personal payslip archive is your fastest proof of employment history when applying for a car loan, mortgage, or visa change. Store digitally and keep at least 6 months current.

**Do not confuse salary certificate with NOC:** A salary certificate confirms income. A No Objection Certificate (NOC) confirms your employer permits a specific activity (e.g. visa sponsorship transfer, second-job permit). They are separate documents and banks cannot substitute one for the other.
