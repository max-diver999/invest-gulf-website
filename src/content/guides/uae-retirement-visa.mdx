**TL;DR:** UAE retirement visa is a **5-year renewable permit** for expats aged **55 and above**. Three qualifying routes: own property worth **AED 2 million**, hold **AED 1 million in savings**, or earn **AED 20,000/month** in passive income. Spouses and children can be sponsored. It is a solid live-and-stay option — not as long-lasting as the [Golden Visa](/guides/uae-golden-visa-property-2026/), but accessible without owning real estate. Retire in Dubai at 57, draw your pension, own a studio in Jumeirah Village Circle — and hold valid UAE residency doing all of it. That is the practical pitch of the UAE retirement visa, a 5-year permit introduced as part of the UAE's effort to retain high-net-worth older residents who are neither employees nor large-scale investors.

The visa gets less attention than the Golden Visa. It should not. For anyone aged 55+ whose wealth sits in savings or pension income rather than AED 2M property portfolios, it is often the simpler, cheaper route to long-term UAE residency.

This guide covers every qualifying route, exact thresholds, what happens at renewal, how to handle dependents, and where retirement visa holders sit relative to other UAE residency pathways.

---

## What Is the UAE Retirement Visa?

The UAE retirement visa — formally issued under federal residency regulations and implemented through GDRFA Dubai and ICP (Federal Authority for Identity, Citizenship, Customs and Port Security) — is a **5-year renewable residency permit** for foreign nationals who are 55 years old or older and can demonstrate financial self-sufficiency through at least one of three qualifying routes.

Unlike an employment visa, no UAE-based job or employer is required. Unlike the Golden Visa, no AED 2M property investment is mandatory. The retirement visa was designed explicitly to allow economically independent older expats to remain in the UAE after ending their employment.

Key characteristics:

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Duration | 5 years, renewable |
| Age requirement | 55+ at time of application |
| Qualifying routes | Property, savings, or income (one is enough) |
| Dependents | Spouse + children sponsorable |
| Work permitted | Not automatically; employment requires separate work permit |
| Path to permanent residency | Not a direct path — renewable on continued eligibility |

---

## Who Can Apply: Age and Nationality

The 55-year minimum age threshold applies to the **principal applicant** only. There is no upper age cap and no restriction by nationality — the visa is available to all foreign nationals regardless of passport.

Applicants who have previously lived in the UAE on employment or investor visas can transition to the retirement visa once they reach 55, provided they meet one of the financial qualifying criteria below.

Applicants currently living outside the UAE can apply on entry or through an approved typing center and online GDRFA/ICP portal flow, typically requiring a medical fitness test in the UAE.

## Three Qualifying Routes: Property, Savings, or Income

Meeting **any one** of the three routes is sufficient. You do not need to combine them, and you do not need all three.

### Route 1 — Property Investment: AED 2 Million

Own real estate registered in your name in the UAE with a **minimum combined market value of AED 2 million**.

Key rules:

- Property must be in a freehold zone or designated area open to foreign ownership.
- The AED 2M threshold is assessed against **registered market value**, not original purchase price.
- A single property or **combination of multiple units** can reach the threshold.
- Off-plan units registered with Oqood are generally acceptable — verify with GDRFA at application, as policies on incomplete properties can vary.
- Jointly owned property may count proportionally — if you own 50% of a AED 4M unit, that is AED 2M qualifying.
- Mortgaged property can qualify provided the registered value meets the threshold and the bank issues an NOC. Equity paid is not the only measure — registered value is.

For a full breakdown of property-linked visa rules and documentation, see [UAE Golden Visa Property 2026](/guides/uae-golden-visa-property-2026/) — many of the property documentation requirements overlap.

### Route 2 — Financial Savings: AED 1 Million

Hold a minimum of **AED 1 million (or equivalent in foreign currency)** in a UAE bank account — either as a fixed deposit or as a maintained balance.

Key rules:

- The savings must be deposited in a **UAE-licensed bank**, not an offshore account.
- Fixed deposits are the most straightforward proof — they lock the balance and generate a clear bank certificate.
- Current or savings accounts with maintained balance may qualify — check your specific bank's letter format and whether GDRFA/ICP will accept it.
- Joint accounts typically require a declaration of your individual share.
- Foreign currency equivalent to AED 1M at prevailing rates is accepted — but expect conversion documentation.

The savings route suits retirees who have liquidated a property abroad and are holding proceeds in cash before deciding whether to buy in the UAE. It is also the fastest qualifying path: no property search, no title deed timeline.

### Route 3 — Monthly Income: AED 20,000

Demonstrate a **regular monthly income of at least AED 20,000** from passive or pension sources.

Accepted income sources typically include:

- **Pension payments** from a government or corporate scheme
- **Dividends** from investment portfolios (consistent, documented)
- **Rental income** from property inside or outside the UAE (documented via tenancy contracts and bank deposits)
- **Annuity or trust distributions**
- Combinations of the above totalling AED 20,000/month

Income must be **regular and verifiable**. Sporadic consultancy fees or variable equity income are harder to qualify — GDRFA expects consistency. Provide at least 3–6 months of bank statements showing recurring credits, plus source documentation (pension award letter, dividend certificates, tenancy contracts).

AED 20,000/month equals approximately AED 240,000 per year or roughly USD 65,500 annually at 2026 rates. For most Western pension recipients this is achievable, particularly with combined pension and investment income.

## Application Process Step by Step

**Step 1 — Pre-entry or In-country:**
If you are outside the UAE, apply for a UAE entry permit first (typically 60-day visit visa or designated retirement visa entry permit where available). If already inside on a valid visa, you can apply in-country before current visa expiry.

**Step 2 — Medical Fitness Test:**
Book at any GDRFA-approved medical fitness center. Standard tests include blood screening and chest X-ray. Results typically come within 1–3 business days.

**Step 3 — Health Insurance:**
Obtain a UAE-compliant health insurance policy. Dubai mandates HAAD/DHA-approved coverage for all residents. Insurance cost varies by age — for a 60-year-old, expect AED 3,000–8,000/year depending on plan scope.

**Step 4 — Submit Application:**
Apply through the GDRFA Dubai portal (for Dubai-based applicants) or ICP Smart Services app/website for other emirates. Upload all documents in the required format.

**Step 5 — Biometrics and Emirates ID:**
Once initial approval is granted, attend a biometric collection appointment. Emirates ID is issued alongside or shortly after visa stamping. See the [Emirates ID Application Guide](/guides/emirates-id-application-guide/) for the full ID process.

**Step 6 — Visa Stamping:**
Visa entry stamp is added to your passport. Residency permit is linked digitally to your Emirates ID. Full residency status is activated.

Total timeline: **2–4 weeks** for a straightforward application. Complex cases involving offshore income documentation, property valuation queries, or attested foreign documents can extend to 6–8 weeks.

## Dependents: Spouse and Children

Retirement visa holders can sponsor:

- **Spouse** — no age restriction; requires marriage certificate (attested to UAE standards)
- **Dependent children** — typically up to age 18; university students may extend to age 25 in some cases; unmarried adult daughters are sponsorable under certain conditions
- **Parents** — not a standard dependent category under retirement visa; separate parent visa options exist

Dependents require their own medical test, insurance, and Emirates ID. They cannot work in the UAE unless they obtain an independent work permit.

If a dependent child reaches 18 and wants to stay in the UAE, they will need to transition to their own residency basis — employment, student visa, or their own qualifying investment.

## Retirement Visa vs Golden Visa: Which One?

Both visas offer UAE residency to financially independent individuals. The right choice depends on your profile.

| Factor | Retirement Visa | Golden Visa |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | 5 years | 10 years |
| Age requirement | 55+ | None |
| Property route threshold | AED 2M | AED 2M |
| Savings route | AED 1M (no age req for income route) | Not available |
| Income route | AED 20,000/month | Not available |
| Work permitted | Requires separate work permit | Yes, with employment |
| Cost and admin burden | Lower | Slightly higher |
| Prestige / long-term security | Standard residency | Premium residency |

**If you are 55+ with AED 1M in savings but no property:** Retirement visa is your route — Golden Visa has no savings-only path.

**If you are 55+ with AED 2M qualifying property:** Evaluate both. Golden Visa gives 10 years and more flexibility; retirement visa is faster to qualify administratively.

**If you are under 55 with AED 2M property:** Golden Visa is the only option — no retirement visa without age threshold.

## Renewal: What You Need to Maintain

The retirement visa is renewable every 5 years, but **renewal is not automatic**. At renewal you must demonstrate that you still meet at least one qualifying condition.

### Property route at renewal:
Property must still be registered in your name at a minimum AED 2M market value. If you have sold the property and not replaced it, the renewal ground disappears. If the property value has declined below AED 2M, you will need to supplement with savings or income proof or purchase additional units.

Selling your qualifying property without a plan creates the same compliance gap as with Golden Visa property holders — see [Golden Visa Renewal Requirements](/guides/golden-visa-renewal-requirements-uae/) for the detailed mechanics, which closely mirror the retirement visa property renewal logic.

### Savings route at renewal:
AED 1M must still be maintained in a UAE bank. If you have drawn down the savings, replenish before renewal appointment.

### Income route at renewal:
Current monthly income must still be verifiable at AED 20,000+. Pension letters, updated bank statements, and refreshed income source documentation will be required.

### Other renewal requirements:
- Fresh medical fitness test
- Updated health insurance
- Emirates ID renewal
- Current passport (must be valid through renewal period)

Tip: Start the renewal process **3–4 months before expiry**. Visa grace periods exist but late renewal creates entry and re-entry complications.

## Re-Entry Rule: How Long Can You Stay Away?

Unlike the Golden Visa's 6-month re-entry rule, the retirement visa follows standard UAE residency re-entry requirements. A UAE resident on a standard residency visa (which is what the retirement visa is, classified as a 5-year residency) typically must enter the UAE at least **once every 6 months** to keep the residency active.

Extended absence beyond 6 months without a return entry can result in automatic visa cancellation in the GDRFA/ICP system. If you plan to spend extended periods outside the UAE, consult your typing center or immigration advisor about approved absence notifications — the retirement visa does not carry the same extended-absence flexibility as the Golden Visa.

## Tax and Lifestyle Context

The UAE has **no personal income tax** on wages, pensions, dividends, or capital gains. This is a structural benefit for retirement income planning: a pension paid into the UAE is not taxed in the UAE regardless of source country (though home-country obligations depend on your tax residency status and treaty arrangements — verify with a tax advisor).

Dubai and Abu Dhabi rank consistently as the safest large cities globally by crime index. Healthcare infrastructure at major private hospitals (American Hospital, Cleveland Clinic Abu Dhabi, Mediclinic) is top-tier and accessible to well-insured residents.

Cost of living is moderate for lifestyle-driven spending: dining, leisure, and travel are accessible. Housing is the largest variable. For retirement visa holders who own qualifying AED 2M property, the housing cost is already absorbed into the qualifying investment.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**1. Underestimating the income documentation threshold.**
AED 20,000/month must be evidenced with consistent, recurring bank credits. One-off large deposits do not substitute for regular income.

**2. Mixing up retirement visa and Golden Visa property thresholds.**
Both are AED 2M for the property route, but the savings and income routes are retirement visa-only. Some applicants confuse AED 1M Golden Visa rumours (which don't exist for the standard property investor route) with the AED 1M retirement visa savings path.

**3. Buying off-plan without checking acceptance.**
Not all off-plan registrations are treated identically across emirates. Oqood registration in Dubai is generally acceptable; confirm for Abu Dhabi or Ras Al Khaimah projects with the local land authority.

**4. Letting insurance lapse between renewal stages.**
A gap in UAE-compliant health insurance can create complications even if your visa technically has validity remaining. Keep insurance continuous.

**5. Ignoring dependent transition planning.**
Children who will turn 18 during the 5-year visa period need an independent residency plan. Do not wait until their dependent visa expires to investigate options.

## Summary: Is the UAE Retirement Visa Right for You?

The UAE retirement visa is the right instrument if you are 55 or older and want long-term UAE residency without the complexity of active property investment at the Golden Visa scale — or if your primary qualifying asset is savings or pension income rather than real estate.

At AED 1M savings or AED 20,000/month income, the barrier is meaningfully lower than it looks from the outside. For context: a GBP 800,000 UK pension pot generating 4% annually equals GBP 32,000/year — approximately AED 152,000/year or AED 12,700/month. Adding supplemental income sources brings most structured retirees above the AED 20,000 threshold.

For the property route, the AED 2M threshold aligns with the minimum Golden Visa property threshold. If you are buying property in this range anyway, assess whether the Golden Visa's 10-year duration makes more sense for your long-term plans.
