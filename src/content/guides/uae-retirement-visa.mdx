Retire in Dubai at 57, draw your pension, own a studio in Jumeirah Village Circle, and hold valid UAE residency doing all of it. That is the practical pitch of the UAE retirement visa, a 5-year permit introduced as part of the UAE's effort to retain high-net-worth older residents who are neither employees nor large-scale investors.

The visa gets less attention than the Golden Visa. It should not. For anyone aged 55+ whose wealth sits in savings or pension income rather than AED 2M property portfolios, it is often the simpler, cheaper route to long-term UAE residency.

This guide covers every qualifying route, exact thresholds, what happens at renewal, how to handle dependents, and where retirement visa holders sit relative to other UAE residency pathways.

### Route 1: Property Investment: AED 2 Million

Own real estate registered in your name in the UAE with a minimum combined market value of AED 2 million.

Key rules:

- Property must be in a freehold zone or designated area open to foreign ownership.
- The AED 2M threshold is assessed against registered market value, not original purchase price.
- A single property or combination of multiple units can reach the threshold.
- Off-plan units registered with Oqood are generally acceptable, verify with GDRFA at application, as policies on incomplete properties can vary.
- Jointly owned property may count proportionally; if you own 50% of a AED 4M unit, that is AED 2M qualifying.
- Mortgaged property can qualify provided the registered value meets the threshold and the bank issues an NOC. Equity paid is not the only measure, registered value is.

### Route 2: Financial Savings: AED 1 Million

Hold a minimum of AED 1 million (or equivalent in foreign currency) in a UAE bank account, either as a fixed deposit or as a maintained balance.

- The savings must be deposited in a UAE-licensed bank, not an offshore account.
- Fixed deposits are the most straightforward proof, they lock the balance and generate a clear bank certificate.
- Current or savings accounts with maintained balance may qualify, check your specific bank's letter format and whether GDRFA/ICP will accept it.
- Joint accounts typically require a declaration of your individual share.
- Foreign currency equivalent to AED 1M at prevailing rates is accepted, but expect conversion documentation.

The savings route suits retirees who have liquidated a property abroad and are holding proceeds in cash before deciding whether to buy in the UAE. It is also the fastest qualifying path: no property search, no title deed timeline.

### Route 3: Monthly Income: AED 20,000

Demonstrate a regular monthly income of at least AED 20,000 from passive or pension sources.

Accepted income sources typically include:

- Pension payments from a government or corporate scheme
- **Dividends** from investment portfolios (consistent, documented)
- **Rental income** from property inside or outside the UAE (documented via tenancy contracts and bank deposits)
- Annuity or trust distributions
- Combinations of the above totalling AED 20,000/month

Income must be regular and verifiable. Sporadic consultancy fees or variable equity income are harder to qualify, GDRFA expects consistency. Provide at least 3–6 months of bank statements showing recurring credits, plus source documentation (pension award letter, dividend certificates, tenancy contracts).

AED 20,000/month equals approximately AED 240,000 per year or roughly USD 65,500 annually at 2026 rates. For most Western pension recipients this is achievable, particularly with combined pension and investment income.

2. Mixing up retirement visa and Golden Visa property thresholds.
Both are AED 2M for the property route, but the savings and income routes are retirement visa-only. Some applicants confuse AED 1M Golden Visa rumours (which don't exist for the standard property investor route) with the AED 1M retirement visa savings path.

3. Buying off-plan without checking acceptance.

4. Letting insurance lapse between renewal stages.
A gap in UAE-compliant health insurance can create complications even if your visa technically has validity remaining. Keep insurance continuous.

5. Ignoring dependent transition planning.
Children who will turn 18 during the 5-year visa period need an independent residency plan. Do not wait until their dependent visa expires to investigate options.
