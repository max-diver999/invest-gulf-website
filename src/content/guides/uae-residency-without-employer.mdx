**TL;DR:** The UAE offers at least five residency routes that require **no employer sponsor**: the 10-year Golden Visa (AED 2M property or exceptional-talent pathway), the 5-year Green Visa (freelancers and self-employed earning AED 360,000/year+), the 5-year Retirement Visa (age 55+, three qualifying options), the 1-year Remote Work Visa (foreign income of USD 3,500/month), and the lower-tier Property Investor Visa (emirate-tier dependent). Your choice depends on your age, income source, asset base, and how long you plan to stay. This guide maps each route, compares costs, and shows which one fits your situation.

---

Most UAE expats arrive on employer-sponsored visas, the company files, the company cancels, and the moment you resign you have 30 days to exit or find another sponsor. That model made sense in 1990. In 2026, more than a third of high-net-worth individuals looking at UAE residency come without a job offer in hand. They are freelancers, remote workers, retirees, property investors, or entrepreneurs who want long-term legal presence in the UAE without tying their right to live there to a single employer's HR department.

The UAE now accommodates all of them. The routes are different, the thresholds are different, and the rights that come with each visa differ significantly. Here is what you actually need to know.

---

## Why Self-Sponsored Residency Matters More Than It Did Five Years Ago

The traditional employment visa creates a fundamental dependency: the employer is the legal sponsor, and losing the job means losing residency. For most salaried workers that remains the default path. But for four growing groups, investors, remote professionals, retirees, and high-earners who went freelance, employer-tied residency carries unacceptable risk.

Self-sponsored UAE visas solve that. The primary holder is their own sponsor. Renewal depends on maintaining the qualifying threshold, property value, income, savings, or professional status, rather than on a single employer's decision. Family sponsorship rights are largely preserved. Emirates ID and UAE banking access follow the same process as employer-sponsored residents.

The practical implications are material: you can switch from employee to consultant, sell a business, retire, or work remotely for clients in three continents, without visa cancellation triggering an exit clock. That is the core argument for self-sponsored residency, and it is why the programmes described below have absorbed a growing share of UAE inflow since 2020.

## Route 1: The 10-Year Golden Visa

The Golden Visa is the UAE's flagship long-term residency product. For property investors, the threshold is **AED 2 million** in registered real estate, the value recorded at DLD or the relevant emirate land department, not including the 4% transfer fee. A single unit or a combination of units aggregated to AED 2M can qualify in many cases, though aggregation rules vary by emirate and case.

**Why it stands apart:** A 10-year validity with full family sponsorship rights and the longest re-entry buffer of any UAE residency category means you are not rebuilding visa paperwork every two years. Renewal is tied to maintaining the qualifying threshold, sell the property below AED 2M and you lose the basis, but otherwise the Golden Visa is the most stable self-sponsored route available.

Off-plan properties registered with Oqood and on developer contracts at or above AED 2M may also qualify, subject to current GDRFA and ICP guidance. The earlier 50% deposit rule before visa eligibility was removed in widely reported policy updates; as of 2026, visa qualification is generally tied to registered contract value rather than down payment made. Confirm with licensed immigration counsel before signing.

Beyond property, the Golden Visa covers exceptional talent in sciences, arts, culture, sport, and medicine, as well as researchers, physicians, and specialists endorsed by a relevant government entity. For these routes there is no AED 2M asset requirement, the pathway is competency-based, not capital-based.

**Costs:** Government fees run approximately AED 4,000–5,500 per principal applicant including medical fitness, Emirates ID, and entry permit. Dependent visas are separate. Legal and typing centre fees vary.

## Route 2: The 5-Year Green Visa

The Green Visa introduced in 2022 fills the gap between the employer visa and the Golden Visa for skilled working professionals who are not employees. There are two main tracks for self-sponsorship: **freelancers** holding a UAE freelance permit, and **self-employed / business investors** operating through a UAE legal entity or free zone company.

**Freelancer track:** You need an active UAE freelance permit from a recognised authority (TECOM, twofour54, RAKEZ, and others), and must show annual income from freelance activity of at least **AED 360,000**, or the equivalent in savings or equivalent supporting documentation, depending on emirate processing.

**Self-employed track:** Similar income threshold, applied to business activity conducted under a UAE trade licence. The entity must be active and the applicant must be the beneficial owner or authorised operator.

**Why it matters:** The Green Visa offers 5-year self-sponsored residency without needing AED 2M in property. For professionals in technology, media, consulting, legal services, or digital work who generate strong income but have not yet committed capital to real estate, it provides a clean midpoint. You hold UAE residency, can open UAE bank accounts, sponsor family (within current ICP limits), and maintain legal presence during business-building phases.

The Green Visa does not carry the same re-entry buffer as the Golden Visa, and the income threshold must typically be demonstrated at renewal. Build your documentation trail, bank statements, tax returns, freelance contracts, continuously rather than scrambling at renewal.

**Costs:** Government fees approximately AED 2,500–4,000 per applicant. Freelance permit fees are separate and vary by authority, typically AED 7,000–15,000 per year.

## Route 3: The 5-Year Retirement Visa

The Retirement Visa is purpose-built for residents aged **55 and above** who want to live in the UAE without working. Three qualifying routes, and you only need to satisfy one:

1. **Property:** Own UAE real estate registered in your name with a market value of at least **AED 2 million**.
2. **Savings:** Hold at least **AED 1 million** in savings deposited with a UAE-recognised financial institution.
3. **Passive income:** Demonstrate a monthly income of at least **AED 20,000** (approximately USD 5,450) from pension, dividends, rental yields, or a combination of passive sources.

The savings and income routes are what make the Retirement Visa distinctive. Not every retiree has a AED 2M property. Many have liquid wealth in bonds, pension funds, or investment accounts. If monthly cash flow from those sources exceeds AED 20,000, the Retirement Visa is accessible without any UAE real estate at all.

**Compared to the Golden Visa:** If you are 55+ and own AED 2M+ in UAE property, both visas may be available. The Golden Visa offers 10 years versus 5, and is generally considered the stronger long-term choice. The Retirement Visa is the accessible route when the qualifying asset is savings or income rather than property.

**Family sponsorship:** Yes. The primary holder can sponsor a spouse and dependent children. Accommodation and insurance proof required for each dependent.

**Renewal:** The 5-year visa is renewable provided qualifying criteria are maintained. Renewal requires fresh medical screening and updated income or asset documentation.

**Costs:** Government fees approximately AED 2,500–4,000 per principal applicant.

## Route 4: The Remote Work Visa (Dubai Virtual Working Programme)

The Remote Work Visa is a **1-year renewable permit** for people who work remotely for a foreign employer or run a business based entirely outside the UAE. It was launched by Dubai's Department of Economy and Tourism in October 2020 and is one of the earliest programmes of its kind globally.

**Eligibility in brief:**
- Employees: active employment contract with a foreign employer, minimum **USD 3,500/month** gross salary
- Self-employed / business owners: evidence of business operation for at least 1 year, average monthly revenue of **USD 3,500+** over the preceding 3 months

**Important constraint:** You must not take UAE-source employment or generate UAE-source income on this visa. It is specifically for continuing to serve foreign clients or employers while physically residing in Dubai.

**Cost and friction:** The all-in cost is approximately **USD 852**, roughly USD 287 in processing fees plus USD 565 in mandatory health insurance. This is the lowest entry cost of any self-sponsored UAE residency route, which makes it an obvious first step for remote professionals who want to test UAE life before committing to a 5-year Green Visa or major property purchase.

**Tax consideration:** Days spent in the UAE on any visa, including the Remote Work Visa, count toward the 183-day threshold for UAE individual tax residency. If you spend over 183 days per year, you may qualify as a UAE tax resident under Ministry of Finance guidance. That does not automatically eliminate home-country tax obligations, those depend on your country of origin's own exit and tie-breaker rules. Get specialist tax advice before restructuring.

## Route 5: The Lower-Tier Property Investor Visa

Below the Golden Visa threshold of AED 2M sits a lower-tier property investor visa with shorter validity, historically referenced at **AED 750,000** in property value, though the floor and conditions have evolved and vary by emirate. As of 2026, the sole-owner AED 750,000 floor has been adjusted in certain emirate programmes; joint ownership and partial-payment scenarios have different rules.

This route offers **2-year residency** with more limited rights than the Golden or Green Visa. Family sponsorship is typically restricted and requires a separate income demonstration. It is primarily relevant for buyers who want some form of legal residency attached to a smaller property purchase, a studio or a cheaper unit in emerging areas, without committing capital to AED 2M.

**Practical note:** For most buyers actively planning UAE residency, the lower-tier investor visa is a fallback rather than a target. The per-year administrative overhead of 2-year renewals, combined with restricted family rights, often makes the case for buying up to the AED 2M Golden Visa threshold if the finances allow.

## How to Choose: A Decision Framework

The right route depends on four variables: your age, your primary income or asset type, how long you want the visa to last, and whether you need to bring family.

**You are under 55, freelance or self-employed, income above AED 360K/year, no major UAE property:**
→ Green Visa is the natural fit. Lower cost than a Golden Visa property route, no property required, 5-year stability.

**You are under 55, remote employee, income at least USD 3,500/month, testing UAE lifestyle:**
→ Remote Work Visa for year 1. Evaluate whether you want to pivot to Green Visa or buy property in year 2.

**You are an investor at any age, buying UAE property at AED 2M+:**
→ Golden Visa attached to the purchase. Ten-year validity and full family sponsorship make it the best long-term choice for property buyers.

**You are 55+, with liquid savings or passive income rather than UAE property:**
→ Retirement Visa via the savings (AED 1M) or income (AED 20K/month) route. No property purchase required.

**You are 55+, buying AED 2M+ in UAE property:**
→ Both Golden Visa and Retirement Visa may be available. Golden Visa offers longer validity (10 years vs 5) and slightly broader rights, generally the better choice at this asset level.

Government fees exclude medical fitness tests (AED 300–600), Emirates ID (AED 370), typing centre charges (AED 200–500), and legal or consultancy fees. Health insurance, mandatory for all UAE residents, costs AED 700–3,500 per adult per year depending on coverage level.

The Remote Work Visa looks expensive on an annualised basis compared to the 10-year Golden Visa. That comparison is fair only if you can actually qualify for the Golden Visa. For people in their first UAE year or with income below AED 2M property-entry levels, the Remote Work Visa is the accessible path, not a suboptimal one.

## Family Sponsorship: What Each Route Allows

All five routes permit some form of family inclusion, but the scope varies:

- **Golden Visa:** Full sponsorship of spouse and children. Widest rights. Children may hold dependent visas until they reach age of majority or obtain their own residency.
- **Retirement Visa:** Spouse and dependent children. Accommodation and insurance per dependent required.
- **Green Visa:** Spouse and children under current ICP rules, but noted as "limited" in official guidance, verify applicable ICP bulletin at the time of application as this has been subject to clarification.
- **Remote Work Visa:** Spouse and dependant children permitted, with each requiring health insurance and separate processing fees.
- **Lower-tier Property Investor Visa:** Limited family sponsorship. Generally requires the sponsor to also demonstrate monthly income above a set salary threshold (historically AED 10,000/month or more) to bring a spouse.

If family sponsorship is a priority, the Golden Visa is the most robust option. If you are a solo expat at the start of a UAE career, the distinctions matter less immediately.

## Practical Application Notes

All five routes are processed through the UAE Federal Authority for Identity and Citizenship (ICP) and, in Dubai, through the General Directorate of Residency and Foreigners Affairs (GDRFA). The practical steps look similar across routes:

1. Confirm eligibility threshold is met (property value, income, savings, or professional credentials)
2. Gather documentation: passport, photos, qualifying evidence (title deed, bank statements, employment contract, freelance permit, etc.)
3. Apply for entry permit via GDRFA/ICP portal or accredited typing centre
4. Complete medical fitness test in the UAE (can often be done at government health centres for AED 300–600)
5. Apply for Emirates ID via ICP
6. Receive visa stamp; Emirates ID card issued within 5–10 working days typically

You generally must be physically in the UAE during the final stages of processing. Remote Work Visa applications can be initiated online but require in-country completion. If your plan involves property purchase and Golden Visa simultaneously, note that the DLD title deed must typically be issued before the visa file is lodged, completion and title issuance are the trigger.

## A Note on UAE Tax Residency

UAE residency and UAE tax residency are separate legal statuses. Holding any UAE visa establishes legal residence; establishing UAE tax residence requires meeting the 183-day physical presence test (or the new centre-of-interests test under the 2023 Cabinet Decision) and then actively managing, or eliminating, ties to your previous tax jurisdiction.

For residents of high-tax countries (UK, Germany, France, Australia, Canada), UAE residency alone does not eliminate home-country tax obligations. Those countries tax based on your own exit rules: the UK's Statutory Residence Test, Germany's extended unlimited tax liability rule for high earners, Australia's domicile test, and so on. Each has its own breaking point.

The Remote Work Visa, Green Visa, and Golden Visa all create the physical-presence foundation for UAE tax residency. What you do with that residency, in terms of where you bank, where you maintain ties, and how you document days, determines whether you actually achieve the tax outcome you are aiming for. This is not a DIY exercise. Engage a cross-border tax adviser before relocating.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I switch from a Remote Work Visa to a Golden Visa?**
Yes. If during your Remote Work Visa year you buy qualifying UAE property (AED 2M+) or meet another Golden Visa category, you can apply for the Golden Visa and cancel the Remote Work Visa. You do not need to leave the country to make this transition, though the application must go through normal channels.

**What happens if the property value drops below AED 2M after I get the Golden Visa?**
The Golden Visa is tied to the registered property value at the time of application. A subsequent drop in market value does not automatically cancel your visa. The threshold matters most at the point of renewal; if the registered value falls below AED 2M by renewal time, you would need to acquire additional qualifying assets or switch to another eligible category.

**Can I hold two UAE residency visas simultaneously?**
No. UAE residents hold one active residency visa at a time. If you hold a Remote Work Visa and subsequently apply for a Golden Visa through a property purchase, the Remote Work Visa must be cancelled before the new visa is activated.

**Is there a UAE residency route based purely on business ownership without active income?**
The Property Investor Visa and the Golden Visa (investor route) are the closest equivalents for pure capital deployment. Operating a UAE company through a free zone or mainland licence with minimal personal income typically falls under the Green Visa framework, which requires demonstrating business activity and income.

**How long can I stay outside the UAE without losing residency?**
Standard employment and family visas are cancelled if the holder remains outside the UAE for more than 6 continuous months. The Golden Visa is commonly cited as allowing longer absence periods, though the exact rules are applied at authority discretion and have been subject to updates. Remote Work, Green, and Retirement Visa re-entry rules generally follow standard residency obligations unless specific exceptions apply. Check current ICP and GDRFA guidance for your visa category before extended travel.
