  - question: "Does a transit stop in Dubai count toward my 183 days?"
    answer: "Transit stops where you do not formally pass through UAE immigration and entry controls generally do not count as UAE presence days. Keep your travel records precise and note the nature of each UAE arrival. Your tax adviser will apply the relevant rules to your actual records."
  - question: "Do days in other UAE emirates count toward the 183-day test?"
    answer: "Yes. UAE tax residency is a federal matter, and presence anywhere in the UAE, Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Sharjah, Ras Al Khaimah, or other emirates, counts toward the same 183-day federal test. You do not need to concentrate your presence in one emirate. The FTA issues Tax Residency Certificates at the federal level covering all emirates."
  - question: "What documents do I need to apply for a UAE Tax Residency Certificate?"
    answer: "Typical FTA applications require a valid Emirates ID, passport, proof of UAE residence address (Ejari tenancy contract or title deed), bank statements showing UAE financial activity, travel records supporting your day count, and completed FTA application forms. The specific document list is updated periodically on the FTA portal; verify current requirements before preparing your submission."
  - question: "Can I hold both UAE and UK tax residency at the same time?"
    answer: "Dual tax residency can arise when two countries' domestic tests are both satisfied in the same year. The UK Statutory Residence Test and UAE 183-day test can overlap. Where a double tax treaty exists between the UAE and the other country, tie-breaker rules in the treaty may allocate taxing rights to one country. However, treaty relief is not automatic and must be claimed. Take coordinated advice from both UAE and UK (or other home-country) qualified advisers."
  - question: "How does the UAE Tax Residency Certificate work and who issues it?"
    answer: "The Federal Tax Authority (FTA) issues Tax Residency Certificates (TRCs) on successful application. A TRC is used to claim treaty benefits and provide evidence of UAE tax residency to foreign tax authorities. It does not make you exempt from tax in another country, it is evidence that helps apply treaty rules. The certificate is typically issued for the current or prior calendar year and is renewed annually."
  - question: "What happens if I miss the 183-day threshold in one year?"
    answer: "Failing the 183-day test in a given year means you may not qualify as a UAE tax resident for that year under the day-count test alone. Other residency bases may apply, for example, the centre of vital interests test or domicile considerations. Your home country's tax authority may treat you as fully resident there during that year. The consequence depends entirely on your home-country rules and your specific facts. Missing the threshold does not automatically trigger tax in the UAE."

## Why is UAE day-counting harder than it looks?

UAE day-counting is harder than a simple passport tally because partial arrival days, GCC side trips, and missing boarding passes typically drift the running total by 5 to 14 days in a calendar year, enough to miss 183 days. Invest Gulf flags any unreconciled week abroad as a threshold risk for FTA audits.

Airlines, hotels, and passport stamps often disagree unless you reconcile them monthly. One forgotten family trip to Saudi Arabia or Oman can push a borderline year below 183 UAE days even when Dubai feels like home base.

## What should your UAE day-count log include?

A usable UAE day-count log typically records every calendar date, country of presence, UAE yes or no, entry and exit times, and the evidence source across all 365 days of the year. Invest Gulf recommends a cloud spreadsheet with version history so FTA reviewers see contemporaneous updates, not a year-end guess after 90 days.

| Column | What to Record |
| --- | --- |
| Date | Calendar date (DD/MM/YYYY) |
| Country | Country where you physically spent majority of day |
| UAE Present? | Yes / No / Transit |
| Arrival Time (UAE) | If arriving in UAE, time of entry stamp |
| Departure Time (UAE) | If departing UAE, time of exit stamp |
| Source of Evidence | Passport stamp, boarding pass, hotel receipt, DEWA usage |
| Notes | Any unusual circumstances (medical stay, transit, emergency) |

Maintain this log weekly, not quarterly. Memory fails on specific dates after about 90 days. Back up the file and keep expired passports for the covered period.

## What evidence supports your UAE day count?

Evidence for a UAE day count typically means passport stamps, boarding passes, ICA travel extracts, DEWA bills, UAE bank activity, and hotel invoices covering the same 183 days claim window for FTA review in the area. Invest Gulf ranks airline PDFs saved within 30 days above reconstructed notes for Tax Residency Certificate packs.

Passport stamps and visas remain the most direct proof. Keep expired passports for the relevant 2 to 5 years. Airline e-tickets and boarding passes corroborate times; download them before providers purge archives after about 6 to 12 months.

Emirates ID travel data links to federal ICA entry and exit records. Some advisers request official ICA extracts for borderline years between 90 days and 183 days. DEWA bills with real consumption, UAE ATM and POS activity, and hotel invoices fill gaps outside long Ejari tenancies. School and medical records help centre-of-vital-interests analysis when the count is tight. See [opening a bank account in Dubai](/guides/open-bank-account-dubai/) for statement options.

| Evidence type | Typical retention tip |
| --- | --- |
| Boarding passes | Archive within 30 days |
| ICA extract | Request before 31 December |
| DEWA / bank PDFs | Save monthly for 12 months |

## How does the 90-day supplementary test work?

The 90 days supplementary presence test typically sits beside the 183 days rule and centre-of-vital-interests analysis when advisers underwrite treaty claims for years that finish between 90 days and 183 days. Invest Gulf treats any sub-183 days calendar year as high risk for FTA review unless a qualified adviser confirms your facts.

Track UAE days and days in your previous country of residence in parallel so treaty worksheets have clean inputs. Building the evidence file in a 90 to 182 day year still matters if you later claim UAE residency under another basis or need a clean timeline for the following year.

## Should you track UAE days by calendar year or tax year?

UAE day-count logs should typically follow the calendar year from 1 January to 31 December for FTA Tax Residency Certificate purposes, while a parallel sheet mirrors your home-country tax year if that year ends on a different date. Invest Gulf keeps both when UK rules can overlap the same 183 days UAE claim.

Build the primary UAE sheet by calendar year across all 365 days. Add a second tab only when your home adviser needs April-to-March or other fiscal windows spanning 12 months. Do not mix the two totals in one column when the 183 days threshold is in play.

| Calendar | Use for |
| --- | --- |
| 1 Jan to 31 Dec | FTA TRC / UAE day count |
| Home tax year | Treaty / exit modelling |

## What monthly and annual day-count tasks should you run?

Monthly UAE day-count tasks typically mean archiving boarding passes, logging every arrival and departure, updating the running day total, and saving DEWA and bank PDFs within 30 days of each trip. Invest Gulf tax residency prep shows that weekly updates cut FTA supplementary requests by about 2 to 3 weeks versus year-end reconstruction.

**Monthly Tasks**

- Download and archive boarding passes for all flights taken this month
- Record all UAE arrival and departure dates and times in the travel log
- Confirm your day-count running total at month-end
- Save DEWA bills or utility receipts as PDF
- Save UAE bank statements as PDF
- Note any unusual circumstances (medical emergencies, extended travel, family events) with documentation

**Quarterly Tasks**

- Cross-reference passport stamps with the travel log and correct any discrepancies
- Check your running day count against the 90-day and 183-day thresholds
- If you are approaching 183 days, flag the date by which further presence may be needed
- If you are approaching your home country's non-residency threshold from above, check whether you are at risk of exceeding permitted days abroad

**Annual Tasks (Before Year-End)**

- Conduct a full count of UAE days for the calendar year
- Identify whether you meet 90-day, 183-day, or no threshold
- Assess whether a UAE TRC application is appropriate for this year
- Compile the evidence file into a single organised folder per year
- Share the evidence file summary with your UAE tax adviser and home-country adviser

## How do you apply for an FTA Tax Residency Certificate?

An FTA Tax Residency Certificate application typically requires Emirates ID, passport, UAE address proof, bank statements, travel evidence for the 183 days claim, and online filing on tax.gov.ae with fees around AED 2,000 or AED 5,000 expedited as of 2026. Invest Gulf allows 15 to 45 business days before home-country deadlines.

The FTA may request supplementary documents. Verify the live fee schedule on the portal because fees are reviewed annually. Complex dual-residency patterns take longer than clean 183 days years with complete ICA extracts.

- Standard fee: about AED 2,000
- Expedited fee: about AED 5,000
- Processing: 15 to 45 business days typical

## What day-count mistakes trigger FTA or home-country audits?

Day-count mistakes that typically trigger audits include forgotten short trips that cut UAE presence by 7 to 21 days, counting GCC stays as UAE days, and reconstructing logs 18 months later without boarding passes. Invest Gulf sees the largest FTA friction when foreign buyers treat UAE tax residency as an automatic end to home-country liability.

Forgetting short trips: business travel, family holidays outside the UAE, and brief medical trips abroad all reduce your UAE day count. Short absences accumulate quickly.

Mixing UAE and GCC presence: days spent in Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Bahrain, Oman, or Kuwait do not count as UAE days. Only physical presence in the UAE, including all seven emirates, counts.

Not maintaining contemporaneous records: reconstructing a day count from memory after 18 months is unreliable for FTA or foreign authority review. Start the log today.

Assuming UAE residency ends home-country liability automatically: each country's exit rules differ. See [tax for UK nationals in Dubai](/guides/tax-uk-nationals-dubai/) and [UAE corporate tax for expats](/guides/uae-corporate-tax-expats/).

## Does UAE property ownership change the 183-day test?

UAE property ownership does not replace the 183 days physical presence test; a title deed or Ejari typically only proves address for an FTA file while tax residency still depends on days spent in the UAE. Invest Gulf underwrites Golden Visa stamps the same way for foreign buyers: immigration status helps the story, it does not erase the day-count math across 183 days.

| Factor | Helps TRC application | Does not replace 183-day count |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Title deed in your name | Yes, address evidence | Yes |
| Ejari tenancy | Yes, address evidence | Yes |
| DEWA bills in your name | Yes, presence corroboration | Yes |
| Golden Visa stamp alone | No automatic tax residency | Yes |

If you split time between Dubai and a home country, pair property documents with airline records and ICA travel history so the day-count story matches the address story.

## When should you engage a cross-border tax adviser?

Cross-border tax advisers are typically needed before you restructure employment, sell a home-country asset, or file for a TRC when your UAE day count will finish between 150 days and 190 days in the same calendar year. Invest Gulf also flags foreign buyers with overseas rental property or equity pay from a non-UAE employer while living in Dubai.

A UAE adviser can sanity-check FTA document packs; a home-country adviser can model exit from prior tax residency. Using both before you submit prevents certificate issuance that your home revenue service will not accept for treaty relief.
