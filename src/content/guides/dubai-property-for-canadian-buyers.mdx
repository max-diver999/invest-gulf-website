## How do Canadian tax rules apply to Dubai property?


Practical example: A Canadian tax resident owns a JVC apartment generating AED 70,000 annual rent (approximately CAD 26,250). The rent is deposited in a UAE bank account.

- UAE tax: AED 0
- Canadian tax: CAD 26,250 reportable as worldwide income
- Canadian tax due: At marginal rate (15%, 20.5%, 26%, 29%, or 33% federally + provincial)
- Foreign tax credit: None available (UAE tax rate 0%)

Provincial variations: Combined federal-provincial rates vary significantly:
- Alberta: 25 to 39% (lowest combined rates)
- Ontario: 29.65 to 46.16%
- Quebec: 37.12 to 53.31% (highest in Canada)

### Capital gains tax implications

Canadian tax residents face capital gains tax on Dubai property disposals:

| Element | Canadian treatment |
|---|---|
| Acquisition cost | Purchase price + DLD 4% + transaction costs in CAD |
| Capital gain | Sale proceeds minus adjusted cost base |
| Inclusion rate | 50% of capital gain is taxable |
| Principal residence exemption | Not available for Dubai investment property |
| Foreign exchange | Gains/losses calculated in CAD at historical rates |

Currency conversion rules: CRA requires using Bank of Canada exchange rates on transaction dates. Significant AED-CAD fluctuations can create additional gains or losses beyond Dubai property appreciation.

### T1135 foreign income verification statement

Mandatory filing: Canadian tax residents with foreign property over CAD 100,000 must file Form T1135 annually.

| T1135 Category | Dubai property reporting requirement |
|---|---|
| Real property outside Canada | Property address, acquisition cost, current value |
| Rental income | Gross rental income in CAD |
| Disposition details | Sale price, costs, capital gain/loss |
| Property management | Related expenses and management arrangements |

Penalties for non-filing:
- Under CAD 100,000: CAD 25/month (max CAD 2,500)
- CAD 100,000 to 1 million: CAD 100/month (max CAD 12,000)  
- Over CAD 1 million: CAD 500/month (max CAD 12,000)

Due date: T1135 must be filed by April 30 (same as personal tax return deadline).

### Departure tax (deemed disposition)

Critical for emigrating Canadians: When you cease Canadian tax residency, CRA deems you to have disposed of all worldwide assets at fair market value, including Dubai property.

| Departure tax scenario | Tax consequence |
|---|---|
| Dubai property appreciated | Pay CGT on 50% of accrued gain without selling |
| Dubai property depreciated | Cannot claim capital loss offset |
| Property held in corporation | Different rules apply, complex |
| Principal residence (if applicable) | Exemption generally not available for Dubai property |

Departure tax mitigation:
- Elect to post security instead of paying tax immediately
- Time property sale before emigration to control gain recognition
- Consider corporate structures (requires professional advice)
- Verify tax treaty benefits with destination country

Example: Canadian resident emigrating to UAE owns Dubai property that appreciated CAD 300,000. Departure tax applies to CAD 150,000 (50% inclusion) at marginal rates, potentially CAD 45,000 to 75,000 in taxes owed without property sale.

## Does Dubai property qualify Canadians for the Golden Visa?


### UAE tax residency considerations

UAE tax residency triggers:
- 183+ days annually in UAE, or
- Permanent home in UAE + substantial economic interests

Canadian tax residency interaction:
- Golden Visa ≠ automatic UAE tax residency
- Canadian tax residency continues until formal departure
- Tax treaty benefits may apply if dual residency

Business opportunities with Golden Visa:
- UAE company formation for Middle East operations
- Access to regional free zones
- Banking and investment opportunities
- No requirement for UAE employment or local sponsor

### Planning UAE residency transition

For Canadians considering UAE tax residency:

Year 1-2: Preparation phase
- Establish UAE Golden Visa through property purchase
- Set up UAE banking and business relationships  
- Begin spending increased time in UAE (but under 183 days)
- Maintain Canadian tax residency for transition planning

Year 3-4: Transition phase
- Gradually reduce Canadian residential ties
- Establish UAE permanent home
- Transition business operations to UAE
- Plan departure tax timing if becoming UAE resident

Year 5+: UAE residency
- Confirm UAE tax residency through 183+ day test
- Complete Canadian departure procedures if applicable
- Benefit from UAE 0% personal income tax
- Maintain property portfolio in tax-free jurisdiction
