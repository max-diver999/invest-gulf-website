## How do SARB exchange controls affect Dubai property purchases?

SARB exchange controls require South African residents to use Authorized Dealer channels, tax clearance, and the R12 million annual offshore allowance before Dubai property funds can leave the country. Invest Gulf SARB checklist sequencing still treats MP336 declarations and documentation retention as mandatory, not optional paperwork.

| Status | Exchange control impact |
|---|---|
| South African resident | R12M annual limit applies |
| Formal emigrant (completed) | No exchange control limits |
| Temporary resident abroad | Still subject to R12M limit |
| Returning resident | Limits reinstated upon return |

1. Tax clearance from SARS (3 to 6 weeks)
2. Authorized Dealer application at a SA bank
3. Foreign investment allowance declaration
4. Funds transfer through approved channels only
5. MP336 statistical reporting
6. Keep files for SARB audit

Unauthorised transfers can attract penalties around 10% of the amount moved, with forced repatriation risk on non-compliant deals.

## What South African tax obligations apply to Dubai property?

South African tax obligations on Dubai property mean SARS taxes worldwide rental income for residents at marginal rates up to 45%, even though UAE local tax is 0%, and CGT uses a 40% inclusion rate for individuals. Invest Gulf tax checklist examples use AED 70,000 annual rent (about R332,000) to show the missing foreign tax credit.

South African tax residents cannot treat Dubai rent as invisible because the money sits in a UAE account. A Business Bay apartment generating AED 70,000 annual rent (about R332,000) creates AED 0 UAE tax and still produces full SARS foreign-income reporting at marginal rates of 18% to 45%, with no foreign tax credit available at a 0% UAE rate. On disposal, individual CGT inclusion is 40% after the R40,000 annual exclusion, and rand depreciation against AED/USD can enlarge the taxable gain beyond local property appreciation. Formal emigration can trigger deemed disposal above R10 million, which is why many buyers complete emigration before a major Dubai purchase. Our underwriting models SARS drag and departure-tax timing before calling Dubai a pure rand hedge for continuing residents.

| Element | South African treatment |
|---|---|
| Rental income | Worldwide income if SA tax resident |
| CGT inclusion | 40% individuals; higher for companies |
| Annual CGT exclusion | R40,000 |
| Emigration deemed disposal | Relevant above R10 million asset bands |

## What checklist should South African buyers run before they sign?

South African buyers should run an 18-point pre-signing checklist covering SARS tax clearance, R12M allowance capacity, emigration timing, Authorized Dealer transfer, DLD registration, UAE banking, Golden Visa above AED 2M, and ongoing SARS reporting. Invest Gulf signing checklist methodology keeps exchange-control certainty ahead of brochure yield.

South African purchases fail more often on paperwork sequencing than on Dubai title mechanics. Tax clearance from SARS can take 6+ weeks, Authorized Dealer approval must precede the UAE trustee transfer, and MP336 declarations need to match the property purpose stated in the allowance letter. Average tickets of AED 1.8 to 3.2M usually consume a large share of the R12 million annual capacity, so multi-year allowance planning matters for couples or staged buys. After title, owners still need UAE banking for rent, a DIFC or UAE Will for succession, and annual SARS foreign-income reporting if they remain tax resident. Golden Visa filing at AED 2M supports emigration evidence but does not itself end South African tax residency. Invest Gulf deal desks therefore refuse to treat a signed SPA as "done" until the SARB file, DLD transfer, and post-purchase compliance calendar are all mapped.
