## SARB exchange controls: the regulatory framework

### Foreign investment allowance structure

South African tax residents can purchase Dubai property using annual offshore investment limits:

| Allowance type | Annual limit | Requirements |
|---|---|---|
| Foreign investment allowance | R1 million | Automatic for residents |
| Discretionary allowance | R11 million | Tax clearance certificate required |
| Combined annual limit | R12 million | Per person, per calendar year |
| Unused allowances | Cannot carry forward | Use-it-or-lose-it annually |

### Authorized Dealer process

**Mandatory steps for Dubai property purchase:**

1. **Tax clearance certificate** from SARS (3–6 weeks processing)
2. **Authorized Dealer application** at South African bank
3. **Foreign investment allowance declaration** (specify Dubai property purpose)
4. **Funds transfer** through approved channels only
5. **MP336 declaration** for statistical reporting
6. **Retention of documentation** for SARB audit compliance

### Exchange control exemptions

**Emigrants gain significant advantages:**

| Status | Exchange control impact |
|---|---|
| South African resident | R12M annual limit applies |
| Formal emigrant (completed) | No exchange control limits |
| Temporary resident abroad | Still subject to R12M limit |
| Returning resident | Limits reinstated upon return |

**Emigration completion requirements:**
- SARB approval of emigration application
- MP336(b) foreign asset declaration
- South African bank account closure/conversion
- SARS emigration tax clearance

### Non-compliance penalties

| Violation | Penalty structure |
|---|---|
| Unauthorised transfers | 10% of transferred amount |
| False declarations | Criminal prosecution possible |
| Documentation failure | Administrative penalties |
| Forced repatriation | Required return of funds to SA |

## South African tax obligations on Dubai property

### Worldwide income taxation

SARS taxes global income for South African tax residents, creating obligations on Dubai property ownership:

| Your status | SARS tax on Dubai rent |
|---|---|
| South African tax resident | Full worldwide taxation, report Dubai income |
| Formal emigrant | No SARS tax on foreign income |
| Temporary abroad (work/study) | Remain SA resident, worldwide taxation |
| Returning resident | Resume worldwide taxation obligations |

**Practical example:** South African tax resident owns Business Bay apartment generating AED 70,000 annual rent (approximately R332,000). Rent deposited in UAE account.

- **UAE tax:** AED 0  
- **SARS tax:** R332,000 reportable as foreign income
- **SARS tax due:** At marginal rate (18%, 26%, 31%, 36%, 39%, or 45%)
- **Foreign tax credit:** None available (UAE rate 0%)

### Capital gains tax implications

South African tax residents face CGT on Dubai property disposals under worldwide taxation:

| Element | South African treatment |
|---|---|
| Capital gain | Sale price minus base cost in Rand |
| CGT inclusion rate | 40% for individuals, 80% for companies |
| Annual exclusion | R40,000 per person |
| Foreign exchange | Gains calculated at disposal date exchange rates |
| Emigrant timing | Pre-emigration disposal = SA CGT; post-emigration = no SA tax |

**Currency conversion impact:** Rand depreciation against AED/USD creates additional CGT beyond Dubai property appreciation. If rand weakens 15% during holding period, effective capital gain includes currency movement.

### Emigration tax (departure charge)

**Critical for emigrants:** Formal emigration triggers deemed disposal of worldwide assets exceeding R10 million:

| Asset value | Emigration tax treatment |
|---|---|
| Under R10 million | No emigration tax |
| R10-20 million | Pay CGT on deemed disposal |
| Over R20 million | Submit valuation, pay CGT on excess |
| Dubai property timing | Purchase after emigration avoids departure charge |

**Strategic timing:** Many South Africans complete emigration first, then purchase Dubai property without South African tax implications.

## Dubai property as rand hedging strategy

### Currency protection mechanics

Dubai property provides natural hedge against rand volatility through AED-USD peg:

| Hedging element | Benefit mechanism |
|---|---|
| AED currency peg | Stable vs USD, appreciating vs rand historically |
| Rental income | USD-equivalent income vs rand depreciation |
| Capital preservation | Property values track USD, not rand |
| Liquidity option | Convert to hard currency through property sale |

**Historical context:** Rand depreciated approximately 8-12% annually vs USD over past decade, making USD-pegged assets attractive for capital preservation.

### Hedging effectiveness analysis

**Scenario modeling (5-year holding period):**

| Scenario | Rand-USD movement | Dubai property impact | Net hedging benefit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Continued depreciation | -10% annually | +61% in rand terms | Strong hedge |
| Moderate depreciation | -5% annually | +28% in rand terms | Moderate hedge |
| Rand stability | Flat | Neutral | Property appreciation only |

**Tax drag on hedging:** South African tax on rental income and CGT reduces hedging effectiveness for residents. Emigrants capture full currency protection without tax erosion.
