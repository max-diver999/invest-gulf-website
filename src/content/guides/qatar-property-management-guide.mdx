Kahramaa (Qatar General Electricity and Water Corporation) is Qatar's sole utility provider. The connection lifecycle for a rental property:

**At initial handover (from developer):**
1. Developer transfers Kahramaa connection to your name.
2. Pay Kahramaa deposit: QAR 1,000–3,000 depending on property type.
3. Confirm meter numbers match the unit title deed.

**At each new tenancy:**
1. Landlord (or manager) has Kahramaa in landlord name with a minimum activity.
2. New tenant submits Kahramaa transfer application with signed lease.
3. Kahramaa transfers connection and bill responsibility to tenant name.
4. Tenant pays their own utility bills directly from this point.

**Between tenancies (vacancy period):**
1. When previous tenant departs, they request Kahramaa disconnection.
2. Landlord (via manager) reconnects in landlord name.
3. Standing minimum charges apply during vacancy: approximately QAR 150–300/month.
4. This creates a cost during vacancy that most yield models ignore: budget it in.

**Kahramaa red flag:** If the previous tenant failed to formally transfer Kahramaa back to landlord name, the disconnection-reconnection process takes longer and may reveal outstanding utility balances under the prior tenant's account that the landlord is not responsible for - but that can delay new tenant connection.

Require your property manager to confirm Kahramaa reconnection in landlord name within 72 hours of each tenancy end.

### Remote Landlord Registration Process

1. **Grant Power of Attorney to property manager**: specific to lease registration authority. This can be done with a Qatar-authenticated POA or through the property management agreement if it explicitly grants this authority.

2. **Manager prepares Arabic-language lease**: must comply with Qatar standard tenancy law format. English translation for your reference, Arabic is the operative version.

3. **Both parties sign**: your POA allows manager to sign on your behalf.

4. **Manager submits to MOJ**: typically within 5 business days of lease start.

5. **Registration certificate issued**: confirms lease is in the official database.

Keep digital copies of: MOJ lease registration certificate, signed lease (Arabic and English), Kahramaa account number in tenant name, tenant identification documents.

### Maintenance Authorisation Structure

Set clear financial limits in your management agreement:

- **Under QAR 500**: Manager can proceed without approval (emergency response).
- **QAR 500–2,000**: Manager notifies owner by email, proceeds unless objected to within 24 hours.
- **Over QAR 2,000**: Requires explicit owner approval with quote from named contractor.

This structure lets the manager handle minor issues promptly without the delays that lead to tenant dissatisfaction and early lease termination. Annual AC service (typically QAR 200–500 per unit) should be scheduled proactively each April before summer - include this in your manager's SLA.

| Water pressure loss | Occasional | QAR 200–800 | Landlord (building system) |
| Intercom/entry system | Occasionally | QAR 300–1,000 | Landlord |
| AC filter cleaning | Quarterly | QAR 100–200 | Tenant |
| Balcony waterproofing (Pearl, humid) | Every 3–5 years | QAR 2,000–8,000 | Landlord |

Qatar's extreme summer humidity (May–September) makes balcony sealing and AC maintenance higher priority than in dryer Gulf climates. Budget for an annual AC service and a balcony inspection every 3 years in waterfront buildings.

### Tax Reporting for Qatar Rent

Qatar levies no income tax on individuals. Rental income from Qatar property is not taxed in Qatar. However:

- Your home country may tax Qatar rental income - check CRS/FATCA reporting obligations.
- Municipality tax (5% of annual rent) is typically the tenant's payment - but verify it is being paid through your registered lease.
- Maintain records: all SC receipts, management fee statements, maintenance invoices - needed for home-country tax reporting and for any Qatar property capital gain calculation on exit.
