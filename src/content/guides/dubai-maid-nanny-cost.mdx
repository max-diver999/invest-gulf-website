# Dubai Maid and Nanny Costs 2026: Live-In, Part-Time, and Full Comparison

TL;DR: Live-in domestic workers in Dubai cost AED 1,800–3,500/month in salary plus employer obligations covering accommodation, meals, annual flights, and medical insurance, total annual employer cost AED 28,000–55,000. Part-time agency cleaners cost AED 35–60/hour with no visa obligation. Nannies and childcare specialists command AED 2,500–5,000+/month for live-in and AED 50–100/hour for part-time. Domestic help at all levels is significantly more accessible in Dubai than in most Western cities, and most expat families with children employ some form of domestic assistance.

> **Legal note:** UAE domestic worker employment is governed by Federal Law No. 10 of 2017 and subsequent MOHRE regulations. Minimum wage, rest day entitlements, accommodation standards, and contract requirements apply. This guide covers financial cost, always verify current legal requirements with MOHRE (Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation) or a licensed recruitment agency.

## Why Domestic Help Is Central to Dubai Expat Life

Unlike in most Western countries, domestic employment in Dubai is standard across a wide range of income levels. Several factors drive this:

**Cost accessibility:** A live-in maid in Dubai costs less than an iPhone payment plan in monthly gross terms, relative to the salaries earned by professional expats. For families where both adults work full-time, the norm in Dubai's dual-income expat community, the economics of domestic help look very different than in high-cost Western labour markets.

**The climate factor:** Heat management, pool maintenance, and keeping a Dubai home clean during sandstorm season generates housework volume that genuinely benefits from additional hands. The practical burden of running a household in Dubai at standards most expats are accustomed to is higher than temperate climates.

**Cultural normalisation:** In Dubai's expatriate community, domestic employment is normalised across nationalities. The absence of social stigma around employing household staff, which exists in some Western contexts, makes the conversation easier to have and the decision straightforward to act on.

### Employer Obligations Beyond Salary

UAE law requires employers (sponsors) to provide:

**Accommodation:** A private room with adequate space for a live-in worker. Many apartment families convert a utility room, study, or maid's room into domestic staff accommodation. The law specifies adequate ventilation, privacy, and space, but enforcement is through complaint-based processes.

**Meals:** Three meals daily, or equivalent food provision.

**Annual return flight:** One return ticket to the worker's home country per year at the employer's cost. Philippines-Dubai round trips cost approximately AED 1,200–2,000 depending on season and booking lead time. Sri Lanka, India, and Indonesia routes typically run AED 800–1,500.

**Medical insurance:** Employers are legally required to provide medical insurance. A basic Dubai health insurance policy for a domestic worker costs approximately AED 600–1,200/year. Premium covers are higher.

**One rest day per week:** Workers are entitled to one day off per week. The specific day is agreed in the employment contract.

**Annual leave:** 30 calendar days per year after the completion of each year of service, or upon termination for workers who have completed less than a full year.

## Part-Time and Agency-Dispatched Cleaning Services

For residents who need cleaning help without the commitment, cost, and administrative responsibility of direct employment and visa sponsorship, licensed cleaning agencies offer flexible alternatives.

### How Agency Cleaning Works

Licensed domestic staffing agencies in Dubai employ cleaners directly, handling visa, salary, insurance, and labour law compliance. Clients pay the agency a per-hour or per-session rate. The client has no employer obligations and no visa responsibility.

This model suits:
- Singles and couples without children who need periodic deep cleaning
- Small families with modest cleaning needs
- Families in transition, newly arrived, between live-in arrangements, or on short-term leases
- Residents who want cleaning but value not managing an employee

### Agency Rate Benchmarks (Mid-2026)

**Standard home cleaning (2–4 hour session):**
- One-off booking: AED 40–65/hour
- Regular weekly booking: AED 35–55/hour
- Monthly subscription package: AED 30–50/hour

**Minimum session length:** Most agencies require a 2-hour minimum booking, making the effective minimum spend AED 80–130 per visit.

**Typical session costs:**
- 2-hour basic clean: AED 100–150
- 3-hour standard clean (1–2BR apartment): AED 140–200
- 4-hour deep clean (2–3BR): AED 180–280
- 5+ hour deep clean (villa, pre/post tenancy): AED 250–400+

### Notable Agencies in Dubai

Several licensed agencies operate widely across Dubai. Rates and service quality vary:

- **Justlife (formerly JustClean Home):** Popular app-based booking, transparent pricing, AED 35–50/hour on standard packages
- **ServiceMarket:** Comparison platform for multiple cleaning agencies; useful for price benchmarking
- **Helping:** Regional platform with professional cleaner booking across Dubai and Abu Dhabi
- **Maids4U, UAE Maids, Clean and Happy:** Established agencies with multiple client reviews; compare rates directly

**Quality consistency:** Agency cleaning quality varies by individual cleaner dispatched rather than agency brand alone. Requesting the same cleaner for repeat bookings, which most agencies accommodate, builds consistency.

## Nanny Options and Childcare Costs

Childcare in Dubai takes several forms beyond formal nursery and school settings.

### Live-In Nanny

A live-in nanny in Dubai typically has either a dedicated childcare role or a combined childcare and light housekeeping role. Cost depends heavily on:

- **Experience and qualifications:** Formal childcare qualifications (CACHE, BTEC, Norland-trained) command significant premiums, AED 5,000–10,000+/month for the most qualified UK or Australian-trained nannies
- **Language:** English-speaking nannies command 30–50% premiums over non-English speakers
- **Infant vs. school-age:** Newborn care specialists, particularly night nannies, earn premium rates

| Nanny Profile | Monthly Salary Range |
|--------------|---------------------|
| Combined maid/nanny (light childcare) | AED 2,200–3,500 |
| Dedicated nanny, general | AED 3,000–5,000 |
| Dedicated nanny, English-speaking | AED 3,500–6,000 |
| Qualified nanny (CACHE/Norland) | AED 6,000–12,000+ |
| Night nanny (newborn specialist) | AED 4,000–8,000 (if live-in) or AED 200–400/night |

### Part-Time Nanny and Babysitting

For families who do not require full-time live-in childcare, part-time nanny services provide flexible coverage.

**Part-time nanny agencies:**
- Babysits.ae, Nanny Lane, The Nanny Dubai: professional platforms with background-checked caregivers
- Rate range: AED 50–100/hour depending on caregiver profile and booking platform
- Regular part-time bookings (e.g., 5 hours per weekday): AED 1,000–2,500/month

**Ad-hoc babysitting:**
- Evening/weekend babysitting: AED 50–80/hour through app platforms
- Minimum 2–3 hours typical

### Au Pair Options

The UAE does not have a formal au pair visa category, workers must be on domestic worker visas. Some families arrange cultural exchange arrangements informally through communities like InterNations or expat Facebook groups, but these are not covered by the domestic worker legal framework and carry risks for both parties.

## Recruiting Domestic Staff

### Direct Recruitment From Abroad

Hiring from source countries (Philippines, Sri Lanka, India, Indonesia, Ethiopia, Kenya) involves:

1. Contracting a licensed recruitment agency in the source country
2. Agency conducts pre-screening, reference checks, and initial vetting
3. Employment contract signed and attested
4. Visa application submitted in UAE (see [domestic worker visa guide](/guides/dubai-domestic-worker-visa/))
5. Medical clearance in source country
6. UAE entry visa issued and worker travels

**Recruitment agency fee (source country):** AED 5,000–15,000 depending on nationality and agency. Filipino workers are subject to POEA-regulated recruitment fees (paid by employer, not worker). Sri Lanka and Indonesia have similar protections.

**Full recruitment cost (first year):**
- Agency fee: AED 8,000–15,000
- UAE visa and medical: AED 5,000–8,000
- First-year salary (AED 2,000/month): AED 24,000
- Annual flight: AED 1,500
- Medical insurance: AED 900
- **Total first-year: AED 39,400–49,400**

### Hiring Within UAE (Transfer)

Workers already in the UAE on valid domestic worker visas can be transferred to a new employer through MOHRE's transfer process. This eliminates overseas recruitment agency fees and reduces visa processing time.

- MOHRE transfer fee: AED 500–1,000
- New contract registration: AED 200–500
- Total transfer cost: AED 700–1,500 plus new employer medical insurance

Domestic workers seeking new employment within the UAE often advertise through:
- Facebook groups (Dubai Maid/Nanny Exchange groups are active)
- Filipino Community Dubai, Sri Lanka Social Community, Expat Mothers Dubai groups
- The Maid (app)
- Word-of-mouth among expat networks

Transfer hiring is faster, cheaper, and allows meeting the candidate in person before committing.

## Managing Domestic Staff in Dubai

### Contracts and Registration

All domestic worker employment contracts must be registered with MOHRE (Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation). The standard contract covers:

- Job description and duties
- Salary amount and payment schedule
- Working hours and rest day
- Accommodation and meal provisions
- Annual leave and return flight entitlement
- Notice period for termination
- Dispute resolution process

Contracts not registered with MOHRE carry legal risk for both employer and employee. Use the MOHRE digital services portal or Tasheel service centres for contract registration.

### Salary Protection System (SPS)

UAE's Wage Protection System (WPS) requires electronic salary payment for domestic workers. Salaries must be paid by the last working day of each month. Delayed payment beyond 10 days triggers an alert in the MOHRE system. Consistent late payment can result in employer fines and restrictions on sponsoring new workers.

### End of Service Benefits

Domestic workers in the UAE are entitled to end-of-service gratuity upon completion of at least one year of employment:
- 14 days' pay per year for the first 5 years
- 30 days' pay per year after 5 years

Budget approximately one additional month's salary per year of service as a provision for end-of-service obligations.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I employ a domestic worker if I rent an apartment rather than owning property?

Yes. Sponsoring a domestic worker visa requires UAE residency status for the employer (and sufficient income, typically AED 6,000+ per month minimum), not property ownership. Renters can and do sponsor domestic workers across Dubai.

### What happens if I leave Dubai but my maid's visa is still active?

You must cancel your domestic worker's residency visa when your own UAE residency ends or when you permanently leave the country. Failing to cancel leaves you with ongoing employer liability and can create issues with your own Emirates ID and residency status. MOHRE's cancellation process can be initiated online or at Tasheel centres.

### Are there any official minimum wage rules for domestic workers in Dubai?

As of 2026, the UAE does not mandate a national minimum wage for domestic workers (it applies to private sector workers above a certain income threshold). However, recruitment regulations from source countries like the Philippines (POEA) set minimum salary standards for OFWs, typically around AED 1,800–2,200/month. These standards are enforced at the source country recruitment stage.

### What is the difference between a maid and a domestic worker legally?

In UAE law, "domestic worker" is the umbrella term covering housemaids, nannies, drivers, cooks, gardeners, and security guards employed in a private household. All fall under the same Federal Law No. 10 of 2017 framework. "Maid" is colloquial terminology for the cleaning and housekeeping role within that category.
