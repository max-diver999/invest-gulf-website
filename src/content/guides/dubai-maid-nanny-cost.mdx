Always verify current legal requirements with MOHRE or a licensed recruitment agency.

## Why is domestic help normal for Dubai expat families?

Most dual-income expat households in Dubai budget for a live-in or part-time domestic worker because childcare, heat, and long commutes make full-time DIY housekeeping difficult. A live-in maid salary of AED 1,800 to 3,500/month is often lower than the opportunity cost of one parent reducing work hours, and part-time agency cleaning avoids visa sponsorship entirely.

Sandstorms, pool upkeep, and larger villas also push cleaning hours above what many Western cities expect. In Dubai's expatriate community, hiring household staff is mainstream across nationalities, which makes pricing and recruitment channels easier to research than in markets where domestic employment is rare.

## What does a live-in maid or nanny cost per month in Dubai?

Expect AED 1,800 to 3,500/month base salary for a general housemaid, with nannies who focus on childcare at AED 2,500 to 5,000+ depending on English fluency and qualifications. Salary is only part of the bill: UAE law treats the sponsor as the employer with ongoing obligations beyond the monthly transfer.

| Worker type | Typical monthly salary (AED) | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| General live-in maid | 1,800 to 2,800 | Cleaning, laundry, basic cooking |
| Experienced live-in maid | 2,500 to 3,500 | Larger villas, higher standards |
| Combined maid/nanny | 2,200 to 3,500 | Light childcare plus housekeeping |
| Dedicated nanny | 3,000 to 6,000 | English-speaking premium common |
| Qualified nanny (CACHE/Norland) | 6,000 to 12,000+ | Rare; corporate packages |

### Employer obligations beyond salary

UAE law requires sponsors to provide:

**Accommodation:** A private room with adequate space. Many apartment families use a maid's room or converted study. Standards are complaint-driven; specify layout before hire.

**Meals:** Three meals daily or equivalent food allowance.

**Annual return flight:** One round trip home per year. Philippines routes often run AED 1,200 to 2,000; India, Sri Lanka, and Indonesia routes commonly AED 800 to 1,500.

**Medical insurance:** Mandatory employer coverage, roughly AED 600 to 1,200/year for basic plans.

**Rest and leave:** One rest day per week and 30 calendar days annual leave after each completed year of service.

First-year sponsors also pay visa processing upfront, typically AED 5,000 to 8,000 on top of the table above. See the [domestic worker visa guide](/guides/dubai-domestic-worker-visa/) for line-item fees.

## How much do part-time cleaners and agency staff cost?

Licensed agencies employ cleaners on their own visas and bill you by the hour or session. You pay AED 35 to 60/hour for standard home cleaning in 2026, with weekly or monthly packages often AED 30 to 50/hour. Minimum bookings are usually two hours, so a single visit lands near AED 80 to 130.

| Session type | Typical cost (AED) |
| --- | --- |
| 2-hour basic clean | 100 to 150 |
| 3-hour clean (1 to 2BR) | 140 to 200 |
| 4-hour deep clean (2 to 3BR) | 180 to 280 |
| Villa deep clean (5+ hours) | 250 to 400+ |

Agency models suit singles, couples, and families who want cleaning without MOHRE employer duties. Platforms such as Justlife, ServiceMarket, and Helping are widely used; quality depends on the individual cleaner, so repeat bookings with the same person help consistency.

## What do nannies and part-time childcare cost in Dubai?

Dedicated nannies sit above general maid rates. English-speaking caregivers and newborn specialists command the top of the range. Part-time babysitting through vetted platforms often runs AED 50 to 100/hour; regular weekday blocks can total AED 1,000 to 2,500/month.

| Nanny profile | Monthly salary range (AED) |
| --- | --- |
| Combined maid/nanny (light childcare) | 2,200 to 3,500 |
| Dedicated nanny, general | 3,000 to 5,000 |
| Dedicated nanny, English-speaking | 3,500 to 6,000 |
| Night nanny (newborn specialist) | 4,000 to 8,000 live-in, or 200 to 400/night |

The UAE has no formal au pair visa. Informal cultural-exchange arrangements exist in expat groups but fall outside the domestic worker legal framework and carry compliance risk for both parties.

## How do you recruit a maid: overseas hire vs UAE transfer?

**Overseas recruitment** through a licensed agency in the worker's home country adds AED 5,000 to 15,000 in agency fees plus AED 5,000 to 8,000 for UAE visa and medical. A simplified first-year budget for a AED 2,000/month salary worker often lands near AED 39,000 to 49,000 all-in including flight and insurance.

**In-UAE transfer** from an existing domestic worker visa cuts cost and time. MOHRE transfer fees are commonly AED 700 to 1,500 plus new medical insurance. Workers advertise in Facebook groups and community networks; meeting in person before signing is standard.

All contracts must be registered with MOHRE and salaries paid through the Wage Protection System (WPS) by the last working day each month. Late payment beyond 10 days triggers MOHRE alerts.

## What end-of-service and exit rules apply?

After one year of service, domestic workers accrue end-of-service gratuity: 14 days' pay per year for the first five years, then 30 days' pay per year. Budget roughly one extra month of salary per year of service as a provision.

When you leave Dubai, cancel the worker's visa with your own residency cancellation. An active sponsored visa left open keeps employer liability on your file.

Renters with UAE residency (typically income above roughly AED 6,000/month, verify with MOHRE) can sponsor workers; property ownership is not required.

## How do Dubai maid costs compare with London or New York?

A full-time live-in package under AED 4,500/month all-in is far below equivalent live-out nanny costs in London or New York, where agency childcare alone can exceed USD 3,000/month. The trade-off is employer responsibility: you manage visa, insurance, accommodation, and MOHRE compliance, not an agency payroll only.

| City (indicative) | Full-time domestic help | Visa burden on household |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dubai live-in maid | AED 2,500 to 4,500/month all-in | Sponsor employer |

| London live-out nanny | GBP 2,500 to 4,000+/month | Work visa separate |
| New York nanny | USD 3,000 to 5,000+/month | Work visa separate |

## What checklist should you run before you sign a contract?

- Confirm MOHRE-standard contract and registered job description
- Inspect maid room ventilation, lock, and bathroom access
- Agree rest day and phone use rules in writing
- Set WPS bank account for salary before day one
- Keep copies of passport, visa, and insurance policy
- Plan annual flight booking window with worker input on dates

Source-country rules matter: Philippines POEA sets minimum salary bands for OFWs; Sri Lanka and Indonesia have similar protections enforced at recruitment stage. Never pass recruitment fees to the worker if source-country law assigns them to the employer.

## Are there official minimum wage rules for domestic workers?

The UAE does not publish one national minimum for all domestic workers, but source-country recruitment rules often floor salaries around AED 1,800 to 2,200/month for Filipino hires. Treat that as a compliance baseline when recruiting overseas, not as a market rate cap. Experienced English-speaking nannies in Dubai routinely earn far above those floors.

## What is the legal label for maids, nannies, and drivers?

Federal Law No. 10 of 2017 uses **domestic worker** for housemaids, nannies, drivers, cooks, and similar household roles. Your MOHRE contract job title and duty list should reflect real work, especially when childcare mixes with cleaning. Misclassified duties create dispute risk at MOHRE if expectations diverge.

## Relocation scenarios that change the budget

**Villa with pool:** Live-in help plus extra deep cleans after sandstorms. **Compact apartment:** Twice-weekly agency clean may beat sponsoring a visa. **Newborn year:** Short-term night nanny, then combined maid/nanny role. | Setup | You pay roughly | Sponsor visa? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Live-in maid all-in | AED 2,500 to 4,500 | Yes |
| Agency clean 8h/month | AED 300 to 600 | No |
| Part-time nanny block | AED 1,000 to 2,500 | No |
| Live-in nanny all-in | AED 3,500 to 7,000+ | Yes |

Figures aggregate salary, amortised visa, insurance, and flight; your floor plan and recruitment path move the total.

Update MOHRE and insurance tariffs once per year when you renew contracts; stale budgets are the main reason families underestimate year-one domestic help spend.

If you already hold UAE residency, check MOHRE sponsor income thresholds before you recruit from abroad; rejection after paying source-country agency fees is costly and slow to unwind.

Part-time agency cleaners remain the lowest-friction option if you are on a short Dubai lease and do not want to cancel a visa when you leave.
