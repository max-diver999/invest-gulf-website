## Why UAE Corporate Tax Now Matters for Expat Freelancers

The UAE built its reputation as a low-tax destination on the absence of personal income tax, and that remains true today: salaries, most personal investment returns, and rental income received by individuals are not subject to income tax in the UAE. However, the federal Corporate Tax that took effect for financial years beginning on or after 1 June 2023 changed the picture for anyone operating a business entity in the Emirates.

Expat freelancers, management consultants, IT contractors, content creators, coaches, and side-business owners who operate under a UAE freelance permit, mainland sole establishment, or free zone company are now within the scope of the corporate tax regime. Depending on revenue, profit, and structure, they may owe tax, may qualify for relief, or may have compliance obligations even when the tax liability is zero.

Understanding the basic framework is no longer optional. This guide sets out what expat freelancers actually need to know. It is written for general information purposes only and does not constitute tax advice for any specific situation. Always verify conclusions with a UAE-registered tax practitioner before making compliance decisions.

## Core Rate Structure and Scope

Federal Decree-Law No. 47 of 2022 established the UAE Corporate Tax framework. The rate structure relevant to freelancers and small business operators is straightforward:

| Taxable Income Band | Corporate Tax Rate |
| --- | --- |
| Up to AED 375,000 per tax period | 0% |
| Above AED 375,000 per tax period | 9% on the excess |
| Pillar Two qualifying multinationals | 15% minimum effective rate |
| Individual employment salaries | Outside corporate tax scope |
| Personal rental income (individual name) | Outside corporate tax scope (current practice widely applied) |

The tax applies to "Taxable Persons" — broadly, UAE-incorporated entities and natural persons conducting business under a UAE licence. A sole establishment operating under a freelance permit is a Taxable Person. A free zone company is a Taxable Person unless it qualifies for the special free zone regime.

Items outside scope include: salaries from employment, dividends received from UAE subsidiaries (generally), and personal investment income held outside a business entity. The distinction between business income and personal income is therefore critical.

## Freelance Permits and Sole Establishments

most expats in the UAE operate under freelance permits issued by authorities such as TECOM, twofour54, Abu Dhabi-based free zones, or under mainland sole establishments with the DED. For UAE corporate tax purposes, a sole establishment is treated as the business of the individual operating it, not as a separate legal entity.

This has several practical implications:

**Revenue and expenses** of the freelance activity are assessed on the business, and net taxable income is calculated after allowable deductions. If taxable income exceeds AED 375,000, the 9% rate applies to the excess.

**Registration with the FTA** via the EmaraTax portal is required for businesses with revenue exceeding AED 1 million in a calendar year. Businesses below this threshold may register voluntarily. Note that the registration obligation is based on revenue, while the tax liability is based on net taxable income — these can differ significantly depending on your cost structure.

**Tax periods** align to the entity's financial year. If you commenced operations mid-year, your first period may cover fewer than 12 months. The first tax period beginning on or after 1 June 2023 triggers the regime.

Freelancers earning below AED 375,000 net taxable profit pay no corporate tax. However, those exceeding AED 1 million in revenue have a registration and filing obligation even if the effective tax owed is zero.

## Small Business Relief: The Key Threshold for Most Freelancers

The Federal Tax Authority introduced Small Business Relief as a transitional support measure. Under this provision, eligible businesses with total revenue below AED 3 million in a tax period (and in all prior periods since June 2023) may elect to be treated as having no taxable income. The effect is that no corporate tax is owed, regardless of actual profit level.

**Key eligibility conditions:**

- Revenue in the relevant tax period must be below AED 3 million.
- Revenue must also have been below AED 3 million in every prior tax period since June 2023.
- The business must not be part of a multinational enterprise group subject to Pillar Two rules.
- The election must be made in the tax return for the relevant period.
- The relief is available for tax periods ending on or before 31 December 2026.

**Practical illustration:** A freelance consultant billing AED 2 million per year with significant expenses generates, say, AED 900,000 in net profit — well above the AED 375,000 zero-rate band. Without relief, the corporate tax on AED 525,000 excess profit would be approximately AED 47,250. With Small Business Relief elected, and assuming the revenue test is met, the tax liability would be zero.

Crucially, Small Business Relief does not eliminate registration or filing obligations for entities that cross the AED 1 million revenue threshold. You must still register with the FTA and file a return; you simply report that you have elected for relief and confirm the revenue qualification.

| Revenue Level | Likely Corporate Tax Position |
| --- | --- |
| Under AED 375,000 net profit, any revenue | 0% rate applies; registration may be required if revenue exceeds AED 1 million |
| Revenue under AED 3 million, Small Business Relief elected | Deemed nil taxable income; no tax owed; filing required |
| Revenue over AED 3 million | Standard 9% rate on profits above AED 375,000; no relief available |
| Free zone company with qualifying income only | 0% on qualifying income; 9% on non-qualifying; substance required |

## Free Zone Companies: Qualifying vs Non-Qualifying Income

A substantial portion of the expat business community operates through UAE free zones — DIFC, DMCC, ADGM, Dubai Internet City, Dubai Media City, RAKEZ, or others. These zones were historically marketed as tax-free environments, and the Corporate Tax law preserves a 0% rate for eligible free zone businesses. However, the conditions are specific and not automatic.

**To benefit from the 0% Qualifying Free Zone Person rate**, an entity must:

1. Be incorporated or registered in a recognised UAE free zone.
2. Earn predominantly Qualifying Income — broadly, income from transactions with other Free Zone Persons, income from overseas customers, or certain specific categories of passive income.
3. Maintain adequate economic substance in the free zone (genuine operations, not just a registered address).
4. Not elect to be subject to standard corporate tax rates.
5. Prepare audited financial statements.
6. Not earn non-qualifying income above de minimis limits.

**Where free zone companies commonly encounter problems:** Revenue from UAE mainland clients, most domestic professional service fees, and intercompany income from mainland group entities often fails the qualifying income test. If you operate a DMCC company and your primary clients are UAE-based businesses, a material portion of your revenue may be subject to 9% tax.

The Ministry of Finance and FTA have published ministerial decisions and guides on qualifying income categories. These are detailed and technical. Do not assume free zone status equals tax exemption without obtaining professional confirmation based on your actual client profile and income structure.

## Allowable Deductions: What You Can Offset Against Taxable Income

The corporate tax law adopts a broadly commercial approach to deductions. Allowable expenses include items incurred wholly and exclusively for the purpose of earning taxable income. Commonly relevant for freelancers:

- Office rent, co-working memberships, and home-office costs (with limitations)
- Professional indemnity insurance and business insurance premiums
- Accounting, legal, and advisory fees
- Software subscriptions used for business
- Equipment and hardware (subject to depreciation rules)
- Travel and accommodation costs directly related to business activities
- Salaries paid to employees, if any

**Not deductible:** Personal expenditure routed through the business, payments to related parties not at arm's length, certain entertainment costs above limits, and fines or penalties.

The practical challenge for sole-trader freelancers is separating personal and business spending. The FTA expects clear records: invoices, bank statements, contracts, and evidence of business purpose. Maintain separate bank accounts for business income and expenses. ## Registration, Filing, and Penalty Framework

Corporate tax registration in the UAE is done through the EmaraTax portal at tax.gov.ae. The process requires a UAE Emirate ID, trade licence or freelance permit details, and financial information.

**Filing obligations after registration:**

- A corporate tax return must be filed within nine months of the end of the tax period.
- Payment of any tax due is required by the filing deadline.
- Amended returns may be filed within a specified period if errors are identified.

**Penalty structure** (subject to FTA updates): Late registration attracts fixed penalties. Late filing and late payment attract percentage-based penalties on amounts due. Maintaining accurate books reduces the risk of inadvertent non-compliance.

The FTA publishes detailed guidance on its website, and guidance is updated as cases are decided and interpretations clarified. Treat the FTA website as the primary source for current requirements.

## Interaction with UAE Tax Residency and Home-Country Obligations

Corporate tax and personal tax residency are separate frameworks. Being a UAE tax resident under the 183-day test or property-based test does not exempt your UAE business from corporate tax, but it also does not create a personal income tax obligation in the UAE. The UAE still does not levy personal income tax.

The more complex question is how UAE corporate tax interacts with your home-country obligations. Consider these scenarios:

**UK residents with UAE businesses:** HMRC's controlled foreign company rules may attribute profits of a UAE business back to a UK-resident shareholder depending on whether the UAE business is effectively managed and controlled in the UK or meets exemptions. UK tax and UAE corporate tax are separate obligations; paying UAE corporate tax does not automatically eliminate UK liability on the same profits.

**German residents with UAE companies:** Germany's foreign tax rules (Außensteuergesetz) may apply to passive-income-type structures. German residents considering UAE company structures should take specific German tax advice alongside UAE advice.

## Key Takeaways

- UAE corporate tax at 9% applies to net business profit above AED 375,000 per year, earned by UAE business entities.
- Employment income, personal investment returns, and individual rental income remain outside corporate tax scope under current practice widely applied.
- Small Business Relief eliminates liability for entities with revenue under AED 3 million (available through tax periods ending 31 December 2026).
- Free zone exemptions are conditional on qualifying income and substance requirements — professional confirmation is essential.
- Registration and filing obligations apply at certain revenue thresholds regardless of whether tax is owed.
- UAE corporate tax does not eliminate home-country tax obligations in countries with worldwide taxation.
