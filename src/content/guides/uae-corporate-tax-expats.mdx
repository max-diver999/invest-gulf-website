## How does small business relief compare for Gulf buyers in 2026?

The Federal Tax Authority introduced Small Business Relief as a transitional support measure. Under this provision, eligible businesses with total revenue below AED 3 million in a tax period (and in all prior periods since June 2023) may elect to be treated as having no taxable income. The effect is that no corporate tax is owed, regardless of actual profit level.

**Key eligibility conditions:**

- Revenue in the relevant tax period must be below AED 3 million.
- Revenue must also have been below AED 3 million in every prior tax period since June 2023.
- The business must not be part of a multinational enterprise group subject to Pillar Two rules.
- The election must be made in the tax return for the relevant period.
- The relief is available for tax periods ending on or before 31 December 2026.

**Practical illustration:** A freelance consultant billing AED 2 million per year with significant expenses generates, say, AED 900,000 in net profit, well above the AED 375,000 zero-rate band. Without relief, the corporate tax on AED 525,000 excess profit would be approximately AED 47,250. With Small Business Relief elected, and assuming the revenue test is met, the tax liability would be zero.

Crucially, Small Business Relief does not eliminate registration or filing obligations for entities that cross the AED 1 million revenue threshold. You must still register with the FTA and file a return; you simply report that you have elected for relief and confirm the revenue qualification.

## How does this comparison stack up for Gulf investors?

A substantial portion of the expat business community operates through UAE free zones, DIFC, DMCC, ADGM, Dubai Internet City, Dubai Media City, RAKEZ, or others. These zones were historically marketed as tax-free environments, and the Corporate Tax law preserves a 0% rate for eligible free zone businesses. However, the conditions are specific and not automatic.

**To benefit from the 0% Qualifying Free Zone Person rate**, an entity must:

1. Be incorporated or registered in a recognised UAE free zone.
2. Earn predominantly Qualifying Income: broadly, income from transactions with other Free Zone Persons, income from overseas customers, or certain specific categories of passive income.
3. Maintain adequate economic substance in the free zone (genuine operations, not just a registered address).
4. Not elect to be subject to standard corporate tax rates.
5. Prepare audited financial statements.
6. Not earn non-qualifying income above de minimis limits.

**Where free zone companies commonly encounter problems:** Revenue from UAE mainland clients, most domestic professional service fees, and intercompany income from mainland group entities often fails the qualifying income test. If you operate a DMCC company and your primary clients are UAE-based businesses, a material portion of your revenue may be subject to 9% tax.

The Ministry of Finance and FTA have published ministerial decisions and guides on qualifying income categories. These are detailed and technical. Do not assume free zone status equals tax exemption without obtaining professional confirmation based on your actual client profile and income structure.
