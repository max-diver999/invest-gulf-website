## Who can buy property in Dubai as a foreigner?

Any nationality may purchase **freehold** in **DLD-designated zones**. Restrictions are geographic, not passport-based. Outside designated areas, ownership is generally limited to UAE nationals. In practice, every major investor community you see marketed internationally sits inside a designated zone.

You receive either a **Title Deed** (ready property) or **Oqood** registration (off-plan). Both are transferable, mortgageable, and inheritable under UAE law. Residency is not required at purchase; many buyers close first and apply for [Golden Visa property routes](/guides/uae-golden-visa-property/) afterward if value thresholds are met.

For ownership types, see [Freehold vs Leasehold Dubai](/guides/freehold-vs-leasehold-dubai/) and [Can foreigners buy property in the UAE](/guides/can-foreigners-buy-property-uae/).

## Which areas are designated freehold zones?

Dubai Land Department publishes the official zone list. Major freehold communities include Dubai Marina, JVC, Business Bay, Downtown, Palm Jumeirah, Dubai Hills, Dubai South, JLT, MBR City, and Creek Harbour. Older villa areas outside the map may remain non-freehold; verify on the DLD portal before paying a deposit.

| Buyer need | Often shortlists |
| --- | --- |
| Yield-focused | JVC, Dubai Sports City, International City |
| Golden Visa + lifestyle | Marina, Downtown, Business Bay at AED 2M+ |
| Premium hold | Palm, Emirates Hills, select DIFC-adjacent towers |
| Off-plan payment plans | Dubai South, MBR City, newer master communities |

## What are the DLD fees and closing costs for foreign buyers?

The headline number is the **4% DLD transfer fee** on the purchase price, paid at registration. Resale buyers also pay trustee fees, agent commission, and often a developer NOC on secondary stock in master communities.

| Cost item | Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DLD transfer fee | 4% of purchase price | Paid at trustee registration |
| Trustee fee | AED 4,000 (typical over AED 500K) | DLD-approved trustee |
| Agent commission | 2% + 5% VAT | Buyer pays on secondary; off-plan varies |
| Developer NOC | AED 500-5,000 | Secondary only |
| SPA legal review | AED 5,000-15,000 | Strongly recommended |
| DLD admin | ~AED 520 | Administrative charges |
| **Typical cash resale total** | **6-9% of price** | Before mortgage fees |

Off-plan: the 4% fee usually triggers at Oqood registration when you sign the SPA. Developers sometimes promote DLD or commission incentives; confirm in the payment schedule, not in a brochure alone. Ongoing holding costs are in [Dubai property taxes explained](/guides/dubai-property-taxes-explained/).

## What is the step-by-step purchase process?

**Secondary (ready) market:** agree price, sign Form F (RERA standard MOU), pay deposit (often 10%), apply for mortgage if needed, obtain developer NOC, book trustee appointment, pay DLD 4% and fees, receive Title Deed. Cash deals with clean titles often finish in 2-3 weeks; mortgaged deals run 4-8 weeks.

**Off-plan:** verify RERA registration and escrow account, sign SPA, pay booking fee (often 5-10%), developer registers Oqood, pay instalments into escrow, snagging at handover, final payment, conversion to Title Deed.

| Stage | Ready resale | Off-plan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Legal check | DLD Unit Profile | RERA project + escrow |
| Deposit | Form F + cheque | SPA booking fee |
| Registration | Title Deed at trustee | Oqood, then deed at handover |
| Typical duration | 2-8 weeks | Years to handover |

## Can foreigners get a UAE mortgage?

Yes. Emirates NBD, Mashreq, FAB, HSBC UAE, and others lend to non-residents subject to policy. Non-residents usually need **25% minimum down payment** (residents often 20%), full income verification, and property valuation.

| Item | Typical non-resident terms |
| --- | --- |
| LTV | Up to 75% on first property (bank policy varies) |
| Rate | EIBOR-linked, often 3.5-5.5% p.a. indicative |
| Fees | ~1% arrangement, AED 2,500-3,500 valuation, 0.25% mortgage registration |
| Timeline | 3-6 weeks from application to offer |

Self-employed buyers should expect two years of audited accounts. Developer post-handover payment plans are not mortgages but can replace bank leverage on off-plan; read late-payment penalties in the SPA.

## What risks hit foreign buyers hardest?

**Title risk:** Only the DLD Unit Profile (via Dubai REST or trustee) proves ownership and encumbrances. **SPA risk:** Developer contracts favour the developer on delays and defects; independent review is cheap relative to price. **Payment fraud:** Confirm escrow or trustee account details by phone on a known bank number, not email alone. **Broker risk:** Insist on a valid RERA BRN; unregistered "agents" have no DLD standing.

Remote purchase checklist:

1. Independent solicitor pulls Unit Profile and reviews SPA
2. DLD-approved registration trustee manages transfer
3. Notarised POA if you do not fly in
4. Written escrow confirmation before any SWIFT
5. Oqood or Title Deed copy from trustee, not only from the seller's agent

## Which buyer profiles fit Dubai in 2026?

**Profile A (yield, AED 700K-1.2M):** Studios and one-beds in JVC or Sports City; gross yields often 7-9% before service charges and management. Check Mollak service charge history per building.

**Profile B (Golden Visa, AED 2M-3M):** Marina, Business Bay, or entry Downtown for 10-year visa eligibility; model service charges at AED 18-28 per sq ft in many towers.

**Profile C (premium, AED 3M+):** Palm or prime Downtown for lifestyle and capital stability; holiday-home licensing can lift revenue but needs DET permits.

**Profile D (first Gulf purchase, AED 500K-900K):** Lower entry in JVC; watch oversupply pockets and snagging quality on newer towers.

## What red flags should stop a deal?

1. Seller refuses Unit Profile or Oqood proof before deposit  
2. Wire instructions that change by email without verbal confirmation  
3. Off-plan project not on RERA escrow list  
4. Developer with long delivery slippage and thin completion track record  
5. Asking price more than 10% above DLD transacted comps  
6. Agent discouraging independent legal review  

Tier-one developers (Emaar, Nakheel, Meraas, Aldar on Dubai projects) still require SPA diligence; brand reduces delivery risk, not contract risk.

Dubai offers the deepest resale liquidity in the GCC, which matters for exit and refinancing. Abu Dhabi and Bahrain can offer lower entry tickets with smaller secondary markets. For portfolio-level context, read the [Dubai property investment guide](/guides/dubai-property-investment-guide/).

| Personal CGT | None on individuals | Check local rules |

| Transaction volume | Very high | Lower outside Dubai |

## What happens when a foreign owner sells?

You sell through a RERA-registered broker, agree Form F, obtain NOC if required, clear service charges and DEWA, and transfer via trustee with the same 4% DLD fee paid by the buyer in standard practice. Mortgage settlement happens at trustee before deed release. Repatriation of proceeds uses your UAE bank account; non-residents should confirm source-of-funds documentation early with the bank.

Plan 4-8 weeks for a clean resale similar to purchase. Keep Form F, original SPA, and service charge receipts for buyer due diligence.

## What should you verify before signing anything?

Confirm zone status, Unit Profile or Oqood, escrow for off-plan, total fee stack including 4% DLD, mortgage pre-approval letter if financed, and independent SPA review. Compare at least three transacted comps from DLD Price Map or a licensed agent with DLD access.
