Most Golden Visa articles describe the rules as they stand on the day of writing, which is exactly why so much of what you read about the property route is wrong: the rules have changed materially at least four times since 2019, and undated advice from 2021 or 2023 still ranks and still circulates. This page is the opposite format, a dated log of what changed and when, kept so a buyer can tell whether the claim in front of them is current or a fossil.

How the current rules work in practice is covered in the [UAE Golden Visa property guide](/guides/uae-golden-visa-property/). This page is the history that explains them.

Insider tip: before acting on any Golden Visa claim, ask one question, what date does this rule carry, because roughly half the qualification advice still circulating online describes the pre-2022 five million dirham regime or the pre-2024 down-payment practice, and both read as plausible to anyone who does not know the timeline.

## Why keep a dated log of Golden Visa property rules?

Because the property route has changed at least four times in seven years, and each change left a layer of outdated advice behind it that still circulates. The threshold fell 60 percent in one reform, from AED 5 million to AED 2 million in October 2022, and the financing rules were rewritten twice more after that.

A dated log solves a specific problem: distinguishing a rule that is wrong from a rule that was right when written. An article claiming the Golden Visa needs AED 5 million of property is not fabricating, it is describing 2021 accurately. An agent insisting a mortgaged buyer must first pay in AED 1 million is repeating what was genuinely common practice before January 2024. Neither claim survives contact with the current rules, and neither can be evaluated without knowing when the rules moved. The log below dates every material change, so each claim you meet can be placed on the timeline rather than argued about in the abstract.

## What has changed in the property route since 2019?

The full timeline, in one table. Each entry is dated to when the change took practical effect, not when it was first rumoured, and the detail sections below expand the three changes that matter most to a buyer today.

| When | What changed | Practical effect |
|---|---|---|
| 2019 | Long-term residence visas launch | Property route opens: AED 5M for a 5-year visa, AED 10M tier for 10 years |
| April 2022 | Cabinet Decision No. 65 of 2022 announced | Overhaul of entry and residence rules signalled, detail to follow |
| October 2022 | Reform takes effect | Property threshold reset to AED 2M for a 10-year visa; off-plan on Oqood and mortgaged property brought into scope |
| 2023 | Emirate-level alignment | Abu Dhabi practice consolidates around DMT-registered value in Law 13 of 2019 zones at the same AED 2M line |
| January 2024 | Down-payment evidence removed | Minimum paid-in amount on mortgaged and off-plan files, frequently cited as AED 1M, no longer required; registered value governs |
| 2026 | Two-year investor visa updated | The old AED 750K sole-owner threshold on the separate 2-year route was removed; joint owners generally need AED 400K+ registered share each |

Three things stand out from the table. The big reset happened once, in October 2022, and everything since has been refinement around financing evidence and adjacent routes. The AED 2 million figure itself has now held for almost four years. And the most recent movement is not on the Golden Visa at all but on the cheaper two-year route beside it, which changes the comparison for buyers below the AED 2 million line.

## What did the October 2022 reset actually change?

Three things at once, which is why it remains the single most consequential date in the log. The threshold for the ten-year visa fell from AED 5 million to AED 2 million of registered property value, off-plan property registered on Oqood from approved developers became eligible before handover, and mortgaged property became eligible with a bank no-objection letter.

The scale of that reset is easy to understate. At AED 5 million the property route was a premium product touching a thin slice of the market; at AED 2 million it reaches a mainstream two-bedroom in a metro-linked Dubai community, which is precisely the stock analysed in [which units clear the AED 2M line](/guides/aed-2m-golden-visa-best-value-units/). Bringing Oqood into scope moved the visa decision to the point of booking rather than handover, a change covered in detail in the [off-plan Golden Visa guide](/guides/golden-visa-off-plan-property-uae/). And admitting mortgaged property meant a buyer no longer needed AED 2 million in cash, only AED 2 million in registered value, which repriced the route for salaried professionals. Any article or agent claim that predates October 2022 describes a different programme and should be read as history.

## What did the January 2024 change do for mortgaged buyers?

It removed the requirement to evidence a minimum paid-in amount before applying. Before January 2024, mortgaged and off-plan applicants were commonly asked to show a paid contribution, frequently cited as AED 1 million or half the value, before the file would progress. Since the change, eligibility is generally assessed on the registered value of AED 2 million or more, regardless of how much of it is financed.

For a financed buyer the arithmetic moved substantially. Under the old practice, a buyer of an AED 2 million unit at 75 percent loan-to-value had paid in AED 500,000 and then faced a gap to the commonly requested AED 1 million before the visa file would move; after January 2024 the same buyer can apply on the registered value with a bank no-objection letter, holding only the 25 percent deposit plus roughly 7 to 9 percent in transaction costs. The bank NOC remains the live requirement on mortgaged files, and practice can still differ file by file, which is why the [mortgage Golden Visa guide](/guides/golden-visa-mortgage-property-uae/) treats the NOC timeline as the critical path rather than the down payment.

## What changed on the 2-year investor visa in 2026?

The adjacent route moved, not the Golden Visa itself. In Dubai's 2026 update to the two-year property investor visa, the old AED 750,000 sole-owner threshold was removed, and joint owners generally need an individual registered share of at least AED 400,000, with current practice administered through the DLD Cube Centre.

This matters to the log because the two routes are persistently confused, and a dated entry on one is routinely reported as a change to the other. The two-year visa is a shorter, cheaper, renewable route with lighter benefits; the Golden Visa remains a ten-year route tied to AED 2 million in registered value, and nothing in the 2026 update touched that threshold. For a buyer below AED 2 million the update is genuinely useful, since it widens access to residency at lower price points, and the full comparison between the routes is set out in [Golden Visa versus investor visa](/guides/golden-visa-vs-investor-visa-uae/). For a Golden Visa applicant it changes nothing.

## What has not changed since October 2022?

The core of the programme has been stable for almost four years, which is itself information. The AED 2 million registered-value threshold has not moved. The ten-year term has not moved. Qualification still rests on registered value with DLD or DMT, not on the price in your sale agreement. Family sponsorship under the primary applicant's visa has remained in place throughout.

Stability matters because every market cycle produces the same two rumours: that the threshold is about to rise, and that the programme is about to be restricted. Both have recurred since 2022 and neither has happened as of August 2026. A useful discipline is to ask what instrument a change would require: the threshold sits at Cabinet-decision level, so a genuine change arrives as a numbered decision reported by official channels, not as an agent's urgency line in a sales conversation. Renewal mechanics, which is where holders actually meet the rules a second time, are covered in the [renewal requirements guide](/guides/golden-visa-renewal-requirements-uae/).

## How do Dubai and Abu Dhabi differ on the property route?

The federal threshold is identical and the registration machinery is not. Dubai files rest on Dubai Land Department title deeds or Oqood certificates; Abu Dhabi files rest on Department of Municipalities and Transport registration, in zones designated under Law 13 of 2019, including Al Reem Island, Yas Island, Saadiyat Island, Al Maryah Island and Masdar City.

| Element | Dubai | Abu Dhabi |
|---|---|---|
| Threshold | AED 2M registered value | AED 2M registered value |
| Registering authority | Dubai Land Department | Department of Municipalities and Transport |
| Off-plan instrument | Oqood certificate | DMT registration of the sale |
| Zone basis | Designated freehold areas | Law 13 of 2019 investment zones |
| Typical stock at the line | Two-bed apartments, outer townhouses | Larger floor plates, thinner resale |

The practical differences are in stock and process rather than principle: Abu Dhabi tends to deliver more floor area per dirham at the threshold with a slower exit, and emirate-level document practice differs enough that a file prepared for one authority should not be assumed to travel to the other unchanged. The Abu Dhabi side is covered in the [Abu Dhabi Golden Visa property guide](/guides/abu-dhabi-golden-visa-property/).

## Which Golden Visa claims are most often out of date?

Four claims account for most of the stale advice we encounter, and each one was true once, which is what keeps it alive. Dating the claim is the fastest way to kill it.

| The claim you will hear | When it was true | Status now |
|---|---|---|
| "You need AED 5 million of property" | 2019 to October 2022 | Superseded; threshold is AED 2M |
| "Off-plan does not qualify" | Before October 2022 | Superseded; Oqood-registered off-plan qualifies |
| "Mortgaged buyers must pay in AED 1M first" | Common practice to January 2024 | Superseded; registered value governs, NOC still requested |
| "The 2-year visa needs AED 750K" | Before the 2026 update | Superseded; sole-owner floor removed, joint owners AED 400K+ each |

There is a fifth category: claims that were never true in any period, usually guarantees, promises that a specific tower confers a visa, or that an agent can secure approval. Qualification is assessed by the authorities on the registered file, and no intermediary controls the outcome. That family of claims belongs to the [scams and red flags guide](/guides/dubai-property-scams-red-flags/) rather than to this log.

## How should you use this log before a purchase?

Treat it as a filter, not as advice. When a claim reaches you, place it on the timeline: if it matches a superseded row, discard it and the source with it; if it matches the current rules, verify it once against the official channels and proceed. The authorities that decide are ICP and GDRFA on the visa side and DLD or DMT on the registration side, and their answer on a specific file outranks every page on the internet, this one included.

Two habits keep a buyer current between updates to this page. First, date-stamp everything you are told, in writing, because an agent who will not put a date on a rule is quoting from memory. Second, before relying on any figure here, check the updated date at the top of this page and the primary sources for anything decided after it. This log records changes through August 2026; the rules in force on your application date are the ones that bind, and the purchase itself should stand on rent and exit before the visa enters the decision at all.
