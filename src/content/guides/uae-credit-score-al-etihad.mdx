Al Etihad Credit Bureau (AECB) is the UAE's centralised credit data repository, a federal government entity established in 2014 that collects and stores credit information from every licensed bank, finance company, and telco operating in the country. Every UAE bank underwriting a mortgage, personal loan, or credit card checks the AECB report as a core part of its decision. Understanding how your score is built, what it means, and how it interacts with mortgage eligibility is essential knowledge for any expat planning to borrow in the UAE.

| Score band | Risk rating | Mortgage impact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 300 to 619 | High risk | Most mainstream banks decline; limited specialist options |
| 620 to 649 | Medium-high risk | Some approvals with higher rates and stricter conditions |
| 650 to 699 | Medium risk | Standard mortgage products accessible; average market rates |
| 700 to 749 | Low-medium risk | Better LTV ratios, rate discounts available |
| 750 to 799 | Low risk | Competitive rates; most products accessible |
| 800 to 900 | Very low risk | Best available rates and terms; preferred client status |

## What Is Al Etihad Credit Bureau?

Al Etihad Credit Bureau typically means the UAE's federal credit repository established in 2014, an information intermediary rather than a lender, covering more than 7 million individuals and 700,000 businesses as of 2025. Every licensed bank and finance company must contribute, so a missed card payment at one bank appears when you apply for a mortgage at another.

Invest Gulf checklist:
- Treat AECB as the single UAE credit file across all banks
- Buy the AED 84 full report before any mortgage shortlist
- Remember AECB is not a bank and does not approve loans itself

Every major UAE bank, Emirates NBD, ADCB, FAB, HSBC UAE, Mashreq, CBD, and others, is a mandatory data contributor. This means your entire UAE credit history, across all institutions, is consolidated into a single report.

## How the UAE Credit Score Works

AECB scores typically combine payment history, utilisation, credit age, product mix, and recent inquiries, with payment history the largest factor and negatives able to weigh for up to 5 years after closure. Utilisation above about 30 percent of card limits often traps scores in the 620 to 680 band even when every payment is on time.

Invest Gulf checklist:
- Keep revolving balances under 30 percent of each limit
- Pause hard inquiries for 6 months before a mortgage file
- Use the AECB app for soft checks that lenders never see

| Factor | What UAE banks review | Practical habit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Payment history | Missed or late EMIs and card bills | Auto-debit every UAE loan and card |
| Utilisation | Balance vs limit on revolving credit | Stay under 30% per card where possible |
| Credit age | How long accounts stay open | Keep oldest UAE card active with a small charge |
| Inquiries | Recent applications | Pause new credit 6 months before a mortgage |
| Product mix | Instalment vs revolving history | One instalment loan plus one card helps thin files |

## How to Check Your AECB Credit Score

Checking your AECB score typically costs AED 10.50 for a basic pull or AED 84 for a full tradeline report via aecb.gov.ae, the mobile app, or selected government centres, usually instant after Emirates ID verification. Self-checks are soft inquiries with zero score impact, unlike hard lender applications.

Invest Gulf checklist:
- Pull the AED 84 report at least 90 days before applying
- Dispute wrong balances and settled-but-open accounts immediately
- Never confuse a soft self-check with a hard bank inquiry

## How Your Credit Score Affects UAE Mortgage Eligibility

Credit score typically affects UAE mortgage eligibility through tighter in-house LTV and debt-service treatment even when Central Bank caps allow 80 percent LTV on properties under AED 5 million and 50 percent DSR. A file at 780 may still see full 80 percent LTV, while 640 can be capped near 70 percent LTV with higher margins.

- Model both Central Bank caps and bank score overlays

## What AECB score do UAE banks expect for mortgages?

Most mainstream UAE banks typically treat 620 as a practical floor for mortgage consideration, though some specialist desks may review 580 to 619 with 30 to 40 percent down payments. Scores from 650 to 699 unlock standard products; from 700 upward LTV and margin improve; above 750 preferential pricing is common.

Invest Gulf checklist:
- Target 700-plus before rate shopping across five banks
- Budget extra down payment if the score sits at 620 to 649
- Re-check the score after utilisation drops below 30 percent

| 620 to 649 | Approval possible with higher rates and tighter LTV |
| 650 to 699 | Standard resident products at market rates |

## How to Improve Your UAE Credit Score

Improving a UAE credit score typically starts with on-time auto-debit on every card and loan, utilisation under 30 percent, and a freeze on new applications for at least 6 months before a mortgage. Keeping a 4-year-old clean card open preserves credit age, while AECB disputes give banks 15 business days to respond before stale errors should clear.

Invest Gulf checklist:
- Auto-debit every UAE obligation before the statement date
- Keep old clean cards open with a small recurring charge
- File disputes for settled-but-open accounts and wait 15 business days

**Don't close old accounts.** If you have a credit card that is 4 years old with a clean payment history, keep it open even if you rarely use it. Closing it removes positive history and shortens your average credit age, both of which reduce your score.

Build a credit history deliberately. If you are new to the UAE and have a thin file, a secured credit card, where your credit limit equals a deposit you place with the bank, is the most reliable entry point. Use it for small purchases and pay it in full each month. After 12 months of clean history, the card typically converts to an unsecured product, and your file has 12 months of positive data.

## What habits damage UAE credit scores?

Habits that damage UAE credit scores typically include guarantor delinquencies, minimum-only card payments that leave utilisation high, and UAE card APRs of 36 to 42 percent on carried balances. Guarantor obligations appear on your AECB file, so another borrower's missed EMI can stain your mortgage readiness.

Invest Gulf checklist:
- Avoid guarantor signatures unless you can fund the full EMI
- Pay revolving balances down, not just the minimum due
- Treat 36 to 42 percent APR as a cashflow emergency, not a lifestyle

## How do expats and non-residents build AECB history?

Expats typically build AECB history by opening a UAE bank account on arrival, taking a modest credit card in month one, and running 12 to 24 months of clean use before a mortgage. A thin file means no score rather than a low score, which many banks still treat as limiting until tradelines exist.

Invest Gulf checklist:
- Open a salary account before chasing cards
- Prefer a secured card if unsecured limits are refused
- Follow the [open a bank account in Dubai guide](/guides/open-bank-account-dubai/) for first-month paperwork

Non-residents, those buying UAE property without a UAE residency visa, face additional challenges because they may have no AECB file at all. Non-resident mortgages are offered by some UAE banks but with stricter conditions: typically 25 to 35% minimum down payment, rates 0.5 to 1% above the resident equivalent, and income verification requirements that are more demanding. Some non-resident mortgage programmes are specifically designed for HNWI buyers; entry points generally start at AED 3 to 5 million property values.

For expats comparing banking options across the Gulf, particularly those who may also spend time in Qatar, Bahrain, or Saudi Arabia, credit bureaus operate separately in each jurisdiction. A clean AECB record in the UAE does not automatically translate into a positive credit profile in another GCC country. The [Gulf banking comparison guide](/guides/gulf-banking-comparison-expats/) covers how banking infrastructure and credit systems differ across GCC states.

## How do you dispute AECB report errors?

Disputing an AECB error typically means filing through the app or website with statements or settlement letters, after which the contributing bank has 15 business days to respond. Allow 30 to 60 days for correction before a mortgage application, because a pending dispute still shows as unresolved risk to underwriters.

Invest Gulf checklist:
- Attach proof of on-time payment or settlement with the dispute
- Diary the 15 business day bank response window
- Escalate to UAE Central Bank consumer protection if the bank refuses a clear error

## How does UAE credit reporting compare to other markets?

UAE credit reporting typically differs from many Western markets because banks are legally required to contribute data to AECB and to check it before extending credit above regulatory thresholds. There is no opt-out while you hold UAE financial products, so one missed EMI follows you across ENBD, FAB, ADCB, HSBC, and Mashreq alike.

Invest Gulf checklist:
- Assume every UAE bank sees the same consolidated file
- Do not rely on home-country scores to replace AECB history
- Build 12 to 18 months of UAE tradelines before a mortgage push

## What should you do before a UAE mortgage application?

Before a UAE mortgage application you typically need an AED 84 full AECB report pulled 90 days early, card utilisation below 30 percent, no new hard inquiries for 6 months, auto-debit on every obligation, and 6 months of primary UAE bank statements ready for underwriters. Most expats can move from a thin file to mortgage-competitive territory within 18 to 24 months with that discipline.

Invest Gulf checklist:
- Dispute errors immediately after the 90-day pre-check
- Keep utilisation under 30 percent across all revolving limits
- Review rates in the [Dubai mortgage rates guide](/guides/dubai-mortgage-rates-2026/) before choosing lenders

For buy-to-let files, score floors are similar but rental income is haircut in DSR math. The [buy-to-let mortgage in Dubai guide](/guides/buy-to-let-mortgage-dubai/) covers that overlay.
