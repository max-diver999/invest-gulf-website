Abu Dhabi pairs strong hospital capacity with a mature mandatory insurance regime under the Department of Health (DOH). Expats rarely pay fully out of pocket for routine care because employers must cover eligible workers and dependants, but plan tiers decide whether Cleveland Clinic, maternity packages, or dental work sit in network. Use this guide alongside the [Gulf healthcare comparison](/guides/gulf-healthcare-comparison/) and [Abu Dhabi cost of living](/guides/abu-dhabi-cost-of-living/) when you model a move.

Emergency care is available 24/7 through 998/999; keep your insurance card and Emirates ID accessible in your phone wallet. For planned elective surgery, ask both the hospital and insurer for written pre-authorisation numbers before admission so billing disputes do not delay discharge.

## Who regulates healthcare and insurance in Abu Dhabi?

The DOH licenses facilities, practitioners, and health products, runs the mandatory insurance framework for residents, sets quality standards, administers Thiqa for UAE nationals, and oversees public health programmes. The Regulation and Supervisory Bureau (RSB) within DOH licenses insurers and third-party administrators (TPAs).

The insurance mandate covers Abu Dhabi city, Al Ain, and Al Dhafra. Abu Dhabi rolled out mandatory cover earlier than Dubai (national schemes from 2006, extended to all residents over time), so insurer networks and employer compliance processes are well established. Cross-read [Abu Dhabi health insurance under DOH](/guides/abu-dhabi-health-insurance-doh/) for plan tiers and sponsor rules.

| DOH focus area | What it means for expats |
| --- | --- |
| Licensing | Only use DOH-licensed hospitals and clinics for insured care |
| Insurance mandate | Employer or self-sponsor must hold compliant cover before visa steps |
| Thiqa | UAE nationals only; expats use private employer or individual plans |
| RSB oversight | Insurer must meet benefit floors and network rules |

## Which public hospitals serve expats in Abu Dhabi?

Public facilities under SEHA (Abu Dhabi Health Services Company) accept expats at government tariffs, usually below private list prices. Emergencies are treated regardless of insurance, but follow-up billing depends on your plan.

### Sheikh Khalifa Medical City (SKMC)

Abu Dhabi's largest public hospital, with 600+ beds and full specialties including cardiology, oncology, neurology, orthopaedics, and emergency medicine. JCI accredited. Strong option when insurance is basic or when you need deep specialist capacity without private list fees.

### Mafraq Hospital

General hospital outside central Abu Dhabi, known for rehabilitation, disability services, and trauma care.

### Tawam Hospital (Al Ain)

Regional cancer referral centre with strong oncology, haematology, and bone marrow transplant programmes. Draws complex cases from across the UAE.

### Corniche Hospital

Public obstetrics and gynaecology hospital with high delivery volume, complex pregnancy expertise, and a leading NICU. Nationals use it heavily; expats can access at government tariffs.

### Oasis Hospital (Al Ain)

Not-for-profit hospital serving Al Ain and Al Dhafra since 1960, respected for primary care, general surgery, and community health.

## How do private hospitals compare for expats?

Private care dominates outpatient life for employed expats. Uninsured specialist visits at premium tiers are expensive; most households rely on Enhanced or Premium employer plans for private hospital networks.

| Service (typical uninsured band) | Indicative cost (AED) |
| --- | --- |
| Initial specialist consultation | 800-1,500 |
| Cardiac surgery | 80,000-200,000+ |
| Chemotherapy course | 50,000-300,000+ |
| Complex spinal surgery | 100,000-250,000+ |

### Cleveland Clinic Abu Dhabi

Flagship private hospital for cardiology, oncology, and neurology. Usually in-network only on Premium or Executive plans; Standard plans may exclude it or apply higher co-insurance.

### Mediclinic Abu Dhabi Network

Clinics and hospitals including Mediclinic Airport Road and Mediclinic Al Noor. Broad community coverage, international staff, DOH licensed.

### NMC Royal Hospital

Mid-to-premium private group strong in general medicine, maternity, and paediatrics; common in employer networks.

### Burjeel Hospital

Premium cardiac, oncology, and orthopaedic programmes under VPS Healthcare.

### LLH Hospital (Life and Luxury Health)

Family and women's health focus on Muroor Road, popular maternity option.

### HealthPlus Fertility Centre

Dedicated fertility clinic; IVF cycles often AED 15,000-35,000. Some plans cover limited fertility benefits after 12 months of enrolment.

## How does mandatory health insurance work in Abu Dhabi?

Employers arrange DOH-compliant plans for eligible employees and dependants. Self-sponsored residents, including many investor visa holders, buy individual cover that meets minimum benefits. Plans differ by salary band and employer policy, not by emirate marketing names alone.

| Plan tier | Typical employer cost (AED/year) | Key features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic (salary under AED 4,000) | 600-1,200 | Emergency, primary, restricted network |
| Standard | 2,500-6,000 | Broader network, caps near AED 150,000, some dental |
| Enhanced | 6,000-12,000 | Private hospitals, maternity, dental, AED 300,000+ limits |
| Premium | 12,000-25,000 | Cleveland Clinic access, international options, high limits |

Golden Visa and property-linked visa holders should read [health insurance for UAE property visas](/guides/health-insurance-property-visa-uae/) before assuming employer cover applies.

## What do uninsured expats pay for care?

Even with insurance, co-pays apply. Without cover, public SEHA tariffs and private list prices add up quickly.

**Public (SEHA) indicative tariffs for non-Emiratis:**

- GP consultation: AED 150-350
- Specialist consultation: AED 300-600
- Emergency visit: AED 200-500
- Overnight stay: AED 700-2,000/night

**Private list prices (no insurance):**

- Cleveland Clinic specialist: AED 800-1,500 per visit
- Mid-range private specialist: AED 400-800
- Private emergency assessment: AED 600-1,500

Budget housing and schooling alongside medical costs using the [Abu Dhabi cost of living guide](/guides/abu-dhabi-cost-of-living/).

## What does maternity care cost in Abu Dhabi?

Plan maternity before pregnancy: most policies impose 10-12 month waiting periods. Enhanced and Premium tiers often include AED 20,000-50,000 maternity caps.

| Setting | Typical expat cost band (AED) |
| --- | --- |
| Corniche Hospital (public) | 3,000-8,000 standard delivery |
| Mediclinic mid-range private | 15,000-35,000 package |
| Cleveland Clinic Abu Dhabi | 30,000-60,000+ without insurance |
| LLH Hospital | 20,000-40,000 package |

Midwife-led options exist at LLH but remain uncommon across the UAE. Confirm hospital network and cash limits on your Schedule of Benefits.

## How does dental care work for expats?

Employer basic plans often cap dental at AED 500-2,000 per year or exclude orthodontics. Budget separate dental cover or self-pay for cosmetic work.

| Procedure | Typical private range (AED) |
| --- | --- |
| Cleaning | 200-500 |
| Filling | 300-800 |
| Crown | 2,500-6,000 |
| Root canal | 1,500-4,000 |

SEHA polyclinics offer basic dental at government tariffs for routine maintenance. Premium plans may include annual cleaning in network.

## Are mental health services available in Abu Dhabi?

Yes, across public and private settings, though insurance caps vary and uptake is still growing.

- **Psychiatry:** SKMC (public), Cleveland Clinic, Mediclinic, NMC
- **Therapy and counselling:** Private practices, AED 300-700 per session; English, Arabic, and other languages available
- **Telehealth:** UAE platforms offer remote sessions for mild-to-moderate needs
- **Insurance:** Premium plans often include 10-20 sessions per year; Standard plans may exclude or cap mental health

## How do expats navigate appointments and claims?

Typical flow: choose in-network provider, present insurance card, pay co-pay at visit, let clinic bill insurer for approved services. Pre-authorisation applies for MRI, elective surgery, and some specialist referrals.

**Verify clinicians:** DOH Health Professionals Registry (hprlink.health.gov.ae) lists licence status, specialty, and workplace.

**Visa medical fitness test (MFT):** Separate from ongoing insurance. Required for residence visa renewal; screens communicable diseases at Preventive Medicine Centres. Results valid 30 days. See [Gulf residency by investment](/guides/gulf-residency-by-investment-guide/) for wider visa medical context.

**Emergency:** Call 998/999. Major trauma may route to SKMC or other public centres first; private ERs exist for insured elective emergencies.

## Common healthcare scenarios for new Abu Dhabi residents

**Employee with family:** Employer adds dependants on Standard or Enhanced plan; check pediatric network and school medical forms before term start.

**Golden Visa holder without local employer:** Buy DOH-compliant individual insurance before visa stamping; Premium tier if you expect Cleveland Clinic or US-style OB care.

**Dubai job, Abu Dhabi home:** Confirm dual-emirate network on insurer portal; some plans treat cross-emirate care as out-of-network.

**Chronic condition:** Request insurer pre-authorisation for ongoing specialists and medication; carry diagnosis letters from home country to avoid coverage gaps.

**Air ambulance and evacuation:** Rare for routine expat life, but international employers sometimes bundle evacuation riders on Premium plans. Read the fine print if you have cardiac history or active oncology treatment; evacuation to home country may still require upfront deposit when insurance disputes medical necessity.

## How do pharmacies, labs, and follow-up care work?

Most outpatient journeys start at a network GP or specialist, then move to in-hospital or standalone labs for blood work and imaging. Insurers often require pre-authorisation for MRI and CT; without it you may pay list price or wait for reimbursement. Pharmacies are widespread in malls and clinics; branded medications match international names but prices vary by pharmacy chain. Chronic medications (diabetes, hypertension, asthma) are usually covered on Enhanced and Premium tiers with co-pay per script. Keep digital copies of discharge summaries when transferring care from Dubai or home country, Abu Dhabi providers often request prior records before repeating expensive diagnostics.

Pediatric care routes through the same public and private split: SEHA facilities for emergencies and vaccinations, private paediatric clinics for routine growth checks. School medical forms typically ask for vaccination history aligned with DOH schedules. For neonatal intensive care, Corniche Hospital and major private maternity units remain the reference points cited by employers in Abu Dhabi relocation packs.

Employer HR teams sometimes issue a generic Dubai DHA card template; Abu Dhabi sponsors must still register DOH-compliant coverage. If your insurer letter shows a Dubai-only policy, fix it before visa medical steps to avoid fines or blocked renewals.
