**Dubai vs Abu Dhabi rental yield** is not a league table with a permanent winner. It is a **tenant-type, building-quality, and net-math** decision. Dubai's mid-market regularly advertises **7–9% gross** in Jumeirah Village Circle, Sports City, and Discovery Gardens. Abu Dhabi's comparable apartments in **Al Reem Island, Khalifa City, and parts of Yas** often print **5–7% gross** — but with a tenant base anchored in government, energy, and finance employment that can produce **lower vacancy** on long-term leases.

**Quick answer:** Dubai wins on gross yields with 7-9% in mid-market districts like JVC and Sports City, netting 5-7% after fees. Abu Dhabi offers 5-7% gross in Al Reem and Khalifa City, netting 4-6% but with lower vacancy from government tenants. Dubai wins for yield maximization, Abu Dhabi for stability.

This comparison focuses on **what reaches your account after fees**, not brochure gross percentages. Both emirates sit under **0% personal income tax** on local employment income. Neither guarantees yield. Both punish buyers who compare Marina gross yields to Yas premium villas without matching bed count, finish level, and service charge reality.

## Snapshot: yield comparison at a glance

| Factor | Dubai | Abu Dhabi |
|---|---|---|
| Typical mid-market gross (1-bed) | 7–9% | 5–7% |
| Typical net after fees (mid) | 5–7% | 4–6% |
| Primary tenant pool | Expat employment, tourism, STR | Government, ADNOC-linked, family long-let |
| Rent data source | Ejari / Dubai REST | ADREC / market reports |
| Short-let optionality | Strong in permitted buildings | Limited; long-let default |
| Transaction depth | 205,000+ deals (2024) | Smaller; thinner resale |
| Service charge range | AED 12–50+ / sq ft | AED 10–35+ / sq ft |
| Yield investor default | Dubai mid-market apartments | Al Reem / Khalifa long-let |

## Gross yield: where each emirate leads

### Dubai — yield leaders

Dubai's yield advantage concentrates in **mid-market apartments** where purchase prices stayed relatively accessible while employment-driven rent held.

| Community | Property | Indicative gross | Tenant type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jumeirah Village Circle | Studio / 1-bed | 7.5–9.2% | Expat long-let |
| Dubai Sports City | 1-bed | 7–8.5% | Sports / hospitality staff |
| Discovery Gardens | 1-bed | 7–8% | Budget expat |
| International City | Studio | 8–9%+ | High turnover, price-sensitive |
| Business Bay (mid towers) | 1-bed | 6–7.5% | Corporate |
| Dubai Marina | 1-bed | 5–7% | Corporate + STR option |
| Downtown Dubai | 1-bed | 4–5.5% | Premium, lower yield |

**Why Dubai gross runs higher:** larger population, more transacted rent data, active secondary market that keeps asking prices in check relative to achievable rent in [yield districts](/guides/best-dubai-areas-rental-yield/).

### Abu Dhabi — yield profile

Abu Dhabi yields are **flatter across districts** — fewer 9% gross outliers, more **5–7% clusters** with family-oriented tenancy.

| Community | Property | Indicative gross | Tenant type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Al Reem Island (mid) | 1-bed | 5.5–7% | Government, finance |
| Khalifa City | 2-bed | 5.5–7% | Family expat |
| Yas Island (select) | 1-bed | 5–6.5% | Entertainment, corporate |
| Saadiyat (premium) | 2-bed | 4–5.5% | Cultural / education sector |
| Al Reef | Townhouse | 5–6% | Family end-user + investor |

**Why Abu Dhabi gross can look lower:** master-planned premium stock carries higher entry price per sq ft; tenant demand favours **long-let stability** over maximum rent extraction.

## Net yield: the number that matters

Gross yield is annual rent divided by purchase price. **Net yield** subtracts the operating stack:

| Cost bucket | Dubai (typical) | Abu Dhabi (typical) |
|---|---|---|
| Service charges | AED 12–25/sq ft mid; 30–50+ premium | AED 10–22/sq ft mid; 25–40+ premium |
| Property management | 5–8% of rent | 5–8% of rent |
| Vacancy (long-let) | 4–8% | 3–6% (often lower in gov-anchored) |
| Ejari / Tawtheeq registration | AED 220 Ejari | Tawtheeq fees per contract |
| Maintenance provision | 0.5–1% of value/year | 0.5–1% of value/year |
| Acquisition (amortised) | ~6–7% DLD stack | AD transfer + admin [verify] |

### Worked example — Dubai JVC 1-bed

| Line item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Purchase price | AED 750,000 |
| Annual rent (Ejari-based) | AED 58,000 |
| Gross yield | 7.7% |
| Service charges (650 sq ft × AED 16) | AED 10,400 |
| Management (6%) | AED 3,480 |
| Vacancy (6%) | AED 3,480 |
| Net rent | AED 40,640 |
| **Net yield** | **5.4%** |

### Worked example — Abu Dhabi Al Reem 1-bed

| Line item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Purchase price | AED 720,000 |
| Annual rent | AED 48,000 |
| Gross yield | 6.7% |
| Service charges (700 sq ft × AED 14) | AED 9,800 |
| Management (6%) | AED 2,880 |
| Vacancy (4%) | AED 1,920 |
| Net rent | AED 33,400 |
| **Net yield** | **4.6%** |

In this pairing, **Dubai wins on net** — but swap in a lower-service-charge Abu Dhabi building with AED 52,000 rent and the gap closes to under 0.5 percentage points. **Building selection beats emirate selection.**

## Tenant profile: why vacancy assumptions differ

| Tenant driver | Dubai impact | Abu Dhabi impact |
|---|---|---|
| Tourism / STR | Seasonal revenue spikes; summer softening | Minimal STR layer |
| Corporate rotation | High in Marina, Business Bay | Steady in gov-adjacent districts |
| Family long-let | Strong in JVC, Silicon Oasis | Strong in Khalifa, Al Reef |
| Government sector | Present but smaller share | Core demand driver |
| School-year cycles | August turnover peaks | Similar but smaller market |

**Dubai investors** should model **6–8% vacancy** on mid-market unless the building has proven low turnover. **Abu Dhabi investors** in Al Reem and Khalifa can sometimes underwrite **4–5%** — but new supply on Yas and Lusail-style mega-projects in Abu Dhabi still requires conservative buffers.

## Short-term rental: Dubai's yield accelerator (sometimes)

Dubai [DET-licensed holiday homes](/guides/dubai-short-term-rental-guide/) in permitted towers can outperform long-let gross by **30–50%** in Marina, JBR, and Business Bay. Costs include:

- DET permit (~AED 1,520/year)
- Tourism Dirham per night
- Management 15–20% of revenue
- Higher cleaning and turnover
- Building NOC requirements

Abu Dhabi **does not offer equivalent STR depth**. Yield investors choosing Abu Dhabi should assume **long-let underwriting only** unless a specific building confirms holiday-home licensing in writing.

## Capital growth vs yield trade-off

| Strategy | Dubai | Abu Dhabi |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum gross yield | JVC, Sports City, Discovery Gardens | Khalifa City, mid Al Reem |
| Yield + liquidity | Business Bay mid, JLT | Al Reem established stock |
| Lower yield, stability | Downtown, Palm | Saadiyat, premium Yas |
| Off-plan yield guesswork | High volume — verify handover rent | Growing but fewer comparables |

Dubai's **205,000+ transactions in 2024** mean resale pricing adjusts faster — good for exit, sometimes painful if you overpaid at peak. Abu Dhabi's **thinner secondary market** can hold asking prices longer, which flatters paper yields until you actually need to sell.

## Acquisition costs and yield on total capital

Yield calculations should use **all-in cost**, not headline price.

**Dubai ready (cash):**
- 4% DLD transfer
- ~AED 4,000 trustee/admin
- 2% broker (secondary)
- **~6–7% on top of price**

**Abu Dhabi ready (indicative):**
- Transfer/registration per ADREC practice
- Broker 2%
- Admin and NOC
- **Budget 5–7% until quoted**

A AED 750,000 Dubai unit with AED 52,500 acquisition stack = **AED 802,500 invested**. At AED 58,000 rent, true gross on capital deployed is **7.2%**, not 7.7%.

## Golden Visa and yield — separate decisions

Both emirates fall under **UAE federal Golden Visa** at **AED 2 million registered property value**. Visa qualification does not improve yield. Many Golden Visa buyers overpay in premium districts yielding **under 5% gross** — rational if residency is primary, wrong if yield is primary.

| Goal | Emirate bias |
|---|---|
| Residency + acceptable yield | Dubai JVC 2-bed cluster or Abu Dhabi Al Reem 2-bed [verify value] |
| Maximum yield only | Dubai mid-market; Abu Dhabi only if net math wins |
| Family live + rent spare room | Abu Dhabi Khalifa / Al Reef |

## Decision framework

**Choose Dubai for yield if:**
- Target **net 5.5%+** on mid-market apartments
- Want **STR optionality** in specific buildings
- Need **Ejari-verified** rent comparables before purchase
- Plan **exit within 3–5 years** and need liquidity

**Choose Abu Dhabi for yield if:**
- Prefer **long-let government/family tenants** with lower turnover
- Found a **low service-charge building** where gross 6.5%+ net clears 5%
- Accept **longer resale timelines** for slightly better tenant stability
- Already live/work in Abu Dhabi and can self-manage

**Choose neither on yield alone if:**
- You are buying **premium waterfront** primarily for lifestyle — underwrite as consumption with upside, not yield product.

## Red flags when comparing yields across emirates

- Comparing **Dubai Marina STR projections** to **Abu Dhabi long-let only** stock
- Using **listing rents** instead of Ejari or Tawtheeq transacted data
- Ignoring **service charge** difference between two towers in the same district
- Assuming **off-plan brochure yield** applies at handover without comparable ready stock
- Picking emirate on **0.3% gross** difference without net model
- Golden Visa purchase in **5% gross district** while calling it an "investment"
