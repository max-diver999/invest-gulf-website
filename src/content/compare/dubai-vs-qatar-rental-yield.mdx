## Which market shows higher gross yield in 2026?

Dubai mid-market apartments lead on headline gross yield in 2026, with JVC and Sports City typically printing 7.5-9.0% gross on studios and one-beds versus Qatar freehold bands of 4.5-6.8% in Lusail, Fox Hills, and The Pearl. Qatar trades about 1-2 percentage points lower on gross but often shows tighter corporate occupancy.

Insider tip: underwrite both markets on net yield after the same 1.5-month void and building-level charges; Dubai brochure gross often collapses in towers above AED 30/sqft while Pearl studios can still clear 4%+ net with a corporate lease.

## How do net yields compare after fees?

Always underwrite net yield, not brochure gross: compression is typically 1-2 percentage points in both markets once management at 5-8%, vacancy of 1-2 months, and building charges enter the model. Invest Gulf compares Dubai JVC and Qatar Lusail on identical fee stacks so a higher Dubai gross does not automatically win after service charges.

Qatar net compresses when purchase price on new Lusail stock runs high relative to rent. Older Pearl studios at lower entry can reach **4%+ net** with stable corporate tenants.

Dubai mid-market one-beds in JVC often start near AED 650,000 purchase with about AED 52,000 annual rent (roughly 8.0% gross), then land near 4.7% net after roughly AED 9,000 service charges, 7% management, 1.5 months vacancy, and AED 2,000 maintenance. Qatar Lusail one-beds around AED 756,000 equivalent entry with about AED 48,500 rent can compress further when building charges near 20 AED-equivalent per sqm scale dominate, sometimes printing near 2.8% net on new stock, while older Pearl studios with corporate tenants still clear 4%+ net. Our underwriting treats both examples as stress cases, not brochure averages, and requires Ejari or local lease proof before ranking either market. Liquidity also diverges: Dubai mid-market resale often clears in 30-90 days against Qatar Pearl marketing windows of 6-12 months, so income investors who may need capital inside 24 months should weight Dubai higher even when Qatar net looks competitive on a single tower.

## Which tenant profile is more stable?

Qatar tenant demand is typically narrower but stickier than Dubai's: energy majors, government-adjacent employers, and embassy staff commonly sign 12-month leases with corporate guarantors, and Pearl or West Bay occupancy often sits around 85-95% versus wider tower variance in Dubai. Our underwriting prefers Qatar when void risk matters more than 1-2 points of gross.

- Qatar: corporate guarantors, 12-month leases, 85-95% occupancy bands in Pearl / West Bay
- Dubai: larger expat pool, faster re-lets, more supply shocks in launch corridors

## What service charges and hidden costs compress yield?

Service charges typically decide the net race: one Dubai tower at AED 40/sqft can lose to a Qatar mid-rise near AED-equivalent 18/sqm charges even when Dubai gross rent looks higher. Dubai bands often run AED 12-35/sqft/year; Qatar building charges sit near 15-25 AED-equivalent per sqm monthly in our underwriting.

| Dubai | Service charge | AED 12-35/sqft/year |

West Bay typically targets embassy and energy-corporate tenants with lower gross (often 4.5-5.5%) but predictable 12-month renewals, while Dubai Marina mixes tourism and long-let demand that can lift gross toward 5.5-7.0% with more NOC complexity. Our analysis treats Pearl corporate leases more like Business Bay than Marina holiday stock.

## What off-plan vs ready stock caution applies in both markets?

Off-plan brochures in Dubai and Lusail typically quote gross yield on launch price without service charges on handover, so ready stock is safer when you can verify tenant mix and charges before a deposit. We ty off-plan models to comparable handed-over units and keep liquidity for 6-12 months of carry until first rent.

1. Verify charges on a handed-over comparable, not a rendering
2. Budget 6-12 months of carry before first rent
3. Reject yield quotes that ignore building charges

## What Dubai acquisition costs affect net yield planning?

Yield math starts after all-in cost: Dubai freehold purchases typically add a 4% DLD transfer fee plus roughly 2% agency and AED 4,000-8,000 trustee lines, which raises effective entry and lowers net yield versus a rent-only spreadsheet. Our underwriting always divides rent by all-in price, not brochure ask.

| Dubai cost line | Typical |
| --- | --- |
| DLD transfer | 4% of value |
| Agency | ~2% (negotiable) |
| Trustee / admin | AED 4,000-8,000 |

Qatar uses its own registration fees and lawyer workflows; compare all-in cost on each side before you rank gross percentages.

## What checklist runs before you buy yield stock?

Run this six-step checklist before any deposit so Dubai vs Qatar yield rankings stay honest after fees, 1-2 month voids, DLD or registration costs, and exit timing. Our research treats missing Ejari or building charge statements as automatic model fails, not optional diligence for income buyers.

1. Pull 2 years of achieved rent (Ejari in Dubai, local equivalents in Qatar)
2. Model net with identical void assumptions (1-2 months)
3. Compare service charges per sqft or sqm on the **same** unit size
4. Stress-test resale: 90-day Dubai exit vs 12-month Qatar exit
5. Confirm freehold / foreign ownership rules for the exact building
6. Add Dubai DLD 4% (or Qatar registration) to purchase price before dividing rent

## How should you monitor yield quarterly after purchase?

Quarterly monitoring typically means tracking achieved rent versus listing ask, void days, and service charge invoices, then flagging any net yield slip above 0.5 points for two consecutive quarters. Our research pulls Dubai Ejari renewals against two neighbouring towers and requires Qatar employer guarantors before any rent discount is accepted.

- Dubai: Ejari renewals vs two neighbour towers each quarter
- Qatar: employer guarantor check before rent discounts
- Both: escalate if net slips more than 0.5 points for two quarters

Currency note: both AED and QAR peg to USD, so yield differences are mostly **rent minus charges**, not FX speculation. Repatriation still needs clean bank KYC trails on both sides.

Family landlords comparing Pearl villas with Dubai villa communities should treat Qatar villa gross as often lower but void risk as smaller when a corporate lease backs the tenant for a full 12 months. Apartment yield investors usually get cleaner 2026 comparables in Dubai JVC studios near 7.5-9.0% gross and Lusail one-beds near 5.8-6.8% gross than in villa stock on either side. We model villa and apartment sleeves separately so a Pearl villa corporate lease does not get averaged into a JVC studio yield score, and so a 6-12 month Qatar exit assumption is not silently replaced with Dubai's 30-90 day resale habit when stress-testing liquidity.

Dubai still leads most like-for-like apartment yield screens in 2026 when JVC and Sports City print 7.5-9.0% gross and 5-7% net after fees, while Qatar Pearl, Lusail, and West Bay freehold more often land at 4.5-6.5% gross and 3.8-5.2% net with stickier corporate tenancy. Service charges of AED 12-35/sqft in Dubai and building charges near 15-25 AED-equivalent per sqm in Qatar can erase a full percentage point of brochure yield if ignored. Transaction depth also diverges: Dubai logged 205,000+ deals in 2024 with 30-90 day mid-market exits, versus Qatar marketing windows that commonly stretch 6-12 months outside Pearl. Buyers who need capital inside 24 months should keep Dubai as the liquidity core; buyers who can hold 5+ years may accept Qatar's lower gross for occupancy stability when leases carry employer guarantors.
