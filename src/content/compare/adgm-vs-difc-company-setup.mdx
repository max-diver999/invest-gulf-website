## How does UAE corporate tax affect ADGM and DIFC entities?

UAE corporate tax from June 2023 typically applies at 9% above AED 375,000 taxable profit, while Qualifying Free Zone Person status can yield 0% on Qualifying Income only if substance and income composition tests are met. Mainland B2C revenue can pull entities back into the 9% band even inside ADGM or DIFC for foreign founders on this market in Invest Gulf models.

Tax checklist:
- 9% above AED 375,000 taxable profit unless QFZP tests pass
- 0% only on Qualifying Income with substance
- Mainland B2C and related-party gaps often break 0% assumptions

Non-qualifying income triggers include significant mainland UAE B2C revenue, passive income above thresholds, or related-party transactions without arm's-length documentation. Many groups hold assets in an ADGM SPC or DIFC PC while running staff and invoices through a mainland LLC. Invest Gulf research treats transfer pricing files as mandatory, not optional.

Mainland ADDED or Dubai DED LLC setup typically fits unrestricted UAE trading, retail, contracting, and government tenders, while ADGM and DIFC excel at cross-border advisory fees, regulated asset management, English-law joint ventures, and property holding via SPV or PC. A single free-zone company is cheaper until 12-month mainland B2C volume forces a second licence under the 9% corporate tax rules above AED 375,000 for foreign founders on this market in Invest Gulf models.

- Choose mainland for retail, contracting, and government tenders
- Choose ADGM or DIFC for English-law funds, family offices, and SPVs
- Budget AED 5,000 to 8,000 per visa plus dual renewals if you pair both

Choose mainland when you need:
- Unrestricted UAE retail or contracting
- Government tender eligibility
- Local invoicing without a free-zone agent layer
