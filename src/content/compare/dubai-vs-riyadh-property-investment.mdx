## How do Dubai and Riyadh property markets compare in 2026?

Dubai is a mature, data-rich market with 200,000+ annual transactions and published DLD pricing, while Riyadh is an earlier-stage foreign-buyer market under Vision 2030 with thinner resale depth. Dubai mid-market yields of 5 to 9% and Golden Visa from AED 2 million contrast with Riyadh Premium Residency from SAR 4 million.

| Factor | Dubai (2026 context) | Riyadh (2026 context) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Foreign ownership | Freehold in designated zones since 2002 | M/14 designated zones, rules still rolling out |
| Typical gross yield | 5-9% mid-market, 4-6% prime | Indicative, verify per district |
| Resale liquidity | Deep secondary market | Limited international resale |
| Residency link | Golden Visa from AED 2M registered | Premium Residency from SAR 4M property |
| Registration | DLD 4% transfer on ready freehold | Regional fees, counsel essential |

## Why does Dubai win on yield data and exit speed?

Dubai typically wins for investors who need measurable net yield, RERA escrow on off-plan, and exit optionality within 24 to 36 months after modelling a 6 to 8% all-in stack above price, including 4% DLD plus trustee and agency fees.

| Dubai district type | Gross yield band | Liquidity | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| JVC / Sports City | 7-9% | High | Income-focused investors |
| Marina / Downtown | 4-6% | Very high | Capital preservation + exit |
| Off-plan launches | Varies | Assignment-dependent | Experienced buyers only |

Critical insight: Dubai yields are net-tested after service charges (often 10 to 25% of gross rent), vacancy near 7 to 8% citywide, and management at 5 to 8% of rent. Invest Gulf checklists rebuild net before SPA.

- DLD registration: transparent 4% transfer fee on ready freehold
- RERA escrow: mandatory protection for [off-plan investments](/guides/dubai-cooling-off-period-off-plan/)
- Mortgage availability: often 20 to 25% down for [expat buyers](/guides/dubai-mortgage-broker-guide/)

Legal review of AED 5,000 to 15,000 in Dubai and mandatory local counsel in Riyadh belong in the cash budget.

| Total cash buyer stack | ~6-8% | ~5-8% estimated |

Dubai risks typically centre on cyclical oversupply and launch competition that stay transparent through DLD data across 200,000-plus annual transactions, while Riyadh risks are more structural around regulatory rollout and multi-year exit timelines. Dubai still needs net rebuilds after 10 to 25% service-charge drag and 7 to 8% vacancy; Riyadh still needs SAR 4 million Premium Residency clarity.

| Risk | Dubai | Riyadh |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Price transparency | High (DLD transactions) | Developing |
| Regulatory clarity | Established RERA/DLD | REGA + zone maps evolving |
| Exit speed | Weeks to months in mid-market | Often years |
| Developer default | RERA escrow mitigates off-plan | Verify sponsor + zone |

AED typically stays USD-pegged for predictable exchange maths on mortgages and exits, while SAR exposure is more of a Saudi growth and oil-diversification story with Public Investment Fund domestic deployment above USD 700 billion in assets.

Dubai's non-oil mix across trade, tourism, finance, and logistics supports multi-sector tenant depth. Riyadh underwriting should stress fiscal and regulatory milestones, not currency marketing alone. Our research prices currency peg stability as a Dubai advantage for expatriate landlords.
