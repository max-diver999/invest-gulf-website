## Are Dubai rental yields higher than Saudi in 2026?

Yes on most apartment comparisons in 2026: Dubai mid-market gross yields of 7-9% in JVC and Sports City typically exceed Riyadh and Jeddah designated-zone gross yields of 4-6.5%. Net spreads narrow once service charges, void periods, and management fees load on both sides.

Insider tip: confirm the REGA designated-zone letter before any Saudi deposit; off-list stock cannot legally lease to foreign owners even when a broker quotes 6%+ gross.

| Market | Typical gross (apartments) | Net band (conservative) | Data depth |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dubai mid-market | 7-9% | 5-7% | Ejari medians, deep resale |
| Riyadh designated | 4-5.5% premium, 5-7% mid | 3-5% | Thinner, growing |
| Jeddah designated | 5-6.5% | 3.5-5% | Tourism plus corporate mix |

## What does a net yield worked example look like?

A worked net example typically shows Dubai ahead by about one point after fees: a JVC one-bed near AED 680,000 with AED 58,000 rent can land about 5.3% net after AED 22,000 costs. Invest Gulf pairs that against Jeddah stock near SAR 650,000 with SAR 42,000 rent and about 4.3% net.

Purchase: AED 680,000. Rent: AED 58,000. Service charges, PM, and void: AED 22,000. Net: AED 36,000, about 5.3% net on price (8.5% gross).

### Jeddah mid-zone 1-bed (illustrative)

Purchase: SAR 650,000 (about AED 637,000). Rent: SAR 42,000. HOA, PM, and void: SAR 14,000. Net: SAR 28,000, about 4.3% net (6.5% gross).

Dubai mid-market one-beds near AED 680,000 with AED 58,000 rent often print about 8.5% gross and 5.3% net after roughly AED 22,000 of service charges, property management, and void. Jeddah designated-zone one-beds near SAR 650,000 (about AED 637,000) with SAR 42,000 rent more often land near 6.5% gross and 4.3% net after SAR 14,000 of HOA, PM, and vacancy. The gap of about one percentage point net matches 2026 field pairs Invest Gulf reviews on mid-market stock, but Saudi PM fees of 8-12% and void of 6-10 weeks can widen that gap if HOA schedules arrive late. Buyers who need cash flow inside 24 months should keep Dubai as the income core; buyers who accept 5-10 year holds may size a 10-25% Saudi sleeve only after REGA zone confirmation and a registered lease pathway.

## What REGA checks apply before you buy in Saudi?

Foreign rental income typically flows only from REGA-listed designated zones under the January 2026 framework, so foreign buyers must confirm zone status before any deposit. Our research treats an off-list purchase as a non-income asset even when a broker quotes 6%+ gross.

| Check | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Zone on current REGA foreign ownership list | Off-list purchases cannot lease legally as foreign owner |
| Developer registration and escrow | Protects against informal sales |
| Lease registration pathway | Required for compliant landlord status |
| Bank account for rent repatriation | Without it, net yield stays theoretical |

Full framework: [Saudi designated zones](/guides/saudi-arabia-property-foreigners-guide/).

## What Dubai costs affect net yield on the UAE side?

Dubai freehold costs typically start with a 4% DLD transfer fee plus annual service charges of AED 12-25+ per sq ft and RERA-compliant management at 5-8% of rent. Ejari registration remains mandatory for long-let compliance, and Invest Gulf converts marketing gross to net only after the building SC statement is in hand.

| Cost line | Typical Dubai note |
| --- | --- |
| DLD transfer | 4% of purchase price plus admin |
| Service charge | AED 12 to 25+ per sq ft/year by tower |
| PM fee | 5 to 8% of rent on long-let |
| Void | 2 to 6 weeks on mid-market apartments |

Use [service charges by area](/guides/service-charges-dubai-by-area/) when converting gross marketing yields to net.

## Who should choose Dubai vs Saudi for rental income?

Choose Dubai when near-term net cash flow, liquid resale, and established PM infrastructure matter more than Vision 2030 upside; choose Saudi designated zones when 5-10 year holds and REGA diligence are acceptable. Many our clients run 60-80% income core in Dubai with 10-25% Saudi satellite exposure.

## What risks compress net yield in each market?

Dubai risks typically include service charge surprises, short-let rule changes, and supply waves, while Saudi risks centre on late HOA schedules, 6-10 week voids, and thinner foreign-owner resale pools. Saudi gross yields compressed from 8-10% in 2022 toward 6-8% in 2026 as prices outran rents under our underwriting.

- Dubai: SC spikes, short-let rules, corridor oversupply
- Saudi: late HOA, slower turnover, REGA resale limits

- Still model: bank FX fees on rent repatriation
- Refresh: Saudi HOA yearly; Dubai SC via Mollak history
