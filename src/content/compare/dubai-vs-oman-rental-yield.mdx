## Dubai versus Oman rental yield at a glance

Dubai mid-market typically delivers 7% to 9% gross and 5% to 7% net, while Oman ITC often prints 5% to 7% gross and 3.5% to 5.5% net. Invest Gulf narrows the gap only when Muscat corporate leases run 2 to 3 years with low turnover. Compare ready one-bedroom long-lets on the same cost basis.

| Market | Gross band | Net band | Lease signal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dubai mid-market | 7 to 9% | 5 to 7% | 12 months |
| Oman ITC | 5 to 7% | 3.5 to 5.5% | 2 to 3 years |

Use the same ready one-bedroom long-let basis in both markets before you compare headline percents.

Dubai mid-market apartments typically show 7% to 9% gross with 5% to 7% net after service charges, management, and about one month void, while Oman ITC stock commonly lands at 5% to 7% gross and roughly 3.5% to 5.5% net once ITC fees and 6 to 12 month resale marketing periods are included. Invest Gulf underwriting keeps Dubai's 4% DLD transfer fee only on Dubai purchases and uses Omani registration costs for Muscat ITC tickets near AED 812,000 equivalent. Longer Oman corporate leases of 2 to 3 years can add 0.5 to 1 point of effective yield versus Dubai's 12-month renewals, but thinner liquidity remains a separate risk over a 5 year hold. Confirm current official rules with counsel before wiring in either market.

- Dubai strength: deeper resale and Ejari evidence
- Oman strength: longer corporate leases
- Shared rule: model net, not marketing gross

## Tourism lets versus corporate leases in Oman ITC stock

Oman ITC zones typically split between long-let only and managed short-stay with operator partners, while Dubai holiday homes need separate licensing and DET costs around AED 1,520 per year. We never assume short-stay income in Muscat without written ITC permission before SPA.

- Dubai holiday homes: building OA rules plus DET licensing costs
- Oman ITC short-stay: developer and MOCI permission in writing
- Corporate Muscat leases: often 2 to 3 years with company guarantees
