# Dubai vs Oman Rental Yield 2026

This comparison keeps Dubai transfer costs in Dubai sections only. Oman ITC models use Omani registration and operator fees, not UAE DLD lines.

## Dubai versus Oman rental yield at a glance

Dubai mid-market gross yields often run 7 to 9 percent with net near 5 to 7 percent after moderate service charges and one month void. Oman ITC gross bands near 5 to 7 percent with net near 3.5 to 5.5 percent once management and longer marketing periods apply. Oman can narrow the gap when corporate leases run two to three years with low turnover.

## What rental yield bands should you model for Dubai?

Anchor on mid-market apartments in areas such as JVC and Business Bay fringes rather than ultra-premium Marina stock if yield is the goal. Dubai acquisition includes roughly 4 percent DLD transfer on secondary sales plus trustee and agency lines; model those only on Dubai purchases. See [highest rental yield areas Dubai](/guides/highest-rental-yield-areas-dubai/) and [Dubai rental yield guide](/guides/dubai-rental-yield-guide/) for district tables.

| Dubai mid-market signal | Planning band |
| --- | --- |
| Gross yield | 7 to 9 percent |
| Net after fees | 5 to 7 percent |
| Typical lease | 12 months |
| Resale marketing | Often weeks to a few months |

## What rental yield bands should you model for Oman ITC zones?

Muscat ITC projects such as Al Mouj and Qurum waterfront often print 6 to 7 percent gross on one-bedroom stock, while Salalah ITC can sit nearer 5 to 6 percent. Foreign rent-out rights require ITC approval; confirm with MOCI and the developer. Cross-read [Muscat Qurum property](/areas/muscat-qurum-property-investment/) and [Al Mouj](/areas/muscat-al-mouj-property-investment/).

| Oman ITC signal | Planning band |
| --- | --- |
| Gross yield | 5 to 7 percent |
| Net after fees | 3.5 to 5.5 percent |
| Corporate lease length | 2 to 3 years common |
| Resale marketing | Often 6 to 12 months |

## What does a one-bedroom net yield example look like?

Muscat ITC illustration: OMR 85,000 buy (roughly AED 812,000), OMR 5,200 rent, OMR 1,400 costs produces about OMR 3,800 net (near 4.5 percent). Dubai comparables with higher gross can still win net if void stays low and service charges moderate, but Muscat corporate leases reduce year-two void risk that Dubai annual renewals carry.

## Tourism lets versus corporate leases in Oman ITC stock

Some ITC zones allow managed short-stay with operator partners; others restrict to long let. Dubai allows regulated holiday homes in selected buildings with separate licensing. Match product to your operator capability; do not assume Airbnb-style income in Muscat without written ITC permission.

## Who should choose Dubai versus Oman for rental income?

Choose Dubai if you prioritise maximum net cash flow, frequent resale optionality, and deep Ejari data. Choose Oman if you value longer corporate leases, Muscat lifestyle exposure, and can hold through slower resale cycles. Many Gulf portfolios hold Dubai core yield plus Oman satellite exposure for lease-length diversification.

## How should you review yields each quarter?

Track Ejari renewals or Dubai void weeks separately from Muscat corporate lease break clauses. When Dubai service charge notices arrive, recompute net immediately; when Oman ITC operators publish occupancy reports, compare against your void assumption. Liquidity stress belongs in the same spreadsheet as yield: if OMR resale inquiries go quiet for two quarters, extend your planned hold by twelve months in the model before you call the asset a failure.

## What to verify: sensitivity table reminder

### Yield comparison specifics

Oman's Muscat apartments yield 5-7 % gross, with significantly lower entry prices (OMR 40,000-80,000 for a 2-bed in Al Mouj). Service charges are minimal (2-4 OMR/sqm/year). Dubai apartments yield 5-9 % gross but start at AED 500,000+ for comparable quality. The trade-off: Dubai's liquidity and transaction volume dwarf Oman's, making exit timing far more predictable.

### Stress-testing your yield assumptions

Oman-specific yield modelling: Muscat gross yields average 5.5-7.5 % versus Dubai's 5-8 % for comparable apartment classes. Oman's advantage is lower entry cost - a 2-bed in Al Mouj starts at OMR 65,000 (roughly 620,000 AED) versus AED 900,000+ for a similar unit in Dubai Marina. However, Oman caps foreign ownership to designated ITC zones, and rental demand is thinner. Model your Oman scenario with a 6-8 week void period (vs 3-4 weeks in Dubai) and 3 % service charges as a share of purchase price. Annual property tax in Oman: 3 % of rental value for commercial, residential currently exempt.



## Side-by-side decision frame for Dubai versus Oman yield

Start with net yield, not developer gross slides. Dubai mid-market apartments often show 7 to 9 percent gross with 5 to 7 percent net after service charges, management, and void. Oman ITC apartments commonly land at 5 to 7 percent gross and roughly 3.5 to 5.5 percent net once ITC service fees and longer marketing periods are included. Dubai's 4 percent DLD transfer fee belongs only in Dubai acquisition models; Oman transfers use Omani registration and municipality costs instead, so **confirm current official rules** with Omani counsel before you wire.

| Factor | Dubai mid-market | Oman ITC (Muscat) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Typical gross yield | 7 to 9 percent | 5 to 7 percent |
| Lease length | 12 months standard | 2 to 3 years corporate common |
| Resale liquidity | High volume, weeks to few months | Thinner, often 6 to 12 months |
| Foreign ownership | Freehold zones across emirate | ITC zones only |
| Transfer costs | DLD fee on Dubai sales | Omani registration stack, not DLD |

Liquidity is the hidden yield tax in Oman. A 6 percent gross asset you cannot sell for a year behaves differently from a 7 percent Dubai flat with active Ejari turnover. Corporate leases in Muscat reduce void between tenants, which can add 0.5 to 1 point of effective yield for conservative investors even when headline gross lags Dubai.

Dubai investors should model service charges from the owners association, property management at 5 to 8 percent of rent, and one month of void annually unless the building shows multi-year tenants. Oman investors should ask the ITC operator for historical occupancy, permitted short-stay rules, and whether corporate housing contracts transfer with sale.

| Investor profile | Lean Dubai | Lean Oman ITC |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Needs fast exit | Yes | Rarely |
| Prioritises cheque yield | Yes | Only if net verified |
| Values 2 to 3 year leases | Secondary | Primary |
| Accepts regulatory complexity | Moderate | Higher, ITC specific |

Currency reporting matters for consolidated portfolios: Oman rents arrive in OMR while Dubai rents sit in AED pegged to USD. Use one reporting currency and mark FX only at transfer, not every month, to avoid noisy IRR math.

When Muscat corporate tenants renew, capture lease length and break clauses in your model. A 6 percent gross with a three-year corporate lease can beat a 7.5 percent gross Dubai flat with annual churn if void and reletting fees stack. Conversely, Dubai's depth of buyers protects you in downturns when Oman ITC resales go quiet.

Dubai mid-market freehold and Oman ITC stock sit in different legal and liquidity systems. A higher gross number in one market can lose on exit time, service charges, and residency linkage.

| Factor | Dubai mid-market | Oman ITC (e.g. Al Mouj style) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ownership frame | UAE freehold districts | ITC foreign ownership rules |
| Tenant pool | Deep expat and tourism mix | Smaller, lifestyle-led pool |
| Liquidity | Usually faster in known clusters | Thinner resale, longer holds |
| Fee stack | DLD transfer and service charges | Oman transaction and service norms |

Pick Dubai if you need liquidity and PM depth. Pick Oman ITC if lifestyle and diversification matter more than speed of exit.
