## How do Dubai and Muscat compare for foreign property buyers in 2026?

Dubai freehold typically fits buyers who want market depth and gross yields often in the 5-9% band, while Muscat ITC stock fits buyers who accept 4-5% yields for lower entry tickets around AED 400,000-600,000. Neither market replaces legal review of ownership rules before deposit for foreign buyers.

| Factor | Dubai | Muscat (ITC zones) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Foreign ownership scope | Freehold zones across the emirate | ITC developments only (verify current official rules) |
| Planning gross yield | 5-9% by district | 4-5% typical |
| 1BR entry (planning) | From about AED 600k outer districts | About AED 400-600k equivalent |
| Residency tie-in | Golden Visa from AED 2M property | Investor visa at a lower property threshold |
| Exit liquidity | Deep secondary market | Thinner buyer pool, longer campaigns |

Sound underwriting starts with ownership eligibility in writing, not brochure maps.

## What transaction and transfer fees should you budget?

Dubai resale transfers typically use 4% DLD on price plus trustee and agency lines near 2% plus VAT. Muscat ITC resales usually total less than Dubai's full stack but still include agency commission and developer NOC costs, with foreign-buyer timelines often stretching 60-90 days versus 30-45 days in Dubai.

| Cost line | Dubai (indicative) | Muscat ITC (indicative) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Government transfer | 4% DLD on price | Ministry registration plus municipal fees (verify current official rules) |
| Agency | Often 2% plus VAT | Often 2% (negotiable) |
| Mortgage NOC | Developer or bank NOC if financed | Developer NOC common on ITC resales |
| Timeline | About 30 to 45 days when docs ready | Often 60 to 90 days for foreign buyers |

Insider tip: model both fee stacks in one AED spreadsheet before you call Muscat "cheaper" on net yield. Checklist: confirm current official fees in writing.

## Which market should you choose?

Market choice in this market typically means Dubai when you need liquidity, wider community choice, and 5-9% gross yield potential with 24 to 36 month exit optionality. Muscat typically fits foreign buyers who want lower entry capital, steadier tenants, and a five-year hold with 4-5% yields.

| Choose Dubai if | Choose Muscat if |
| --- | --- |
| You may sell within 24 to 36 months | You can hold five or more years |
| Yield and data transparency drive the decision | Lower ticket and stability matter more |
| You want mortgage and broker depth | You accept ITC-only geography |
| Golden Visa at AED 2M is on your roadmap | Lower investor-visa thresholds fit your budget |

Buyer scenario: many portfolios hold Dubai for exit optionality and Muscat for cost-efficient Gulf exposure.

Muscat ITC stock typically underwrites 4-5% gross yields, apartment entry around AED 400,000-600,000 for like-for-like planning, investor visa routes at a lower property threshold than UAE Golden Visa, and foreign-buyer closes often needing 60-90 days. Transfer stacks differ: Dubai ready sales budget 4% DLD plus agency near 2% plus VAT, while Muscat ITC fees usually total less than Dubai's full stack but still include agency and developer NOC. ## Why do some investors still choose Muscat ITC stock?

Muscat typically offers Gulf access at 20-30% lower entry points, with one-bedroom ITC planning around AED 400,000-600,000 equivalent and steadier 4-5% gross yields. Tenant demand concentrates in government and energy employers, with leases often running 2-3 years and less speculative churn than Dubai growth corridors.

- Apartment entry: about AED 400,000-600,000 for many 1BR ITC units
- Yield band: 4-5% gross with lower service-charge drag than many Dubai towers
- Trade-off: thinner secondary buyers and longer marketing periods

## How do investor visas compare between Dubai and Muscat?

Dubai Golden Visa typically requires AED 2 million property for longer renewable residency terms, while Oman's investor visa uses a lower property threshold and still supports family sponsorship. Visa rules change, so confirm current official thresholds before you size the ticket for either market as a foreign buyer in 2026.

- Dubai: AED 2M property pathway, deeper PRO ecosystem
- Muscat: lower property threshold, renewal tied to ownership maintenance
- Shared: family sponsorship and business-rights questions need local counsel

Red flag: never buy a weaker investment only to chase a visa headline number.

Dubai freehold purchases typically close in 30-45 days once mortgage approval and NOC are ready, while Muscat ITC deals often need 60-90 days for foreign-buyer KYC and Ministry registration. Keep 6-12 months of carrying costs before first rent, and treat Muscat secondary exits as multi-month campaigns in the market.

- Carry buffer: 6-12 months before first rent in either market
- Exit rule: inside 24 months, Dubai liquidity usually beats Muscat depth
