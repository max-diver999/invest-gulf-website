Al Mouj Muscat is Oman's flagship ITC, a marina, golf, and residential masterplan on the Boushar coastline with more foreign freehold stock and rental history than any other Omani development. It is not Dubai Marina: yields are lower, liquidity is thinner, and the tenant base is oil and gas, hospitality, and government-adjacent employment.

**See also:** [Oman ITC zones](/guides/oman-itc-zones-property/), [Living Al Mouj](/guides/living-muscat-al-mouj/), [Qurum comparison](/areas/muscat-qurum-property-investment/).

> **YMYL note:** ROP investor residency and ITC title rules change. Confirm current official rules with ROP and a licensed Omani lawyer. This is not legal advice.

## What does the Al Mouj market look like in 2026?

Al Mouj is the deepest foreign-freehold market in Oman, with more than a decade of marina, golf, and retail operations behind it. Apartments typically gross **4-6%** and villas **3.5-5%**, but net returns often land at **1.5-2.5%** after service charges, vacancy, and management. Entry in 2026 planning bands runs about **OMR 80K-120K** for a one-bedroom, **OMR 110K-180K** for two-bedroom stock, and **OMR 200K-400K+** for golf-facing villas at the OMR 2.597 USD peg.

Foreign buyers should treat Al Mouj as a lifestyle plus residency-diligence play, not a Dubai-style flip market. Plan **180-360 days** to sell secondary stock unless you price from net yield from day one.

## How is Al Mouj split by phase and pocket?

| Pocket | Character | Typical unit | Rental maturity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Marina Promenade | Waterfront towers, cafes, yacht views | 1-3BR apartments | High |
| Golf precinct | Fairway villas and townhouses | 3-5BR villas | High |
| Central retail spine | Carrefour, clinics, services | Walk-up from multiple phases | High |
| Lagoon / inner marina | Quieter water views | Premium 2-4BR | Medium-high |
| Outer villa phases | More space, more driving | 4-5BR+ | Medium |
| Newer tower launches | Modern MEP, thinner rental history | 1-3BR | Rising |

Marina Promenade is the default social address: school buses at lobbies, weekend marina walks, and the strongest short-term assignee demand. Golf precinct trades walkability for space and higher summer AC load. Before you buy, ask which owners association (OA) governs the unit and request **three years of audited service charge accounts**.

## What yield should Gulf buyers underwrite?

Use gross yield only as a screening number. A marina two-bedroom bought around **OMR 115K** renting near **OMR 550-750/month** can show **5-6% gross**, but after **OMR 2-4/sqft** service charges, a few weeks of void, and **5-8%** management, net often sits near **2-3%**.

| Scenario | Gross (indicative) | Main drag | Net (planning) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Marina 2BR ~OMR 115K | 5-6% | Service charge + void | ~2.5-3% |
| Golf villa ~OMR 280K | 4-4.5% | SC + larger AC bill | ~2-2.7% |
| Central 1BR ~OMR 85K | 5.5-6% | SC + turnover | ~3-3.5% |

Premium ITC net under **2%** is normal. If a broker quotes Dubai-style **5% net**, rebuild the model with OA invoices and a realistic void allowance.

## What infrastructure supports tenancy?

Al Mouj's moat is operational infrastructure: working marina, Greg Norman golf, supermarket and clinic cluster, and a stable PDO and energy-sector tenant pool. Weak spots are **thin after-school options on-site** (most families drive to Qurum or Madinat Qaboos schools) and **summer AC costs** on Kahramaa bills that push turnover if rents are set too high.

Tourism footfall helps F&B on the promenade but does not replace long-term corporate tenancy for most investors. Short lets, where permitted, face ITC bylaws and community rules that differ from Dubai holiday-home norms. Read the OA short-stay policy before you model Airbnb-style income.

## Does Al Mouj count toward Oman investor residency?

ITC freehold **may** support the cited **OMR 250,000** ROP investor property threshold, but **no purchase auto-grants residency** (confirm current official rules). You still need a separate ROP application, source-of-funds documentation, and compliance with minimum value and eligible asset types.

Budget legal and PRO time alongside the SPA. A sub-OMR 250K marina apartment can be a solid rental asset and still fail the investor residency test. Cross-check with [Oman property investment guide](/guides/oman-property-investment-guide/) and [Gulf residency by investment](/guides/gulf-residency-by-investment-guide/) before you wire a deposit for visa purposes.

## How does Al Mouj compare to Qurum for investors?

| Factor | Al Mouj (ITC) | Qurum |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Foreign freehold | Yes, in ITC title | Generally not for non-GCC |
| Tenant pool | Expat assignees, marina lifestyle | Broader Omani and expat mix |
| Liquidity | Thinner, longer marketing | Deeper local rental market |
| Price tone | Premium for marina/golf | Central Muscat benchmark |

Qurum sets **rental demand benchmarks** for Muscat; Al Mouj sets the **foreign-owned lifestyle address**. Many families rent in Qurum and socialise in Al Mouj, which is why yield diligencers track both markets. See [Muscat Qurum property investment](/areas/muscat-qurum-property-investment/).

## What red flags should pause a purchase?

Walk away or pause when: the seller assumes **any** ITC deed triggers ROP residency; gross **6%** is marketed as net; a **new phase** has no rental track record; service charge schedules are missing; or the agent promises **Dubai-like liquidity** in under 90 days.

Also verify **title type on the register**, not the brochure, and confirm the unit sits inside the ITC freehold zone you expect. Use an **Omani property lawyer** (not developer counsel) for SPA review.

## Who is Al Mouj a good fit for?

**Strong fit:** Gulf and international buyers who want Muscat marina life, can hold **5+ years**, accept **1.5-2.5% net**, and may pursue **OMR 250K+** residency diligencing. **Weak fit:** yield-first investors comparing to Dubai mid-market; buyers who need quick resale; families who must walk to school without a car commute.

Remote workers can tenancy-test the market, but employer housing allowances still anchor pricing for two-bedroom marina stock.

## What do service charges do to net yield?

Request **OA audited accounts**, reserve fund balance, and planned capex for facade and MEP over the next **24 months**. Service charge escalation of **10-15%** year-on-year destroys net yield faster than a single vacant month.

| DD item | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Last 3 years SC history | Spots step-ups and arrears |
| Reserve fund | Facade and pump replacements |
| Planned capex | Special assessments hit net |

If the seller refuses written SC history, treat that as a walk-away signal on this stock.

## How do schools and family tenancy affect rent?

Most international schools sit **15-25 minutes** by car toward Qurum and Madinat Qaboos. Family tenants pay for marina safety and golf amenities but still benchmark against **OMR 550-750/month** for a two-bedroom promenade unit. Overshooting pushes void toward **60-90 days**.

School bus fees and dual-car households are normal. Investors who target PDO and energy employers with housing allowances tend to see smoother renewals than generic short-term listings.

## How liquid is the secondary market?

| Asset type | Typical marketing | Price achievement vs ask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1BR Marina Promenade | 120-180 days | 88-94% |
| 2BR lagoon view | 150-240 days | 85-92% |
| Golf villa | 180-360 days | 82-90% |

List from **net yield**, provide tenancy history, SC receipts, and OA clearance. Seasonality: **Oct-Feb** assignee traffic peaks; summer listings linger unless priced for instant yield.

## Al Mouj vs Muscat Bay: which ITC for you?

Both are ITC waterfront concepts, but Al Mouj has **longer operating history**, more secondary turnover data, and a deeper marina retail spine. Muscat Bay may appeal to buyers who want newer stock but accept **thinner rental comparables**. Compare on **title register**, **OA fees**, and **actual achieved rents**, not renderings alone.

## What should you budget for furnishing and running costs?

Furnished units can command premia in the expat assignee segment; furnishing payback often runs **18-24 months** if you buy quality once. Insurance, minor repairs, and Kahramaa summer spikes belong in the ownership budget alongside SC.

Repatriation: the **OMR peg to USD** helps planning, but outbound transfers still need **source-of-funds paperwork**. Budget **2-4 weeks** after sale for bank clearance, not same-day UAE-style corridors.

## How does Al Mouj compare to UAE mid-market yield plays?

Dubai mid-market towers may show **6-8% gross** with faster resale, but transfer and agency costs on UAE freehold are part of that story. Al Mouj trades yield for **peg stability, lower density, and ITC lifestyle**. It is not a yield substitute for Dubai; it is a **hold, rent, and optionally residency** choice.

See [Dubai vs Muscat property investment](/compare/dubai-vs-muscat-property-investment/) and [UAE vs Oman property investment](/compare/uae-vs-oman-property-investment/).

## What legal steps matter on an ITC purchase?

Engage Omani counsel for SPA review, confirm **ITC freehold** on the title register, and map **municipality lease registration** steps if you rent the unit out. Do not conflate UAE escrow and Oqood language with Omani process; your lawyer should translate each milestone.

## When is Al Mouj the wrong fit?

Skip Al Mouj if you need **liquidity under six months**, **5%+ net** without vacancy fiction, automatic residency on a sub-threshold unit, or nightlife-led single life. Also reconsider if you refuse to operate with **OMR-denominated** planning and local legal counsel.
