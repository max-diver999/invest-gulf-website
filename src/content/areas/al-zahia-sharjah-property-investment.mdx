Al Zahia is Sharjah's family-community investment play: a Majid Al Futtaim masterplan with City Centre mall anchor, school catchment, parks, and freehold villas and apartments that attract longer-tenancy family renters at 6.5 to 8.5% gross yield.

## What is Al Zahia for Sharjah property investors in 2026?

Al Zahia is a Majid Al Futtaim freehold community in Sharjah where family stock typically ranges from AED 500,000 apartments to AED 4 million villas, with planning gross yields of 6.5 to 8.5% for investors who underwrite school-linked tenancy in the area rather than peak studio turnover across 5 years.

Invest Gulf frames the masterplan as retail plus schools plus parks. Foreign buyers register freehold title with the Sharjah Real Estate Registration Department. Compare community thesis with [Sharjah property investment](/guides/sharjah-property-investment-guide/) and the higher-turnover alternative at [Aljada](/areas/aljada-sharjah-property-investment/).

| Segment | Entry (planning) | Gross yield | Tenant profile |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Apartments | AED 500K to 1.1M | 7 to 8.5% | Couples, small families |
| Townhouses | AED 1M to 2M | 6.5 to 8% | School-age families |
| Villas | AED 1.5M to 4M | 6 to 7.5% | Senior expats, UAE nationals |

## How much do Al Zahia apartments, townhouses, and villas cost?

Al Zahia acquisition costs typically start near AED 500,000 for entry apartments and climb toward AED 1 million to AED 2 million for townhouses, while villas often require AED 1.5 million to AED 4 million, with the area pricing around AED 750 to 1,050 per sq ft in 2026 planning ranges used by Invest Gulf.

- Apartments: fastest rent velocity, higher yield percentage
- Townhouses: longer school-year leases, mid yield
- Villas: higher absolute rent (often AED 80,000 to 150,000/year), lower yield percent

Price discipline: treat portal asks as marketing until you pull three closed comps in the same MAF phase. Dubai family communities such as Arabian Ranches usually price higher for similar bedroom counts, which is why Al Zahia attracts yield-seeking family landlords.

## What rental yield should landlords model in Al Zahia?

Gross yield in the area typically ranges from 6.5% to 8.5% by unit type, with apartments often clearing 7% to 8.5% and townhouses or villas closer to 6% to 7.5% after higher purchase prices, before service charges of about AED 9 to 13 per sq ft on Invest Gulf models.

Net planning band after management and void is usually 5% to 7%. Family stickiness can cut vacancy versus commuter Sharjah stock, but do not erase void from the model.

Worked apartment illustration (planning only):

| Line | Figure |
| --- | --- |
| Purchase | AED 750,000 |
| Gross rent | AED 58,000/year (~7.7%) |
| Service + management | AED 9,000 to 12,000 |
| Void allowance | 4 to 6 weeks |
| Net planning | ~5.5% to 6.5% |

Invest Gulf underwriting for Al Zahia family stock treats school catchment as a renewal engine, not a guarantee. A typical two-bedroom apartment priced near AED 750,000 can illustrate about AED 55,000 to AED 60,000 gross annual rent when leased to a dual-income household, which is roughly 7.3% to 8.0% gross before service charges of AED 9 to 13 per sq ft and agency fees. Family tenants often prefer 24-month contracts aligned to the school year, and first renewals hold in a large share of school-enrolled cases when the building and bus route remain convenient. Model 4 to 6 weeks void on apartments and 4 to 8 weeks on villas even in strong catchments, then stress rent growth at 3% to 5% rather than assuming Dubai-style capital jumps every year.

## Why do family tenants improve net yield versus commuter stock?

Family tenants in Al Zahia typically sign 24 to 36 month leases tied to school years, which can cut vacancy toward about 3 weeks versus roughly 6 weeks in commuter buildings and lift effective net yield by about 0.5% to 1.5% when renewals stick in the area.

| Cost factor | Commuter pattern | Family pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vacancy | ~6 weeks | ~3 weeks |
| Re-marketing | ~AED 1,500/void | ~AED 800/void |
| Collection stress | Higher | Lower when dual income stable |

Primary renter profile:

- Children aged 5 to 16 in community or nearby schools
- Combined household income often AED 15,000 to 25,000/month
- Hybrid Dubai or Sharjah employment, not pure Marina commuting

**Insider tip:** Before you underwrite “school premium,” ask for the exact bus route and walking distance to GEMS Al Zahia or the named feeder; a unit 500 metres closer can justify 5% to 12% more rent, while a unit outside the catchment sells on price alone.

## How does City Centre Al Zahia support rents and renewals?

City Centre Al Zahia is the MAF retail anchor that typically supports a 5% to 10% rent premium versus non-mall Sharjah stock of the same bedroom count, because walkable groceries, cinema, and weekend dining reduce the urge for families to relocate every 12 months in the area on Invest Gulf rent comps.

| Retail signal | Landlord reading |
| --- | --- |
| Walkable groceries and cinema | Shorter void marketing |
| 5% to 10% rent premium thesis | Needs same-bedroom comps |
| Service charges near AED 15/sqft cap talk | Still verify written schedule |

Invest Gulf treats mall footfall as vibrancy, not yield. MAF landscaping and security help defend asks when Aljada studios undercut on headline percentage.

## How does Al Zahia compare with Aljada for investors?

Al Zahia is the longer-tenancy family play at roughly 6.5% to 8.5% gross, while Aljada typically wins on studio and one-bedroom commuter yield with more turnover; Invest Gulf chooses Al Zahia when void cost and school renewals in the area matter more than maximum percentage points.

| Factor | Al Zahia | Aljada |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Thesis | Family stability | Commuter yield |
| Anchor | City Centre + schools | Urban / university energy |
| Void risk | Lower on 2BR+ | Higher on studios |
| Dubai commute | 40 to 90 minutes | Often shorter border paths |

## What commute times should landlords underwrite to Dubai?

Commute from Al Zahia to Dubai Marina or Business Bay typically means 40 to 60 minutes off-peak and 60 to 90 minutes at peak on the E311, so for a 3 year tenancy Invest Gulf underwrites Sharjah or hybrid workers rather than daily Marina office runs that burn 10% to 15% of weekday waking hours.

- Off-peak: plan 40 to 60 minutes (still a long Dubai day)
- Peak: plan 60 to 90 minutes on E311
- Border zones such as Al Nahda usually beat Al Zahia for pure Dubai commuting
- Driver budgets of AED 2,500 to 4,000/month change net yield math fast

If your buyer persona needs a 30-minute Dubai office pin, Al Zahia is the wrong community thesis.

## Is Al Zahia freehold and can units reach Golden Visa thresholds?

Al Zahia is a designated Sharjah freehold zone where foreign buyers can take full ownership through Sharjah registration, while Golden Visa planning still hinges on whether the specific unit’s registered value reaches about AED 2 million under current federal rules that Invest Gulf treats as planning inputs only for the area.

- Standard apartments often sit below AED 2 million
- Larger 2BR, townhouses, and villas are likelier candidates
- Always verify SPA registered value, not brochure ask

Residency products change; confirm ICA and local registration rules before marketing a visa story. Broader context: [Gulf residency by investment](/guides/gulf-residency-by-investment-guide/) and [Dubai property investment guide](/guides/dubai-property-investment-guide/).

## What should off-plan buyers verify in MAF Al Zahia phases?

Off-plan Al Zahia phases typically use construction-linked payment plans with launch premiums of about 5% to 8% above ready secondary in the same phase, and that premium often compresses toward parity within roughly 12 months after handover when snagging and service-charge actuals are clear for the developer.

Checklist before deposit:

1. Confirm the plot sits inside the MAF Al Zahia masterplan, not a nearby non-MAF label
2. Request prior-phase handover quality notes (Phase 1 to 3, 2019 to 2023 window)
3. Model service charges with AED 9 to 13/sqft base, stress to AED 15/sqft
4. Compare townhouse psf against early apartment phases
5. Keep Golden Visa and yield models on separate sheets

Majid Al Futtaim’s Al Zahia offering should be underwritten building by building. A townhouse bought near AED 1.4 million with AED 95,000 annual rent is about 6.8% gross before AED 12,000 to AED 25,000 community costs on larger homes; a villa at AED 2.2 million aiming for AED 130,000 rent is closer to 5.9% gross and only becomes a Golden Visa conversation if registered value clears AED 2 million. Invest Gulf research treats City Centre footfall and school enrolment as demand supports, yet still requires three Sharjah registration or portal comps, a Mollak-equivalent service history where available, and a void line of 4 to 8 weeks. Buyers who skip masterplan boundary checks sometimes purchase “Al Zahia adjacent” stock that never receives MAF community management, which is a different risk book entirely.

## What red flags should pause an Al Zahia purchase?

Red flags are deal-stoppers that typically include non-MAF buildings marketed under the community name, studio yield chasing when Aljada fits better, townhouse service charges of AED 12,000 to 25,000 ignored in the net model, and Golden Visa claims on units clearly below AED 2 million registered value in the area.

- Studio-max thesis: buy Aljada, not Al Zahia family stock
- Missing Ejari or registration comps in the same phase
- Service-charge quotes without written community schedule
- Commute promises of “30 minutes to Marina” at peak

Invest Gulf checklist before you commit: pull three comparables in the same MAF phase, confirm masterplan inclusion, and keep Sharjah fee lines separate from any Dubai DLD template. Self-managing from abroad is possible, yet Sharjah tenancy practice usually favours local management for a first Northern Emirates asset.
