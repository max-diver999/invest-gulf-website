Remraam is bought for one reason and it is not the one most listings lead with. The community sits at the entry end of the Dubai apartment market, with prices from roughly AED 1.2 million, and everything that makes it work as an investment follows from that number rather than from any feature of the community itself.

## What does Remraam actually cost?

Entry pricing, and the comparison that makes the number meaningful is against the other outer communities rather than against the Dubai average.

| Community | Entry price | Per square foot | Developer | Location |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Remraam apartments | AED 1.2M+ | AED 1,950+ | Various | Al Tawayyan |
| Town Square apartments | AED 1.3M+ | AED 2,100+ | Nshama | Town Square |
| Serena community | AED 1.4M+ | AED 2,200+ | Dubai Properties | Dubailand |

The spread across those three is roughly 13% on price per square foot, and what it buys is position. All three are outer communities with the same basic proposition, and Remraam sits at the bottom of the band because it is furthest from the established employment centres.

That discount is the asset. A buyer in this band is not paying for a view, a beach or a golf course, and treating the lower figure as a bargain misreads it. It is the market pricing a longer commute, and the investment question is whether the rent achievable here, net of charges and vacancy, produces a better return on the smaller capital than the alternatives do on larger capital.

## Can a foreign buyer own here?

Yes, outright. Remraam sits in designated freehold territory, so title passes to the buyer in their own name with no lease term attached and no plot-level eligibility check of the kind that governs Dubai's older districts.

- Title is freehold and issued in the buyer's name in the Dubai Land Department record
- Ownership carries no residency requirement, and residency does not follow from ownership
- The purchase can be registered jointly, with the share split recorded on the deed

The designated list and the verification method sit in [the Dubai freehold areas list](/guides/freehold-areas-dubai-list/). For this community they settle the question rather than complicate it, which moves the whole analysis onto the economics of the building.

The other side of the same figure applies at resale. The purchase-intent audience here is about 30 people a month across every stage of intent, so selling a Remraam apartment means reaching a narrow pool, and pricing correctly matters more than marketing reach does.

## What does the school inside the community do?

It removes the school run, and that is worth more to a landlord than the rating on its own suggests.

South View School sits inside Remraam and holds a Very Good KHDA rating. For a family living here the commute to a campus is measured in minutes rather than in a crossing of a main road at the morning peak. For comparison, the same school is a 20 to 25 minute drive for families in Dubai South, and it appears in their planning precisely because the Outstanding-rated alternatives sit further away again.

School proximity is the strongest single driver of family tenant demand in Dubai. A family that has placed a child at a campus inside their own community faces a real cost in moving, and that cost is what turns a twelve-month tenancy into a multi-year one. Each avoided turnover saves both a void period and a re-letting commission near 5% of annual rent, and across a five-year hold that is worth more to the realised return than a fraction of a point on the headline yield.

The wider picture of how families in this part of the emirate choose campuses is in [schools near Dubai South](/guides/schools-near-dubai-south/).

## What does the location actually cost?

This is the part of the analysis that a yield figure hides, and being direct about it is more useful than a list of amenities.

Remraam is an outer community. The drive to the Marina and Jumeirah Lakes Towers employment cluster is a real commute, and the drive to DIFC is longer again. That distance is priced into the purchase, which is why the entry figure is where it is, and it is also priced into the rent, which is why the yield looks attractive before costs.

What it means for the tenant pool is that this community serves households for whom the commute is acceptable or irrelevant: families prioritising space and school placement over proximity, households working in the western and southern employment areas rather than the coastal ones, and tenants whose budget places them in this band regardless.

What it means for the owner is that the tenant pool is large but specific, and it does not overlap much with the pool competing for Marina or Downtown apartments. Remraam is not a cheaper alternative to those communities. It is a different market with different tenants, and a rent assumption borrowed from the coastal communities does not transfer.

## What does a purchase cost here?

The transaction stack is identical across the emirate, and at this price point the percentage is what matters because the entry costs are a meaningful share of the capital at risk.

| Cost line | Rate | On a AED 1,200,000 purchase |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DLD transfer fee | 4% of price | AED 48,000 |
| Agency commission | 2% plus 5% VAT | AED 25,200 |
| Trustee office fee | Capped by DLD regulation | AED 4,200 |
| Developer no-objection certificate | AED 500 to 5,000 | AED 1,500 |
| Title deed issuance | AED 250 to 580 | Included in the trustee bundle |
| **Total closing** | **6% to 7%** | **about AED 79,000** |

With a mortgage, add registration at 0.25% of the loan, an arrangement fee near 1% and a valuation of AED 2,500 to 3,500, which takes the all-in figure closer to 8%. On a AED 1.2 million purchase that is roughly AED 96,000 of costs that never appear in the yield calculation unless the buyer puts them there. A gross yield quoted against AED 1.2 million rather than against the AED 1.28 million actually committed overstates the return from the first line of the model. The itemisation sits in [the cost of buying property in Dubai](/guides/cost-of-buying-property-dubai/).

## An insider tip on the entry band

Insider tip: in the entry band the service charge is the single number that decides whether a purchase works, and it should be obtained per square foot from the owners association in writing before any offer. At AED 1,950 per square foot, a charge difference of half a dirham per square foot between two buildings changes the net yield by more than a five percent difference in the asking price does, because the charge is a recurring cost against a modest rent while the price difference is a one-off. Ask for the reserve fund position in the same request. In stock of this age a thin reserve means a future special assessment, which lands on whoever holds the unit when the work is scheduled rather than on the seller who deferred it. Both figures exist, both are available for the asking, and neither appears in a listing or in an agent's gross yield calculation. A buyer who has them is comparing buildings. A buyer who does not is comparing asking prices, which in this band is the least informative number available.

## Who rents in Remraam, and how long do they stay?

Three groups, and the mix is what makes the vacancy assumption defensible rather than optimistic.

Families with children at South View School are the longest-tenure group and the most valuable to a landlord. The campus inside the community is the reason they are here, and moving means either changing school or acquiring the commute they moved to Remraam to avoid. That is a real switching cost, and switching costs are what produce renewals.

Households working in the western and southern employment areas form the second group. For them the commute that prices this community down is not a penalty at all, because their workplace is on the same side of the city. This group is stable for structural reasons and largely invisible in the market commentary, which tends to measure every Dubai community by its distance from DIFC.

Budget-led tenants form the third. They are here because the rent fits, and they behave like the mid-market apartment tenant across the emirate: they renew when the rent is reasonable and they move when it is not. Their cycle runs closer to the Dubai apartment average of 14 to 20 months than to the family figure.

The practical consequence is that the same building can produce quite different returns depending on which tenant it attracts, and that the unit configuration influences which one it gets. A larger unit with school-run convenience reaches the family group. A studio or one bedroom reaches the budget group and should be modelled on the shorter cycle, with a void and a re-letting commission near 5% of annual rent each time it turns over.

## How does the entry band behave at resale?

Predictably, provided the seller understands who the buyer is.

The purchase-intent audience is small, at roughly 30 searches a month on the buying phrase, so a resale here is not sold by reaching a wide market. It is sold by pricing correctly against recent transactions in the same building or a directly comparable one, and the Dubai Land Department transaction record makes that an evidence question rather than a negotiation from an asking price.

The buyer pool for a resale is mostly two groups: investors looking for entry-band income on modest capital, and tenants in the community buying rather than renewing. The second group is larger than it looks in an affordable community with a school, because a household already settled here has already accepted the trade the community represents.

What the seller controls is the same short list that governs the purchase. A unit in a building with a healthy reserve fund, a clear service charge account and a maintained common area transacts on its comparables. A unit in a building carrying deferred maintenance transacts after a discussion about who pays for it, and that discussion is slower and more expensive at the point of sale than it would have been as an owner.

A tenanted unit is a specific product for the investor half of that pool, because the income is contracted rather than projected. Where the tenancy is a family on a multi-year pattern, that is a stronger proposition than a vacant unit at the same price, and it is worth presenting as such rather than treating the tenancy as an obstacle to a sale.

## What should a buyer check in Remraam?

Six items, ordered by how much each can move the result.

- The service charge per square foot for the specific building, taken from the owners association
- The reserve fund position, since a thin one converts into a future special assessment
- The building's maintenance record, because in the entry band the gap between a well-run building and a poorly run one shows up in both achievable rent and vacancy
- The handover pipeline nearby, since new competing supply in an outer community affects rents directly
- The walking route to South View School, which is what the family tenant is actually paying for
- The current tenancy where one exists, including remaining term and rent, since it transfers with the property

We verify the charge and the reserve fund before the yield model, because in this band those two figures move the net more than anything else on the list.

## Who should look at Remraam, and who should not?

It suits an investor buying for income on modest capital, who will underwrite on net rather than gross and who accepts that this band is bought for rent rather than for resale growth. It suits a family buying to live in who wants space and a campus inside their own community, and for whom the commute is acceptable. It suits a first purchase in Dubai where the entry price keeps the capital at risk small while the buyer learns how the market works.

It is a poorer fit for a buyer expecting capital appreciation, which is not what the entry band delivers. It is a poorer fit for anyone whose working life is anchored in the Marina, DIFC or Downtown, because the commute is the community's defining characteristic. And it is a poorer fit for a buyer who will not do building-level diligence, since in this band the spread between buildings within one community is wider than the spread between communities.

## Where should a Remraam buyer start?

Field note on sequence.

1. Shortlist specific buildings rather than the community, because that is where the variation sits
2. Get the service charge per square foot and the reserve fund position in writing from the owners association
3. Check the handover pipeline nearby, since competing new supply in an outer community moves rents quickly
4. Walk the route to South View School, because that proximity is the community's strongest tenant argument
5. Model the net against capital committed at transfer, meaning price plus the 6% to 8% of entry costs
6. Set the vacancy assumption from the apartment cycle rather than the villa one, at a re-letting commission near 5% of annual rent each time
