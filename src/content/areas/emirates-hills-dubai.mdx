## Can a foreign buyer own a villa in Emirates Hills?

Yes, and the position here is older and better established than in most of Dubai. Emirates Hills was part of the original premium tranche opened to foreign freehold ownership after the 2002 liberalisation, alongside parts of Palm Jumeirah, Dubai Marina and the downtown core. Title passes to the buyer outright.

That matters less as a legal question than as a market one. A community that has been foreign-ownable since the beginning of the freehold market has a two-decade transaction history in international hands, which is a deeper record than most Dubai communities can offer and a more reliable basis for understanding how the asset behaves over a long hold.

- Title is freehold and held in the buyer's own name, with no lease term and no expiry
- The community was part of the initial designation rather than a later addition, so the ownership record runs back to the start of the market
- Ownership carries no residency requirement, and residency does not follow automatically from ownership
- Corporate and jointly held structures are common at this value level and are registered on the deed

The designated list and the verification method are set out in [the Dubai freehold areas list](/guides/freehold-areas-dubai-list/), and the wider ownership rules in [can foreigners buy property in the UAE](/guides/can-foreigners-buy-property-uae/). For this community both are background rather than an obstacle.

## What is the search demand telling you here?

Roughly 12,100 monthly searches run on the community name in the UAE and about 590 on the unambiguous purchase phrase. The ratio is what makes this community unusual.

| Query | Monthly searches | What the searcher wants |
| --- | --- | --- |
| emirates hills | 12,100 | Mixed: the address itself, the golf club, residents, directions, reputational interest |
| villas for sale in emirates hills | 590 | Purchase, unambiguous |

That is roughly one purchase search for every twenty on the community name. Compare it with a mid-market villa community, where the same ratio commonly runs at one in a hundred or worse because the community name is carrying heavy rental and directions traffic from a large resident population. Emirates Hills has a small resident population and a large reputational profile, and the buying audience is a bigger share of a smaller total.

The practical reading is that the audience arriving on this community name is closer to the transaction than the equivalent audience elsewhere. It is still not large in absolute terms. Five hundred and ninety searches a month across every stage of intent is a specialised audience, which is exactly what a community of a few hundred houses should have.

## Who currently ranks for this community?

The visible results for the community name are dominated by the two UAE portals and by the specialist brokerages that concentrate on Dubai's premium tier, rather than by the general listings sites that lead in mid-market communities.

That composition reflects how the market actually operates. Houses at this value level are frequently transacted through a small number of brokers who know the individual properties and the individual owners, and a portion of the stock is marketed discreetly rather than listed openly. A buyer who works only from public listings is seeing a subset of what is available.

The consequence for a buyer is that the search results are a starting point rather than a market survey. The useful move is to establish a relationship with brokers active in this specific community, because the inventory they can describe is wider than the inventory they have published, and in a community of one-off houses the difference between the two matters more than it does where the product is standardised.

## What actually sets the price of a house here?

Land first, then the building, and the building is where the disagreements happen.

The plot is the stable component. Its size, its frontage, its position relative to the Montgomerie golf course and the lakes, and its aspect are fixed characteristics that can be compared across the community with reasonable confidence. Two plots of similar size in similar positions are genuinely comparable to one another.

The house is the variable component, and it is variable in ways that resist averaging. Age matters, because construction and specification standards have moved across two decades. The architect and the quality of the original build matter. The condition of the finishes and the plant matters, and on a house of this size a full refurbishment is a project with a seven-figure budget rather than a redecoration. The layout matters most of all, because a large house built to one generation's taste can be difficult to adapt to what buyers currently want, and adaptation on this scale is expensive.

Position within the community is the third component, and it behaves more like the land than like the building. The community is laid out around the Montgomerie golf course and a series of lakes, and a plot with an uninterrupted outlook over either carries a premium that is durable because the outlook cannot be built out. A plot with a similar footprint set back from both is a different asset at a different price, and the gap between them is one of the few adjustments in this community that can be evidenced across several transactions rather than argued from first principles. Establishing which category a plot falls into is therefore worth doing precisely rather than approximately.

That is why a price per square foot across Emirates Hills is not a useful number. It blends a recently completed house on a large plot with an original house awaiting refurbishment on a similar plot, and the two are separated by a margin that no per-foot average can express. The disciplined approach prices the land against comparable plots, then treats the house as either an addition to that value or a cost against it, depending honestly on its condition and layout.

## What does a purchase cost in transaction terms?

The stack is identical to the rest of the emirate. The percentages do not change with value, but at these prices the absolute figures are large enough to change how a purchase is planned and funded.

| Cost line | Rate | On a AED 30,000,000 purchase |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DLD transfer fee | 4% of price | AED 1,200,000 |
| Agency commission | 2% plus 5% VAT | AED 630,000 |
| Trustee office fee | Capped by DLD regulation | AED 4,200 to 5,250 |
| Developer no-objection certificate | AED 500 to 5,000 | AED 5,000 |
| Title deed issuance | AED 250 to 580 | Included in the trustee bundle |
| **Total closing** | **about 6%** | **about AED 1,840,000** |

The trustee fee and the title deed issuance are capped in absolute terms rather than charged as a percentage, so they shrink to rounding at this level. What does not shrink is the transfer fee, which at 4% of a large number is the single largest line in any Dubai purchase and is payable in cash at transfer.

Where a mortgage is involved, add registration at 0.25% of the loan, an arrangement fee near 1% and a valuation. Lending against a bespoke property is a different exercise from lending against a standard unit, because the bank's valuer faces the same comparability problem the buyer faces, and a conservative valuation on a one-off house is a common outcome. A buyer relying on a specific loan-to-value should establish the bank's position on the individual property early rather than assuming the standard terms. The full itemisation sits in [the cost of buying property in Dubai](/guides/cost-of-buying-property-dubai/).

## What does it cost to run a house of this size?

Every running cost in Dubai scales with either plot or built area, and in this community both are large at once. That compounding is what buyers most often underestimate.

The community charge is levied per square foot of plot. Dubai villa community charges broadly run in the region of AED 1.20 to AED 2.50 per square foot, and Emirates Hills plots are among the largest in the emirate, so the annual figure is correspondingly substantial. It is a fixed cost that does not vary with whether the house is occupied.

Cooling is the owner's arrangement on a villa rather than a building service, and a house of this footprint carries a standing cooling cost through the Dubai summer that is a genuine budget line rather than an incidental one.

Grounds are a contract rather than a task. Landscaping on a large plot, pool maintenance, and in many houses water features and irrigation systems are maintained under monthly agreements. Where a house includes staff accommodation, that carries its own associated costs.

Insurance and planned maintenance complete the picture. On a building of this value, structural and contents cover is standard, and a maintenance reserve for plant replacement is prudent rather than optional, because the mechanical systems in a twenty-year-old house of this complexity reach the end of their service lives on a schedule that can be anticipated.

The reason to itemise all of this is that the running cost is a meaningful fraction of the rental value here in a way that it is not on a mid-market villa. An owner who models the return on rent alone will be surprised by the net.

## An insider tip on buying a bespoke house

Insider tip: commission an independent building survey before agreeing a price on any Emirates Hills house, and commission it from a surveyor who works on properties of this scale rather than on standard villas. On a bespoke house the plant is bespoke too, and the specification of the cooling, the pool systems, the water features, the generators where fitted and the smart systems varies from house to house in ways a standard villa inspection does not cover. The survey does two things at once. It quantifies the deferred maintenance, which on a house of this age and complexity is routinely a seven-figure number and is a legitimate negotiating position rather than a reason to withdraw. And it establishes what the systems actually are, which the buyer will need from the day of transfer in order to contract the maintenance. Buyers who skip this step because the house presents well are pricing the finishes and inheriting the plant. In a community where the building is the variable component of the value, a survey is the only instrument that measures the variable.

## What does the rental market look like?

It exists, it pays the highest rents in the emirate, and it is a small and specific market rather than a deep one.

The tenant is a senior executive household, frequently on a corporate housing arrangement, and occasionally a family taking a long let while building or renovating elsewhere. That pool is small in absolute numbers because the number of households in the emirate that can take a house at this rent is small, and it is concentrated in a handful of sectors.

The practical consequence is that letting here is a search for one suitable tenant rather than a selection among several, and the marketing period reflects that. A landlord in this community plans for a longer letting period than a landlord in a mid-market villa community and prices the holding cost of that period into the return from the outset.

Once let, tenancies tend to be long and stable, for the same structural reasons that apply across the villa market and more strongly here. A household that has moved into a house of this size does not move again casually, and corporate arrangements typically run on multi-year cycles.

What the pattern tells a buyer is what this asset is principally for. Most owners in Emirates Hills are occupiers rather than landlords, and the community is bought to be lived in. An investment case exists, but it is a case about long-term capital in a limited-supply address rather than a case about yield, and anyone building the second case should model it on the first.

## A worked example on the entry cost

Worked example on the capital required rather than on a projected return, because the entry figure is what most often needs correcting. Take a house at AED 30,000,000 purchased for cash. Closing costs land at roughly AED 1,840,000, so the capital committed at transfer is about AED 31,840,000.

Now add what a bespoke house typically needs. If the survey identifies deferred maintenance and plant replacement, that is a further budget to be funded in the first year or two rather than spread across the hold. If the layout requires adaptation, refurbishment on this scale is a project with its own timeline during which the house generates nothing.

Against that, the annual running costs described above continue from the transfer date whether or not the house is occupied. A buyer modelling this asset should therefore work from three numbers rather than one: the purchase price, the entry costs plus immediate capital works, and the annual carrying cost. A yield calculated against the headline price alone will be wrong by a margin that matters at this scale. The general method sits in [the Dubai rental yield guide](/guides/dubai-rental-yield-guide/), and the adjustment for this community is to apply it to committed capital rather than to the price.

The comparison set is small, and each alternative is a different proposition rather than a substitute.

The distinction that runs through the table is between typed products and one-offs. Everywhere except Emirates Hills, a buyer can identify a type and find comparable transactions in that type. Here they cannot, and that changes both the valuation method and the time a purchase takes.

It also changes what supply means. A master community can deliver more of the same product. Emirates Hills cannot, because the land was allocated and built out, and what comes to market is what an existing owner chooses to sell. Limited supply of a non-repeatable asset is the structural argument for the address, and it is a different argument from the yield case that applies to most Dubai property.

## What does limited supply mean in practice?

Emirates Hills is built out. The land was allocated, the plots were sold and the houses were constructed, and there is no mechanism by which the community produces more of itself. That single structural fact shapes everything about how the market behaves.

In a master community still delivering phases, supply responds to demand. When prices rise the developer releases more units, and that release caps what a resale can achieve because a buyer always has the alternative of buying new. Emirates Hills has no such release. What comes to market is what an existing owner decides to sell, and the number of owners deciding to sell in any given period is small.

The first consequence is that available inventory is thin in absolute terms at any moment. A buyer looking for a specific combination of plot size, position and house condition may find that nothing currently on the market matches, and the correct response is patience rather than compromise, because the next house that fits may not appear for some time and equally may appear next month. This is a market where the search runs on the seller's timetable rather than the buyer's.

The second consequence is that the community does not have a single prevailing price level that moves as a block. Because each transaction is a one-off house sold to a particular buyer, individual prices reflect the specific asset and the specific negotiation as much as they reflect the market. A run of transactions at one level does not establish a level in the way it would in a typed community, and a single high or low transaction is weak evidence about the next one.

The third consequence is the one that matters most to a long holder. An asset that cannot be reproduced is a different proposition from an asset that can. The argument for this community has always rested on the fact that the plots are finite, the position is fixed and no future development can create an equivalent, rather than on any yield calculation. A buyer who understands that is buying the right thing. A buyer looking for a income multiple is looking at the wrong community.

## How does a sale actually proceed here?

More slowly and more privately than in the rest of the Dubai market, and a buyer who expects the mid-market rhythm will misread what is happening.

Discretion is the first difference. A meaningful share of what is available in this community is not published. Owners at this value level frequently prefer not to advertise that they are selling, and brokers hold that inventory quietly and introduce it to buyers they know are qualified and genuine. A buyer working only from portal listings sees the public subset, and the public subset is not the market.

Qualification is the second difference. Before a viewing on a house at this level, a seller's broker will generally want to establish that the buyer is real, funded and serious. That is not obstruction, it is the normal filter in a market where each viewing is an imposition on an occupied private home rather than a walk through a vacant unit. A buyer who arrives with proof of funds and a clear brief moves faster than one who does not.

Time is the third difference. From first viewing to transfer, a purchase here typically runs longer than a mid-market transaction, and the elongation comes from the survey, from the negotiation over what the survey finds, and where a mortgage is involved from the valuation of a bespoke asset. None of these steps can usefully be compressed, and attempting to compress them is how buyers end up with unquantified deferred maintenance.

The negotiation itself is the fourth difference. In a typed community, the argument is about where within a known range this unit sits. Here the argument is about what the house is worth at all, and both sides know that the comparable evidence is weak. That makes the survey and the plot analysis the strongest instruments a buyer has, because they are the only pieces of evidence in the room that are not opinion.

## What should a buyer check here?

Eight items, weighted toward the building rather than toward eligibility.

- The plot size, frontage and position, since these are the stable component of the value and the basis of any genuine comparison
- The independent building survey, covering the bespoke plant as well as the structure and finishes
- The age and provenance of the build, and whether the specification has been maintained or has fallen behind current standards
- The layout against current buyer preferences, because adaptation on this scale is a major project rather than an adjustment
- Any extension or external modification, and whether it carries approval, since an unapproved change surfaces at no-objection certificate stage
- The community charge on this specific plot for the coming year, which is a function of plot size rather than of the house
- The full annual running cost, itemised, including cooling, grounds, pool, insurance and any staff accommodation
- The title deed checked against the plot, since at this value level the deed rather than the marketing is the description of what is sold

We verify the plot characteristics and the survey findings before engaging on price, because in this community those two documents contain nearly all of the information that determines what the asset is worth.

## Who should look at Emirates Hills, and who should not?

It suits a buyer purchasing a principal residence at the top of the Dubai market who wants a large plot, a private setting and an address with a two-decade record in international ownership. It suits a buyer prepared to treat the house as a project where the survey warrants it, and to fund that project alongside the purchase. It suits long-horizon capital in a non-repeatable address, where the argument is limited supply rather than income.

It is a poorer fit for a buyer who wants to underwrite from comparable transactions in a standard product, because that method does not apply here and substituting a community average for it produces a number that describes nothing. It is a poorer fit for a buyer whose case rests on rental income, since the running costs are substantial and the tenant pool is specific. And it is a poorer fit for a buyer who needs a defined exit timetable, because a one-off house at this value is sold to a particular buyer rather than into a queue, and that is a process measured in the time it takes to find the right person rather than in weeks.

## Where should an Emirates Hills buyer start?

Field note on sequence, because in this community the order of the work saves the most time and money.

1. Establish the plot first: size, frontage, position relative to the course and the lakes, and what comparable plots have transacted at
2. Treat the house as a separate question from the land, and be explicit about whether it adds to the plot value or costs against it
3. Commission an independent survey from a surveyor experienced with houses of this scale and specification, before agreeing a price
4. Price the deferred maintenance and any layout adaptation as a funded budget rather than as a future intention
5. Itemise the annual running cost on this specific plot and house, including cooling, grounds and the plot-based community charge
6. Establish the lender's position on this individual property early if a mortgage is part of the plan, because bespoke assets value conservatively
7. Work with brokers active in this specific community, since a portion of the stock is marketed discreetly rather than listed
8. Model the return against committed capital, meaning price plus entry costs plus immediate works, rather than against the headline price

The single most useful thing to understand about Emirates Hills is that the ordinary Dubai valuation method does not transfer to it. Land is comparable, houses are not, and the gap between those two facts is where every negotiation in this community takes place.
