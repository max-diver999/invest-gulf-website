The stock is predominantly mid-rise apartment buildings handed over between 2010 and 2022, with a small volume of townhouses.

## An insider tip on the Silicon Oasis tenant base

Insider tip: Dubai Silicon Oasis rents move more slowly in both directions than Jumeirah Village Circle rents, and the reason is the tenant source rather than the building stock. Demand here comes from technology employers, the free zone authority and the academic institutions inside the district, which produces tenants who choose the location for the commute rather than for the price and who therefore stay longer and negotiate less. The practical consequence is a vacancy assumption of 5% to 6% rather than the 6% to 7% a general mid-market district warrants, worth roughly AED 600 a year on a AED 60,000 rent, and a slower rent response when the wider market moves. A landlord who values predictability over upside should read that as the district's main feature; a landlord expecting to ride a rent cycle should read it as its main limitation.

## What should a buyer check in Dubai Silicon Oasis?

Five items, and the district's own quirk is the developer mix.

- The service charge across two years of approved budgets, since the AED 12 to 15 range is narrow but the trajectory still varies by building
- Whether cooling is included in the charge or billed separately with a capacity component
- The building's completion year, because stock from 2010 to 2014 is entering its first major plant replacement cycle
- Parking allocation, which is generally better here than in the older tower districts but is not universal
- Distance from the unit to the Dubai Silicon Oasis Authority campus and the academic cluster, because that walk is what the tenant is choosing

## Dubai Silicon Oasis against JVC and Discovery Gardens

The three yield-belt districts are frequently compared on price per square foot alone, which misses the variable that decides whether a yield survives a cycle.

- **Dubai Silicon Oasis** at 6.8% to 8.2% gross: employer-anchored demand, steadiest tenancies, charges at AED 12 to 15, slowest rent response
- **Jumeirah Village Circle** at 7.5% to 9.2%: broadest tenant pool, fastest re-letting, largest handover pipeline, charges AED 11 to 19
- **Discovery Gardens** at 7.0% to 8.5%: oldest stock, lowest charges at AED 11 to 14, resale buyer pool almost entirely investors

Silicon Oasis is the choice for a landlord who wants fewer surprises and can accept a slightly lower headline yield. JVC is the choice for the fastest re-let and the deepest resale. Discovery Gardens is the choice for the lowest running cost with the narrowest exit.

## How liquid is Dubai Silicon Oasis at resale?

Moderate, and slower than the tower districts, which is the cost of the district's stability rather than a defect in it.

- Studios and one-bedrooms between AED 520,000 and AED 900,000: 8 to 14 weeks, buyer pool almost entirely investors
- Two-bedrooms between AED 1 million and AED 1.4 million: 10 to 16 weeks, a mix of investors and end-users working in the district
- Three-bedrooms and townhouses above AED 1.5 million: 3 to 5 months, end-user weighted
- Tenanted units at below-market rents: add 3 to 5 weeks

The comparison that matters is against Jumeirah Village Circle, where the same money clears in half the time. Silicon Oasis pays for that in a slightly higher yield and a steadier tenancy, and the trade is sensible for a buyer holding five years or more and poor for a buyer who may need an exit inside two.

## What are the risks specific to Silicon Oasis?

Three, and the first is the mirror image of the district's main strength.

Employer concentration. Demand here is anchored to the technology cluster, the free zone authority and the academic campus. That produces the stability the district is known for, and it means the rental market is correlated with a small number of employers rather than with the city-wide professional pool. In normal conditions this is invisible; in a downturn affecting that specific sector it is not.

Plant age in the earlier buildings. Stock handed over between 2010 and 2014 is entering its first major chiller, lift and pump replacement cycle, and whether that arrives as a routine expense or as a special levy depends on whether the owners association funded a reserve.

Resale depth. The buyer pool is narrower than in the tower districts and skews to investors, which means presentation matters less and the yield arithmetic matters more. A unit priced above the district comparable will sit rather than negotiate.

None is a reason to avoid the district. All three argue for choosing a building with a funded reserve and a documented charge history, and for treating the holding period as five years rather than two.

## A worked Silicon Oasis purchase

This worked example takes a one-bedroom in Dubai Silicon Oasis at AED 720,000, 800 square feet, in a building charging AED 13 per square foot, bought for cash and let long-term. The purpose is to show what the district's low charge does to the gap between gross and net.

Acquisition. The transfer fee at 4% is AED 28,800. Agency commission at 2% plus VAT is AED 15,120. The trustee fee is about AED 4,200 and the no-objection certificate around AED 2,000. Closing costs total roughly AED 50,120, a premium of 7.0%, taking the all-in figure to AED 770,120.

Income. A one-bedroom of that size lets in the AED 56,000 range, which is 7.8% gross on the purchase price and 7.3% on the all-in figure.

Deductions. The service charge is AED 10,400. Management at 6% of rent is AED 3,360. A vacancy allowance at 5.5%, reflecting the district's steadier tenancies, is AED 3,080. A maintenance provision at 0.5% of value is AED 3,600. Ejari and utility reconnection between tenancies add roughly AED 1,000 in an average year. Total deductions are AED 21,440.

Net rent is about AED 34,560, which is 4.8% on the purchase price and 4.5% on the all-in figure. The gap between gross and net is 3.0 percentage points on a low-charge building, which illustrates the point that management, vacancy and maintenance together matter as much as the service charge in this price band. Break-even on the entry cost from income alone runs to about seventeen months.

## Is Dubai Silicon Oasis right for a first purchase?

For a yield-first buyer with a five-year horizon and a preference for predictability, this district is among the most straightforward entries in Dubai.

Entry is low. A studio at AED 520,000 or a one-bedroom at AED 720,000 puts the total commitment including closing costs and the first year under AED 800,000, which is inside reach for a buyer priced out of the central districts.

The charge is low. At AED 12 to 15 per square foot the district sits at the bottom of the Dubai range, which is worth roughly half a percentage point of net yield against a comparable JVC building at AED 17.

The tenancy is stable. Employer-anchored demand produces longer tenancies and a 5% to 6% vacancy assumption rather than 6% to 7%, and across a five-year hold fewer turnovers are worth more than a slightly higher headline yield.

What it is not is liquid. At 8 to 14 weeks to resale on the smaller units this is a hold rather than a trade, and a buyer who may need an exit inside two years should look at Dubai Marina instead and accept the higher charge.

## How does financing work in Silicon Oasis?

Central bank ceilings apply as everywhere: up to 80% for a UAE national and 75% for an expatriate resident on a first property under AED 5 million, with non-residents at 50% to 65% and at pricing above resident rates. Valuations here are generally uncontentious because the stock is standardised and the comparable set within a single building is usually adequate. Where a shortfall does appear it is small in absolute terms at this price level, which is a material practical advantage over the villa market. ## What does the handover pipeline mean for Silicon Oasis rents?

Checking the pipeline before buying is the cheapest forward indicator available in any Dubai district, and in Silicon Oasis it matters less than in Jumeirah Village Circle for a structural reason worth understanding.

Off-plan registration makes scheduled completions public years ahead, so a buyer can establish how many units are due within the district over the next eight quarters. Silicon Oasis has a smaller live pipeline than the outer belt because much of its developable land is allocated to the technology and academic clusters rather than to residential towers, which is why rents here move more slowly in both directions.

The practical effect is that a landlord in this district is less exposed to the void risk that a block of simultaneous handovers creates elsewhere, and correspondingly less able to capture a sharp rent rise when the wider market tightens. That is the same trade the tenant base already implies, and a buyer choosing this district for its stability should treat both halves of it as the same decision rather than as two separate ones.
