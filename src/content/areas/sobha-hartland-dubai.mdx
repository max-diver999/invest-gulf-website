Dubai has a naming habit that costs buyers money: a successful community gets a numbered sequel in the same district, and the two then share a name in every listing, map and search result while differing in stage, product and price. Sobha Hartland and Sobha Hartland 2 are the clearest current example, and separating them is the first work any buyer here has to do.

## Which Hartland is the listing about?

Two communities, one developer, one district, and a number that is easy to drop.

Sobha Hartland is the original. Our developer comparisons characterise it by family skew, Dubai Creek proximity and a quality premium, which is the profile of a settled community rather than a launch.

Sobha Hartland 2 is the expansion phase. The corpus describes it as an 8 million square foot waterfront master community in Mohammed Bin Rashid City, developed by Sobha Realty around three crystal lagoons, with apartment handovers in 2027 to 2028 and villa timing that depends on the selected phase.

The practical difference is stage before anything else. One is a delivered community where a buyer purchases a completed home and can inspect it. The other is largely a forward purchase with a payment schedule and a handover date. Those are different transactions with different risks, and a comparable drawn from one does not price the other.

We verify which community a listing or a comparable actually refers to before using it, for the same reason it matters in [DAMAC Hills against DAMAC Hills 2](/uae/dubai/damac-hills-2/): numbered sequels in the same district produce the most persistent mispricing in this market. The wider district context sits on [the MBR City page](/areas/mbr-city-property-investment/).

## What are the recorded price bands?

For Sobha Hartland 2 the corpus records a full ladder, and it is worth reading as a range rather than as a price.

| Product | Entry figure |
| --- | --- |
| One-bedroom apartment | AED 1.5 million |
| Two-bedroom apartment | AED 2.2 million |
| Three-bedroom apartment | AED 3 million |
| Villas | from AED 16 million |
| Mansions | from AED 60 million |

Apartment handovers are described as 2027 to 2028, and villa delivery is phase-specific.

Two cautions attach to that table and both matter. These are planning references rather than a live sales offer, and unit availability, payment schedules, service charges, registration and handover notices all change. And the spread from AED 1.5 million to AED 60 million means the community contains several distinct markets that do not price against one another, so an average across it describes nothing that is for sale. Price a specific unit against recent transactions in its own band and phase, and treat every figure from another band as context rather than as evidence.

## What actually protects an off-plan payment?

Three documents and one account, and the sequence is not optional.

Confirm the exact unit and the registered project first. A reservation on a project that is not registered, or on a unit that is not the one named in the paperwork, is exposure with no remedy attached.

Pay only into the RERA-approved escrow account named in the contract. This is the single most important line in any Dubai off-plan purchase. Funds paid anywhere else, including to a broker, into a developer's general account or against an invoice that names a different account, sit outside the protection the escrow regime exists to provide.

Retain the Oqood registration evidence for as long as construction continues. Oqood is the interim registration of an off-plan interest, and the evidence of it is what establishes the buyer's position before a title deed exists.

What controls the transaction is the sale and purchase agreement, the escrow details, the Dubai Land Department records and the developer's current handover notice. Community marketing does not. A broker message does not. An indicative gross yield figure certainly does not, and treating any of them as a commitment is the error the escrow regime cannot fix afterwards. The full process is set out in [the Dubai off-plan property guide](/guides/off-plan-property-dubai-guide/).

## Can a foreign buyer own here?

Yes, outright. The community sits within Mohammed Bin Rashid City in designated freehold territory, so title is issued in the buyer's own name with no lease term, and Dubai's freehold framework applies with no annual property tax on the holding.

- Title is freehold and recorded in the Dubai Land Department register
- Ownership carries no residency requirement, and residency does not follow from ownership
- The same framework applies across the price bands, from the apartment entry point to villa and mansion purchases

The designated list is in [the Dubai freehold areas list](/guides/freehold-areas-dubai-list/). Because the legal position is settled here, the diligence weight falls entirely on the off-plan process described above.

## What does the transaction cost?

The stack is the same across the emirate, and on an off-plan purchase the transfer fee arrives at title transfer rather than at reservation, which is a timing distinction worth planning for. The illustration uses AED 1,500,000, the recorded one-bedroom entry figure.

| Cost line | Rate | On a AED 1,500,000 purchase |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DLD transfer fee | 4% of price | AED 60,000 |
| Agency commission | 2% plus 5% VAT | AED 31,500 |
| Trustee office fee | Capped by DLD regulation | AED 4,200 |
| Developer no-objection certificate | AED 500 to 5,000 | AED 2,000 |
| Service charge deposit | Set by the community | Confirm in the offer |
| **Total closing** | **6% to 7%** | **about AED 97,700** |

On an off-plan purchase, add the Oqood registration cost at the point of interim registration, and budget the service charge deposit alongside it. The itemisation is in [the cost of buying property in Dubai](/guides/cost-of-buying-property-dubai/).

## An insider tip on numbered communities

Insider tip: when a developer launches a numbered sequel in the same district, ask for the project's RERA registration number rather than its marketing name, and use that number for every subsequent check. The names are close enough that a listing, a valuation and a comparable set can each refer to a different one of the two without anybody noticing, and the registration number is the only identifier that cannot slide between them. The same request does a second job on an off-plan purchase: the registration number is what you use to confirm that the escrow account named in your contract belongs to the project you are actually buying into, which is the check that the entire escrow protection depends on. Buyers who work from the marketing name are relying on everyone in the chain having been careful with a single digit. Buyers who work from the registration number are relying on a record. The number is on the project documents and any developer will supply it, and a reluctance to do so is itself the answer to a different question.

## How does a handover window actually behave?

Described as a range, experienced as a moving target, and a buyer who plans around the optimistic end of it carries the cost of the difference.

Apartment handovers in the expansion phase are described as 2027 to 2028. That is already a range rather than a date, and villa timing is stated as phase-specific, which means the answer depends on which phase a specific unit sits in rather than on the community as a whole. Neither of those is unusual or a criticism. It is how large master communities are delivered.

What matters is what the buyer builds on top of it. Three commitments are commonly made against a handover date and each one has a cost when the date moves. A tenancy promised to an incoming tenant. A mortgage offer with an expiry. And a personal move-in plan with a lease ending on the other side.

The disciplined approach is to work from the developer's current handover notice rather than from the timeline given at launch, and to re-check it as construction proceeds rather than once. The notice is the document that carries the developer's present position, and it supersedes marketing material, a broker's recollection and the buyer's own memory of what was said at reservation.

For an investment model the same point applies to the income. Rent does not begin at the described handover, it begins when the unit is handed over, fitted where necessary, marketed and let. Between the earliest and latest ends of a described range, plus a letting period, the first rent can arrive a long way from where a spreadsheet built at reservation assumed. Modelling the first year conservatively costs nothing if delivery is early and prevents a shortfall if it is not.

## What does the Creek position contribute?

It is the reason the original community carries its premium, and it works differently from a beach or marina address.

Creek proximity within Mohammed Bin Rashid City places the community close to the centre of the city rather than at its coastal edge, which suits a household whose life runs across Dubai rather than along the shoreline. That is a different proposition from a waterfront address bought for the view and the leisure, and it attracts a different resident: the family skew the corpus records is a direct consequence of the position.

For a landlord that translates into the characteristics that matter over a hold rather than in a listing. A family-oriented community close to the city centre draws tenants who sign longer and move less, because their reasons for being there are structural, schooling, work and routine, rather than seasonal. Each avoided turnover saves a void period plus a re-letting commission near 5 percent of annual rent, and across a five-year hold that difference is worth more to the realised return than a fraction of a point on the headline yield.

The expansion phase adds a second kind of amenity to the same district, since it is built around three crystal lagoons across 8 million square feet. Water features of that scale are a genuine draw and they are also a running cost, because lagoons require treatment, circulation and maintenance funded through the service charge. That is the same arithmetic as any amenity-led community: fair when the amenity raises the achievable rent, expensive when it does not.

The practical consequence for a buyer choosing between the two Hartlands is that they are optimised differently. The original offers a settled community with delivered infrastructure and a transaction record to price against. The expansion offers newer product and a larger amenity provision, at the cost of a delivery timeline and a service charge that has to fund what it provides. Neither is the better choice in the abstract, and the choice between them is the one this page exists to make possible.

## What should a buyer check here?

Eight items, weighted toward the off-plan process because that is where the exposure sits.

- Which community the listing refers to, confirmed by project registration number rather than by name
- The RERA project registration itself, before any reservation payment
- The escrow account named in the contract, verified as belonging to that registered project
- The exact unit, matched between the offer, the sale and purchase agreement and the registration
- The Oqood registration evidence, obtained and retained through construction
- The payment schedule and what each milestone is tied to
- The current handover notice, since described handover windows move
- The service charge position and deposit, confirmed in the offer rather than estimated

We verify the registration and the escrow account before anything else, because those two checks are what make the remaining diligence worth doing.

## What kind of hold is this?

A quality-premium family hold rather than a yield play, and being clear about that prevents the most common disappointment.

The corpus characterises Sobha Hartland by family skew, Creek proximity and a quality premium, and sets it against DAMAC Hills as a different hold style. A premium of that kind is paid for build quality, finish and the settled character of the community, and it is recovered over a long hold through resilience and tenant quality rather than through a high running yield.

That makes the developer judgement central. A buyer paying a quality premium is making a bet on the builder, and the merit of that bet is a question about Sobha rather than about the district. Our assessment sits in [the Sobha Realty review](/guides/sobha-realty-review/), and the comparison against the other major developers is in [Emaar against Sobha](/compare/emaar-vs-sobha/).

The corollary is that an investor underwriting this community against yield-belt returns is applying the wrong benchmark, in the same way that underwriting a yield-belt apartment against a quality premium would be. Both are defensible purchases and they answer different questions.

## Who should look at Sobha Hartland, and who should not?

It suits a buyer who wants a family community with a quality premium and Creek proximity, and who is buying for a long hold rather than for running yield. It suits an off-plan buyer prepared to run the registration and escrow checks properly rather than relying on marketing. It suits a buyer who has formed a view on Sobha as a developer, since that view is most of the investment case.

It is a poorer fit for a buyer seeking the highest gross yield available in Dubai, which sits in the mid-market apartment belt. It is a poorer fit for anyone who wants to buy a completed home immediately in the expansion phase, where apartment handovers are described as 2027 to 2028. And it is a poorer fit for a buyer unwilling to distinguish the two communities by registration number, because every figure they gather will be a blend of the two.

## Where should a buyer start?

Field note on sequence.

1. Establish which community, by RERA project registration number rather than by name
2. Confirm the project registration before any reservation payment
3. Verify that the escrow account in the contract belongs to that registered project
4. Match the exact unit across offer, agreement and registration
5. Obtain and retain the Oqood evidence once interim registration is done
6. Read the payment schedule against the current handover notice rather than the launch timeline
7. Budget the 6% to 7% of closing costs at title transfer, plus the service charge deposit
