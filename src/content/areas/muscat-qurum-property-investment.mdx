Muscat Qurum is not a foreign investment destination. It is Oman's most established expat residential district and the rental benchmark against which every ITC investment should be underwritten. Foreigners cannot buy freehold in Qurum. They can study Qurum to understand what tenants pay, why they pay it, and whether [Al Mouj](/areas/muscat-al-mouj-property-investment/) pricing is rational.

## Can foreigners buy property in Qurum?

Foreigners cannot buy freehold in Qurum because the district is general Muscat residential outside ITC designation; non-GCC capital belongs in [ITC freehold zones](/guides/oman-itc-zones-property/) such as Al Mouj or Muscat Bay. We treat Qurum as a rent benchmark only, never as a foreign purchase target, and redirects 100% of foreign buy files to ITC stock under current rules.

Redirect checklist:
- Confirm the plot is outside ITC designation
- Move capital to Al Mouj or Muscat Bay freehold
- Keep Qurum comps only for rent underwriting

## Why do Qurum tenants pay a location premium?

Qurum tenants typically pay a location premium because embassy corridors, international schools, Al Qurum Natural Park, and walkable urban stock support long-term expat family demand over multi-year leases. Our underwriting compares that premium with Al Mouj marina and golf amenities when testing whether ITC asking rents stay within about 15% of Qurum comps.

Premium drivers to verify:
- Embassy walking distance
- School catchment for ABS or BSM
- Park and urban walk scores versus marina trade-offs

## What yields do Omani owners see in Qurum?

Omani owners in Qurum typically see about 5 to 7% gross on apartments, with premium Shati Al Qurum waterfront often closer to 4.5 to 6% and net around 3 to 5% after service charges. Foreign investors reach similar economics only through ITC stock such as Al Mouj, which our underwriting compares against these Qurum floors.

Yield bands to model:
- Gross 5 to 7% on standard Qurum apartments
- Gross 4.5 to 6% on Shati waterfront
- Net about 3 to 5% after charges for Omani landlords

## What drives Qurum rental demand?

Qurum rental demand is typically driven by embassy housing budgets, corporate employment in banking and oil and gas, and school-linked family leases near American British School corridors. Housing allowances in the area run about OMR 300 to 1,000 per month, which our underwriting treats as a demand floor rather than a 5 to 7% yield claim.

Demand checks:
- Embassy budget continuity
- Corporate housing allowance letters
- School-year lease renewal timing

**Embassy and diplomatic housing:** Predetermined budgets create a stable demand floor for roughly 12 to 24 month leases.

**Corporate employment:** Banking, oil and gas offices, healthcare, and education clusters feed mid-tier leases at about OMR 400 to 800 allowances.

| Industry sector | Qurum employee base (indicative) | Typical housing allowance |
| | | |
| Banking and finance | 2,500 to 3,500 | OMR 400 to 800 |
| Oil and gas (Muscat offices) | 1,800 to 2,500 | OMR 500 to 1,000 |
| Healthcare and education | 2,000 to 3,000 | OMR 300 to 600 |

Neither district wins every category; our underwriting matches tenant type to lifestyle before stretching ITC rents more than 15% past Qurum two-bed comps near OMR 500 to 700.

Neither wins every category. Tenant choice is urban convenience versus resort living.
