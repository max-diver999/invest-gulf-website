## What do City Walk numbers look like in 2026?

City Walk in 2026 is a Meraas freehold urban village where one-bedroom apartments typically trade from AED 1.6 million and gross yields sit near 4.5-6.0% on Q1 Ejari rents. Net yields after AED 20-30 per sq ft service charges usually land at 3.5-4.8%, so the area suits capital preservation more than pure yield plays.

City Walk apartments typically cost AED 1.6 million to AED 2.8 million for one-bedroom units in Q1 2026, with two-bedroom stock ranging AED 2.5 million to AED 4.5 million and off-plan phases often priced AED 2,200-3,200 per sq ft. Gross rental yields of 4.5-6.0% compress to about 3.5-4.8% net after service charges of AED 20-30 per sq ft, management near 6% of rent, and a 5% vacancy allowance. A worked one-bedroom near AED 2.1 million with AED 115,000 annual rent shows 5.48% gross and 3.71% net once AED 20,500 service charges and related costs are deducted. Downtown-adjacent towers 5-10 minutes from Burj Khalifa often ask 15-25% more for similar floor plates, so buyers trade the Burj address premium for walkable retail. Foreign buyers can purchase freehold with up to 75% LTV on ready stock and should budget 4% DLD transfer plus 2% agency.

- Planning band: AED 1.6M-2.8M for 1BR, AED 2.5M-4.5M for 2BR
- Yield band: 4.5-6.0% gross, 3.5-4.8% net after charges
- Transfer stack: 4% DLD + 2% agency on ready freehold

## Why does City Walk command a premium?

City Walk commands a premium because tenants typically pay for walkable access to 70-plus retail outlets inside a low-density Meraas street format near Downtown. Equivalent Downtown towers often price 15-25% higher, while Meraas fit-out can support 10-20% rent premiums over similar Business Bay stock in the area.

- Urban walkability: daily errands without a car in most phases
- Downtown proximity: 5-15 minutes to DIFC, offices, and Dubai Mall
- Build quality: design-forward mid-rise product with smart-home specs on newer phases
- Finite density: urban-village masterplan, not a 50-tower cluster

Insider tip: pay for walkability only when the exact stack faces quiet residential streets, because retail frontage can erase the rent premium a disciplined model uses for lifestyle tenants.

## What does a City Walk net yield look like on a typical 1BR?

A typical City Walk one-bedroom underwrites about AED 115,000 gross rent on a roughly AED 2.1 million purchase, which is 5.48% gross before costs. After AED 20,500 service charges at AED 25 per sq ft on 820 sq ft, 6% management, 5% vacancy, and maintenance, net yield lands near 3.71%.

|---|---|
| Gross rent (Ejari transacted, Q1 2026) | AED 115,000 |
| Gross yield | 5.48% |
| Service charges (AED 25 psf × 820 sq ft) | AED 20,500 |
| Property management (6% of rent) | AED 6,900 |
| Ejari registration + admin | AED 400 |
| Vacancy allowance (5%) | AED 5,750 |
| Maintenance provision | AED 3,500 |
| Total costs | AED 37,050 |
| Net income | AED 77,950 |
| Net yield | 3.71% |

That 3.71% net yield is below mid-market communities. The investment case rests on capital preservation, Meraas quality, and Downtown-adjacent address, not cash-flow maximisation. For UK or European investors, zero UAE personal income tax on rental returns improves the after-tax comparison significantly. A disciplined method keeps vacancy at 5% and service charges at the live building rate before calling any City Walk deal "cash-flow positive."

## Who rents in City Walk?

City Walk tenants are typically DIFC and Downtown professionals, media workers near Al Quoz, and affluent couples who will pay more rent to walk to retail for 12-24 months. Corporate furnished leases of 6-12 months also appear, while families with one child value Green Planet and pedestrian streets over pure price in the area.

- DIFC and Downtown professionals who want walkable urban lifestyle
- Creative and media industry workers in nearby Al Quoz and Media City
- Affluent expat couples without children prioritising F&B and entertainment access
- Corporate lease tenants on 6-12 month furnished contracts
- Young families with one child who value Green Planet and pedestrian safety

Average tenancy length runs 12-24 months. Tenants who choose City Walk specifically want the urban-village lifestyle, they do not compromise to save rent. That preference sustains rent levels even when Business Bay offers cheaper alternatives 10 minutes away. Buyer scenario: underwrite renewals every 12 months, not 36-month sticky leases.

## How does retail and F&B support City Walk rents?

City Walk's open-air high street supports tenant stickiness because 70-plus outlets, Green Planet, Hub Zero, and cinema options sit beside residential phases and reduce the need for mall trips. Footfall sustains F&B operators and gives landlords a leasing story beyond square footage, provided frontage noise and delivery access are checked on the exact stack.

- Retail density: 70-plus outlets within walking distance of most residences
- STR planning: AED 1,520 DET permit, AED 400-650 peak nightly, 65-78% peak occupancy
- Charge sensitivity: AED 20-30 psf can move net yield by hundreds of basis points

Short-term letting in City Walk requires a DET permit at AED 1,520 per year and typically underwrites AED 400-650 nightly for a one-bedroom in peak season with 65-78% peak occupancy, below JBR beach demand. Long-term tenants often stay 12-24 months and pay for access to 70+ retail and F&B outlets plus a 5-15 minute link to DIFC and Dubai Mall. Service charges of AED 20-30 per sq ft mean an 820 sq ft unit can face an AED 8,200 annual gap between the low and high band, roughly 7% of a AED 115,000 gross rent. Meraas delivery near 91% supports off-plan confidence, yet SPA charge estimates often understate live budgets. Invest Gulf red-flag checklist before SPA: pull three years of service-charge history, confirm building STR permission in writing, and price any new phase against ready comps in the same stack.

## Should you buy ready stock or off-plan in City Walk?

Ready City Walk stock typically means rent can start after fit-out, while Meraas off-plan often needs 24-36 months without income despite roughly 91% historical delivery confidence. New phases can add competing listings at handover, so price SPA plans against live AED service-charge evidence in the area.

- No rental income during construction (24-36 months)
- Meraas's ~91% delivery rate provides developer confidence
- SPA service charge estimates often understate actual costs
- Handover-year investor listings can compress prices temporarily
- New phases may benefit from expanded retail and Central Park amenities

For income now, ready stock. For lower cash-at-purchase and newer product, off-plan from Meraas.

## What service charges apply by City Walk building type?

City Walk service charges typically range AED 20-30 per sq ft depending on building age and amenity load, which on an 820 sq ft apartment is AED 16,400 to AED 24,600 per year. Original residences often sit AED 20-25 per sq ft, newer City Walk Residences AED 22-28, and premium penthouses AED 25-30.

| Building type | Service charge range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Original City Walk residences | AED 20-25 per sq ft | Established budgets |
| City Walk Residences (newer) | AED 22-28 per sq ft | Enhanced amenities |
| Premium penthouses | AED 25-30 per sq ft | Larger common-area allocation |

On an 820 sq ft apartment, the difference between AED 20 and AED 30 per sq ft is AED 8,200 per year, 7% of gross rent. Model the specific building. Checklist: reject SPA service-charge estimates that sit more than 15% below the latest audited building budget.

## What due diligence red flags apply in City Walk?

City Walk due diligence fails fast when service-charge history is missing, STR rules are verbal only, or off-plan plans lack RERA escrow registration. Premium prices mean a thin data gap on AED 20-30 psf charges or DET permission can erase the thesis in the first 12 months for the area.

- Missing multi-year service charge statements
- Verbal-only short-let approval without building bylaws
- Off-plan SPA without verified RERA escrow details
- New phase handover colliding with your resale window

## How does City Walk compare with other central Dubai communities?

City Walk typically wins on urban walkability and Meraas mid-rise product at a 15-25% discount to equivalent Downtown towers, while Business Bay usually wins on higher gross yield percentages. Gross yields of 4.5-6.0% sit close to Downtown's 4.5-6.5% band, so the area choice is lifestyle and density, not a large yield gap.

Insider tip: for a City Walk purchase, compare the exact unit with similar ready apartments in Downtown and Business Bay, not a district-wide average, then check frontage noise, parking, and the current listings in the same building before you treat short-term letting as part of the underwriting.

## Which buyer profile fits City Walk?

City Walk fits buyers who accept 3.5-4.8% net yield for Meraas urban retail and Downtown proximity inside a freehold zone, not investors hunting maximum cash yield under AED 1 million entry. Beach or Burj-address seekers should price JBR or Downtown instead, where gross bands often sit near 4.5-6.5%.

- Fit: capital-preservation buyers who value walkable retail and 5-15 minute Downtown access
- Poor fit: sub-AED 1 million tickets, pure 7%+ net yield hunters, beach STR theses
- Buyer scenario: hold 5+ years, underwrite net after AED 20-30 psf charges, ignore brochure STR unless DET permission is written

Checklist: if the area thesis needs Burj Khalifa branding or beach occupancy, skip City Walk and price Downtown or JBR comps instead.
