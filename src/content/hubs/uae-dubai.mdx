Dubai is the deepest property market in the Gulf and the only one where a foreign buyer can complete a purchase end to end without ever holding a residence visa. That combination, open freehold plus a registered title system, is why roughly two thirds of Dubai transactions involve a foreign buyer and why the practical questions here are about cost and product rather than about permission.

## Where can foreigners buy property in Dubai?

Foreign nationals can buy freehold in more than 60 designated zones, and that list covers every community a non-resident buyer is likely to shortlist. Outside those zones the available interest is leasehold of up to 99 years, which is a different asset with a different resale profile.

The zone status sits on the title deed and in the Dubai Land Department record. It is checkable before any money moves, and checking it is the first item in the [step-by-step purchase process](/guides/how-to-buy-property-dubai-step-by-step/). The practical failure mode is not buying in the wrong zone, it is discovering at trustee stage that the unit is leasehold when the model assumed freehold resale liquidity.

| Ownership form | Where it applies | What the buyer gets | Resale profile |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Freehold | 60-plus designated zones | Title deed in own name, land and unit, unlimited term | Deepest buyer pool, standard mortgage market |
| Leasehold, up to 99 years | Selected non-designated areas | Right to occupy and sublet for the lease term | Narrower buyer pool, price discount widens as term shortens |
| Usufruct or musataha | Commercial and some mixed-use plots | Defined use right for a fixed period | Institutional buyers, not a retail resale market |

Our underwriting treats the zone status as a gate rather than a preference: a leasehold interest priced against freehold comparables is the most common way a Dubai model overstates a resale value by 15% or more.

## What does a Dubai purchase actually cost?

Budget 6% to 7% above the purchase price on a cash resale, and closer to 8% with a mortgage. The cost stack is regulated and the rates are published, so a quote that differs materially from the table below deserves a question rather than a signature.

| Cost line | Rate | Who pays | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DLD transfer fee | 4% of price | Buyer by convention | Paid at the trustee office on completion |
| Oqood registration, off-plan | 4% of price | Buyer | Replaces the transfer fee, paid at booking |
| Agency commission | 2% plus 5% VAT | Buyer | RERA regulated, some launches charge the buyer nothing |
| Trustee office fee | AED 4,000 to 5,250 | Split, in practice the buyer | Capped by DLD regulation |
| Mortgage registration | 0.25% of the loan | Buyer | DLD charge, separate from the bank fee |
| Bank arrangement fee | About 1% of the loan | Buyer | Minimum around AED 10,000 |
| Developer NOC | AED 500 to 5,000 | Seller, usually passed on | Resale only |
| Title deed issuance | AED 250 to 580 | Buyer | Bundled with the trustee fee |

On a ready apartment at AED 1.5 million bought for cash, that is roughly AED 98,000 of closing costs, an effective premium of 6.5% over the price. ## What is the entry price by product type?

There is no single Dubai entry price, but there are reliable floors by product. A studio in the mid-market apartment belt starts around AED 550,000, a one-bedroom around AED 750,000, a townhouse in an outer master community around AED 1.8 million, and an established-community villa around AED 3.8 million.

Two thresholds change the shape of a purchase rather than just its size. The first is roughly AED 1 million, below which the product set narrows to studios and one-bedrooms in yield-led communities. The second is AED 2 million, the registered value that unlocks the 10-year Golden Visa. Buyers targeting the visa threshold face a specific trade-off, because the cheapest units that clear AED 2 million are not the same units that produce the best yield. That trade-off is worked through unit by unit in [the AED 2M Golden Visa value analysis](/guides/aed-2m-golden-visa-best-value-units/), and the sub-AED 1M end is covered in [a first Dubai investment under AED 1M](/guides/dubai-first-investment-under-aed-1m/).

Buyer scenario: a purchaser targeting residency on a AED 2 million budget faces a direct trade, because the cheapest qualifying units are rarely the best-yielding ones and the gap runs to about 2 percentage points of gross yield.

## How does a foreign buyer complete a purchase remotely?

Most non-resident purchases complete without the buyer flying in. The sequence runs from a signed reservation to a Form F sale agreement, a developer no-objection certificate issued in 1 to 7 business days, then registration at a trustee office where the title deed is issued the same day, under a power of attorney.

The two steps that fail most often are the money and the counterparty. Funds must arrive from an account in the buyer's own name with documented source of funds, which is covered in [buying UAE property by bank transfer](/guides/buying-property-uae-bank-transfer/). The counterparty check is faster: every broker in Dubai carries a RERA broker registration number that can be verified in minutes, and the method is set out in [RERA broker verification](/guides/rera-broker-verification-dubai/). The DLD REST app verifies the unit itself, including whether the title is clear, and that process is described in [property due diligence with the REST app](/guides/dubai-rest-app-property-due-diligence/).

Our checklist for a remote purchase runs in this order: source-of-funds documentation, broker registration number, title record in the REST app, then the power of attorney chain, which takes 7 to 14 days and should start before a completion date is agreed.

## What are the real risks in the Dubai market?

Three risks show up repeatedly in buyer files, and none is the market crash that dominates search interest. The service charge at AED 12 to 25 per square foot is the largest recurring deduction, off-plan handover slippage runs a year or more on many schemes, and a sale above AED 30 million can take 18 months.

The first is the service charge. It is the single largest recurring deduction from net yield, it is set by the owners association rather than by the buyer, and it rises. A building with a low headline price and a high charge per square foot can produce a worse net return than a more expensive building next door. Where a charge looks wrong, there is a formal escalation route, described in [service charge dispute escalation](/guides/dubai-service-charge-dispute-escalation/).

The second is off-plan handover slippage. Escrow law protects the money, not the calendar. A twelve-month delay on a leveraged purchase changes the return profile materially, and the compensation route is narrower than most buyers expect. The mechanics are in [developer delay compensation](/guides/dubai-developer-delay-compensation-claim/).

The third is liquidity at the top end. Below about AED 5 million the resale market is deep and comparables are easy. Above roughly AED 30 million, a sale can take twelve to eighteen months at a target price. That is not a defect, it is the normal behaviour of a thin trophy market, but it belongs in the model before purchase rather than after. Red flag: a yield projection above AED 20 million that assumes a twelve-week exit is describing a market that does not exist at that price point.

## Which freehold zones matter in practice?

The designated list runs past 60 entries, and the zones carrying real transaction volume are far fewer. Grouping them by purpose is more useful than reading the register alphabetically, because a yield-belt community at AED 950 per square foot and a waterfront address at AED 3,200 answer different questions.

| Tier | Zones | What defines the tier |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Yield belt | Jumeirah Village Circle, Jumeirah Village Triangle, Dubai Silicon Oasis, Dubai Sports City, Discovery Gardens, International City, Dubai Production City, Dubai South | Mid-market apartments, service charges at the low end, deepest tenant demand |
| Urban core | Business Bay, Jumeirah Lakes Towers, Dubai Marina, Downtown Dubai, DIFC, City Walk, Al Furjan | Corporate tenants, metro access, strongest short-let case |
| Waterfront | Palm Jumeirah, Bluewaters Island, Dubai Harbour, Dubai Creek Harbour, Dubai Islands, JBR | Supply constrained, lowest yields, highest capital preservation |
| Family villa | Arabian Ranches, Dubai Hills Estate, Damac Hills, Tilal Al Ghaf, The Springs, Mudon, Town Square, The Valley, Villanova | Long tenancies, school-driven demand, land component in the price |
| Emerging | Mohammed Bin Rashid City, Meydan, Dubailand cluster, Jebel Ali Village, Expo City | Master plans mid build-out, launch pricing, delivery risk |

Two practical points about the register. First, freehold status attaches to the plot, not to the developer or the building name, so a project marketed as being in a freehold community can sit on an adjacent leasehold plot. Second, the register is the authority and the listing is not. Verifying the plot number against the Dubai Land Department record takes minutes and is the cheapest due diligence available in this market.

## How does a Dubai purchase run, step by step?

A Dubai resale purchase runs through six stages, and a cash transaction typically clears in three to six weeks from agreed price to title deed. A mortgaged purchase adds two to four weeks for valuation and liability settlement, and a seller with an existing loan adds roughly a month to that.

| Stage | What happens | Typical timing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Offer and reservation | Price agreed, reservation form signed, deposit of 10% held by the agent or trustee | 1 to 3 days |
| Form F, the memorandum of understanding | The Dubai Land Department standard sale contract, signed by both parties | Same week |
| Developer no-objection certificate | The developer confirms service charges are clear and consents to transfer | 1 to 7 business days |
| Mortgage steps, if financed | Valuation, final offer, seller liability letter, bank settlement of any existing loan | 2 to 4 weeks |
| Trustee appointment | Both parties or their attorneys attend a registration trustee office, manager cheques handed over | 2 to 5 business days after the certificate |
| Title deed issued | The Dubai Land Department issues the deed in the buyer's name, same day at the trustee office | Same day |

The bottleneck is almost never the buyer. It is the developer certificate on a building with an unresolved service charge arrear, or the seller's bank on a mortgaged property, where discharge and reconveyance run on the lender's timetable rather than the transaction's. Ask on day one whether the seller has a mortgage, because that single answer changes the realistic completion date by a month.

An off-plan purchase runs differently. The reservation is followed by a sale and purchase agreement with the developer, Oqood registration at 4%, and then a payment schedule tied to construction milestones. There is no trustee appointment and no title deed until handover. The full walkthrough of both routes is in [how to buy property in Dubai step by step](/guides/how-to-buy-property-dubai-step-by-step/).

## What does a foreign buyer need to produce?

Foreign buyers need less documentation in Dubai than in most jurisdictions and more than the marketing suggests. A passport is the identity requirement, no residence visa is needed, and the substantive test is source of funds. A mortgage adds six months of statements, and a remote purchase adds a power of attorney taking 7 to 14 days.

A passport copy is the identity document, and a UAE residence visa is not required. For a cash purchase the substantive requirement is source of funds: banks and the trustee will want to see that money arrived from an account in the buyer's own name, with documentation showing where it came from. A transfer routed through a third party, an exchange house or a corporate account the buyer does not obviously control is the most common cause of a stalled completion.

For a mortgage, add six months of bank statements, proof of income, a credit report from the home country in some cases, and for self-employed applicants two to three years of company accounts. Non-resident applications take longer than resident ones and are declined more often on documentation than on affordability.

For a remote purchase, add a power of attorney, notarised in the country of signing, legalised or apostilled, attested by the UAE embassy where required and translated into Arabic by a legal translator. Allow seven to fourteen days for that chain, and start it before agreeing a completion date rather than after. ## How is rent regulated once you own?

Rent regulation is the part of the Dubai model that most yield projections ignore, and it decides whether a rising market ever reaches the landlord. Increases are capped by the RERA rental index under Decree No. 43 of 2013, banded from no increase permitted up to a maximum of 25%, and applied only at renewal.

Rent increases are governed by Decree No. 43 of 2013 and calculated by the RERA rental index, which compares the current contract rent with the average market rent for the same area, unit type and bedroom count. The permitted increase is banded.

| Current rent against the RERA average | Maximum increase at renewal |
| --- | --- |
| Within 10% of the average | No increase permitted |
| 11% to 20% below the average | 5% |
| 21% to 30% below the average | 10% |
| 31% to 40% below the average | 15% |
| More than 40% below the average | 20% |
| More than 50% below the average | 25% |

Three consequences follow. An increase can only be applied at renewal, never mid-contract, and the landlord must serve notice of the proposed new rent ninety days before the renewal date. A tenant already at or near the market rate cannot lawfully be increased at all, which is why a building with rents already at market has less yield growth ahead of it than a building with long-standing below-market tenancies. And the index is the authority, not a broker's opinion or a portal average, which means the calculator output in the Dubai REST app is the number that would be upheld at the Rental Disputes Centre.

For an investor this reframes the purchase question. A tenanted unit with a rent 35% below the index average carries a lawful path to a 15% uplift at the next renewal. A tenanted unit already at market carries none. Both look identical in a listing. The full calculation method is in [the RERA rent increase calculator](/guides/dubai-rent-increase-calculator-rera/).

## What does the short-let route require?

Short-let in Dubai is legal, licensed and materially more work than a long lease. The permit is issued by the Department of Economy and Tourism, held per unit and renewed annually, and the owners association can prohibit the activity regardless of the permit. The net uplift is typically 1 to 2 percentage points of yield.

The permit is issued by the Department of Economy and Tourism, is held per unit, is renewed annually and requires the unit to be registered, inspected and compliant on safety equipment. The owners association can also restrict or prohibit short-let in a building irrespective of the permit, and an increasing number of towers do.

The economics were set out on [apartments for sale in Dubai](/uae/dubai/apartments-for-sale/) and the summary is that the gross uplift is large and the net uplift is one to two percentage points of yield after the operator share, furnishing, utilities, the permit and seasonality. It is a real premium earned by running a small hospitality operation rather than by owning an asset.

The decision belongs at the shortlist stage rather than after purchase, because it changes which buildings are eligible. Confirm the building's position with the owners association before committing, not with the selling agent.

## What does it cost to sell?

Exit costs in Dubai are lower than entry costs and the timeline is longer than most owners plan for. A seller pays agency commission of 2% plus VAT, clears service charges to the transfer date, and pays no capital gains tax. The realistic sale window is four to twelve weeks in the mid-market.

The seller pays agency commission of 2% plus VAT, the developer no-objection certificate where the developer charges it to the seller, and any outstanding service charges up to the transfer date. There is no capital gains tax in the UAE. Where the property carries a mortgage, the seller settles the loan and obtains a liability letter and discharge, which is the step that sets the pace.

The realistic sale timeline is four to twelve weeks in the mid-market and considerably longer at the top end, where a unit above roughly AED 30 million can take twelve to eighteen months to clear at a target price. A tenanted unit sells more slowly to owner-occupiers, because the tenant's contractual right to remain until the end of the term is not something a buyer can waive away.

Three items belong on the pre-listing checklist. Request the mortgage liability letter on day one rather than after an offer. Confirm the tenancy position and the notice already served, because a promise of vacant possession that the tenancy does not support is the most common reason a Dubai sale collapses. And where the property is the asset supporting a Golden Visa, arrange the replacement before selling, because the residency lapses with the qualifying asset. ## Personal name or company?

Most foreign buyers should hold Dubai property in their own name, and the exceptions are specific rather than general. Personal ownership is the only route that qualifies for the AED 2 million Golden Visa threshold, carries no entity maintenance cost, and transfers as a routine registry transaction rather than as a share sale.

Personal ownership is simpler, cheaper and the only route that qualifies for the Golden Visa under the real-estate category, because the threshold is measured against a title deed in the applicant's own name. It also carries the lowest ongoing cost, since there is no company to maintain.

A corporate holding structure has genuine uses. Multiple owners with differing shares are cleaner as shareholdings than as fractional title. A portfolio being assembled for eventual sale as a whole transfers more cheaply as shares than as a series of property transfers. And some estate-planning objectives are easier to meet at the corporate level.

The costs are real: an offshore or free zone company to hold Dubai property must be of a type the Land Department recognises, which narrows the options and raises the setup and annual maintenance cost. Not every free zone entity is accepted. Confirm the specific structure with the Land Department before committing to it, because a structure that cannot be registered on title is not a structure at all.

## What happens to a Dubai property on death?

Without a registered will, a non-Muslim owner's UAE estate can be distributed under principles that will not match home-country expectations, and the process is slow. Registered estates typically clear probate in 4 to 12 weeks through the DIFC Courts, against 12 to 36 months where no will has been registered at all.

The DIFC Wills Service Centre allows non-Muslim individuals to register a will under common law principles covering Dubai and other UAE assets, with probate through the DIFC Courts. Registered estates typically clear probate in four to twelve weeks against twelve to thirty-six months for unregistered ones. A property will covering only Dubai-registered property is the narrower and cheaper option; a full will covers wider UAE assets. Abu Dhabi has an equivalent registry through ADGM.

For an owner whose Dubai property is a meaningful part of the estate, registering a will is the highest-value administrative step available and it is frequently the last thing anyone does. The comparison of the two registries is in [UAE wills, DIFC and ADGM](/guides/uae-will-difc-adgm/).

## Where is the market in its cycle?

The steep appreciation phase of 2021 to 2023 has given way to a slower and more segmented market, and the segmentation is the point. Prime waterfront and villa stock in supply-constrained communities behaves differently from mid-market apartment stock in communities absorbing several thousand handovers inside a twelve-month window. Prime waterfront and villa stock in supply-constrained communities has behaved differently from mid-market apartment stock in communities with large handover pipelines, and a single city-wide direction describes a portfolio nobody owns.

Three structural features have not changed. Supply is concentrated in off-plan, which means the handover pipeline is visible years in advance and can be modelled rather than guessed. Demand is broad by nationality, which reduces dependence on any single source market. And the currency peg to the dollar removes the exchange-rate risk that dominates most emerging property markets, while importing US interest rate policy into UAE mortgage pricing.

What a buyer should take from the cycle is a holding period rather than a timing decision. The transaction cost stack of 6% to 7% on entry and roughly 2% on exit means a purchase needs several years of either yield or appreciation simply to break even. ## An insider tip on reading a Dubai deal

Insider tip: ask the seller's agent for the last two approved service charge budgets and the owners association reserve fund balance before discussing price at all. In Dubai the charge runs AED 12 to 25 per square foot and is the single largest deduction between the gross yield in the listing and the net yield the owner receives, typically 2 to 3 percentage points. A tower whose charge moved from AED 14 to AED 19 in two years has already taken most of a percentage point out of the return, and it will keep moving, because the association sets it rather than the owner. A building with an unfunded reserve and plant approaching its first replacement cycle carries a special levy that lands on whoever holds the unit when the work is scheduled. Neither figure appears in any listing, both are available on request, and together they separate two apparently identical units.

## Can a non-resident get a Dubai mortgage?

Non-residents can borrow in the UAE, and the terms are the main thing to model. Banks lend at 50% to 65% of value against a resident ceiling of 75% to 80% on a first property under AED 5 million. Non-resident rates sit above resident pricing, the arrangement fee is about 1% of the loan.

The consequence for a Golden Visa buyer is specific. Leverage reduces the equity registered in the owner's name, and the visa threshold is measured against registered value with the bank position taken into account, so a mortgaged purchase at the AED 2 million line is not automatically a qualifying purchase. Confirm the position with the issuing authority before treating leverage and residency as compatible in the same transaction.

## What tax applies to a Dubai property?

Dubai charges no annual property tax, no capital gains tax on a personal sale and no personal income tax on rent. The only transaction tax is the 4% Dubai Land Department transfer fee, paid once, and VAT at 5% applies to agency commission and some commercial property rather than to residential resale.

The tax that does apply is usually the buyer's own. A UK tax resident is taxed on worldwide income, so Dubai rent is reportable in the United Kingdom regardless of the UAE position. Indian residents face a similar reporting obligation with its own remittance framework. The country-by-country position is set out in [Dubai property taxes explained](/guides/dubai-property-taxes-explained/), for British buyers in [Dubai property for British buyers](/guides/dubai-property-for-british-buyers/) and for Indian buyers in [tax for Indian NRIs on Dubai property](/guides/tax-indian-nri-dubai-property/).
