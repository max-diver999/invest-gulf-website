Dubai is the deepest property market in the Gulf and the only one where a foreign buyer can complete a purchase end to end without ever holding a residence visa. That combination, open freehold plus a registered title system, is why roughly two thirds of Dubai transactions involve a foreign buyer and why the practical questions here are about cost and product rather than about permission.

## Where can foreigners buy property in Dubai?

Foreign nationals can buy freehold in more than 60 designated zones, and that list covers every community a non-resident buyer is likely to shortlist. Outside those zones the available interest is leasehold of up to 99 years, which is a different asset with a different resale profile.

The zone status sits on the title deed and in the Dubai Land Department record. It is checkable before any money moves, and checking it is the first item in the [step-by-step purchase process](/guides/how-to-buy-property-dubai-step-by-step/). The practical failure mode is not buying in the wrong zone, it is discovering at trustee stage that the unit is leasehold when the model assumed freehold resale liquidity.

| Ownership form | Where it applies | What the buyer gets | Resale profile |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Freehold | 60-plus designated zones | Title deed in own name, land and unit, unlimited term | Deepest buyer pool, standard mortgage market |
| Leasehold, up to 99 years | Selected non-designated areas | Right to occupy and sublet for the lease term | Narrower buyer pool, price discount widens as term shortens |
| Usufruct or musataha | Commercial and some mixed-use plots | Defined use right for a fixed period | Institutional buyers, not a retail resale market |

## What does a Dubai purchase actually cost?

Budget 6% to 7% above the purchase price on a cash resale, and closer to 8% with a mortgage. The cost stack is regulated and the rates are published, so a quote that differs materially from the table below deserves a question rather than a signature.

| Cost line | Rate | Who pays | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DLD transfer fee | 4% of price | Buyer by convention | Paid at the trustee office on completion |
| Oqood registration, off-plan | 4% of price | Buyer | Replaces the transfer fee, paid at booking |
| Agency commission | 2% plus 5% VAT | Buyer | RERA regulated, some launches charge the buyer nothing |
| Trustee office fee | AED 4,000 to 5,250 | Split, in practice the buyer | Capped by DLD regulation |
| Mortgage registration | 0.25% of the loan | Buyer | DLD charge, separate from the bank fee |
| Bank arrangement fee | About 1% of the loan | Buyer | Minimum around AED 10,000 |
| Developer NOC | AED 500 to 5,000 | Seller, usually passed on | Resale only |
| Title deed issuance | AED 250 to 580 | Buyer | Bundled with the trustee fee |

On a ready apartment at AED 1.5 million bought for cash, that is roughly AED 98,000 of closing costs, an effective premium of 6.5% over the price. The full itemisation with worked mortgage examples sits in the [cost of buying property in Dubai](/guides/cost-of-buying-property-dubai/) guide.

## Which Dubai communities produce which yields?

The yield spread across Dubai is wide enough that community choice, not timing, is the main variable a buyer controls. The pattern is consistent: mid-market apartment stock with modest service charges produces the highest gross yield, and prime waterfront produces the lowest.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Jumeirah Village Circle | 7.5% to 9.2% | 5.4% to 7.1% | Young professionals, couples | High |
| Dubai Silicon Oasis | 6.8% to 8.2% | 5.0% to 6.4% | Tech and academic sector | Medium |
| Discovery Gardens | 7.0% to 8.5% | 5.2% to 6.6% | Budget professionals | Medium |
| Business Bay, mid-range towers | 6.5% to 8.0% | 4.8% to 6.2% | Corporate tenants | High |
| Jumeirah Lakes Towers | 6.0% to 7.5% | 4.4% to 5.9% | Business community | High |
| Dubai Marina | 5.5% to 7.0% | 4.0% to 5.5% | Expat lifestyle, short-let suitable | Very high |
| Downtown Dubai | 4.8% to 6.2% | 3.5% to 4.8% | Corporate, high net worth | Very high |
| Palm Jumeirah | 4.2% to 5.8% | 3.0% to 4.3% | High net worth, premium short-let | High |
| Arabian Ranches villas | 4.0% to 5.5% | 3.0% to 4.2% | Families on long leases | Medium |

The gap between gross and net is the part most listings omit. Service charges alone run AED 12 to 25 per square foot per year and vary more by tower quality than by district, which is why two units in the same postcode can differ by a full percentage point of net yield. ## What is the entry price by product type?

There is no single Dubai entry price, but there are reliable floors by product. A studio in the mid-market apartment belt starts around AED 550,000. A one-bedroom in the same belt starts around AED 750,000. A townhouse in an outer master community starts around AED 1.8 million, and a villa in an established community such as Arabian Ranches or Dubai Hills starts materially higher.

Two thresholds change the shape of a purchase rather than just its size. The first is roughly AED 1 million, below which the product set narrows to studios and one-bedrooms in yield-led communities. The second is AED 2 million, the registered value that unlocks the 10-year Golden Visa. Buyers targeting the visa threshold face a specific trade-off, because the cheapest units that clear AED 2 million are not the same units that produce the best yield. That trade-off is worked through unit by unit in [the AED 2M Golden Visa value analysis](/guides/aed-2m-golden-visa-best-value-units/), and the sub-AED 1M end is covered in [a first Dubai investment under AED 1M](/guides/dubai-first-investment-under-aed-1m/).

## How does a foreign buyer complete a purchase remotely?

Most non-resident purchases complete without the buyer flying in. The sequence is a signed reservation, a Form F sale agreement, a developer no-objection certificate on resale, then registration at a DLD trustee office where the title deed is issued. A notarised and attested power of attorney lets a nominated representative sign at trustee stage.

The two steps that fail most often are the money and the counterparty. Funds must arrive from an account in the buyer's own name with documented source of funds, which is covered in [buying UAE property by bank transfer](/guides/buying-property-uae-bank-transfer/). The counterparty check is faster: every broker in Dubai carries a RERA broker registration number that can be verified in minutes, and the method is set out in [RERA broker verification](/guides/rera-broker-verification-dubai/). The DLD REST app verifies the unit itself, including whether the title is clear, and that process is described in [property due diligence with the REST app](/guides/dubai-rest-app-property-due-diligence/).

## What are the real risks in the Dubai market?

Three risks show up repeatedly in buyer files, and none of them is the market crash that dominates search interest.

The first is the service charge. It is the single largest recurring deduction from net yield, it is set by the owners association rather than by the buyer, and it rises. A building with a low headline price and a high charge per square foot can produce a worse net return than a more expensive building next door. Where a charge looks wrong, there is a formal escalation route, described in [service charge dispute escalation](/guides/dubai-service-charge-dispute-escalation/).

The second is off-plan handover slippage. Escrow law protects the money, not the calendar. A twelve-month delay on a leveraged purchase changes the return profile materially, and the compensation route is narrower than most buyers expect. The mechanics are in [developer delay compensation](/guides/dubai-developer-delay-compensation-claim/).

The third is liquidity at the top end. Below about AED 5 million the resale market is deep and comparables are easy. Above roughly AED 30 million, a sale can take twelve to eighteen months at a target price. That is not a defect, it is the normal behaviour of a thin trophy market, but it belongs in the model before purchase rather than after.

## Can a non-resident get a Dubai mortgage?

Yes, and the terms are the main thing to model. UAE banks lend to non-residents at a lower loan-to-value than to residents, typically 50% to 65% against a resident ceiling of 75% to 80% for a first property under the central bank rules. Rates for non-residents sit above resident pricing, the arrangement fee is about 1% of the loan and a bank-approved valuation is mandatory.

The consequence for a Golden Visa buyer is specific. Leverage reduces the equity registered in the owner's name, and the visa threshold is measured against registered value with the bank position taken into account, so a mortgaged purchase at the AED 2 million line is not automatically a qualifying purchase. Confirm the position with the issuing authority before treating leverage and residency as compatible in the same transaction.

Current pricing and the lender list sit in [Dubai mortgage rates](/guides/dubai-mortgage-rates-2026/) and [the UAE mortgage bank list](/guides/uae-mortgage-banks-list/). The regulatory ceilings themselves are in [UAE central bank mortgage rules](/guides/uae-central-bank-mortgage-rules/).

## What tax applies to a Dubai property?

There is no annual property tax in Dubai, no capital gains tax on a personal sale and no personal income tax on rent. The transaction tax is the 4% DLD transfer fee, paid once. VAT at 5% applies to agency commission and to some commercial property, not to residential resale.

The tax that does apply is usually the buyer's own. A UK tax resident is taxed on worldwide income, so Dubai rent is reportable in the United Kingdom regardless of the UAE position. Indian residents face a similar reporting obligation with its own remittance framework. The country-by-country position is set out in [Dubai property taxes explained](/guides/dubai-property-taxes-explained/), for British buyers in [Dubai property for British buyers](/guides/dubai-property-for-british-buyers/) and for Indian buyers in [tax for Indian NRIs on Dubai property](/guides/tax-indian-nri-dubai-property/).
