Abu Dhabi is the cheaper half of the UAE property market and the more restricted one. Prices sit roughly 30% below Dubai for equivalent product, the transaction cost is half, and the Golden Visa threshold is identical, which means the same AED 2 million buys considerably more here. The constraint is that foreign freehold exists in nine zones rather than sixty, so the shortlist is short before it is even a shortlist.

## Which Abu Dhabi zones are open to foreign buyers?

Nine, and the list is the first filter on any Abu Dhabi search. A property outside these zones cannot be held freehold by a foreign national, regardless of price, developer or how the listing is worded.

| Investment zone | Product | Price per sq ft | Gross yield | Character |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Saadiyat Island | Apartments and villas | AED 1,800 to 3,500 | 4% to 5.5% | Culture district, museums, NYU |
| Yas Island | Apartments and villas | AED 1,200 to 2,200 | 5.5% to 7% | Entertainment, schools, family demand |
| Al Reem Island | Apartments | AED 900 to 1,400 | 7% to 8.5% | Dense urban, closest to the CBD |
| Al Raha Beach | Apartments and villas | AED 1,100 to 1,900 | 5.5% to 7.5% | Waterfront, airport corridor |
| Al Maryah Island | Apartments | AED 1,600 to 2,600 | 5% to 6.5% | Financial district, ADGM |
| Masdar City | Apartments | AED 1,000 to 1,500 | 6% to 7.5% | Sustainability cluster, institutional tenants |
| Al Reef | Apartments and villas | AED 700 to 1,000 | 9% to 9.5% | Lowest entry, highest yield, slowest exit |
| Khalifa City, selected projects | Villas and apartments | AED 800 to 1,200 | 7% to 8% | Established, airport proximity |
| Hudayriyat Island | Villas | Under development | Not yet established | Newest zone, leisure-led |

Al Ghadeer sits on the Dubai border and functions as a tenth entry point in practice, with prices of AED 600 to 900 per square foot and yields of 8% to 8.5%. ## What does an Abu Dhabi purchase cost?

Materially less than Dubai, and the gap is in the transfer fee rather than in the professional fees.

| Cost line | Abu Dhabi | Dubai |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Transfer fee | 2% to DMT | 4% to DLD |
| Registration admin | AED 1,000 to 4,000 | Trustee fee AED 4,000 to 5,250 |
| Agency commission | 2% plus VAT | 2% plus VAT |
| Mortgage registration | 0.25% of loan | 0.25% of loan |
| Typical cash all-in | 3% to 4% | 6% to 7% |

On an AED 2 million purchase the saving is roughly AED 60,000, which is close to a full year of net rent on a mid-market unit. For a buyer weighing the two emirates on total cost of entry rather than on headline price alone, this is the line that decides it.

The registration authority is the Department of Municipalities and Transport rather than the Dubai Land Department, and the off-plan contract register is Tawtheeq rather than Oqood. The instruments do the same work under different names.

## Which Abu Dhabi communities produce which yields?

The spread here is wider than Dubai's and the relationship between yield and exit speed is more pronounced.

| Community | Gross yield | Estimated net | Price per sq ft | Days on market | Negotiation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Al Reef | 9% to 9.5% | 6.5% to 7.5% | AED 700 to 1,000 | 90 to 150 | 5% to 12% |
| Al Ghadeer | 8% to 8.5% | 6% to 7% | AED 600 to 900 | 75 to 120 | 3% to 8% |
| Al Reem Island | 7% to 8.5% | 5.5% to 6.5% | AED 900 to 1,400 | 60 to 90 | 2% to 8% |
| Yas Island | 5.5% to 7% | 4.5% to 5.5% | AED 1,500 to 2,200 | 45 to 75 | 0% to 5% |
| Saadiyat Island | 4% to 5.5% | 3.5% to 4.5% | AED 2,000 to 4,000 | 30 to 60 | 0% to 3% |

Read the last two columns together with the first. Al Reef pays nearly double the gross yield of Saadiyat and takes three times as long to sell at a price the seller accepts. That is not an inefficiency to arbitrage, it is the market pricing liquidity, and a buyer who needs a defined exit date should read the table from the bottom rather than the top. ## How does Abu Dhabi differ from Dubai as a market?

Four differences change the shape of a purchase.

The tenant base is employment-anchored rather than tourism-anchored. Abu Dhabi demand comes from government, energy, finance in the ADGM cluster and the education sector, which produces longer tenancies and lower seasonality than Dubai but a smaller pool of short-let demand. Where a Dubai model leans on short-let upside, the Abu Dhabi equivalent does not.

The developer landscape is concentrated. Aldar dominates supply across Yas, Saadiyat and Al Ghadeer with a delivery record around 92%, and around 88% of its sales go to non-UAE nationals. Concentration cuts both ways: counterparty risk is lower than with a small developer, and price discovery is thinner because a single seller sets the launch benchmark.

The secondary market is thinner. Dubai registers more than 205,000 transactions a year, which produces comparables for almost any unit. Abu Dhabi comparables are sparser, which widens the valuation range on a mortgaged purchase and lengthens the sale process.

The zone restriction is absolute. In Dubai the question is which of sixty freehold areas suits the budget. Here the question is whether the suitable product exists inside nine zones at all, and for some buyer profiles the honest answer is that it does not.

## Who is Abu Dhabi right for?

The yield-first buyer with patience. Al Reef and Al Ghadeer produce the highest net yields available anywhere in the UAE at the lowest entry prices, and the cost of that is a slow exit. A buyer holding for income over seven to ten years is being paid properly for accepting that.

The Golden Visa buyer optimising for space. The AED 2 million threshold is federal, so the same qualifying purchase buys roughly 30% more area here. On Al Reem Island that is the difference between a one-bedroom and a two-bedroom at the threshold, and the yield is higher too. The threshold mechanics are in [the UAE Golden Visa property route](/guides/uae-golden-visa-property/).

The end-user working in Abu Dhabi. Commuting from Dubai is possible and widely done, and it is an hour and a half each way in bad traffic. For anyone employed in the capital, buying in the capital removes a cost that never appears in a yield model.

It is less suited to the buyer who needs liquidity, the buyer whose model depends on short-let income, and the buyer who wants a wide freehold choice. Those buyers are better served in Dubai, and the comparison is set out on the [Dubai property](/uae/dubai/) hub.

## What does the money side look like?

Two federal rules and one emirate rule govern the financing of an Abu Dhabi purchase.

The federal rules are the central bank loan-to-value ceilings, which apply across the UAE: up to 80% for a UAE national and 75% for an expatriate resident on a first property under AED 5 million, stepping down above that value and for second properties. Non-residents borrow on narrower terms, typically 50% to 65%, and at pricing above resident rates. Those ceilings are set out in [UAE central bank mortgage rules](/guides/uae-central-bank-mortgage-rules/).

The emirate rule is the 2% transfer fee and a registration administration charge of AED 1,000 to 4,000, both paid to the Department of Municipalities and Transport. Mortgage registration is 0.25% of the loan, the same as Dubai.

The line that catches non-resident buyers is valuation. With fewer comparable transactions than Dubai, an Abu Dhabi valuation can land below the agreed price more often, and the buyer funds the difference in cash. On a leveraged purchase, get an indicative valuation before committing to a price rather than after. The lender landscape is in [the UAE mortgage bank list](/guides/uae-mortgage-banks-list/) and current pricing in [Dubai mortgage rates](/guides/dubai-mortgage-rates-2026/), which covers the same national lenders.

## What are the risks specific to Abu Dhabi?

Three, and none of them is price.

Liquidity is the first. A unit in Al Reef can take five months to sell at a price the seller accepts, and the negotiation band at that point runs to 12%. A model that assumes an exit within a year is not compatible with the highest-yielding communities in this emirate.

Supply concentration is the second. Where one developer sets the launch price across several communities, a large phase released into a soft market resets the resale benchmark for every private seller in that community at once. That is a structural feature of a concentrated market rather than a criticism of any developer, and it argues for buying completed stock in mature phases where the exposure is smaller.

Zone drift is the third and least discussed. Investment zone boundaries and the list of qualifying projects inside Khalifa City have been adjusted over time. A listing describing a property as freehold is not evidence that it is, and the check belongs in the Department of Municipalities and Transport record before a deposit rather than in the marketing.

## Where should an Abu Dhabi buyer start?

Start with the zone list, because it removes most of the market before any other filter applies. Then choose between the yield end, Al Reef and Al Ghadeer, and the liquidity end, Yas and Saadiyat, based on the intended holding period rather than on the yield number.
