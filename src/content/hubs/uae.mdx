The UAE is seven property markets under one federal visa rule. The rule is the same everywhere: AED 2 million of registered value buys a ten-year residency, no income tax applies, and foreign ownership is permitted in designated areas. Everything else differs by emirate, starting with how many designated areas there are, which ranges from more than sixty in Dubai to none at all in Sharjah.

## Dubai or Abu Dhabi?

This is the real decision for most foreign buyers, because the northern emirates serve a different budget and a different risk appetite.

Dubai wins on choice and liquidity. More than sixty freehold zones, product from AED 480,000 studios to eight-figure Palm villas, and more than 205,000 registered transactions a year producing comparables for almost any unit. That depth supports valuation on a mortgage, supports a fast exit and supports short-let as a genuine strategy rather than a marginal one.

Abu Dhabi wins on cost. Equivalent product prices roughly 30% below Dubai per square foot, the transfer fee is half, and the all-in closing stack is 3% to 4% against 6% to 7%. Yields at the value end, Al Reef and Al Ghadeer, exceed anything Dubai offers. The trade is a nine-zone universe and a resale market where a high-yield unit can take five months to sell.

The decision rule that holds up: if the buyer may need to exit on a timetable, or wants a wide community choice, Dubai's premium is worth paying. If the buyer will hold for income across a long period and the required product exists inside the nine zones, Abu Dhabi is the better arithmetic. The two markets are covered in detail on [property for sale in Dubai](/uae/dubai/) and [property for sale in Abu Dhabi](/uae/abu-dhabi/).

## What do the northern emirates offer?

The lowest entry prices in the country and the thinnest markets, in that order.

Ras Al Khaimah is the most developed of the four, with freehold communities on Al Marjan Island, Al Hamra Village and Mina Al Arab, and a leisure-led investment thesis anchored on the integrated resort under construction on Al Marjan. Prices run AED 700 to 1,600 per square foot with yields of 6% to 9%, and the buyer pool has broadened since the resort was announced. The emirate is covered in [the Ras Al Khaimah property investment guide](/guides/ras-al-khaimah-property-investment-guide/).

Ajman offers the lowest apartment entry prices in the UAE, with freehold available in selected communities and yields in the 7% to 9% band. The constraint is exit: the buyer pool is largely UAE residents rather than international, and days-on-market figures are longer than anywhere in Dubai.

Sharjah does not offer freehold to foreign nationals. What is available is a usufruct interest of up to 100 years in designated developments, which is a real and registrable right but a different asset with a narrower resale market. Buyers attracted by Sharjah's rents, driven by Dubai commuters seeking lower housing costs, should understand that they are buying a long lease rather than a title.

Umm Al Quwain and Fujairah have small freehold offerings and markets too thin to support reliable yield figures. They suit a buyer with a specific reason to be there rather than a portfolio allocation.

## What is the same in every emirate?

Four things, and they are the reason the UAE is treated as one market despite being seven.

The Golden Visa threshold is federal. AED 2 million of registered value qualifies for the ten-year residency wherever the property sits, measured on the title rather than on the agreed price. The practical consequence is that the same qualifying purchase buys very different amounts of property depending on the emirate, and the mechanics are in [the UAE Golden Visa property route](/guides/uae-golden-visa-property/).

The tax position is federal. No annual property tax, no capital gains tax on a personal sale, no personal income tax on rent. The corporate tax regime introduced in 2023 does not reach personal residential rental income.

The mortgage ceilings are federal. Central bank rules set loan-to-value limits at up to 80% for nationals and 75% for expatriate residents on a first property under AED 5 million, with non-residents borrowing on narrower terms of roughly 50% to 65%. Those ceilings are in [UAE central bank mortgage rules](/guides/uae-central-bank-mortgage-rules/), and the lender list in [the UAE mortgage bank list](/guides/uae-mortgage-banks-list/).

The buying mechanics rhyme. Each emirate has a land department, a registered title, an escrow requirement on off-plan and a contract register. The names differ, the sequence does not.

## What differs enough to change a decision?

The zone rule differs most. Sixty-plus zones in Dubai against nine in Abu Dhabi against none in Sharjah is not a detail, it is the first filter and it removes most of the country for most buyers.

The transfer fee differs by a factor of two between Dubai and Abu Dhabi, which on a AED 2 million purchase is roughly AED 40,000.

Liquidity differs by an order of magnitude. Dubai's transaction volume is what makes a fast exit possible and a mortgage valuation predictable. Nothing else in the UAE approaches it.

The tenant base differs. Dubai demand carries a large tourism and short-let component. Abu Dhabi demand is employment-anchored, which produces longer tenancies and lower seasonality. The northern emirates are driven by domestic affordability and Dubai commuting.

## What does a UAE purchase cost, emirate by emirate?

The professional fees are broadly the same across the country. The transfer fee is not, and it is the line that decides the total.

| Cost line | Dubai | Abu Dhabi | Northern emirates |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Transfer or registration fee | 4% | 2% | 2% to 4% |
| Registration administration | AED 4,000 to 5,250 | AED 1,000 to 4,000 | AED 1,000 to 4,000 |
| Agency commission | 2% plus VAT | 2% plus VAT | 2% plus VAT |
| Mortgage registration | 0.25% of loan | 0.25% of loan | 0.25% of loan |
| Bank arrangement fee | About 1% of loan | About 1% of loan | About 1% of loan |
| Typical cash all-in | 6% to 7% | 3% to 4% | 4% to 6% |

On a AED 2 million purchase the difference between the Dubai and Abu Dhabi stacks is close to AED 60,000, which is most of a year of net rent on a mid-market unit. For a buyer making a single purchase that is a meaningful saving. For a buyer building a portfolio it compounds into an extra unit over time.

What the table does not capture is the cost of the exit. A Dubai unit that sells in six weeks and an Abu Dhabi unit that sells in five months carry different holding costs during the sale, and on a leveraged position that difference can exceed the transfer fee saving entirely.

## Which emirate suits which buyer?

The income buyer with a long horizon. Abu Dhabi's value communities and Ajman produce the highest net yields in the country, and both trade liquidity for that. A ten-year holder is compensated properly; a three-year holder is not.

The income buyer who wants an exit option. Dubai's mid-market belt yields 6.8% to 9.2% with the deepest resale market in the region. The yield is a little below Abu Dhabi's best and the optionality is far greater.

The residency buyer. Every emirate qualifies at AED 2 million, so the question is only what that figure buys. In prime Dubai it is a studio or a small one-bedroom. On Al Reem Island it is a two-bedroom with a higher yield. In Ajman it is a townhouse.

The end-user. The commute decides it. Anyone employed in Abu Dhabi should buy in Abu Dhabi, and the households that commute from Dubai consistently underestimate what an hour and a half each way costs them over several years.

The capital preservation buyer. Prime Dubai and Saadiyat are the two markets with the depth and the international recognition to support that objective. The northern emirates are not, whatever the yield says.

## Where should a UAE buyer start?

Start with the constraint that cannot move. If residency is the objective, AED 2 million is the floor and the question becomes which emirate gives the most property at that figure. If income is the objective, the yield tables narrow it to Abu Dhabi's value communities or Dubai's mid-market belt, and the holding period decides between them. If the purchase is a home, the school run and the commute decide it and the yield discussion is secondary.
