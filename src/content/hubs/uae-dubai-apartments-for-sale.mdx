Apartments are the volume product in Dubai and the one where the price spread is widest. The same two-bedroom footprint can cost AED 950,000 in Dubai Silicon Oasis or AED 4.2 million in Downtown, and the yield relationship runs in the opposite direction to the price. This page sets out what each price band buys and what it earns.

## What does a Dubai apartment cost by community?

Price per square foot is the only comparison that survives a move between communities, because unit sizes vary more than prices do. A 1,000 square foot two-bedroom in JVC and a 700 square foot two-bedroom in Downtown are not comparable on headline price and are directly comparable per square foot.

| Community | Price per sq ft | Studio from | One-bedroom from | Position |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Discovery Gardens | AED 850 to 1,000 | AED 480,000 | AED 650,000 | Lowest entry, older stock |
| Jumeirah Village Circle | AED 950 to 1,250 | AED 550,000 | AED 750,000 | Deepest mid-market supply |
| Dubai Silicon Oasis | AED 900 to 1,150 | AED 520,000 | AED 720,000 | Tech corridor, steady tenancy |
| Dubai Sports City | AED 900 to 1,200 | AED 530,000 | AED 730,000 | Yield belt, thinner resale |
| Dubai South | AED 950 to 1,250 | AED 560,000 | AED 780,000 | Airport and Expo corridor |
| Business Bay | AED 1,500 to 2,100 | AED 850,000 | AED 1,150,000 | Corporate tenants, canal frontage |
| Jumeirah Lakes Towers | AED 1,300 to 1,800 | AED 780,000 | AED 1,050,000 | Metro-served business district |
| Dubai Marina | AED 1,800 to 2,600 | AED 950,000 | AED 1,400,000 | Highest liquidity, short-let suitable |
| Downtown Dubai | AED 2,200 to 3,200 | AED 1,300,000 | AED 1,750,000 | Prime, capital preservation |
| Palm Jumeirah | AED 2,800 to 5,000 | Not typical | AED 2,400,000 | Trophy, fixed supply |

Bands are working ranges for planning rather than quotations. Individual units move outside them on floor, view, finish and service charge history.

## Which apartments produce the best net yield?

Gross yield is the number in listings and net yield is the number that reaches the owner. The gap is not a rounding error, it is 2 to 3 percentage points, and most of it is one line: the service charge.

| Community | Gross yield | Estimated net | Service charge per sq ft |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Jumeirah Village Circle | 7.5% to 9.2% | 5.4% to 7.1% | AED 12 to 16 |
| Discovery Gardens | 7.0% to 8.5% | 5.2% to 6.6% | AED 11 to 14 |
| Dubai Silicon Oasis | 6.8% to 8.2% | 5.0% to 6.4% | AED 12 to 15 |
| Business Bay | 6.5% to 8.0% | 4.8% to 6.2% | AED 15 to 22 |
| Jumeirah Lakes Towers | 6.0% to 7.5% | 4.4% to 5.9% | AED 15 to 20 |
| Dubai Marina | 5.5% to 7.0% | 4.0% to 5.5% | AED 16 to 24 |
| Downtown Dubai | 4.8% to 6.2% | 3.5% to 4.8% | AED 20 to 28 |

The operative point is that the charge scales with amenity load rather than with rent. A tower with a full leisure deck, valet parking and a concierge carries a charge that a walk-up in the same community does not, and the rent premium rarely covers the difference. The method for reading a service charge budget before purchase is in [service charges by area](/guides/service-charges-dubai-by-area/), and the wider yield mechanics including vacancy and management assumptions are in [the Dubai rental yield guide](/guides/dubai-rental-yield-guide/).

## What does the purchase cost on top of the price?

On a cash resale, budget 6% to 7% above the price. On a one-bedroom at AED 1.1 million that is roughly AED 72,000: a 4% DLD transfer fee of AED 44,000, agency commission of AED 22,000 plus VAT of AED 1,100, a trustee fee of about AED 4,200 and a developer no-objection certificate. With a mortgage, add 0.25% mortgage registration, about 1% bank arrangement and a valuation of AED 2,500 to 3,500.

Two costs surprise buyers who model only the transfer fee. The first is the no-objection certificate, which the developer issues on resale at AED 500 to 5,000 and which the seller conventionally pays and conventionally passes on. The second is the DEWA connection and deposit between tenancies, AED 2,000 to 4,000, charged to the landlord rather than the tenant. The full itemisation with mortgage worked examples is in [the cost of buying property in Dubai](/guides/cost-of-buying-property-dubai/).

## Studio, one-bedroom or two-bedroom?

The unit mix decision is a yield decision, not a lifestyle one, unless the buyer intends to occupy.

Studios produce the highest gross yield per dirham invested and the highest tenant turnover. In JVC and Discovery Gardens they let quickly and re-let quickly, which is an advantage on yield and a cost on management time and re-letting fees. They also have the narrowest resale buyer pool, because owner-occupiers rarely buy studios.

One-bedrooms are the liquidity sweet spot. They let to the largest tenant segment in Dubai, resell to both investors and end-users, and carry the smallest gap between gross and net yield because the service charge per square foot is spread across a slightly larger area with the same fixed-cost amenities.

Two-bedrooms trade yield for tenant stability. Families sign longer leases and move less, which cuts vacancy and re-letting cost, but the rent per square foot is lower than on a studio in the same building. In prime areas the two-bedroom is the volume resale product and therefore the most liquid.

For a buyer under AED 1 million, the practical shortlist is a studio or a one-bedroom in the yield belt, and the community choice matters more than the unit type. That comparison is worked in [a first Dubai investment under AED 1M](/guides/dubai-first-investment-under-aed-1m/), and the community-level view across the whole city is in [best areas to buy property in Dubai](/guides/best-areas-buy-property-dubai/).

## What should a buyer check before signing?

Check four things, in this order, and none of them is the price.

Check the service charge history, not the current rate. A building that raised the charge 18% in two years will raise it again, and that trajectory is visible in the approved budgets.

Check whether the tower is on the short-let permitted list if short-let income is part of the model. Not every building allows it, and the owners association can withdraw permission.

Check the title in the Dubai Land Department REST app rather than relying on a listing. It shows the registered owner, whether a mortgage is registered against the unit and whether the title is clear, and the process takes minutes. It is described in [property due diligence with the REST app](/guides/dubai-rest-app-property-due-diligence/).

Check the broker registration number. Every practising broker in Dubai holds one and it is verifiable on demand, as set out in [RERA broker verification](/guides/rera-broker-verification-dubai/).

## Short-let or long-let on a Dubai apartment?

Short-let raises gross revenue and raises cost by more than most models allow, so the honest comparison is net to net rather than headline to headline.

A one-bedroom in Dubai Marina that lets long-term at AED 95,000 a year can gross AED 140,000 to 160,000 on a well-run short-let operation. Against that, the operator takes 15% to 20% of gross, the unit needs furnishing at AED 45,000 to 70,000 and replacement on a three to four year cycle, utilities and internet move from the tenant to the owner, and the Department of Economy and Tourism holiday home permit carries an annual fee per unit. Occupancy is seasonal: the same unit that runs at 85% from October to April can sit at 45% to 55% in July and August.

Netted out, the short-let premium on a mid-market Dubai apartment is usually 1 to 2 percentage points of yield rather than the 4 to 5 points the gross figures imply. It is a real premium and it is worth having, but it is earned by operating a small hospitality business rather than by owning an asset. Buildings that permit short-let also tend to carry higher service charges, because the amenity load that makes them attractive to short-stay guests is the load the owners association is paying for.

The decision rule that holds up: a buyer who will not be in the country and does not want an operator relationship should model long-let and treat any short-let upside as optional. A buyer who wants the unit available for personal use several weeks a year should model short-let, because long-let makes personal use impossible.

## Off-plan or ready apartment?

An off-plan apartment is cheaper at entry and more expensive in certainty. The payment plan spreads the outlay across construction, Oqood registration at 4% replaces the transfer fee at booking, and launch pricing typically sits below comparable completed stock in the same community. Nothing is earned during the plan period, so the whole construction window is an outflow against an estimated handover date.

A ready apartment costs more at entry and answers more questions. The service charge is a documented number rather than a developer projection. The rent is verifiable against Ejari records rather than a rental guarantee in a brochure. The building's actual finish quality, lift performance and parking allocation are inspectable. On a yield-first purchase these are the variables that decide the return, and none of them can be inspected before handover on an off-plan unit.

The case for off-plan strengthens where the buyer has a long horizon, no income requirement during construction and a preference for staged payments over a single large outlay. It weakens sharply where leverage is involved, because a delayed handover extends the period of paying finance costs against no rent. The compensation route where a developer misses its committed date is narrower than most buyers assume and is set out in [developer delay compensation](/guides/dubai-developer-delay-compensation-claim/). The wider off-plan framework, including escrow, Oqood and developer checks, is on the [Dubai off-plan](/uae/dubai/off-plan/) hub.

If the objective is residency, the AED 2 million registered value is the constraint and the shortlist narrows to larger units in mid-tier communities or smaller units in prime.
