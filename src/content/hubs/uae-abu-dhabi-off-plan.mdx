## What do post-handover plans really cost?

A post-handover plan defers 30% to 40% of the price over one to three years after keys, which is genuinely useful and routinely mismodelled. The pitch is that rental income services the deferred instalments, and the arithmetic that decides it is net rather than gross.

On an AED 1.2 million two-bedroom with 35% deferred over three years, the instalment is AED 11,700 a month against net rent of roughly AED 4,900. The deferred payment is two and a half times the net rent, and the gap is funded from the buyer's other resources for the full three years.

The pitch is that rental income services the deferred instalments. The arithmetic that matters is net rather than gross. Take a two-bedroom on Al Reem at AED 1.2 million with 35% deferred over three years: that is AED 420,000, or AED 11,700 a month. Gross rent on the same unit is around AED 7,500 a month. Net rent after a service charge at AED 14 per square foot, management at 6% and a vacancy allowance at 7% is closer to AED 4,900 a month.

The deferred instalment is therefore roughly two and a half times the net rent, and the gap is funded from the buyer's other resources for three years. That does not make the plan a bad instrument. It makes the phrase about rent servicing the balance a marketing claim rather than a financial one. Our project check runs to four items, each with a clear pass and fail signal, and three of them are answerable in a single afternoon. The fourth, pricing against comparable stock, is the one that catches a developer funding construction from sales velocity rather than from balance sheet.

## What the concentrated market means for a buyer

Aldar's position across Yas, Saadiyat and Al Ghadeer produces two effects that pull in opposite directions, and a buyer needs to hold both at once. Concentration lowers counterparty risk, with around 92% of committed schedules delivered, and it lowers price discovery at the same time.

The protective effect is real. A master developer with a long completion record, an approved escrow structure and a balance sheet independent of any single project is the lowest-risk counterparty available in off-plan anywhere in the UAE. Buyers who would not consider an unknown developer in Dubai can reasonably consider a master-planned Abu Dhabi launch.

The pricing effect works against the buyer at exit. Where one seller sets the launch benchmark and controls the phasing, a private reseller at handover competes with new stock priced by the same organisation that set the price they paid. The developer can offer a payment plan; the reseller cannot. The developer can absorb commission; the reseller pays it.

The practical response is to buy in mature phases of a community rather than in the first phase of a new one, and to treat any pre-handover exit plan as optional rather than as the strategy. Completed stock in a finished phase removes the competition problem entirely, which is one reason the ready market on Al Reem and Al Reef suits income buyers better than a launch does.

## How should the sale and purchase agreement be read here?

The sale and purchase agreement should be read clause by clause, by someone acting for the buyer. Six provisions carry almost all the risk, the same six as in Dubai with one local variation, and the first two are the completion date with its 12-month grace period and the remedy on delay.

The completion date and the permitted extension sit in different clauses and they add. A stated date with a twelve-month grace period is a commitment to the later date, and only that one belongs in the model.

The remedy on delay. In most agreements it amounts to a right to terminate and recover payments after a further defined period, without compensation for the time the capital was held. That is narrower than most buyers assume.

The specification annex. Anything described as indicative or subject to change will change. What is committed is what is itemised, and renders are not part of the contract.

The area variance clause, which permits the delivered area to differ from the contracted area by a stated percentage without adjustment. On a 950 square foot apartment a 5% shrink is 47 square feet, real money at AED 1,200 per square foot.

The assignment provision, which sets the threshold for a pre-handover resale, the fee for the no-objection certificate and whether the developer can refuse. Without reading it, a pre-handover exit plan is an assumption.

The Abu Dhabi variation is registration. Confirm that the agreement commits the developer to register the contract through Tawtheeq and names the Department of Municipalities and Transport approved escrow account. Both are standard on a compliant project and their absence is the clearest possible signal to stop.

## What does the escrow structure protect?

Escrow protects the application of the money rather than the schedule, and that distinction separates an informed buyer from an optimistic one. Payments on a registered project sit in a project-specific account at an approved bank, drawn against verified construction progress rather than at the developer's discretion.

Buyer payments on a registered project go into a project-specific account at an approved bank. The developer draws against verified construction progress rather than at will, which means funds cannot be diverted to another project, to working capital or to shareholders. In a market where the historical failure mode was exactly that diversion, this is a strong protection.

What it does not protect is the handover date, the finish quality or the price at completion. A project can be fully compliant and still deliver two years late into a market where the unit is worth less than the buyer paid.

The operative buyer rule is simple and absolute. Every payment goes to the named escrow account, referencing the unit, and never to a personal account, a corporate account or an agent. Each milestone gets its own transfer with the unit reference. A request to pay anywhere else is not an administrative shortcut, it is the failure mode the whole framework exists to prevent.

## How does assignment work in a thin market?

Assignment mechanics here are the same as Dubai and the outcome is different, which is the part that matters. Most developers permit a transfer once 30% to 40% of the price has been paid, subject to a no-objection certificate and a fee, but the resale pool for an unbuilt unit is far narrower than in Dubai.

Most developers permit assignment once 30% to 40% of the price has been paid, subject to a no-objection certificate and an administrative fee that is sometimes a percentage of price rather than a flat sum. Below the threshold the position is not transferable.

Three conditions have to hold for the exit to exist in practice. The developer has to issue the certificate, and a developer with unsold inventory in the same project has no incentive to move quickly. There has to be a buyer for an unbuilt unit, and that pool is narrower here than in Dubai at every price point. And the price has to work, which is where most assignment plans fail: the seller is asking a buyer to pay a premium over the original price for a position the master developer may still be selling directly, at the original price, with a payment plan the private seller cannot match and commission the private seller has to pay.

The honest framing is that assignment in Abu Dhabi is a genuine option in a rising market and close to unavailable in a flat one. A purchase whose thesis depends on it is a purchase with an untested exit.

## Can an Abu Dhabi off-plan purchase be financed?

An Abu Dhabi off-plan purchase can be financed partly, and later than most buyers expect. Banks generally do not lend against an unbuilt unit on completed-property terms, so the mainstream route is that the buyer funds the plan and arranges a mortgage at or near handover against a valued asset.

Banks generally do not lend against an off-plan unit during construction on the same terms as a completed property. Some offer construction-linked facilities on projects from developers they have approved, at lower loan-to-value and higher pricing. The mainstream route is that the buyer funds the plan from their own resources and arranges a mortgage at or near handover against a completed asset.

That creates a specific risk in this emirate. The mortgage is approved at handover against a valuation made at handover, and Abu Dhabi valuations are already conservative because the comparable set is thin. A buyer who committed at launch and finds the unit valued below the contract price at completion funds the difference in cash on top of the balance.

The conservative structure is to treat the off-plan purchase as a cash commitment and any mortgage at handover as an improvement rather than a requirement. ## What does a worked Abu Dhabi off-plan purchase cost?

This worked example takes a two-bedroom on Yas Island launched at AED 1.6 million on a 60/40 plan, with handover committed for Q3 2028 and a twelve-month grace period, held to completion and then let. The purpose is to price the delay rather than only the plan.

## Where should an off-plan buyer go next?

One test resolves most of these decisions before any project comparison begins.
