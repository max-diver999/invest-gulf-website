What it does not yet offer is Dubai-scale liquidity, established rental indices, or a long foreign-buyer transaction history. ## Where the June 2026 map leaves a foreign buyer

The Executive Regulation of the Law of Real Estate Ownership by Non-Saudis was approved on 23 June 2026 along with the map of designated areas, so the question a buyer faces has changed shape. It is no longer whether foreigners may own property in Saudi Arabia, but whether the specific address sits inside the approved geography.

Three categories now matter. Inside the designated areas, non-Saudis may buy on freehold terms, subject to project-level registration. Outside them, a foreign resident may still hold a single residential unit for personal use, which is a living arrangement rather than a portfolio position. Makkah and Madinah stay restricted to Muslim buyers regardless of residency. Riyadh has nine designated zones and Jeddah has 57, so the two largest markets are open in parts rather than closed or open outright.

Cost has firmed up alongside eligibility. Transfer charges on non-Saudi disposals are capped at 5%, and a realistic all-in acquisition model lands near 10% once registration, brokerage and legal review are included. That is roughly double the Dubai load, and it is the number first-time Saudi buyers most often underestimate when comparing the two markets on headline price alone.
