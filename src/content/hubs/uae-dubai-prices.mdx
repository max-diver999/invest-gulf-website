## Which source should a buyer trust?

Three price sources exist in this market and they measure different things, which is why quoting them interchangeably produces contradictory conclusions about the same 12 months. Our method takes direction from the official index, level from registered comparables, and treats an asking price as an opening position rather than a valuation.

- Dubai Land Department index: prices actually paid on registered transactions, verified at a trustee office
- Portal listings: what sellers hope to receive, above transacted prices in a flat market
- Agency reports: variable quality, and the reliable ones state whether the sample is transactions or listings

The Dubai Land Department index is built from registered transactions. It records what was paid, after negotiation, and it is the only source with that property. It lags real time by the registration cycle, which is a feature rather than a defect for anyone making a purchase decision.

Portal asking prices record what sellers hope to receive. In a rising market they trail transacted prices and look conservative. In a flat or falling market they sit above transacted prices and look optimistic, because sellers adjust expectations more slowly than the market adjusts. The gap between asking and transacted is itself a useful signal about market direction.

Agency market reports sit between the two and vary in quality. The reliable ones state their sample and whether it is transactions or listings. The unreliable ones report a percentage change without either.

The practical method is to take the direction from the DLD index, the level from registered comparables for the specific building or cluster, and to treat the asking price as an opening position rather than a valuation. ## What moves the price of an individual unit?

Six variables account for most of the price spread within a single community, and only two are visible in a listing photograph. Floor and view move an apartment price by 15% to 30% between the lowest and highest comparable units in one tower, and finish condition moves it by a further 10% to 20%.

- Floor and view, steeper where the outlook is protected by a park or waterway
- Finish condition, where the gap usually exceeds the refurbishment cost
- Service charge history, capitalised by every buyer modelling net yield
- Tenancy status, which cuts both ways depending on the rent locked in
- Parking allocation, a binary in older towers
- Payment terms, worth 2% to 4% in a slow market

Floor and view move an apartment price by 15% to 30% between the lowest and highest comparable units in the same tower. The premium is steeper where the view is protected by a permanent feature such as a park, a waterway or a golf course, and shallower where it can be built out.

Finish condition moves it by 10% to 20%. A unit handed over eight years ago with original fittings prices below a refurbished neighbour, and the gap is usually larger than the refurbishment cost, which is where value is created on a resale purchase.

Service charge history moves it more than most buyers price in. A rising charge is capitalised into resale value by any buyer modelling net yield, and it should be capitalised at the offer stage too. The method for reading it is in [service charges by area](/guides/service-charges-dubai-by-area/).

Tenancy status cuts both ways. A tenanted unit with a below-market rent locked for another eighteen months is worth less to an investor and considerably less to an owner-occupier, who cannot take possession. A vacant unit in a strong letting season is worth a premium.

Parking allocation is a binary in some towers and a material discount where a unit has none.

Payment terms matter at the margin. A cash buyer able to complete in three weeks has real negotiating leverage against a mortgaged buyer with a six-week approval process, and that leverage is worth 2% to 4% on price in a slow market.

## How do prices relate to yields?

Prices and yields relate inversely and reliably in Dubai. The communities at the bottom of the price table produce gross yields of 7.0% to 9.2% and the ones at the top produce 4.2% to 6.2%, because rents compress far less across the city than prices do across the same distance.

A studio in Discovery Gardens at AED 480,000 and a one-bedroom in Downtown at AED 1.75 million do not rent for proportionally different amounts. The Downtown unit commands a large premium in absolute rent and a much smaller premium relative to its price, which is why its gross yield lands at 4.8% to 6.2% against 7.0% to 8.5% at the entry end.

That relationship is what makes price alone a poor basis for a purchase decision. ## How is the official index built, and what does it miss?

The Dubai Land Department index is built from registered transactions, which is both its strength and the source of its two limitations. A registered transaction is a price actually paid, after negotiation, verified at a trustee office, and no listing portal or valuation model covering the same 12 months can claim that.

Its limitations are timing and mix. Registration happens at completion rather than at agreement, so the index lags a turning market. And an index built from whatever transacted reflects what sold rather than what a fixed basket is worth, which is why a heavy off-plan quarter can show a lower average without any property having fallen.

Its strength is that a registered transaction is a price actually paid, after negotiation, verified at a trustee office. No listing portal, agency report or valuation model has that property. When the index moves, something real has moved.

Its first limitation is timing. A transaction is registered at completion, which is weeks after the price was agreed and, on an off-plan sale, potentially years before the unit exists. The index therefore describes a market that was, not one that is, and in a turning market it lags. For a buyer this is a feature rather than a defect, because a purchase decision benefits from confirmed data rather than sentiment, but it means the index will not call a turn.

Its second limitation is mix. An index built from whatever transacted in a period reflects what sold, not what a fixed basket is worth. A quarter with heavy off-plan volume in an outer community and thin prime activity will show a lower average than the previous quarter without any individual property having fallen. Reading a headline average as a change in value is the most common misreading of this data.

The way to use it correctly is at community and product level rather than city level, and alongside the specific comparables for the building or cluster in question. The index tells you the direction of a segment; the comparables tell you the level of a unit.

## Why price per square foot beats total price

Price per square foot beats total price because a headline figure is a function of two variables and only one of them is about value. A two-bedroom at AED 1.5 million tells you nothing until you know whether it is 900 square feet at AED 1,667 or 1,400 square feet at AED 1,071, and those are different market tiers.

A two-bedroom at AED 1.5 million tells you nothing until you know whether it is 900 square feet or 1,400. The first is AED 1,667 per square foot and the second AED 1,071, and those sit in different market tiers entirely. Unit sizes in Dubai vary far more than prices do, because developers size to a price point rather than to a standard.

There are three traps in the metric itself, and all three are avoidable.

The first is the area basis. Dubai listings usually quote built-up area, but some quote saleable or gross area including a share of common space, and the difference can be 10% to 20%. Comparing a price per square foot calculated on two different bases produces a meaningless number. Confirm which basis is in use before comparing.

The second is villas. A villa price divided by built-up area ignores the plot, which is the component that holds value. Two villas at the same price per built square foot on plots differing by 40% are not comparable. For villas, price the plot separately.

The third is the balcony. Large terraces inflate built-up area and contribute far less to rent than internal space does, so a unit with a very high external ratio looks cheap per square foot and lets like a smaller apartment.

## What has the cycle actually done?

The cycle has moved segments rather than a single market, and the segment behaviour has been consistent enough to be useful. The 2021 to 2023 phase lifted almost everything, which is why it produced so many confident generalisations, and what followed has separated supply-constrained prime from mid-market stock absorbing large handover pipelines.

The 2021 to 2023 phase was a broad repricing driven by inbound relocation, a wide nationality mix of buyers and a supply pipeline that had been paused. It lifted almost everything, which is why it produced so many confident generalisations that have not held since.

What followed has been segmented. Prime waterfront and established villa communities, where supply is fixed or nearly so, have behaved differently from mid-market apartment stock in communities with large handover pipelines. The mechanism is straightforward: a community delivering several thousand units into a twelve-month window competes with its own resale market, and the price effect is local rather than city-wide.

That is the single most useful thing to take from the cycle. The variable that predicts a community's price behaviour over the next two years is its handover pipeline, and the pipeline is visible in advance because off-plan registration makes it public. A buyer can look up what is scheduled to complete in a community and when, and that is a better input than any forecast.

The currency dimension sits underneath all of it. The dirham is pegged to the dollar, which removes the exchange-rate risk that dominates most emerging property markets and imports US interest rate policy into UAE mortgage pricing. For a dollar-denominated buyer that is close to a neutral position. For a sterling, euro or rupee buyer the peg means the Dubai purchase carries a currency view whether or not the buyer intended one.

## How does the RERA rent cap feed back into price?

The RERA cap feeds back into price directly, and it is the least-modelled input in Dubai yield projections. Increases at renewal are banded under Decree No. 43 of 2013, from no increase permitted where the rent sits within 10% of the area average up to a maximum of 25% where it sits more than half below.

Rent increases at renewal are capped by the RERA rental index under Decree No. 43 of 2013, banded by how far the current rent sits below the area average. Where the rent is within 10% of the average, no increase is permitted at all. The bands rise through 5%, 10%, 15% and 20% to a maximum of 25% where the rent is more than half below the average. The full method is in [the RERA rent increase calculator](/guides/dubai-rent-increase-calculator-rera/).

What this means for pricing is that two identical units can carry different forward income. A unit let at 35% below the index average has a lawful 15% uplift available at the next renewal. A unit already at market has none. The first is worth more, and the listing shows neither.

It also means that in a community where rents have risen sharply, the gap between contract rents and market rents is where the value sits. Buying a tenanted unit with a stale rent is buying a rent increase, subject to the cap and to the notice requirement. Buying a freshly re-let unit at market is buying today's yield with no built-in growth.

For a buyer running the numbers, the correct question about a tenanted unit is not what it earns now but what the index permits at the next renewal, and that is answerable in the Dubai REST app before making an offer.

## How should a buyer build a comparable set?

A comparable set is five to eight registered transactions, tightly matched, within the last six months. Match on building first, then floor band, then size band, then view orientation, then condition, because a comparable six floors below in the same tower is a stronger signal than a same-month sale in a different building.

Match on building first, then on floor band, then on size band, then on view orientation, then on condition. A comparable from a different tower in the same community is a weaker signal than a comparable from six floors below in the same tower, because service charge, lift performance, parking and management differ by building.

Discard listings from the set entirely. A listing is an asking price and belongs in a separate exercise about seller expectations. Registered transactions are the evidence.

Adjust explicitly rather than intuitively. Higher floor, add a percentage. No parking, deduct one. Fully refurbished against original condition, adjust for the refurbishment cost plus a margin. Writing the adjustments down converts a feeling into an offer that can be defended in a negotiation.

Where the comparable set is thin, which is the normal position for villas and for anything above roughly AED 5 million, widen the time window before widening the location, because a nine-month-old transaction in the same building is a better guide than a same-month transaction in a different one.

## Valuation against price on a mortgage

Valuation and price are different numbers produced by different processes, and the gap between them is the buyer's cash. On a AED 4 million villa a 5% shortfall is AED 200,000 payable at completion on top of the deposit and the fees, and shortfalls are more common on villas and prime apartments than on mid-market stock.

The agreed price is what the parties negotiated. The valuation is what a bank-approved valuer certifies, using their own comparable set and their own adjustments, on behalf of a lender managing downside risk rather than a buyer managing an opportunity.

Valuers price conservatively where evidence is thin, which is why villas and prime apartments show valuation shortfalls more often than mid-market apartments do. On a AED 4 million villa a 5% shortfall is AED 200,000 that the buyer funds in cash, on top of the deposit and the fees.

Three practical responses. Get an indicative valuation before agreeing a price on any leveraged purchase above the mid-market. Build the possibility of a shortfall into the cash plan rather than into the hope. And treat a valuation materially below an agreed price as information rather than as an obstacle, because a valuer with a comparable set the buyer has not seen may be describing the market more accurately than the negotiation did.

## How much room is there to negotiate?

There is more room to negotiate than the listing implies and less than a first-time buyer hopes. A unit listed under 30 days typically transacts within 2% to 4% of asking; at 90 days the range widens to 5% to 9%; beyond six months the reason usually stops being price and becomes an arrear, a tenancy or a title complication.

A unit listed for under thirty days in a community with active demand typically transacts within 2% to 4% of asking. The same unit at ninety days transacts within 5% to 9%, because the seller has now seen the market's answer to the asking price. Beyond six months the discount widens further and the reason usually stops being price: an unresolved service charge arrear, a structural issue, a title complication or a tenancy the buyer cannot dislodge.

Investor sellers negotiate on numbers and owner-occupier sellers negotiate on circumstances. An investor holding a unit that yields below target will move on price to redeploy capital. An owner-occupier who has already committed to a new home will move on price to meet a date. Neither is visible in a listing, and both are answerable with one question to the agent about why the seller is selling.

Completion speed is the most underused lever. A cash buyer who can complete in three weeks against a mortgaged buyer needing six is worth 2% to 4% on price in a slow market, and that advantage disappears entirely the moment the buyer mentions it too early. Establish the price first, then the terms.

## Do off-plan and ready prices mean the same thing?

Off-plan and ready prices do not mean the same thing, and comparing them directly is the most common pricing error in this market. An off-plan price buys a future asset in instalments with 4% Oqood at booking; a ready price buys an inspectable asset with a documented service charge and a verifiable rent.

An off-plan price is a price for a future asset, paid in instalments, with a 4% Oqood registration at booking. A ready price is a price for an existing asset with an inspectable finish, a documented service charge, a verifiable rent and a 4% transfer fee at completion. The launch price sitting 10% below comparable ready stock is not a 10% discount, it is the market's price for accepting delivery risk and forgoing income during construction.

The correct comparison holds the timeline constant. Take the off-plan price, add the Oqood registration, add the opportunity cost of capital across the construction period at whatever rate the buyer's alternative use of the money would earn, and extend that period by the full grace period in the contract rather than the marketed handover date. Set the result against the ready price plus its transfer fee. On a four-year build with a twelve-month grace period, a launch discount of 10% is frequently consumed entirely.

That does not make off-plan the wrong product. It makes the launch discount the wrong reason to choose it. The right reasons are the payment profile and the horizon, both of which are set out on the [Dubai off-plan](/uae/dubai/off-plan/) hub.

## What does the handover pipeline do to a community's prices?

The handover pipeline is the most predictive and least used piece of public information in this market. Off-plan registration makes it visible years ahead, and a community absorbing several thousand units inside 12 months sees landlords competing for the same tenant pool, which caps rents before it moves prices.

Off-plan registration makes the supply pipeline visible years ahead. A buyer can establish how many units are scheduled to complete in a given community and in which quarters, and that number does more to explain the next two years of rents and prices in that community than any macro view.

The mechanism runs through rent before it runs through price. A community absorbing several thousand handovers inside twelve months sees landlords competing for the same tenant pool, which caps rents and lengthens void periods. Lower achieved rent lowers the yield an investor will pay for, and the price follows. In a community with no material pipeline, the same demand meets fixed supply and the effect reverses.

Two refinements matter. Handover schedules slip, and slippage smooths the shock rather than removing it, so a pipeline concentrated on paper is usually a longer and shallower pipeline in practice. And the unit mix matters as much as the count: two thousand studios and one-bedrooms compete for a different tenant from two thousand three-bedroom villas, so a large pipeline in a different product type is not a threat to a given unit.

The practical instruction for anyone buying for income is to check the pipeline for the specific community and product type before committing, and to prefer a community where the pipeline is behind rather than ahead. That single check separates a yield that holds from one that is about to be competed away.

## Reading a price in three numbers

A Dubai price only means something alongside three other numbers, and a buyer who writes all four down before making an offer is doing more analysis than most of the market. None of the four requires a paid data subscription, and all four are available before the emotional commitment to a specific apartment.

The price per square foot, on a confirmed area basis, against five to eight matched registered transactions.

The service charge per square foot, from the approved budget rather than the listing, because it is the difference between the gross yield advertised and the net yield received, and it is capitalised into resale value by every future buyer.

The current rent against the RERA index average, because that gap is the lawful rent growth available and it is not visible in the price.

The community handover pipeline for the next eight quarters, because that is what decides whether the current rent holds.

None of the four requires a paid data subscription. The transactions and the index are in the Dubai REST app, the service charge budget comes from the owners association through the seller, and the pipeline is a matter of public registration. What they require is the discipline to gather them before the emotional commitment to a specific apartment, which is the part most buyers skip.

## What do transaction costs do to the price you should pay?

Transaction costs set a floor under the holding period, and the arithmetic is worth doing before rather than after. Entry runs 6% to 7% cash and closer to 8% mortgaged, exit runs about 2%, so a full round trip costs 8% to 10% of the purchase price before the property has done anything at all.

Entry costs run 6% to 7% on a cash resale and closer to 8% with a mortgage. Exit costs are lower, roughly 2% for agency commission plus VAT and the developer no-objection certificate, with no capital gains tax. A full round trip is therefore about 8% to 10% of the purchase price before the property has done anything.

On a mid-market apartment netting 5.4% a year, recovering that round trip from income alone takes roughly eighteen to twenty-two months. On a prime apartment netting 3.8%, it takes closer to two and a half years. On a villa netting 4.2% with a higher absolute cost base, the same.

Two conclusions follow that a price table cannot show. A purchase intended to be held under two years is very unlikely to work on income alone, and needs capital growth to justify it, which converts an investment into a forecast. And the entry cost differential between emirates is not a rounding item: Abu Dhabi's 3% to 4% all-in stack against Dubai's 6% to 7% shortens the break-even period by roughly a third on comparable yields, which is set out on [property for sale in Abu Dhabi](/uae/abu-dhabi/).

The price you should pay is therefore a function of how long the money is staying, and the honest version of the question is not what the property is worth but what it is worth to hold for the period actually planned.
