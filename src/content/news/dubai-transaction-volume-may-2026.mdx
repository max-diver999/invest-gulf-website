## What did May 2026 transaction data show?

May 2026 transaction volume is Dubai Land Department's monthly print of ready and off-plan deals, and the market recorded **AED 25.6 billion**, up **12%** versus April 2026. Off-plan alone represented about **64%** of value, roughly **AED 16.4 billion**. Invest Gulf reads that mix as continued developer-pipeline dominance, not a ready-stock drought.

Dubai Land Department reported **AED 25.6 billion** in property transactions for May 2026, a **12%** rise versus April 2026, with off-plan capturing about **64%** of value (**AED 16.4 billion**). The print sits on top of 2025's **205,000** full-year deals and follows January 2026's **AED 107.9 billion** quarterly start, so May looks like continuity rather than a one-month outlier. Volume at this level supports liquidity for resale and refinancing, but individual unit pricing still varies by community, product type, and service charge load. Buyers should not treat the emirate-wide total as a substitute for tower-level comps or Ejari rent evidence. Golden Visa planning still hinges on **AED 2 million+** registered freehold where eligibility rules apply, and the **4% DLD** transfer or Oqood line remains in every all-in cost model. Use the monthly figure to gauge market heat, then underwrite the specific building, payment plan, and net yield after fees.

## Why does off-plan still dominate deal value?

Off-plan still dominates deal value because developers keep launching master-community phases with long payment plans, and buyers accept construction risk for entry pricing that typically undercuts ready stock. May's **64%** off-plan share of **AED 25.6 billion** fits the **60-70%** band seen through 2025 across the market.

Developer sales strategies have adapted to this volume environment. Major developers now structure **3-5 year** post-handover plans that reduce initial capital needs and widen the international buyer pool. Foreign buyers managing currency exposure across markets often prefer staged instalments to a full ready-cash ticket. Our analysis links that structure to sustained off-plan value share, not a temporary campaign. May 2026 is a continuity print after January's **AED 107.9 billion** quarterly start, not a one-month spike. Structural supports still include Golden Visa thresholds at **AED 2 million+** registered freehold, no individual capital gains tax on typical residential resale, and **6-8%** gross yields in several established communities (verify per building).

International participation remains a driver: cross-border buyers use Dubai as a dollar-linked asset hub with clear freehold registration in designated zones. High-volume Dubai areas typically mean communities with deep ready stock and active off-plan pipelines: **Dubai Marina**, **Downtown**, **Business Bay**, and **JVC** for completed units, plus **MBR City**, **Dubai Hills**, and **Dubai South** for launches. History still points to those clusters for most of the **AED 25.6 billion** print.

Investors watching the rest of 2026 typically treat high transaction value as a liquidity signal, not a waiver of **4% DLD**, escrow rules, or service charge drift on completion. Off-plan buyers still need SPA schedules and RERA registration before booking, even when monthly volume prints near **AED 25.6 billion**.
