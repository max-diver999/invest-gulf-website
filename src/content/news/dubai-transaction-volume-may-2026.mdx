Dubai Land Department reported AED 25.6 billion in property deals for May 2026, up 12% from April. The strong numbers follow Dubai's record-breaking 2025 with 205,000 transactions.

<TldrBlock text={"Dubai property transactions totalled AED 25.6 billion in May 2026, with off-plan developments capturing 64% of the volume. This performance maintains Dubai's strong momentum following January 2026's record quarterly start of AED 107.9 billion."} />

## What did May 2026 transaction data show?

DLD reported **AED 25.6 billion** in property transactions for May 2026, a **12%** increase versus April 2026. Ready and off-plan deals combined; off-plan alone represented about **64%** of value, roughly **AED 16.4 billion**.

| Metric | May 2026 | Context |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Total volume | AED 25.6B | +12% vs April 2026 |
| Off-plan share | 64% | In line with 2025 60-70% band |
| 2025 full-year deals | 205,000 | Baseline for comparison |
| Q1 2026 start | AED 107.9B quarter | Momentum into May |

Volume at this level supports liquidity for resale and refinancing, but individual unit pricing still varies by community, product type, and service charge load.

## Why does off-plan still dominate deal value?

Off-plan captured **64%** of May value because developers continue to launch master-community phases with long payment plans and because buyers accept construction risk for entry pricing.

Developer sales strategies have adapted to this volume environment. Major developers now structure payment plans extending 3-5 years post-handover, reducing initial capital requirements and broadening buyer accessibility. This approach particularly benefits international investors managing currency exposures across multiple markets.

For developer and yield context, see [best Dubai developers for rental yield](/guides/best-dubai-developers-rental-yield/) and [best off-plan areas in Dubai 2026](/guides/best-off-plan-areas-dubai-2026/).

## How does May fit the trend since January 2026?

May's performance builds on January 2026's record quarterly start of **AED 107.9 billion**, establishing Dubai as a sustained high-volume market. Structural supports include Golden Visa thresholds on eligible freehold, no individual capital gains tax on typical residential resale, and rental yields that still average **6-8%** in several established communities (verify per building).

International participation remains a driver: cross-border buyers use Dubai as a dollar-linked asset hub with clear freehold registration in designated zones.

## Which areas typically lead monthly volume?

While granular May 2026 area splits may publish later, history points to **Dubai Marina**, **Downtown**, **Business Bay**, and **JVC** for ready stock, plus **MBR City**, **Dubai Hills**, and **Dubai South** for off-plan launches.

Area choice should follow tenant demand and handover risk, not headline volume alone. See [best areas to buy property in Dubai](/guides/best-areas-buy-property-dubai/).

## What should investors watch for the rest of 2026?

High transaction value does not remove **4% DLD**, escrow rules, or service charge drift on completion. Off-plan buyers should still read SPA payment schedules and RERA project registration before booking.

Tax and fee framing for total cost: [Dubai property taxes explained](/guides/dubai-property-taxes-explained/). Off-plan consumer protections: [Dubai cooling-off period](/guides/dubai-cooling-off-period-off-plan/).

If monthly volume stays above **AED 20 billion**, expect continued developer launches; if volume softens while launches continue, selective communities may see longer resale timelines.
